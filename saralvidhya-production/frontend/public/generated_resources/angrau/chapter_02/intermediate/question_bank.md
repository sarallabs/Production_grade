# Question 1
**Question:** Compare the larval form of a butterfly (Lepidoptera) with that of a ground beetle (Carabidae). Which pair of larval classifications correctly categorizes these two insects based on body structure and appendages?

**Options:**
- A) Butterfly: Eruciform; Ground Beetle: Campodeiform
- B) Butterfly: Scarabaeiform; Ground Beetle: Vermiform
- C) Butterfly: Elateriform; Ground Beetle: Eruciform
- D) Butterfly: Campodeiform; Ground Beetle: Scarabaeiform

**Correct Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Correct. Lepidopteran larvae (caterpillars) are eruciform (cylindrical with thoracic legs and abdominal prolegs). Ground beetle larvae (Carabidae) are campodeiform (elongate, active, dorso-ventrally flattened predators with well-developed thoracic legs).
**Source Reference:** 3.1 Larval Types (txt_013) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 2

**Question:** A student examines an insect pupa enclosed inside a hard, dark-brown capsule formed by the retained 4th/5th larval cuticle. What pupal classification type and insect order does this represent?

# Question 3
**Question:** Which of the following statements correctly differentiates facultative diapause from obligatory diapause?

**Options:**
- A) Facultative diapause occurs in every generation regardless of environment (univoltine), whereas obligatory diapause is triggered by day length.
- B) Obligatory diapause is a genetically enforced arrest occurring in every generation regardless of external conditions, whereas facultative diapause is induced by token environmental stimuli (such as photoperiod) in multivoltine species.
- C) Obligatory diapause occurs only during summer drought (aestivation), whereas facultative diapause occurs only during winter (hibernation).
- D) Obligatory diapause is regulated by PTTH, whereas facultative diapause is regulated exclusively by Bursicon.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Obligatory diapause is a hereditary, gene-controlled arrest that occurs in every generation (univoltine insects). Facultative diapause allows continuous generations unless induced by environmental token stimuli like photoperiod or temperature.
**Source Reference:** 6.2 Diapause Types (txt_016, txt_017) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 2

# Question 4
**Question:** In hypermetamorphosis (as seen in blister beetles, Meloidae), what is the functional advantage of the specialized 1st instar larva (triungulin) compared to later larval instars?

**Options:**
- A) The triungulin is stationary and secretes a puparium, while later instars build cocoons.
- B) The triungulin is an active, campodeiform crawler adapted for searching and attaching to host bees, whereas later instars become scarabaeiform/grub-like feeders.
- C) The triungulin possesses fully functional wings for aerial dispersal, whereas later instars lose their wings.
- D) The triungulin feeds exclusively on plant nectar, whereas later instars are carnivorous.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Hypermetamorphosis features morphologically distinct larval instars: the 1st instar (triungulin) is active, long-legged, and campodeiform for host searching, while subsequent instars become fat, sedentary, grub-like feeders inside host nests.
**Source Reference:** 4. Hypermetamorphosis (txt_014) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 2

# Question 5
**Question:** Differentiate exarate pupae from obtect pupae based on appendage attachment and taxonomic distribution.

**Options:**
- A) Exarate pupae have appendages tightly glued to the body wall (Lepidoptera); Obtect pupae have free appendages (Coleoptera).
- B) Exarate pupae have free, unattached appendages (Coleoptera, Hymenoptera); Obtect pupae have appendages firmly glued to the pupal body wall (Lepidoptera, some Diptera).
- C) Exarate pupae possess functional mandibles; Obtect pupae are enclosed in a larval puparium.
- D) Exarate pupae occur exclusively in hemimetabolous insects; Obtect pupae occur only in ametabolous insects.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. In exarate pupae, the legs and wing pads are free and movable away from the body. In obtect pupae, appendages are glued tightly against the body surface by a chitinous secretion.

# Question 6
**Question:** An entomologist records the developmental threshold of a caterpillar population exposed to decreasing daylight hours in autumn. At 11.5 hours of light per day, exactly 50% of the population enters diapause. What technical term denotes this photoperiodic threshold?

**Options:**
- A) Absolute photoperiod
- B) Critical day length
- C) Diapause induction quota
- D) Thermal summation limit

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. The critical day length is defined as the specific photoperiod (hours of light per 24h cycle) at which 50% of an insect population is induced to enter diapause.

# Question 7
**Question:** Analyze the biological difference between Exopterygota and Endopterygota regarding wing development during post-embryonic growth.

**Options:**
- A) Exopterygotes develop wings internally inside imaginal discs during the pupal stage; Endopterygotes develop wing pads externally on nymphal segments.
- B) Exopterygotes develop wing pads externally on the thoracic dorsum over successive nymphal moults; Endopterygotes develop wings internally from invaginated epidermal imaginal discs, everted during pupation.
- C) Exopterygotes undergo complete metamorphosis; Endopterygotes undergo no metamorphosis.
- D) Exopterygotes have 4 developmental stages; Endopterygotes have 3 developmental stages.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Exopterygota (hemimetabolous insects) show external wing pad development in nymphs. Endopterygota (holometabolous insects) develop wings internally within imaginal discs during larval stages, which evertoexternally during the pupal stage.
**Source Reference:** 2.2 & 2.3 Wing Development Pathways (txt_011, txt_012) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 2

# Question 9
**Question:** How do decticous pupae differ from adecticous pupae structurally and functionally?

**Options:**
- A) Decticous pupae have functional, articulated mandibles used to chew out of the cocoon; Adecticous pupae lack functional mandibles.
- B) Decticous pupae are enclosed in a puparium; Adecticous pupae have free appendages.
- C) Decticous pupae occur in Diptera; Adecticous pupae occur in Neuroptera.
- D) Decticous pupae undergo diapause; Adecticous pupae undergo quiescence.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Correct. Decticous pupae possess strong, articulated mandibles that the pharate adult uses to chew through the pupal shell or cocoon (e.g., Neuroptera, Trichoptera). Adecticous pupae lack functional mandibles (e.g., Lepidoptera, Diptera).
**Source Reference:** 3.2 Pupal Types (txt_014) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 2

# Question 10
**Question:** A scarabaeiform larva (such as a white grub of *Holotrichia*) is discovered in soil. Which anatomical features distinguish this larva from an eruciform larva?

**Options:**
- A) C-shaped stout body with well-developed thoracic legs and no abdominal prolegs.
- B) Slender cylindrical body with 5 pairs of abdominal prolegs and crochets.
- C) Legless maggot-like body with head reduced into mouthhooks.
- D) Flattened active body with long cerci and tail filaments.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Correct. Scarabaeiform larvae (grubs) are C-shaped, fleshy, stout soil-dwellers with a distinct sclerotized head capsule and 3 pairs of thoracic legs, but lacking abdominal prolegs. Eruciform larvae possess abdominal prolegs with crochets.
**Source Reference:** 3.1 Larval Types (txt_013) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 2
