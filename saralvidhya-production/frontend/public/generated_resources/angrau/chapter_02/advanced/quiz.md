# Question 1
**Question:** Evaluate the physiological consequence on a 5th instar silkworm larva if its corpora allata are surgically allatectomized (removed) early in the 3rd instar. What developmental pathway will occur?

**Options:**
- A) The larva will continue moulting indefinitely into giant supernumerary 6th and 7th instar larvae without ever pupating.
- B) The larva will undergo premature apolysis and pupate precociously into a miniature pupa and dwarf adult after the next moult.
- C) The larva will immediately enter obligatory diapause due to lack of PTTH.
- D) The larva will melt its cuticle and die immediately without undergoing apolysis.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Juvenile Hormone (JH) secreted by the corpora allata maintains larval characteristics. Surgical removal of corpora allata (allatectomy) drops JH titers to zero prematurely, causing the young larva to skip remaining larval instars and undergo precocious metamorphosis into a miniature pupa/adult.
**Source Reference:** 5.3 Endocrine Control & JH Titers (txt_015) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 2
**Question:** Assess the biochemical interplay between Ecdysone and Juvenile Hormone (JH) titers during the final larval instar of a holometabolous insect to explain how pupal differentiation is triggered.

**Options:**
- A) High JH titer combined with zero Ecdysone triggers pupation.
- B) High Ecdysone titer in the presence of a critical low/declining JH titer induces the epidermal cells to switch commitment from larval cuticle synthesis to pupal cuticle synthesis.
- C) Equal high concentrations of both JH and Ecdysone prevent pupation and induce adult structures directly.
- D) Ecdysone inhibits PTTH release from the brain, which shuts down corpora allata activity completely.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Ecdysone acts as the master gene-activating hormone for moulting, but the *qualitative nature* of the moult (larval-to-larval vs larval-to-pupal vs pupal-to-adult) is determined by the titer of JH. A high Ecdysone pulse in the presence of low JH reprograms epidermal cells for pupal differentiation.
**Source Reference:** 5. Hormonal Regulation of Metamorphosis (txt_015) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 3
**Question:** An experimenter exposes diapausing pupae of the cabbage white butterfly (*Pieris brassicae*) to warm temperature (25°C) immediately after diapause initiation in autumn, without prior cold exposure (chilling). What will be the developmental outcome?

**Options:**
- A) The pupae will break diapause immediately and emerge as adults within 48 hours.
- B) The pupae will remain trapped in prolonged diapause because the physiological mechanism ("diapause development" or cold refractoriness completion) requires a specific chilling period to restore PTTH secretion.
- C) The pupae will transform into giant hypermetabolous triungulin larvae.
- D) The pupae will undergo immediate apolysis into exarate adult-like nymphs.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Termination of physiological diapause typically requires a period of cold conditioning (thermo-refractory phase). Without chilling, the brain remains refractory to warm temperatures, preventing PTTH release and keeping the insect dormant despite favorable ambient temperatures.
**Source Reference:** 6.4 Diapause Termination & Environmental Conditioning (txt_016, txt_017) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 4
**Question:** Critique the following hypothesis: *"Insects undergoing facultative diapause monitor daily photoperiod to sense immediate nutritional depletion."* Is this statement accurate?

**Options:**
- A) Yes, because light energy directly fuels gut enzyme synthesis in diapausing larvae.
- B) No, because photoperiod acts as a token stimulus—an environmental cue that predicts impending seasonal adversity long before actual temperature drop or food depletion occurs.
- C) Yes, because photoperiod is only detected by abdominal oenocytes during feeding.
- D) No, because facultative diapause is strictly gene-fixed in every generation and ignores photoperiod completely.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Photoperiod acts as a *token stimulus*—a reliable, predictive environmental calendar cue. Insects do not use day length as a measure of current starvation, but rather as an advance warning trigger to initiate physiological metabolic suppression before winter or drought sets in.
**Source Reference:** 6.3 Photoperiod & Token Stimuli (txt_017) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 5
**Question:** In a laboratory colony of *Bombyx mori* (silkworm), diapause in eggs is determined during the embryonic development of the *mother* generation under long-day conditions. How should an insect physiologist classify this transgenerational endocrine mechanism?

**Options:**
- A) Paternal inheritance of Bursicon hormone through sperm transfer.
- B) Maternal neuroendocrine induction, where photoperiod experienced by the female parent regulates Diapause Hormone (DH) secretion from her suboesophageal ganglion, acting on developing oocytes.
- C) Direct photoreception by the eggshell (chorion) after oviposition.
- D) Quiescent arrest caused by environmental cold operating on the newly laid zygote.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. In *Bombyx mori*, egg diapause is maternally determined: photoperiod and temperature experienced by the female during her embryonic/larval stages control whether her suboesophageal ganglion secretes Diapause Hormone (DH) into her eggs during oogenesis.
**Source Reference:** 6.4 Stage Specificity: Embryonic Diapause in *Bombyx mori* (tbl_005, txt_016) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 6
**Question:** Compare the ecological trade-offs between Holometabolous and Hemimetabolous life cycle strategies. Which statement best explains why ~88% of insect species are Holometabolous?

**Options:**
- A) Holometabola eliminate the pupal stage, allowing larvae to reproduce directly.
- B) Holometabola completely decouple larval feeding niches from adult reproductive/dispersal niches, reducing intra-specific competition for food and habitat.
- C) Hemimetabola undergo hypermetamorphosis, which requires higher energetic outlay during every instar.
- D) Holometabola possess wing pads externally, protecting them from predators during immature stages.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Complete metamorphosis allows extreme ecological specialization: larvae specialize entirely on feeding and growth (often in hidden niches), while adults specialize in dispersal and reproduction. This separation reduces resource competition between immatures and adults.
**Source Reference:** 2.3 Ecological Significance of Holometabola (txt_012) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 7
**Question:** Formulate the expected result when synthetic Juvenile Hormone Analog (JHA / Methoprene) is sprayed onto a field population of 5th instar crop-damaging caterpillars just before their pupal moult.

**Options:**
- A) The caterpillars will instantly molt into fertile adults within 12 hours.
- B) The exogenous JHA will disrupt the normal drop in JH titer, resulting in developmental failure, morphogenetic monstrosities (larval-pupal intermediates), and sterility/death.
- C) The caterpillars will enter obligatory aestivation and resume feeding next spring.
- D) The JHA will stimulate prothoracic glands to produce excess Ecdysone, doubling adult size.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Exogenous Juvenile Hormone Analogs (JHAs) act as insect growth regulators (IGRs). Applying JHA when JH should naturally be zero prevents normal pupal differentiation, creating non-viable larval-pupal chimeras that fail to emerge or reproduce.
**Source Reference:** 5.3 Applied Endocrinology & IGRs (txt_015) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 8
**Question:** Analyze the hormonal control mechanism operating during adult eclosion (emergence from pupa). Which specific neurohormone triggers the post-eclosion tanning and hardening of the new adult cuticle?

**Options:**
- A) Ecdysone
- B) Prothoracicotropic hormone (PTTH)
- C) Bursicon
- D) Diapause Hormone (DH)

**Correct Answer:** C
**Rationale:** Correct. Bursicon is a neurotropic peptide hormone released from the ventral nerve cord after eclosion; it acts on epidermal cells to induce cuticular sclerotization (tanning and hardening) and wing expansion.

# Question 9
**Question:** Distinguish the structural characteristics of coarctate pupae in Diptera from decticous exarate pupae in Neuroptera during adult emergence.

**Options:**
- A) Coarctate adults use an inflatable frontal sac (ptilinum) to push off the puparium cap; Decticous pharate adults use articulated, functional mandibles to chew through the cocoon.
- B) Coarctate adults chew their way out using larval mandibles; Decticous adults dissolve the pupa with Ecdysone.
- C) Coarctate pupae have free appendages; Decticous pupae have appendages glued to the abdomen.
- D) Both pupal types rely exclusively on abdominal wriggling with no specialized structures.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Correct. In coarctate Diptera (Schizophora), the emerging adult inflates the ptilinum (an eversible head sac) to pop open the operculum of the puparium. In decticous pupae (e.g., lacewings), the pharate adult uses functional pupal mandibles to bite open the cocoon.
**Source Reference:** 3.2 Pupal Types & Emergence Adaptations (txt_014) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+

# Question 10
**Question:** Evaluate why obligatory diapause species are predominantly *univoltine* (one generation per year), whereas facultative diapause species are typically *multivoltine* (multiple generations per year).

**Options:**
- A) Obligatory diapause is genetically programmed to occur in every individual of every generation regardless of weather, limiting the species to one annual cycle; Facultative diapause allows continuous generations during favorable seasons until triggered by short photoperiod in autumn.
- B) Obligatory diapause requires high temperatures in summer; Facultative diapause occurs only in freezing polar regions.
- C) Obligatory diapause is induced by PTTH drops; Facultative diapause is induced by Ecdysone spikes.
- D) Univoltine species lack corpora allata glands entirely.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Correct. Obligatory diapause is a fixed genetic adaptation present in all individuals of a species, restricting it to one generation per year (univoltine). Facultative diapause allows flexible multivoltine cycles during spring/summer, halting development only when autumn environmental cues signal winter approach.
**Source Reference:** 6.2 Voltinism and Diapause Strategies (txt_016, txt_017) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 3+
