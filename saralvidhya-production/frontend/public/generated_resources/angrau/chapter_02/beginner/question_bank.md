# Question 1
**Question:** How does the lecture text define the term "morphogenesis" in insect post-embryonic development?

**Options:**
- A) The state of arrested development enforced by an internal physiological mechanism rather than concurrently unfavourable conditions.
- B) All post-embryonic changes that involve growth and moulting (larval stages), differentiation (pupal stages), and maturation and reproduction (adult stages) from egg stage to adult stage.
- C) The dramatic transformation in body form that links the larval, pupal, and adult phases of holometabolous insects only.
- D) The gradual external development of wing pads over successive nymphal instars in exopterygote insects.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Morphogenesis is defined as all post-embryonic developmental changes spanning growth and moulting in larvae, tissue differentiation in pupae, and maturation/reproduction in adults.
**Source Reference:** 1. Morphogenesis: The Framework of Post-Embryonic Development (txt_002, txt_008) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 1

- C) Aestivation

# Question 5
**Question:** What is the primary defining feature of Ametabolous development in Apterygote insects?

**Options:**
- A) The immature stage undergoes complete tissue histolysis inside a coarctate puparium.
- B) Development is direct, with young hatching as miniature adults lacking only fully developed sexual organs, and no pupal stage exists.
- C) Wings develop externally through five distinct nymphal instars.
- D) At least one larval instar differs radically in morphology and feeding habits from the rest.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Ametabolous insects (e.g., silverfish) undergo direct development without metamorphosis; the young resemble miniature adults and no pupal stage occurs.

# Question 6
**Question:** In Hemimetabolous (incomplete) metamorphosis, what term is consistently used to designate the immature stage between egg and adult?

**Options:**

- B) Nymph
- C) Pupa

**Question:** Which endocrine structure in the insect head secretes PTTH (Brain Hormone) to initiate the moulting cascade?

# Question 10
**Question:** How does the lecture text distinguish "quiescence" from "diapause"?

**Options:**
- A) Quiescence is genetically fixed in every generation, whereas diapause is an immediate response to sudden cold.
- B) Quiescence is an immediate, non-programmed response directly caused by unfavorable environmental conditions, whereas diapause is a physiologically enforced dormancy triggered in advance.
- C) Quiescence only occurs in adult beetles, whereas diapause occurs exclusively in eggs.
- D) Quiescence requires high Juvenile Hormone titers, whereas diapause requires high Ecdysone levels.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Correct. Quiescence is an immediate temporary arrest directly forced by adverse conditions (ceasing when conditions improve), while diapause is a neurohormonally mediated state of dormancy triggered in advance by token stimuli.
**Source Reference:** 6.1 Core Concepts: Quiescence vs Diapause (txt_016) | Mindmap Node Depth: Level 1
