### Card 1
**Mindmap Location:** Chapter 13 Digestive System > 1. Three Primary Regions > 1.1 Foregut (Stomodaeum)  
**Depth Level:** L-3  
**Bloom's Taxonomy:** Applying  
**Difficulty:** Medium  

**Q:** What structural modification allows the proventriculus of honeybees (*Apis mellifera*) to strain pollen out of crop nectar?  
**A:** Four triangular lip-valves lined with backward hair-spines pull pollen grains into the ventriculus while retaining nectar in the crop.

**Mindmap Location:** Chapter 13 Digestive System > 1. Three Primary Regions > 1.1 Foregut (Stomodaeum)  
**Depth Level:** L-3  
**Bloom's Taxonomy:** Analyzing  
**Difficulty:** Medium  

**Q:** Why does the foregut crop remain incapable of nutrient absorption despite holding digested food?  
**A:** The crop is lined with a thick, impermeable cuticular intima and lacks an epithelial brush border with microvilli.

**Mindmap Location:** Chapter 13 Digestive System > 1. Three Primary Regions > 1.1 Foregut (Stomodaeum)  
**Depth Level:** L-3

**Q:** What physiological valve function is performed by the stomodaeal (cardiac) valve at the foregut-midgut boundary?  
**A:** It serves as a one-way muscular sphincter preventing midgut digestive enzymes and food from regurgitating into the crop.

**Depth Level:** L-3  
**Bloom's Taxonomy:** Applying  
**Difficulty:** Medium  

**Q:** How does the origin of Type I Peritrophic Membrane differ from Type II Peritrophic Membrane?  
**A:** Type I PM is delaminated from microvilli along the entire midgut epithelium; Type II PM is continuously produced by an anterior cardiac ring organ.
