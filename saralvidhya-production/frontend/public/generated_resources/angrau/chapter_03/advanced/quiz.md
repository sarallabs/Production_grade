# Multiple Choice Questions

### Question 1

**Question:** A solid 1 m³ rock cube (total surface area = 6 m²) is fractured into smaller cubes of edge length 1 cm (10⁻² m). What is the resulting total exposed surface area?

- **A)** 60 m²
- **B)** 600 m² (a 100-fold expansion)
- **C)** 6,000 m²
- **D)** 60,000 m²

**Correct Answer:** B) 600 m² (a 100-fold expansion)

**Explanation / Rationale:** Number of cubes N = (100)³ = 10⁶. Surface area per cube = 6 * (0.01)² = 0.0006 m². Total area S = 10⁶ * 0.0006 = 600 m² (100-fold increase). (Key Takeaway: Subdividing a 1m cube into 1cm cubes produces a 100-fold surface area expansion.)

### Question 2

**Question:** Why do unconsolidated coarse gravel deposits weather significantly slower than fine volcanic ash deposits under identical rainfall regimes?

- **A)** Gravel clasts are composed entirely of organic matter.
- **B)** In gravel beds, water percolates between impermeable clasts rather than through the mineral matrix, whereas ash has high porosity and immense reactive surface area.
- **C)** Volcanic ash is completely impermeable to moisture.
- **D)** Gravel deposits experience 10x higher lightning strikes.

**Correct Answer:** B) In gravel beds, water percolates between impermeable clasts rather than through the mineral matrix, whereas ash has high porosity and immense reactive surface area.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Water bypasses gravel clasts via inter-particle voids without entering internal pores, while volcanic ash has high internal porosity and massive surface area. (Key Takeaway: Inter-particle percolation in gravels minimizes internal mineral contact compared to volcanic ash.)

### Question 3

**Question:** During an extreme thunderstorm, what primary physical mechanism enables high-voltage lightning discharges to fracture solid rock crags?

- **A)** Gradual chemical oxidation of feldspar lattices over several days.
- **B)** Instantaneous electrical superheating of mineral crystal lattices causing explosive localized differential thermal expansion.
- **C)** Continuous cryogenic ice expansion within joints.
- **D)** Electrochemical leaching of silica into groundwater.

**Correct Answer:** B) Instantaneous electrical superheating of mineral crystal lattices causing explosive localized differential thermal expansion.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Lightning delivers immense electrical energy that instantly superheats mineral crystals, creating violent thermal shock and explosive rock fragmentation. (Key Takeaway: Lightning causes instantaneous thermal superheating that shatters rock outcrops.)

- **B)** Its transport capacity escalates from moving pebbles/stones to carrying massive boulders weighing several tonnes.

### Question 5

**Question:** How does differential vertical wind abrasion create the distinctive morphology of desert Mushroom Rocks?

- **A)** Upper winds carry dense granite cobbles while lower winds carry pure air.
- **B)** Dense, abrasive sand grains saltate primarily within 1 meter of the desert surface, concentrating undercut grinding at the rock base while upper sections endure milder fine-silt abrasion.
- **C)** Desert bedrock dissolves chemically exclusively at the ground level due to high acidity.
- **D)** Termites consume the lower half of the rock monolith.

**Correct Answer:** B) Dense, abrasive sand grains saltate primarily within 1 meter of the desert surface, concentrating undercut grinding at the rock base while upper sections endure milder fine-silt abrasion.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Heavy quartz sand saltates in the lowest 1 meter, concentrating severe undercut abrasion near the base while upper rock zones remain wider. (Key Takeaway: Saltating sand concentrated in the bottom 1 meter undercuts rock pedestals.)

### Question 6

**Question:** Why does polymineralic Basalt weather faster than monomineralic or quartz-rich Granite despite basalt having a fine-grained crystalline texture?

- **A)** Basalt has twice the linear thermal expansion of sandstone.
- **B)** Basalt is dominated by ferromagnesian minerals (olivine, pyroxene, Ca-plagioclase) that are chemically far more unstable at surface conditions than the quartz and orthoclase of granite.
- **C)** Basalt is highly permeable like porous sandstone.
- **D)** Basalt reflects 90% of atmospheric solar radiation.

**Correct Answer:** B) Basalt is dominated by ferromagnesian minerals (olivine, pyroxene, Ca-plagioclase) that are chemically far more unstable at surface conditions than the quartz and orthoclase of granite.

**Explanation / Rationale:** According to Goldich's stability series, high-temperature ferromagnesian minerals in basalt are chemically less stable at surface T/P than quartz and alkali feldspar. (Key Takeaway: High-temperature mafic minerals in basalt decompose faster than quartz-rich granite.)

### Question 7

- **A)** Dark minerals exhibit zero thermal conductivity.
- **B)** Dark ferromagnesian minerals have lower albedo, absorbing solar radiation faster and reaching higher peak temperatures, creating steeper thermal gradients with cooler rock interiors.
- **C)** Dark minerals expand strictly in one dimension.
- **D)** Dark rocks are immune to frost wedging.

**Correct Answer:** B) Dark ferromagnesian minerals have lower albedo, absorbing solar radiation faster and reaching higher peak temperatures, creating steeper thermal gradients with cooler rock interiors.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Low albedo dark minerals absorb more solar energy, heating up to 20°C hotter than surrounding light minerals, driving severe differential thermal shear. (Key Takeaway: Low albedo dark minerals generate steep diurnal thermal expansion gradients.)

### Question 8

- **B)** They form stable coordination complexes with structural polyvalent metal cations (Fe³⁺, Al³⁺, Ca²⁺), pulling them out of crystal lattices into aqueous solution.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Organic ligands chelate and solubilize polyvalent cations from silicate crystal lattices, destabilizing the mineral framework. (Key Takeaway: Root chelating ligands strip structural cations from mineral lattices.)

- **D)** It cools soil temperature to below freezing.

**Correct Answer:** B) It elevates the partial pressure of CO₂ (pCO₂) in soil air up to 100x atmospheric levels, creating high concentrations of carbonic acid (H₂CO₃) that supplies reactive H⁺ ions.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Soil air pCO₂ reaches 10-100x atmospheric levels due to respiration, dissolving into soil water as carbonic acid that rapidly hydrolyzes silicates. (Key Takeaway: Elevated soil pore pCO₂ generates carbonic acid that drives mineral hydrolysis.)

### Question 10

**Question:** What fundamental geochemical consequence occurs when physical weathering pulverizes rock clasts down to colloidal clay dimensions (1 µm)?

- **A)** Specific surface area expands to millions of square meters per cubic meter, transforming mineral surfaces into massive ion-exchange and chemical reaction hubs.
- **B)** All chemical decomposition ceases immediately.
- **C)** Rock permeability drops to zero and soil formation terminates.
- **D)** The minerals transform entirely into metallic iron.

**Correct Answer:** A) Specific surface area expands to millions of square meters per cubic meter, transforming mineral surfaces into massive ion-exchange and chemical reaction hubs.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Colloidal reduction expands surface area to 6,000,000 m²/m³, vastly accelerating cation exchange, nutrient adsorption, and biochemical reactions. (Key Takeaway: Colloidal pulverization creates millions of square meters of reactive interface for soil genesis.)
