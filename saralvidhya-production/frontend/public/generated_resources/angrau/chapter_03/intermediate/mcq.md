# Multiple Choice Questions

### Question 1

**Question:** Why does coarse-textured porous Sandstone weather more rapidly than dense, crystalline Basalt?

- **A)** Sandstone contains higher concentrations of ferromagnesian olivine.
- **B)** The high permeability of porous sandstone allows deep water and air circulation into its internal pore network.
- **C)** Sandstone reflects 100% of incident solar radiation.
- **D)** Basalt dissolves instantly in pure rainwater.

**Correct Answer:** B) The high permeability of porous sandstone allows deep water and air circulation into its internal pore network.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Rock permeability is the primary determinant of weathering rate; porous sandstone permits rapid fluid ingress whereas dense basalt restricts flow. (Key Takeaway: Permeability controls weathering velocity by governing internal fluid circulation.)

### Question 2

**Question:** Under diurnal temperature fluctuations, how does the cubical thermal expansion of Quartz compare to that of Feldspar minerals?

- **A)** Quartz cubical expansion is half that of feldspar.
- **B)** Quartz cubical expansion is precisely twice (2.0x) that of feldspar.
- **C)** Quartz expands linearly while feldspar does not expand.
- **D)** Quartz and feldspar possess identical thermal expansion coefficients.

**Correct Answer:** B) Quartz cubical expansion is precisely twice (2.0x) that of feldspar.

**Explanation / Rationale:** The cubical thermal expansion of quartz is twice that of feldspars, generating intergranular micro-shear stresses during solar heating. (Key Takeaway: Quartz expands cubically at twice the rate of feldspar.)

### Question 3

**Question:** What bursting pressure is exerted by freezing water when confined within rigid rock fissures in alpine regions?

- **A)** 150 Mg m⁻²
- **B)** 500 Mg m⁻²
- **C)** 1465 Mg m⁻² (1465 tonnes/m²)
- **D)** 9000 Mg m⁻²

**Correct Answer:** C) 1465 Mg m⁻² (1465 tonnes/m²)

**Explanation / Rationale:** Freezing water in confined crevices expands by 9% and exerts a bursting pressure of 1465 Mg m⁻² (1465 tonnes/m² / ~14.4 MPa). (Key Takeaway: Confined frost wedging generates 1465 Mg m⁻² of bursting pressure.)

### Question 4

**Question:** According to the eolian transport power law, how does the particle-carrying capacity of wind scale with its velocity (v)?

- **A)** Proportional to velocity squared (v²)
- **B)** Proportional to velocity cubed (v³)
- **C)** Proportional to the sixth power of velocity (v⁶)
- **D)** Inversely proportional to velocity (1/v)

**Correct Answer:** C) Proportional to the sixth power of velocity (v⁶)

**Explanation / Rationale:** The sediment-carrying capacity of wind increases exponentially, proportional to the sixth power of its velocity (v⁶ law). (Key Takeaway: Wind sediment carrying capacity scales with the sixth power of velocity.)

### Question 5

**Question:** Why is mechanical rock disintegration significantly greater near a river's source than near its mouth?

- **A)** Water near the mouth is completely frozen year-round.
- **B)** River headwaters have steep gradients, high kinetic velocities, and carry coarse angular bedload that collides forcefully with bedrock.
- **C)** Rivers near their source contain high concentrations of synthetic industrial acids.
- **D)** River mouths experience higher diurnal solar radiation.

**Correct Answer:** B) River headwaters have steep gradients, high kinetic velocities, and carry coarse angular bedload that collides forcefully with bedrock.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Steep mountain headwaters provide high kinetic energy and angular suspended matter that maximize hydraulic rock abrasion. (Key Takeaway: High velocity and angular bedload make river sources zones of intense disintegration.)

### Question 8

**Question:** How do deep tap roots facilitate sub-surface chemical and physical weathering after plant death?

- **A)** They solidify into impermeable granite plugs.
- **B)** They decompose to leave open cylindrical macropores that funnel gravitational water and atmospheric gases deep into bedrock.
- **C)** They absorb all sub-surface oxygen, preventing chemical reactions.
- **D)** They compress bedrock into metamorphic quartzite.

**Correct Answer:** B) They decompose to leave open cylindrical macropores that funnel gravitational water and atmospheric gases deep into bedrock.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Decayed tap roots leave macropore channels that enable deep percolation of rainwater and air into sub-regolith rock strata. (Key Takeaway: Decayed root macropores channel water and air deep into subsoil rock layers.)

### Question 9

**Question:** What ecological phenomenon describes the biological mixing and vertical mass transport of soil and mineral particles by subterranean organisms?

- **A)** Exfoliation
- **B)** Cryofracture
- **C)** Pedoturbation
- **D)** Saltation

**Correct Answer:** C) Pedoturbation

**Explanation / Rationale:** Pedoturbation is the biological turning, churning, and vertical transport of soil and weathered mineral fragments by ants, termites, and burrowers. (Key Takeaway: Pedoturbation is the biological mass transport and mixing of soil horizons.)

### Question 10

**Question:** Why does physical fragmentation of a rock mass accelerate subsequent chemical mineral decomposition?

- **A)** It converts all silicate minerals into pure quartz.
- **B)** It exponentially increases the total exposed specific surface area, providing abundant reactive atomic sites for aqueous chemical attack.
- **C)** It raises the rock's temperature to magmatic melting points.
- **D)** It eliminates all moisture from the rock interior.

**Correct Answer:** B) It exponentially increases the total exposed specific surface area, providing abundant reactive atomic sites for aqueous chemical attack.

**Explanation / Rationale:** Chemical reactions occur at particle surfaces; physical fragmentation increases specific surface area exponentially, accelerating dissolution kinetics. (Key Takeaway: Physical fragmentation expands reactive surface area for chemical attack.)
