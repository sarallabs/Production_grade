# Multiple Select Questions

### Question 1

**Question:** A 1 m³ solid rock cube is progressively subdivided down to colloidal clay dimensions (1 µm). Which mathematical and geochemical statements are correct? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** Total exposed surface area expands from 6 m² up to 6,000,000 m² (6 km²).
- **B)** Specific surface area increases inversely with particle edge dimension (S ∝ 1/l).
- **C)** Chemical dissolution reaction velocity increases by orders of magnitude due to abundant reactive atomic sites.
- **D)** Total mass and mineral volume decrease to zero.
- **E)** The expanding surface area provides extensive negative charge sites for nutrient cation adsorption.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C, E

**Explanation / Rationale:** Pulverizing a 1m cube (6 m²) to 1 µm yields 6,000,000 m² (a 1,000,000-fold expansion); S ∝ 1/l; reaction rates surge; cation exchange sites multiply. Mass and volume are conserved. (Key Takeaway: Colloidal subdivision creates 6 km² of reactive surface area per cubic meter.)

### Question 2

**Question:** Why does polymineralic Granite undergo granular disintegration under diurnal thermal cycling? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** Quartz grains expand cubically at twice (2.0x) the rate of adjacent feldspar grains during solar heating.
- **B)** Dark biotite and hornblende crystals absorb solar radiation faster, enduring sharper thermal stress than leucocratic minerals.
- **C)** Differential volumetric expansion sets up intense microscopic shear stresses along intergranular crystal boundaries.
- **D)** Repeated diurnal expansion and contraction weakens intergranular cohesion, causing individual mineral grains to dislodge.
- **E)** Granite expands linearly at ten times the rate of sandstone.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C, D

**Explanation / Rationale:** Quartz expands 2x vs feldspar; dark minerals heat faster; differential expansion generates intergranular shear stress, causing granular disintegration. Granite linear expansion is 0.5x sandstone, not 10x. (Key Takeaway: Differential thermal expansion between quartz and feldspar drives granular disintegration in granite.)

### Question 3

**Question:** In comparing the weathering dynamics of river systems from headwaters to mouth, which statements are scientifically valid? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** River headwaters have high stream gradients that generate velocities capable of transporting cobbles (1.2 m/s) and boulders (9.0 m/s).
- **B)** Coarse, angular suspended clasts in headwaters act as abrasive cutting tools against exposed bedrock.
- **C)** Near the river mouth, reduced stream velocity causes sediment deposition rather than bedrock abrasion.
- **D)** Disintegration of rock clasts is substantially greater near the source of a river than near its mouth.
- **E)** River mouths exhibit zero dissolved chemical ions.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C, D

**Explanation / Rationale:** Headwater velocities (1.2–9.0 m/s), angular bedload abrasion, downstream deposition, and source-dominated disintegration gradients are verified fluvial laws. River mouths carry high dissolved ions. (Key Takeaway: High velocity and coarse bedload make headwaters zones of intense mechanical disintegration.)

### Question 4

**Question:** Which biogeochemical processes occur when living plant roots interact with rock minerals in the rhizosphere? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** Root radial turgor pressure mechanically splits rock joints and microfissures.
- **B)** Root exudates containing citric, oxalic, and malic acids lower rhizosphere pH and chelate polyvalent metal cations.

- **D)** Rhizosphere respiration raises soil air CO₂ levels, generating carbonic acid for silicate hydrolysis.
- **E)** Plant roots convert all rock silica into pure elemental gaseous carbon.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C, D

### Question 5

**Question:** What specific roles do subterranean Ants and Termites perform in the weathering of tropical and subtropical soils? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** Pedoturbation: Excavating fine mineral grains from deep saprolite horizons and depositing them in surface mounds.

### Question 6

### Question 7

**Question:** Which statements accurately reflect the differences between primary minerals and secondary minerals in soil science? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** Primary minerals (quartz, feldspars, micas, pyroxenes) formed at high temperatures/pressures in igneous and metamorphic rocks.
- **B)** Secondary minerals (kaolinite, smectite, gibbsite, goethite) are synthesized at low surface temperatures during weathering.
- **C)** Primary silicate minerals are thermodynamically unstable when exposed to surface water and oxygen.
- **D)** Secondary clay minerals possess high surface area and cation exchange capacity crucial for crop nutrition.
- **E)** Secondary minerals are formed exclusively in deep magmatic magma chambers.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C, D

**Explanation / Rationale:** Primary minerals form under deep igneous/metamorphic conditions and are unstable at the surface; secondary clays/oxides are synthesized at surface conditions and supply CEC. Secondary minerals do not form in magma. (Key Takeaway: Secondary clays and sesquioxides are low-temperature weathering synthesis products.)

### Question 8

**Question:** Why is the v⁶ wind carrying capacity power law critical in geomorphology and soil erosion? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** A slight increase in wind velocity causes a massive exponential surge in sediment transport capacity.
- **B)** Doubling wind velocity increases potential kinetic sediment carrying power by a factor of 64 (2⁶ = 64).
- **C)** Tripling wind velocity increases transport capacity by a factor of 729 (3⁶ = 729).
- **D)** Strong gale-force desert winds can transport vast volumes of abrasive sand, causing rapid eolian sandblasting.
- **E)** Wind velocity has zero mathematical impact on sediment transport.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C, D

**Explanation / Rationale:** The v⁶ power law dictates that transport capacity increases by 2⁶ = 64x for double speed and 3⁶ = 729x for triple speed, explaining extreme storm sandblasting. (Key Takeaway: The v^6 power law causes carrying capacity to surge exponentially with wind speed.)

### Question 10

**Question:** Which physical and chemical factors determine why massive Granite weathers into rounded boulders and exfoliation domes? (Select all that apply)

- **A)** Granite is a massive, homogeneous plutonic rock with widely spaced orthogonal joints.
- **B)** Diurnal heating and cooling causes outer rock shells to expand and contract while the interior remains cool, causing exfoliation.
- **C)** Corners and edges of rectangular joint blocks weather from multiple faces simultaneously, rounding the blocks (spheroidal weathering).
- **D)** Quartz grains resist chemical hydrolysis, preserving the structural core of the rock mass.
- **E)** Granite is soluble in water like pure rock salt.

**Correct Answers:** A, B, C, D

**Explanation / Rationale:** Homogeneous structure, thermal exfoliation spalling, corner rounding (spheroidal weathering), and quartz chemical resistance shape rounded granite domes. Granite is not water-soluble like halite. (Key Takeaway: Thermal exfoliation, joint spacing, and corner rounding create rounded granite domes.)
