First up, permeability is your primary rate factor. Porous sandstone weathers way faster than basalt, which beats solid granite. Unconsolidated volcanic ash weathers faster than coarse gravels, because water flows straight through it just like a thirsty sponge second temperature and exfoliation diurnal stresses baking in the indian sun then cooling down at night cause crazy differential expansion quartz expands twice as much as feldspar and granite's expansion is half of sandstones dark rocks heat up even faster this literally bakes the stone until it peels like an onion skin creating those beautiful exfoliated deck and domes third water and frost water speed dictates its carrying power 15 cm a second carries sand, 30 moves gravel, 1.2 meters rolls stones, and 9 meters per second tosses massive boulders, meaning erosion is worse at the source. Up in the Himalayas, glaciers crush rock, and frost wedging hits hard. Freezing water expands 9%, becoming a hydraulic wedge pushing with 1465 mg per square meter of pressure. Fourth is wind action. Wind transport follows the V6 carrying power law. This eolian sandblasting acts like a billion microscopic chisels carving out those iconic mushroom rocks you see in the Thar Desert and the Arah Valleys. Finally, atmospheric electricity. Lightning strikes act as sudden mechanical breakers, causing explosive thermal shock fracturing right out of the sky. From the micro-expansion of quartz to the crushing force of Himalayan ice, physical weathering proves nature relentlessly breaks things down just to build the earth back up.
