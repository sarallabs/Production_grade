## 📚 Section A: Advanced Theoretical MCQs

### Question 1
M. A. K. Halliday’s Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL) revolutionized 20th-century linguistics by proposing that:
- [ ] A) Grammar is an arbitrary mathematical puzzle completely detached from human society
- [x] B) Language architecture is fundamentally functional: its internal lexicogrammatical and semantic systems directly mirror the social purposes for which humans use it
- [ ] C) Spoken language cannot be analyzed scientifically
- [ ] D) Only written literature contains communicative metafunctions

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Halliday established that natural language is not an extrinsic code; its grammatical architecture evolved functionally to meet human social demands.

### Question 2
In Halliday’s SFL, what is the grammatical realization of the **Ideational (Experiential)** Metafunction?
- [ ] A) The system of Mood and Modality
- [x] B) The system of Transitivity (Processes, Participants, and Circumstances)
- [ ] C) The system of Theme and Rheme
- [ ] D) Phatic conversational greetings

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Experiential meaning represents reality through Transitivity: actions/states (processes), entities involved (participants), and contextual settings (circumstances).

- [ ] C) Through alphabetical spelling conventions

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: The Interpersonal metafunction enacts social relationships and speaker-listener roles through Mood choices and modal evaluations.

In Systemic Functional Linguistics, what constitutes the **Textual Metafunction**?

### Question 5
How does Halliday’s concept of **Multifunctionality** critique traditional sentence-parsing models?
- [ ] A) It proves that sentences can only contain one word

### Question 8: Deconstructing Halliday’s SFL Analysis of "Song: To Celia"
**Prompt**: Using M. A. K. Halliday’s analysis of Ben Jonson’s line (*"Or leave a kiss within the cup, and I'll not ask for wine"*), explain in detail how this single clause simultaneously operates across all three metafunctions:
1. **Ideational (Experiential)**
2. **Interpersonal**
3. **Textual**

**Model Answer Key**:
* **Ideational (Experiential)**: The clause represents human physical processes: an action of leaving an entity (**kiss**) in a spatial circumstance (**within the cup**), linked logically through parataxis (*"and"*) to a verbal/mental process of requesting a physical substance (**wine**).
* **Interpersonal**: The clause executes a reciprocal social transaction: an imperative request (*"leave a kiss..."*) paired conditionally with a promissory offer (*"and I will not ask..."*). The poet sets up an intimate, reciprocal exchange: *"If you do this for me, then I undertake to do that for you."*
* **Textual**: The clause is organized rhythmically and melodically to be spoken or sung, deploying thematic markers (*"Or", "I"*) to balance the line and create poetic cohesion.

### Question 9: SFL Analysis of the Philippine Linguistic Landscape
**Prompt**: Select the public regulatory signage *"BAWAL TUMAWID: NAKAMAMATAY"* commonly observed in Metro Manila transit thoroughfares. Conduct a rigorous Systemic Functional Linguistics dissection covering:
1. **Field of Discourse** & its experiential realization (Transitivity).
2. **Tenor of Discourse** & its interpersonal realization (Mood/Modality).
3. **Mode of Discourse** & its textual realization (Thematic structure & typography).

**Model Answer Key**:
* **Field (Experiential Realization)**: The situational field is municipal pedestrian traffic control and public safety. Realized through the material process of crossing (*"tumawid"*) and the relational/attributive consequence of fatality (*"nakamamatay"*).
* **Tenor (Interpersonal Realization)**: The tenor reflects a sharp power asymmetry—state regulatory authority commanding the citizenry. Realized through a negative imperative mood (*"Bawal"*) and unhedged high-obligation modality enforcing compliance through existential consequence.
* **Mode (Textual Realization)**: The mode is written, visual, public graphic signage. Realized through stark typographic contrast, terse syntax, and prominent thematic placement (*"BAWAL TUMAWID"*) engineered for immediate cognitive uptake by rapid commuters.

### Question 10: Extrinsic vs. Intrinsic Functional Paradigms
**Prompt**: Compare Bronislaw Malinowski’s extrinsic functional view of language with M. A. K. Halliday’s intrinsic Systemic Functional Linguistics. Address how each theorist defines the relationship between "social use" and "grammatical structure."

**Model Answer Key**:
* **Malinowski's Extrinsic Paradigm**: Malinowski viewed function as synonymous with external use—what humans *do* with language in concrete cultural contexts (pragmatic action, ritual, phatic communion). In this view, language is an instrument applied from the outside to achieve social tasks; grammar is seen primarily as an external vehicle.
* **Halliday's Intrinsic Paradigm (SFL)**: Halliday deepened this insight by proving that function is not merely an external use; it is fundamentally inscribed into the internal grammatical system itself. Natural language grammar evolved into three overarching metafunctions (Ideational, Interpersonal, Textual) precisely because human beings required language to simultaneously represent experience, negotiate relationships, and create cohesive text. Social use and grammatical structure are thus intrinsically united.
