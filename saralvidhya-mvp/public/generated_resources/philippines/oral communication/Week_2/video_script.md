# Video Scripts & Visual Storyboards: Functions of Communication (Week 2)

---

## 🎬 Module 1: The 5 Jobs Language Does Every Day (Beginner Video)
* **Target Audience**: High school students & beginner learners
* **Estimated Runtime**: 3:30 minutes
* **Visual Style**: Relatable student animation, colorful icons, lively dynamic text

| Timestamp | Visual / Graphic Screen | Voiceover / Audio Script |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0:00 - 0:35** | **Scene 1**: Student looking confused at a busy street corner with various signs and people talking. Text: *"Why Do We Talk?"* | *"Have you ever wondered why humans talk all day long? We don't just speak to make noise; every conversation has a specific job to do! In Oral Communication, these are called the Functions of Communication."* |
| **0:35 - 1:45** | **Scene 2**: 5 animated icons pop onto the screen: A Stop Hand (Control), Two Smiling Faces (Social), A Trophy (Motivation), A Glowing Heart (Emotion), and A Megaphone (Information). | *"Scholars Sipacio and Balgos divide communication into 5 core functions: Control and Regulation to guide behavior; Social Interaction to build friendships; Motivation to inspire hard work; Emotional Expression to share feelings from the heart; and Information Dissemination to share facts and news."* |
| **1:45 - 2:30** | **Scene 3**: Real-life street signs appearing: *"Bawal Tumawid"*, a doctor's prescription pad, and friends laughing in a school canteen. | *"You see these everywhere! A traffic cop stopping cars is Control. Asking an elevator neighbor about the weather is Phatic Communion—polite small talk that oils the social wheels. And shouting 'We passed the exam!' is Emotional Expression."* |
| **2:30 - 3:30** | **Scene 4**: Comparison between a cartoon character shouting angrily (Aggressive), hiding under a desk (Passive), and smiling calmly with an *"I"* speech bubble (Assertive). | *"When communicating, always be Assertive! Express your needs honestly and politely using 'I' statements, like: 'I feel stressed when deadlines change, so can we plan ahead?' Speak clearly, respect others, and make every word count!"* |

## 🎬 Module 2: Extrinsic Language Models & The Linguistic Landscape (Intermediate Video)
* **Target Audience**: Senior High School students
* **Estimated Runtime**: 5:00 minutes
* **Visual Style**: Clean split-screen historical graphics, typography motion design, real Philippine signage footage

| Timestamp | Visual / Graphic Screen | Voiceover / Audio Script |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0:00 - 1:15** | **Scene 1**: Archival photos animating: Bronislaw Malinowski in the Trobriand Islands (1923), Karl Bühler (1934), and Roman Jakobson (1960). Text: *Extrinsic Theories: Function Equals Use*. | *"Long before linguists studied grammar under a microscope, they asked a simple question: What do people use language for? Early extrinsic functional models viewed function as synonymous with external social utility."* |
| **1:15 - 2:45** | **Scene 2**: Interactive diagram of Bühler’s Organon Model branching into Jakobson’s 6 Factors: Context (Referential), Addresser (Emotive), Addressee (Conative), Message (Poetic), Contact (Phatic), and Code (Metalingual). | *"Jakobson mapped 6 communicative functions to 6 fundamental factors. When you test your mic by saying 'Can you hear me?', that's Phatic. When you define a difficult word, that's Metalingual. And when a poet crafts rhythmic stanzas, that's the Poetic function."* |
| **2:45 - 4:00** | **Scene 3**: High-definition video montage of Metro Manila: pedestrian overpass signs (*"Bawal Tumawid"*), COVID-19 health reminders, and San Miguel Beer billboards (*"Iba ang May Pinagsamahan"*). | *"In the Philippine Linguistic Landscape, public signs reflect social order and cultural values. Regulatory signs use imperative control to protect lives, while commercial billboards evoke cultural camaraderie and bayanihan to motivate brand loyalty."* |
| **4:00 - 5:00** | **Scene 4**: Student presentation resolving conflict using reflective listening and assertive dialogue. | *"Understanding these functions equips you to overcome communication breakdowns. By listening actively and framing conflict with assertive 'I' statements, you transform friction into collaborative understanding."* |

## 🎬 Module 3: Systemic Functional Linguistics & The Semiotic Matrix (Advanced Video)
* **Target Audience**: Advanced scholars, linguists & educators
* **Estimated Runtime**: 6:30 minutes
* **Visual Style**: Architectural data visualization, tripartite semiotic waveforms, elegant dark-mode academic presentation

| Timestamp | Visual / Graphic Screen | Voiceover / Audio Script |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0:00 - 1:45** | **Scene 1**: Dynamic title card: *M. A. K. Halliday’s Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL)*. A single clause split into 3 glowing parallel strands. | *"M. A. K. Halliday revolutionized modern linguistics by proving that function is not an extrinsic afterthought—it is built into the internal grammatical architecture of every natural language. Every clause is inherently multifunctional, weaving three simultaneous strands of meaning: Ideational, Interpersonal, and Textual."* |
| **1:45 - 3:30** | **Scene 2**: Transitivity diagram: Process (Action), Participant (Actor/Goal), Circumstance (Locative). Parallel Mood/Modality meter showing speech roles (Statement, Question, Offer, Command). | *"The Ideational metafunction represents human reality through Transitivity. Concurrently, the Interpersonal metafunction enacts social relationships and power hierarchies through Mood and Modality. And the Textual metafunction weaves these strands into a cohesive, flowing message through Theme and Rheme."* |
| **3:30 - 5:00** | **Scene 3**: Flowchart linking Context of Situation to Semantics: `Field -> Experiential`, `Tenor -> Interpersonal`, `Mode -> Textual`. Ben Jonson's poetic analysis highlighted. | *"Halliday demonstrates an exact systematic realization matrix: Field determines Experiential meaning; Tenor determines Interpersonal meaning; and Mode determines Textual texture. A clause cannot be physically sliced into separate parts; it simultaneously executes all three metafunctions."* |
| **5:00 - 6:30** | **Scene 4**: Sociolinguistic signage analysis transitioning into a summary graphic of communication as a constitutive social matrix. | *"From public health mandates to complex literary verse, language directly mirrors human social architecture. To master communication is to master the systemic semiotics that bind society together."* |
