# Podcast Script: The Jobs Language Does Every Day (Beginner Level)

**Episode**: Functions of Communication — Why We Talk, Connect & Share  
**Target Audience**: High School Learners & Beginners  
**Format**: Engaging Mentor-Student Dialogue  
**Duration**: Approx. 6–8 minutes  
**Hosts**: 
* **Leo**: An eager, curious student host.
* **Ms. Diaz**: A supportive oral communication teacher.

---

### [Intro Music & Sound Effect: Cheerful acoustic guitar strum with a warm chime]

**Leo**: (Enthusiastic) Hey there, listeners! Welcome back to *Speak Up Smart*, the podcast where we break down the secret powers of everyday speech. I'm Leo, and today we are looking into a super fascinating question: *Why do we actually talk?* Joining us today is our awesome teacher and mentor, Ms. Diaz. Hello, Ms. Diaz!

**Ms. Diaz**: Hello, Leo! Hello to everyone tuning in. I’m thrilled to be here.

**Leo**: So Ms. Diaz, most of us talk all day long without thinking twice. We text our friends, ask our parents for food, and listen to teachers in class. But linguists say language always has a job to do. What are the big five jobs—or functions—of communication?

**Ms. Diaz**: You hit the nail on the head, Leo. Scholars Sipacio and Balgos tell us that communication functions are the practical and social purposes for which we use language. They group them into five core functions:
1. **Control / Regulation**
2. **Social Interaction**
3. **Motivation**
4. **Emotional Expression**
5. **Information Dissemination**

**Leo**: Let’s break those down with real-life examples! What does **Control / Regulation** look like?

**Ms. Diaz**: Think of a traffic enforcer raising a hand at a crowded intersection, or a teacher saying, *"Please submit your assignments before 5:00 PM."* The main purpose here is to manage, direct, and guide human behavior so that things stay safe and orderly.

**Leo**: Ah, got it! Rules, instructions, and safety warnings. What about **Social Interaction**?

**Ms. Diaz**: That’s the fun, warm side of language! It’s greeting your classmate in the hallway, sharing a joke during lunch, or having a family dinner. Anthropologist Bronislaw Malinowski called small talk like *"Nice weather today, isn't it?"* **Phatic Communion**. It acts like social grease to break awkward silence and build friendly bonds.

**Leo**: Phatic communion—I love that phrase! And then we have **Motivation** and **Emotional Expression**. How do they differ?

**Ms. Diaz**: Great distinction, Leo! 
* **Motivation** is about giving energy, hope, and courage to help someone reach a goal. Think of a basketball coach at halftime yelling, *"You have the heart and the skills; don't give up now!"*
* **Emotional Expression**, on the other hand, is speaking from your heart. It’s venting your feelings—like crying tears of joy and shouting, *"I passed the entrance exam!"* or confiding in a best friend when you feel stressed.

**Leo**: And number five is **Information Dissemination**—like news broadcasts reporting typhoon signals or teachers lecturing about history!

**Ms. Diaz**: Exactly! Conveying objective, factual data so people can learn and make smart choices.

---

### [Audio Transition: Quick, modern drum rhythm]

**Leo**: Ms. Diaz, what about when we see signs on the street? Like those famous Philippine signs: *"BAWAL TUMAWID: NAKAMAMATAY"*?

**Ms. Diaz**: That is part of what linguists call the **Linguistic Landscape**—how language is visible on public signs! That specific sign is pure **Control / Regulation**: it restricts pedestrian behavior to save lives.

**Leo**: To wrap things up, Ms. Diaz, if students find themselves in a heated argument with a teammate, what communication style should they use?

**Ms. Diaz**: Always choose the **Assertive Style**! Don’t be passive by staying silent and holding a grudge, and don’t be aggressive by shouting insults. Speak assertively using **"I" statements**—say: *"I feel overwhelmed when tasks are delayed, so could we agree on a schedule together?"* That way, you stand up for your feelings while treating others with total respect.

**Leo**: Assertive communication with "I" statements—that's a game-changer! Thank you so much for joining us, Ms. Diaz.

**Ms. Diaz**: You’re very welcome, Leo. Keep communicating with purpose, everyone!

**Leo**: That’s all for today’s episode of *Speak Up Smart*. Catch you next time!
