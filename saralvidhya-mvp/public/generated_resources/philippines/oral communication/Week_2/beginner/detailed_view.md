## 1. Primary Scholarly Definitions (Standard Grounding)

To understand oral communication, we anchor our learning in official scholarly definitions:

> ### Primary Scholarly Definitions
> * **Sipacio & Balgos (2016)**:
>   > *"Communication functions refer to the fundamental social and practical purposes for which people use language and interaction: Control/Regulation, Social Interaction, Motivation, Emotional Expression, and Information Dissemination."*
> * **M. A. K. Halliday (1978, 1989)**:
>   > *"Language has evolved functionally: its internal grammatical and semantic architecture directly mirrors the social purposes for which humans use it."*
> * **Bronislaw Malinowski (1923)**:
>   > *"Phatic communion is language used to establish ties of union by mere exchange of words, functioning as a social grease to break awkward silence and build goodwill."*

## 2. Deep Dive: The Five Core Functions of Communication

Sipacio & Balgos (2016) classify all communication into five main functions:

```

│                       FIVE CORE FUNCTIONS OF COMMUNICATION                  │
├─────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Control /        │ Managing, directing, and regulating human behavior    │
│    Regulation       │ and social conduct.                                   │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Social           │ Establishing, maintaining, and strengthening social   │
│    Interaction      │ bonds and interpersonal relationships.                │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Motivation       │ Inspiring, encouraging, and mobilizing individuals to │
│                     │ pursue goals or improve performance.                  │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Emotional        │ Venting, conveying, and expressing personal feelings, │
│    Expression       │ affections, passions, and inner states.               │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

│    Dissemination    │ and educational instructions.                         │
└─────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 🚦 Function 1: Control / Regulation
* **Definition**: Communication that directs, controls, or manages the behavior of people.
* **Why it matters**: Without rules, schools would be chaotic and streets would be dangerous!
* **Verbal Clues**: Words of command like *"Do not"*, *"Submit on time"*, *"Please follow directions"*.
* **Nonverbal Clues**: A police officer raising a hand, a teacher standing tall at the front of the room, or wearing a uniform.
* **School Example**: The teacher says: *"Cellphones must be placed on silent mode inside the laboratory."*

### 🤝 Function 2: Social Interaction
* **Definition**: Communication that builds friendships, shows warmth, and helps us enjoy spending time with others.
* **Why it matters**: It satisfies our emotional need for belonging and companionship.
* **Verbal Clues**: Casual greetings, compliments, and invitations (*"Hi! How was your weekend?", "Do you want to study together?"*).
* **Nonverbal Clues**: Big smiles, friendly handshakes, looking someone in the eyes, and nodding.
* **School Example**: Saying hello to a new student and showing them around the campus during recess.

### 🏆 Function 3: Motivation
* **Definition**: Communication that encourages, energizes, or inspires people to work hard and not give up.
* **Why it matters**: It turns fear of failure into positive energy.
* **Verbal Clues**: Inspiring words like *"You can do it!"*, *"Don't give up!"*, *"I believe in your talent!"*
* **Nonverbal Clues**: Enthusiastic voice, thumbs-up, clapping, and a firm, confident handshake.
* **School Example**: A student leader giving a pep talk to the school sports team before the championship game.

### ❤️ Function 4: Emotional Expression
* **Definition**: Communication that allows people to release their inner feelings, like happiness, sadness, worry, or joy.
* **Why it matters**: Talking about feelings helps us feel better and lets others support us.
* **Verbal Clues**: Expressive words like *"I feel so proud of you!"*, *"I'm really worried about my exams."*, *"Thank you from the bottom of my heart!"*
* **Nonverbal Clues**: Hugs, smiling broadly, crying tears of joy, or sighing in relief.
* **School Example**: Hugging your best friend after finding out that both of you made the honor roll.

### 📢 Function 5: Information Dissemination
* **Definition**: Communication that gives clear, factual information, data, or directions.
* **Why it matters**: It helps people learn, stay safe during emergencies, and solve everyday problems.
* **Verbal Clues**: Facts, numbers, clear instructions, and announcements (*"Typhoon Signal No. 2 is raised"*, *"Turn left at the library"*).
* **Nonverbal Clues**: Maps, whiteboards, diagrams, and speaking in a calm, clear voice.
* **School Example**: The principal announcing the schedule for final exams over the public speaker system.

## 4. Communication Styles: Becoming an Assertive Communicator

When you express yourself, there are three main styles you can use:

1. **Passive**: You stay quiet, bottle up your feelings, and let others decide everything for you. This leads to sadness and frustration.
2. **Aggressive**: You shout, interrupt others, and bully people to get your way. This destroys trust and friendships.
3. **Assertive (The Best Way!)**: You speak up honestly and politely, respecting your own feelings while also respecting others.

> 🌟 **Beginner Tip**: When a friend forgets to return your notebook, don't scream at them (aggressive) and don't stay silent (passive). Speak **assertively**: *"Hi! I need my notebook today to study for our quiz. Could you please bring it to school tomorrow?"*
