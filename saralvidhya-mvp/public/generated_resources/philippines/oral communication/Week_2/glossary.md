# Glossary: Functions of Communication & Linguistic Terminology

---

### A
* **Assertive Communication**: A communication style wherein an individual expresses their feelings, boundaries, and needs directly, honestly, and respectfully without violating the rights of others.

### C
* **Conative Function**: In Bühler’s and Jakobson’s models, language oriented toward the listener/addressee (Second Person) designed to command, persuade, or direct action.
* **Context of Situation**: In Halliday’s SFL, the immediate social environment of an interaction characterized by Field, Tenor, and Mode.
* **Control / Regulation**: A core function of communication aimed at managing, directing, or restricting human behavior and enforcing social or organizational order.

### E
* **Emotional Expression**: A core function of communication facilitating catharsis, emotional vulnerability, and the sharing of internal affective states.
* **Emotive Function**: In Roman Jakobson’s model, language oriented toward the addresser, expressing immediate subjective attitude and emotion.
* **Experiential Meaning**: The subcomponent of the Ideational metafunction representing the world of reality through the grammatical system of Transitivity (processes, participants, circumstances).

### F
* **Field of Discourse**: In SFL, the social activity or subject matter taking place in the communicative event; realized by Experiential meaning.

### I
* **Ideational Metafunction**: In Halliday’s SFL, the grammatical resource for understanding, representing, and reflecting upon human experience and logical relationships.
* **Information Dissemination**: A core function of communication conveying objective facts, scientific data, news, and instructional steps.
* **Interpersonal Metafunction**: In SFL, language functioning as social action to establish roles, negotiate power, and express attitudes (realized by Mood and Modality).

### L
* **Linguistic Landscape**: The visibility, prominence, and sociolinguistic salience of languages on public signage, billboards, street names, and commercial advertisements within a territory.

### M
* **Metalingual Function**: In Roman Jakobson’s model, language used specifically to describe, explain, clarify, or define language itself.
* **Mode of Discourse**: In SFL, the role assigned to language in the interaction (channel, spoken vs. written, rhetorical texture); realized by Textual meaning.
* **Motivation**: A core function of communication inspiring, encouraging, and mobilizing individuals toward goal attainment and resilience.
* **Multifunctionality**: The SFL principle stating that every natural language clause simultaneously integrates Ideational, Interpersonal, and Textual meanings.

### P
* **Phatic Communion**: Language used to establish social bonds, break silence, and maintain goodwill rather than transmit factual information (coined by Malinowski).
* **Poetic Function**: In Jakobson’s model, language oriented toward the message itself, foregrounding aesthetic rhythm, sound patterns, and linguistic form.

### R
* **Referential Function**: In Jakobson’s model, language oriented toward context, portraying objective, cognitive reality.

### S
* **Social Interaction**: A core function of communication focused on building friendships, nurturing camaraderie, and fulfilling interpersonal belonging.
* **Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL)**: A linguistic framework founded by M. A. K. Halliday demonstrating that grammatical architecture directly mirrors human social purposes.

### T
* **Tenor of Discourse**: In SFL, the social participants involved, their relative power status, and their degrees of social distance or intimacy; realized by Interpersonal meaning.
* **Textual Metafunction**: In SFL, language creating texture and coherence, organizing ideational and interpersonal meanings into a unified message.
* **Transitivity**: The lexicogrammatical system encoding experiential meaning through processes (verbs), participants (actors/goals), and circumstances (time/place).
