  root((Functions of Communication & Functional Theories of Language))
    1. Human Need to Interact
      Biological & Survival Needs
        Physical Cooperation
        Coordinated Labor
        Warning of Hazards
      Psychological & Emotional Well-Being
        Emotional Validation
        Affiliation and Belonging
        Self-Concept Formation
      Social Cohesion & Order
        Establishing Laws and Norms
        Transmitting Cultural Heritage
        Collective Problem-Solving
    2. Core Functions of Communication (Sipacio & Balgos)
      Control / Regulation
        Purpose: Direct behavior, maintain order, enforce obedience
        Verbal: Imperatives, rules, authoritative warnings
        Nonverbal: Uniforms, firm tone, upright posture, boundary markers
        Examples: Traffic officer, classroom attendance policies
      Social Interaction
        Purpose: Build camaraderie, nurture relationships, belonging
        Verbal: Casual greetings, compliments, invitations, teasing
        Nonverbal: Warm smiles, handshakes, open body language
        Examples: Vacation chats, family dinners, greeting neighbors
      Motivation
        Purpose: Uplift morale, inspire perseverance, stimulate energy
        Verbal: Inspiring metaphors, encouragement, aspirational goals
        Nonverbal: Energetic delivery, firm gestures, eye contact
        Examples: Coach halftime pep talk, commencement address
      Emotional Expression
        Purpose: Release vulnerabilities, vent feelings, seek empathy
        Verbal: Emotive adjectives, heartfelt confessions, exclamations
        Nonverbal: Crying, laughter, sighs, trembling voice
        Examples: Passing board exam, eulogy tribute, confiding worries
      Information Dissemination
        Purpose: Convey objective facts, news, data, instructions
        Verbal: Declaratives, statistics, logical sequences, citations
        Nonverbal: Measured speech rate, diagrams, slides, maps
        Examples: Geometry lecture, typhoon signals, museum tour
      Overlapping Functions in Practice
        State of the Nation Address (SONA): Information + Motivation + Control
        Doctor's Consultation: Information + Control + Motivation
    3. Extrinsic Functional Theories of Language
      Bronislaw Malinowski (1923)
        Pragmatic Active: Coordinating immediate physical survival
        Pragmatic Narrative: Recounting past events to pass knowledge
        Magical / Ritual: Ceremonial rites influencing unseen forces
        Phatic Communion: Social grease to tie union and break silence
      Karl Bühler (1934) - Organon Model
        Expressive (1st Person): Speaker-oriented inner emotional states
        Conative (2nd Person): Addressee-oriented commands & persuasion
        Representational (3rd Person): External context-oriented facts
      Roman Jakobson (1960) - Six Factors
        Context -> Referential Function (Cognitive / factual reality)
        Addresser -> Emotive Function (Speaker attitude & feelings)
        Addressee -> Conative Function (Imperative influence on listener)
        Message -> Poetic Function (Aesthetic form, rhythm & rhyme)
        Contact / Channel -> Phatic Function (Testing & maintaining channel)
        Code -> Metalingual Function (Clarifying language itself)
      James Britton (1970)
        Expressive: Immediate thoughts & feelings (starting point)
        Transactional: Participant role to get things done
        Poetic: Spectator role creating contemplated verbal art
      Desmond Morris (1967) - Ethological Model
        Information Talking: Rational cooperative data exchange
        Mood Talking: Externalizing internal physical/emotional states
        Exploratory Talking: Playful aesthetic wordplay
        Grooming Talking: Social grooming equivalent to monkey flea-picking
    4. Systemic Functional Linguistics (M.A.K. Halliday)
      Multifunctionality of Language
        Language architecture inherently mirrors social purposes
        Every clause weaves three distinct strands simultaneously
      Ideational Metafunction
        Experiential: Transitivity (Processes, Participants, Circumstances)
        Logical: Relations between clauses (Parataxis & Hypotaxis)
      Interpersonal Metafunction
        Social action enacting roles, status, attitudes & power
        Mood System: Statements, Questions, Offers, Commands
        Modality: Probability, necessity, obligation, willingness
      Textual Metafunction
        Creating texture and coherence distinguishing text from list
        Thematic Structure: Theme (starting point) and Rheme (rest)
        Information Structure: Flow of Given and New information
        Cohesive Devices: Reference, ellipsis, conjunctions, lexical
      Context of Situation vs Semantic Components
        Field of Discourse -> Experiential Meaning (Transitivity)
        Tenor of Discourse -> Interpersonal Meaning (Mood/Modality)
        Mode of Discourse -> Textual Meaning (Theme/Cohesion)
        Ben Jonson Case Study: "Song To Celia" multifunctional analysis
    5. Practical & Applied Frameworks
      Philippine Linguistic Landscape
        Definition: Salience of languages on public signage
        Official Regulatory Warnings: Control / Regulation ("Bawal Tumawid")
        Public Health Advisories: Control + Information ("Magsuot ng Mask")
        Commercial Billboards: Motivation + Social ("Iba ang may Pinagsamahan")
        Tourism Welcome Signs: Social + Information ("Welcome to Baguio")
        Multilingual Reality: English, Filipino & Regional Languages
      Communication Breakdown & Styles (Guide to Effective Communication)
        Passive Style: Suppressing needs, breeding resentment
        Aggressive Style: Intimidation, shouting, destroying safety
        Assertive Style: Direct, respectful, honest boundaries
        Breakdown Causes: Selective listening, defensiveness, ambiguity
        Restoration Strategies: Active listening, reflective feedback, I-statements
