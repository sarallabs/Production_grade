## 1. Primary Scholarly Definitions (Standard Grounding)

The analysis of communicative function is grounded in established scholarly benchmarks:

> ### Primary Scholarly Definitions
> * **Sipacio & Balgos (2016)**:
>   > *"Communication functions refer to the fundamental social and practical purposes for which people use language and interaction: Control/Regulation, Social Interaction, Motivation, Emotional Expression, and Information Dissemination."*
> * **M. A. K. Halliday (1978, 1989)**:
>   > *"Language has evolved functionally: its internal grammatical and semantic architecture directly mirrors the social purposes for which humans use it."*
> * **Bronislaw Malinowski (1923)**:
>   > *"Phatic communion is language used to establish ties of union by mere exchange of words, functioning as a social grease to break awkward silence and build goodwill."*

### Verbal & Nonverbal Markers by Function

| Function | Verbal Markers | Nonverbal Markers | Applied Scenario |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Control / Regulation** | Imperatives, rules, guidelines, explicit warnings (*"Wear face masks"*) | Upright stance, firm tone, raised hand, uniform, boundary markers | Teacher enforcing classroom test rules; traffic officer |
| **Social Interaction** | Informal greetings, inquiries, invitations, teasing (*"How was your trip?"*) | Warm smile, handshake, proximity, relaxed posture, nod | Friends chatting over lunch; welcoming a neighbor |

## 3. Extrinsic Functional Theories: "Function Equals Use"

Before analyzing grammar, linguists classified language based on its external social utility:

```
                     EXTRINSIC THEORIES: FUNCTION EQUALS USE
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Malinowski (1923): Pragmatic (Active/Narrative) vs. Magical/Ritual     │
  │ Bühler (1934):     Expressive (1st P) - Conative (2nd P) - Repr (3rd P)│
  │ Jakobson (1960):   Emotive, Conative, Referential, Poetic, Phatic, Meta│
  │ Britton (1970):    Expressive, Transactional (Participant), Poetic     │
  │ Morris (1967):     Information, Mood, Exploratory, Grooming Talking    │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Bronislaw Malinowski (1923)**:
   * **Pragmatic Active**: Language directly coordinating survival activities (e.g., commands during hunting).
   * **Pragmatic Narrative**: Storytelling to transmit practical skills and cultural memory.
   * **Phatic Communion**: Social conversation without factual substance (e.g., *"Nice day, isn't it?"*) designed to maintain social equilibrium.
2. **Karl Bühler (1934) — Organon Model**:
   * *Expressive*: Reveals speaker's inner state (First Person).
   * *Conative*: Directs or influences the listener (Second Person).
   * *Representational*: Depicts external reality and facts (Third Person).
3. **Roman Jakobson (1960) — Six Communicative Factors**:
   * Context $\to$ **Referential**; Addresser $\to$ **Emotive**; Addressee $\to$ **Conative**; Message $\to$ **Poetic**; Channel $\to$ **Phatic**; Code $\to$ **Metalingual**.

## 5. Overcoming Communication Breakdowns
Effective communication requires an **Assertive Communication Style**—standing up for personal rights while respecting others. 
* To prevent selective listening or defensiveness, deploy **Active Listening**, **Reflective Paraphrasing**, and **"I" Statements** (*"I feel concerned when assignments are submitted late because it delays our review"*).
