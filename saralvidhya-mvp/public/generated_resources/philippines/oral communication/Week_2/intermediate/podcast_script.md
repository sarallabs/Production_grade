# Podcast Script: Functional Theories & Sociolinguistic Landscapes (Intermediate Level)

**Episode**: Beyond the Surface: Extrinsic Language Models & Sociolinguistic Signage  
**Target Audience**: Senior High School Students & Undergraduates  
**Format**: Analytical Academic Dialogue  
**Duration**: Approx. 8–10 minutes  
**Hosts**: 
* **Marco**: An articulate student researcher.
* **Prof. Alvarez**: A professor of applied sociolinguistics.

### [Intro Music & Sound Effect: Sophisticated, rhythmic electronic melody]

**Marco**: Welcome back to *The Sociolinguistic Lens*, where we decode the structural functions, cultural realities, and hidden architectures of human speech. I’m Marco, and today we’re exploring Lesson 2 of Oral Communication: the functional theories of language and the dynamic ways speech acts operate in the real world. Joining us is Professor Alvarez. Welcome, Professor!

**Prof. Alvarez**: Thank you, Marco! Delighted to be here to explore how language works both inside the classroom and out in society.

**Marco**: Professor Alvarez, in introductory units, students learn the five core functions from Sipacio and Balgos—Control, Social Interaction, Motivation, Emotional Expression, and Information Dissemination. But historically, linguists developed fascinating **extrinsic models** of language where "function equals use." Could you walk us through the major extrinsic theories?

**Prof. Alvarez**: I'd be glad to. The journey begins in 1923 with anthropologist **Bronislaw Malinowski**, who studied the Trobriand Islanders. Malinowski noticed that language was not originally used for abstract philosophical debate, but for direct survival:
* **Pragmatic Active**: Commands coordinating physical actions during fishing or hunting.
* **Pragmatic Narrative**: Recounting past events to pass down practical survival skills.
* And he coined **Phatic Communion**: Language used as social grease to establish ties of union and dissolve awkward silence, like asking *"How do you do?"*

**Marco**: And then in 1934, psychologist Karl Bühler introduced the famous **Organon Model**!

**Prof. Alvarez**: Exactly! Bühler looked at language through Plato’s grammatical persons:
1. **Expressive Function** (First Person / Addresser): Externalizing the speaker’s inner emotional state.
2. **Conative Function** (Second Person / Addressee): Directing, commanding, or persuading the listener to act.
3. **Representational Function** (Third Person / Context): Describing external facts and objective reality.

**Marco**: In 1960, Roman Jakobson expanded Bühler’s model into his renowned **Six Communicative Factors and Functions**. How did Jakobson map them?

**Prof. Alvarez**: Jakobson established an elegant correspondence:
* Orientation toward **Context** gives us the **Referential Function** (factual data).
* Orientation toward the **Addresser** gives us the **Emotive Function** (feelings).
* Orientation toward the **Addressee** gives us the **Conative Function** (imperative influence).
* Orientation toward the **Message** itself gives us the **Poetic Function** (aesthetic form, rhythm).
* Orientation toward the **Contact or Channel** gives us the **Phatic Function** (testing the medium: *"Can you hear me?"*).
* And orientation toward the **Code** gives us the **Metalingual Function** (using language to explain language itself: *"What does this word mean?"*).

### [Audio Transition: Clean synthesized frequency sweep]

**Marco**: That brings us directly to a fascinating practical application: the **Philippine Linguistic Landscape**! When commuters travel through Metro Manila or Cebu, they see signs in English, Filipino, Taglish, and regional languages. How do communicative functions operate on public signage?

**Prof. Alvarez**: Public signage is a living mirror of social function!
* Regulatory warnings like **"BAWAL TUMAWID: NAKAMAMATAY"** deploy the **Control / Regulation** function with high-obligation authority to enforce pedestrian safety.
* Public health posters like **"Magsuot ng Face Mask"** combine **Control** with **Information Dissemination**.
* And commercial billboards, like San Miguel Beer’s famous slogan **"Iba ang May Pinagsamahan"**, tap into **Motivation and Social Interaction**, evoking cultural camaraderie (*bayanihan* and *pakikisama*) to drive consumer loyalty.

**Marco**: That’s such a powerful insight—every billboard and warning sign is executing a precise communicative function! Now, in everyday conflict resolution, how can communicators prevent breakdowns?

**Prof. Alvarez**: By adopting an **Assertive Communication Style** supported by **Active Listening** and **"I" statements**. Instead of attacking someone aggressively with *"You always mess up the slides!"*, reframe it: *"I feel anxious when slides are edited right before a presentation because it disrupts our timing."* You express your needs honestly while maintaining mutual dignity.

**Marco**: Assertive communication, active listening, and functional awareness! Professor Alvarez, thank you for sharing your deep expertise with us today.

**Prof. Alvarez**: It was my pleasure, Marco. Keep examining the world through a functional lens!

**Marco**: That wraps up today’s episode of *The Sociolinguistic Lens*. Until next time, stay observant and communicate with purpose!
