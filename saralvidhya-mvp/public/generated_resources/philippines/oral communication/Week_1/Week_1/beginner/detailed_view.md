## 1. Primary Scholarly Definitions (Standard Grounding)

No matter your level of study, learning the official definitions developed by communication scholars gives you an academic foundation:

> ### Primary Scholarly Definitions
> * **McCornack (2014) & Sipacio & Balgos (2016)**:
>   > *"Communication is a process of sharing and conveying messages or information from one person to another within and across channels, contexts, media, and cultures."*
> * **Verderber, Sellnow, & Verderber (2017)**:
>   > *"Communication is the process of creating shared meaning."*
> * **Beebe & Beebe (2017)**:
>   > *"Communication is the process of presenting a message to an audience and co-creating understanding through symbolic interaction."*
> * **Julia T. Wood (2012)**:
>   > *"Communication is a systemic process in which people interact with and through symbols to create and interpret meanings."*

## 2. Public Speaking in a Digital Age

Public speaking is defined by Verderber, Sellnow, and Verderber (2017) as **a sustained, formal presentation by a speaker to an audience**.

### Why Does It Matter for Beginners?
1. **Personal Empowerment**: James Humes, an author and presidential speechwriter, once said that public speaking is *"the language of leadership"*. When you stand up and share an idea, you are taking leadership in your classroom or school.
2. **Career Advantage**: Employers across all industries consistently say that clear, polite speaking is the top skill they look for in job candidates.
3. **Free Speech and Civic Voice**: In a democracy, being able to speak freely helps citizens discuss ideas peacefully and think for themselves.

> 💡 **Tackling Speech Anxiety**: It is normal for your heart to beat fast before speaking! Practice speaking out loud at home, look at friendly faces in the room, and remember that the audience wants you to do well.

## 3. Good Manners in the Digital Age: Netiquette & House Rules

When you talk in class, join online video meetings, or text on social media, you represent yourself. Good rules keep communication safe and friendly:

```
       [ Respect ]  ──────►  Be polite & listen when others speak
       [ Honesty ]  ──────►  Tell the truth; never copy others' work
       [ Kindness ] ──────►  No hateful comments or cyberbullying
```

* **No ALL CAPS**: Writing in all capital letters looks like you are screaming.
* **Keep It Concise**: Do not spam long paragraphs in group chats; stay on topic.
* **Avoid Flaming**: Flaming means sending angry, hurtful insults. If you disagree, say it gently.
* **Avoid Plagiarism & Cyberplagiarism**: Never copy words, photos, or assignments from the internet without saying where you found them.

## 4. The 6-Step Communication Cycle in Daily Life

Every conversation follows 6 easy-to-follow steps:

```
[1. Idea] ──► [2. Encode] ──► [3. Transmit] ──► [4. Receive] ──► [5. Decode] ──► [6. Feedback]
  Think        Put into         Speak or         Hear or          Understand       Respond back
               words            send text        read             meaning          (nod/reply)
```

### Real-Life Story: The Daphne & Rico Interaction
1. **Idea Generation**: Daphne knows she likes her classmate Rico, but only as a friend.
2. **Encoding**: She chooses gentle words in Filipino: *"Rico, mahal kita bilang kaibigan."*
3. **Transmission**: She speaks directly to Rico in a quiet spot.
4. **Reception**: Rico hears the sound of her voice and watches her serious face.
5. **Decoding**: Rico understands that Daphne does not want to be his girlfriend.
6. **Feedback**: Rico frowns softly and nods, showing he received her honest answer.

## 5. The Building Blocks of Communication & Types of Noise

To speak well, you must recognize these core parts:
* **Speaker**: The person who has the idea.
* **Message**: What is said, written, or signaled.
* **Channel**: The path used (voice in air, telephone, SMS, video).
* **Receiver**: The listener or reader.
* **Feedback**: The listener's reaction (nod, reply, question).
* **Context**: Where the conversation happens (classroom, dining room, online chat).
* **Noise (Interference)**: Anything that blocks or ruins the message.

### 🛑 The 4 Types of Noise
1. **Physical Noise**: Loud motorbikes, dogs barking, bad phone signal.
2. **Physiological Noise**: A severe headache, feeling sleepy, or being hungry.
3. **Psychological Noise**: Worrying about an exam, daydreaming, or feeling angry.
4. **Semantic Noise**: Using big slang words or terms that the other person does not understand.

## 6. The Three Communication Models Explained Simply

```
1. LINEAR MODEL (One-Way Street):
   [ Speaker ] ──────────────── Message ───────────────► [ Listener ]
   Example: Listening to a recorded announcement at a train station. There is no feedback!

2. INTERACTIVE MODEL (Two-Way Turn-Taking):
   [ Speaker ] ─── Message ───► [ Listener ]
       ▲                              │
       └──────── Feedback ────────────┘
   Example: Two people texting back and forth. Schramm (1954) showed that people must share
   a common language and background (Field of Experience) to understand each other.

3. TRANSACTIONAL MODEL (Live Simultaneous Chat):
   [ Person A ] ◄────── Continuous Live Interaction ──────► [ Person B ]
   Example: Talking face-to-face where both people smile, nod, and react at the exact same time.
```

## 7. The 7 Cs Checklist for School & Life

Use this handy 7-point checklist whenever you write an essay or speak in class:

1. **Clear**: Use simple, easy-to-understand words.
2. **Concise**: Keep it short and to the point.
3. **Concrete**: Share specific examples and facts.
4. **Correct**: Check your facts and spelling.
5. **Complete**: Give all the details needed so people can respond.
6. **Courteous**: Always be polite and positive.
7. **Considerate**: Think about how your words will make the other person feel.
