### Question 1: Defining Communication
According to communication scholars Verderber, Sellnow, and Verderber (2017), communication is best defined as:
- [ ] A) Memorizing long vocabulary words for an exam
- [x] B) The process of creating shared meaning
- [ ] C) Speaking continuously without waiting for a reply
- [ ] D) Sending text messages without checking for typos

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Verderber et al. emphasize that communication is not just making sounds or sending words; it is the process of creating shared meaning between people.

### Question 2: Public Speaking as a Superpower
Former presidential speechwriter James Humes described public speaking as "the language of leadership." What did he mean by this?
- [ ] A) Only government officials and mayors are allowed to speak in public
- [x] B) Whenever you stand up and speak to a group, you are auditioning for leadership
- [ ] C) Leaders never feel nervous when speaking in front of others
- [ ] D) Public speaking is only useful for people who want to become politicians

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Public speaking empowers you to voice your ideas, solve problems in your school or community, and inspire others to work together.

### Question 3: Tackling Speech Anxiety
You are about to give a two-minute speech in class, and you notice your heart beating fast and your hands feeling shaky. What is the most helpful strategy?
- [ ] A) Cancel your speech and leave the classroom immediately
- [x] B) Take two or three slow, deep breaths, look at a friendly friend in the audience, and remember that feeling nervous is normal
- [ ] C) Speak as fast as you can without breathing so you can finish quickly
- [ ] D) Read with your head down staring strictly at your shoes

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Public speaking apprehension is 100% natural. Deep breathing calms your heart rate, and connecting with friendly listeners helps you focus on helping your audience.

### Question 4: Netiquette & Good Digital Manners
In online chats, emails, and social media, what does writing your entire message in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS signal?
- [ ] A) It shows that you have great grammar
- [ ] B) It means you are whispering politely
- [x] C) It is interpreted as shouting or yelling at your reader
- [ ] D) It makes your message load faster on the internet

> **Answer**: **C**  
> **Explanation**: Netiquette rules state that typing in ALL CAPS looks like shouting. Always use standard sentence casing.

### Question 5: Stopping "Flaming"
A classmate posts an opinion about a school project that you completely disagree with. Which of the following responses shows good netiquette and avoids "flaming"?
- [ ] A) *"You are completely stupid and your idea is trash!"*
- [ ] B) Post angry memes and tell everyone to block that person
- [x] C) *"I see your point, but here is another perspective we could consider to make the project even better."*
- [ ] D) Send 20 angry face emojis in all capital letters

> **Answer**: **C**  
> **Explanation**: Flaming means sending hostile, insulting attacks. Good netiquette focuses on discussing ideas politely rather than attacking people personally.

### Question 6: Identifying Types of Noise
Match the everyday scenario with the correct type of noise:
* *Scenario*: You are trying to listen to your teacher, but your stomach is growling loudly because you skipped breakfast, giving you a headache.
What kind of noise is this?
- [ ] A) Physical Noise
- [x] B) Physiological Noise
- [ ] C) Psychological Noise
- [ ] D) Semantic Noise

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Physiological noise comes from biological conditions inside your body (hunger, illness, fatigue, or physical pain).

### Question 7: The 6 Steps of Communication
In the Daphne & Rico story (Sipacio & Balgos, 2016), Daphne decides she wants to tell Rico that she cares for him only as a friend. When she chooses her words and says *"Rico, mahal kita bilang kaibigan"*, which two stages of the cycle just happened?
- [ ] A) Feedback and Noise
- [x] B) Encoding and Transmission
- [ ] C) Reception and Decoding
- [ ] D) Idea Generation and Feedback

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Daphne encodes her feelings into words (Encoding) and vocalizes them directly to Rico across the airwaves (Transmission).

---

### Question 8: The Receiver's Turn
When Rico hears Daphne's words, feels heartbroken, frowns softly, and looks away without speaking, what is his reaction called in the communication cycle?
- [ ] A) Encoding
- [ ] B) Transmission
- [x] C) Feedback
- [ ] D) Semantic Noise

> **Answer**: **C**  
> **Explanation**: Rico’s nonverbal frown and silence provide feedback, letting Daphne know he received and understood her message.

### Question 9: Comparing Models
What is the main difference between a **Linear Model** (like listening to a radio broadcast) and an **Interactive Model** (like texting a friend)?
- [ ] A) Linear models only use English
- [x] B) Linear models are one-way streets with no feedback, while interactive models include two-way turn-taking feedback
- [ ] C) Interactive models do not use channels
- [ ] D) Linear models are always faster than interactive models

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Linear models are one-way transmissions (no feedback). Interactive models are circular exchanges where listeners send feedback back to the speaker.

### Question 10: Applying the 7 Cs Checklist
You want to invite a classmate to work on a poster project. Which message follows the 7 Cs of being **Complete**, **Concise**, and **Courteous**?
- [ ] A) *"Hey. Project. Come over."*
- [ ] B) *"I was wondering if maybe whenever you have a little bit of free time over the next several days or weeks you might possibly want to meet up somewhere."*
- [x] C) *"Hi Maria! Can we meet at the school library today at 3:30 PM to finish our science poster? Let me know if that time works for you!"*
- [ ] D) *"MEET ME AT 3:30 PM AT THE LIBRARY OR ELSE!"*

> **Answer**: **C**  
> **Explanation**: Option C gives all necessary details (Complete), gets straight to the point (Concise), and uses a polite, friendly tone (Courteous).
