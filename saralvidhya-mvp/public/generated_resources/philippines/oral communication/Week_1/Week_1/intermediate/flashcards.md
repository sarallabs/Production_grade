# Flashcards: Elements & Models of Communication (Intermediate Level)

---

### Card 1
**Front**: How do McCornack (2014) and Sipacio & Balgos (2016) define communication?  
**Back**: *"Communication is a process of sharing and conveying messages or information from one person to another within and across channels, contexts, media, and cultures."*

---

### Card 2
**Front**: How do Beebe & Beebe (2017) define communication?  
**Back**: *"Communication is the process of presenting a message to an audience and co-creating understanding through symbolic interaction."*

---

### Card 3
**Front**: What are the four dimensions of Julia T. Wood’s (2012) definition of communication?  
**Back**: 
1. Communication is a process.  
2. Communication is systemic.  
3. Communication involves symbols.  
4. Communication involves meaning (content & relationship levels).

---

### Card 4
**Front**: Differentiate between the Content Level and Relationship Level of meaning.  
**Back**: Content Level is the literal, explicit message conveyed by words; Relationship Level reflects the relational dynamic (power, warmth, status) communicated through nonverbal cues and tone.

---

### Card 5
**Front**: What are the 9 essential elements of the communication process?  
**Back**: Speaker (Source), Message, Encoding, Channel, Receiver, Decoding, Feedback, Context, and Noise (Interference).

---

### Card 6
**Front**: Explain the major limitation of Linear Models of communication (Shannon-Weaver, Lasswell).  
**Back**: Linear models view communication as a one-way conveyor belt from sender to passive receiver and completely omit feedback and audience adaptation.

---

### Card 7
**Front**: What revolutionary concept did Wilbur Schramm (1954) introduce to communication theory?  
**Back**: The "Field of Experience" (shared cultural background, language, and values) and circular feedback loops.

---

### Card 8
**Front**: What characterizes the Transactional Model of communication?  
**Back**: Communicators act as simultaneous transceivers who continuously and dynamically co-create meaning within shared physical and psychological contexts.

---

### Card 9
**Front**: What are the 5 contextual levels of communication?  
**Back**: Intrapersonal (self-talk), Interpersonal (dyadic), Small Group (3-10 people), Public Communication (>10 people), and Mass Communication (mediated to large anonymous audiences).

---

### Card 10
**Front**: What are the 5 foundational standards of ethical communication?  
**Back**: Honesty, Integrity, Fairness, Respect, and Responsibility.

---

### Card 11
**Front**: Explain the difference between Plagiarism and Cyberplagiarism.  
**Back**: Plagiarism is presenting another's ideas or works as one's own without attribution; Cyberplagiarism specifically involves extracting digital text, media, or data from the internet without citation.

---

### Card 12
**Front**: What are the three factors in Lloyd Bitzer's Rhetorical Situation?  
**Back**: Speaker, Audience, and Occasion (converging to resolve an Exigence through discourse).

---

### Card 13
**Front**: What does the 7 Cs principle of "Consideration" entail?  
**Back**: Adapting the message to the audience's background, education, and perspective, adopting the empathetic "you-attitude".

---

### Card 14
**Front**: How can a speaker overcome semantic noise caused by specialized jargon?  
**Back**: By defining technical terms clearly, using accessible analogies, and tailoring vocabulary to the audience's knowledge level.
