# Podcast Script: Navigating Models, Systems & Noise (Intermediate Level)

**Episode**: Beyond the Transmission Belt: Dynamic Communication Models & The 7 Cs  
**Target Audience**: Senior High School & Undergraduate Students  
**Format**: Co-Hosted Dialogue / Academic Conversation  
**Duration**: Approx. 8–10 minutes  
**Hosts**: 
* **Maya**: An analytical student researcher.
* **Dr. Santos**: A senior communication theorist and educator.

### [Intro Music & Sound Effect: Thoughtful, rhythmic synthesizer transition]

**Maya**: Welcome back to *The Dialogue Engine*, where we dissect the mechanics, ethics, and architectures of human interaction. I’m Maya, and today we’re diving into Lesson 1 of Oral Communication in Context. Joining me is Dr. Santos, associate professor of communication studies. Dr. Santos, thanks for stepping into the booth today!

**Dr. Santos**: Delighted to be here, Maya. Looking forward to unpacking the theoretical foundations of human discourse with you.

**Maya**: Let’s dive straight in. When students first approach communication, they often imagine a simple linear process: Person A speaks, Person B listens. But the discipline has moved far beyond that. Could you take us through the historical evolution from linear to transactional models?

**Dr. Santos**: Absolutely. In the mid-20th century, we had linear frameworks like Harold Lasswell’s 1948 formula—*Who says What in Which channel to Whom with What effect*—and Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver’s 1949 Mathematical Model for the Bell Telephone Company. These models viewed communication as a one-way action conveyor belt. The source encodes, the transmitter sends a signal across a noisy channel, and the receiver collects it.

**Maya**: But human interaction isn’t a one-way telephone wire, right?

**Dr. Santos**: Exactly! The fatal limitation of linear models is the total absence of **feedback**. They treat the audience as a passive bucket waiting to be filled with information. In 1954, Wilbur Schramm revolutionized communication theory by introducing the **Interactive Model**. He introduced two game-changing concepts:
1. **Circular Feedback Loops**: Communication is turn-based and circular.
2. The **Field of Experience**: The sum of an individual’s cultural background, linguistic repertoire, personal memories, and social values.

**Maya**: And as Schramm demonstrated, if the sender’s and receiver’s fields of experience do not overlap, severe miscommunication occurs!

**Dr. Santos**: Precisely. Think of a medical doctor presenting research full of advanced pharmaceutical abbreviations to a rural barangay community. Even if the sound system is crystal clear, there is insufficient overlap in their fields of experience. That produces severe **semantic noise**.

---

### [Audio Transition: Subtle rhythmic pulse]

**Maya**: But theoretical development didn’t stop with Schramm. In the 1970s and onward, scholars like Dean Barnlund, and later Julia T. Wood, gave us the **Transactional Model**. What makes transactional communication so radical?

**Dr. Santos**: The breakthrough concept is that communicators act as **simultaneous transceivers**. In an interactive model, you speak, then you stop and wait for me to speak. But in real face-to-face dialogue, you and I are constantly transceiving concurrently! While I’m talking right now, I am actively monitoring your eye contact, your nods, and your facial micro-expressions. I’m adapting my syntax in real time based on your nonverbal cues. Meaning is not transferred; it is **dynamically co-created**.

**Maya**: That brings us directly to the **Rhetorical Situation** formulated by Lloyd Bitzer and synthesized by Verderber, Sellnow, and Verderber. Could you break down the concept of **Exigence** for our listeners?

**Dr. Santos**: In classical rhetoric, an exigence is an objective or perceived crisis, imperfection, or defect that puts pressure on society and can be resolved or mitigated through discourse. When a crisis occurs—whether it's a public health advisory, a natural disaster response, or school budget concerns—that crisis creates an exigence compelling a leader to speak. The speaker must analyze their **Audience** and recognize situational **Constraints** to craft transformative discourse.

### [Audio Transition: Clean, crisp bell tone]

**Maya**: Now, in our modern world, so much of this happens in mediated digital environments—ICT platforms, video conferences, social networks. What ethical obligations govern our public communication today?

**Dr. Santos**: The National Communication Association (NCA) Credo for Communication Ethics gives us the moral benchmark. Ethical communication enhances human worth and dignity by fostering truthfulness, fairness, responsibility, personal integrity, and respect for self and others. In the digital age, we must be vigilant against:
* **Cyberplagiarism**: Extracting digital text or multimedia without citation.
* **Flaming**: Resorting to hostile, aggressive ad hominem attacks online.
* **Context Collapse**: Forgetting that remarks recorded in a private meeting can be permanently clipped, broadcast, and scrutinized worldwide.

**Maya**: To wrap up, how do Cutlip, Center, and Broom’s **7 Cs** help students practice effective communication every day?

**Dr. Santos**: The 7 Cs are your diagnostic Swiss Army knife:
1. **Completeness**: Provide all necessary facts.
2. **Conciseness**: Eliminate redundant filler.
3. **Consideration**: Adopt the *"you-attitude"*—tailor content to your listeners’ needs.
4. **Concreteness**: Use verifiable facts and specific examples.
5. **Courtesy**: Show diplomatic goodwill and respect.
6. **Clearness**: Maintain focused sentence structure and plain vocabulary.
7. **Correctness**: Ensure grammatical and factual precision.

**Maya**: Master the 7 Cs, adapt to your audience, and listen actively! Dr. Santos, thank you so much for sharing your insights with us today.

**Dr. Santos**: Thank you, Maya. It’s been an absolute pleasure.

**Maya**: To all our listeners, keep questioning, keep analyzing, and keep communicating with purpose. Join us next week on *The Dialogue Engine*!
