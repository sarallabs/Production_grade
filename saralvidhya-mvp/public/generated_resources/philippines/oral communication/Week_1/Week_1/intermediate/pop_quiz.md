### Question 1: Scholarly Definition Comparison
McCornack (2014) along with Sipacio & Balgos (2016) define communication as a process of sharing and conveying messages across channels, contexts, media, and cultures. In contrast, Julia T. Wood (2012) emphasizes that communication is:
- [ ] A) An unmediated, direct injection of ideas into passive listeners
- [x] B) A systemic process in which people interact with and through symbols to create and interpret meanings
- [ ] C) An engineering formula calculating signal voltage across copper cables
- [ ] D) A static transaction that ends as soon as words are uttered

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Wood’s framework highlights four interrelated dimensions: communication is a continuous process, embedded within open systems, mediated by arbitrary/abstract symbols, and operates on content and relationship levels of meaning.

### Question 2: The Core Flaw of Linear Models
Why do modern communication scholars critique early linear models, such as Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver’s (1949) Mathematical Model?
- [ ] A) They included too many complex feedback channels
- [x] B) They treat human communication as a one-way transmission conveyor belt and completely omit feedback and audience adaptation
- [ ] C) They focused exclusively on face-to-face interpersonal interactions
- [ ] D) They overemphasized psychological and emotional interference

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: The Shannon-Weaver model was engineered for telephone signal fidelity. In human communication, treating listeners as passive terminal endpoints ignores active decoding, immediate feedback, and real-time adaptation.

### Question 3: Schramm’s Interactive Breakthrough
In Wilbur Schramm’s (1954) Interactive Model, why is the concept of overlapping **Fields of Experience** essential for successful communication?
- [ ] A) It ensures that both speakers have high-speed internet connections
- [x] B) Receivers can only decode symbols based on their own cultural background, language fluency, education, and life experiences
- [ ] C) It forces communicators to take 10-minute pauses before replying
- [ ] D) It proves that verbal words always take precedence over nonverbal body language

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Schramm demonstrated that communication breaks down when communicators have little to no shared background. If fields of experience do not overlap, messages are misinterpreted or rejected.

### Question 4: The Transactional Shift
What fundamental transformation occurs when transitioning from Schramm’s Interactive Model to Barnlund’s (1970) Transactional Model?
- [ ] A) Communicators stop using spoken words and rely only on emojis
- [ ] B) Communicators stop taking alternating, turn-based roles and act as **simultaneous transceivers** who continuously co-create meaning
- [ ] C) The channel between communicators is eliminated entirely
- [ ] D) Feedback is delayed until the interaction has completely ended

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: In transactional communication, participants act as simultaneous senders and receivers (transceivers). While you speak, you are actively decoding the listener’s eye contact, posture, and facial expressions in real-time.

### Question 5: Diagnosing Noise in School Environments
During a science presentation, speaker Maya presents research data. Two students in the back row cannot follow her argument because Maya uses dense, undefined biochemistry abbreviations. Meanwhile, the school bell rings loudly outside.
Which two noise categories are present?
- [ ] A) Physiological Noise and Psychological Noise
- [x] B) Semantic Noise (unfamiliar jargon) and Physical Noise (bell acoustics)
- [ ] C) Physical Noise and Physiological Noise
- [ ] D) Psychological Noise and Environmental Feedback

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: The loud bell is external **Physical Noise**. The confusing, undefined biochemical abbreviations create **Semantic Noise** (linguistic/jargon barrier).

### Question 7: Unpacking the Rhetorical Situation
In Lloyd Bitzer’s (1968) classical theory of the Rhetorical Situation, what is an **Exigence**?
- [ ] A) The physical podium and stage lighting used by a speaker
- [x] B) A real or perceived crisis, imperfection, or urgent defect that puts pressure on society and can be addressed or modified through discourse
- [ ] C) The personal demographic profile of the speaker
- [ ] D) The time limit imposed by an event organizer

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: An exigence is an urgency or societal imperfection that calls for a response and can be resolved or mitigated through purposeful public address.

### Question 8: Cyberplagiarism vs. Traditional Plagiarism
What specific characteristic differentiates **Cyberplagiarism** from traditional academic plagiarism?
- [ ] A) Cyberplagiarism only applies to spoken speeches delivered on television
- [x] B) Cyberplagiarism specifically involves downloading, copying, or extracting digital texts, multimedia, or code from the internet without proper attribution
- [ ] C) Cyberplagiarism is legally permitted in high school classrooms
- [ ] D) Cyberplagiarism requires paying money to an online author

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Cyberplagiarism is the extraction or copy-pasting of online and internet-sourced content without crediting the original creator or URL source.

### Question 9: Applying the 7 Cs (*The "You-Attitude"*)
Which of the 7 Cs of Effective Communication is demonstrated when a speaker prepares a presentation by carefully analyzing the audience’s background, values, and education level to frame the message empathetically?
- [ ] A) Concreteness
- [x] B) Consideration
- [ ] C) Conciseness
- [ ] D) Correctness

> **Answer**: **B**  
> **Explanation**: Consideration means putting yourself in the shoes of the audience (the *"you-attitude"*), respecting their mindset, and tailoring examples to their level of understanding.

### Question 10: The Role of Immediate Feedback
In a face-to-face formal business presentation, how should an audience-centered speaker adapt when they notice listeners furrowing their brows and whispering to one another during a financial explanation?
- [ ] A) Speak louder and faster to finish the presentation before listeners ask questions
- [ ] B) Reprimand the listeners for being disrespectful
- [x] C) Pause, acknowledge the nonverbal feedback, and offer a concrete analogy or ask if clarification is needed
- [ ] D) Ignore the audience completely and stick strictly to the pre-written script

> **Answer**: **C**  
> **Explanation**: In transactional communication, nonverbal feedback provides real-time data. An ethical, audience-centered speaker pauses and adapts to ensure mutual understanding.
