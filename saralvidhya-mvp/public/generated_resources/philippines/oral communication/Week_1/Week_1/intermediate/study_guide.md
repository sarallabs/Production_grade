# Study Guide: Foundations, Elements & Models of Communication (Intermediate Level)

---

## 🎯 Modular Objectives & Senior High School Competencies
By working through this guide, you will master the following core competencies:
1. Distinguish between linear, interactive, and transactional models of human communication.
2. Diagram the 6-stage communication sequence and assess where breakdowns occur.
3. Diagnose the 4 categories of noise and apply targeted remediation techniques.
4. Evaluate public discourse using Lloyd Bitzer's Rhetorical Situation framework and the 7 Cs.

## 📊 Quick-Reference Comparison: Theoretical Models

| Feature | Linear Models (Shannon-Weaver) | Interactive Models (Schramm) | Transactional Models (Barnlund, Wood) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Information Flow** | One-way / unidirectional | Two-way / turn-based | Multidirectional / simultaneous |
| **Communicator Role** | Discrete sender & passive receiver | Alternating sender and receiver turns | Simultaneous transceivers (co-creators) |
| **Role of Feedback** | Absent or ignored | Delayed or sequential reply | Continuous live verbal & nonverbal cues |
| **Contextual Role** | Limited to channel noise | Overlapping Fields of Experience | Embedded in shared sociocultural systems |

```

│ Noise Category       │ Underlying Root Cause           │ Strategic Action                       │
├──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
```

## 💡 Practical Synthesis Prompt
**Case Study**: A senior high school student delivers an oral report on environmental conservation to a group of grade school students. The speaker uses terms like *"anthropogenic climate forcing"* and *"biome degradation"*, leading the audience to look confused and distracted.
* **Diagnosis**: High semantic noise due to mismatched Fields of Experience.
* **Solution**: Apply the 7 Cs of **Consideration** and **Clearness** by replacing technical terms with relatable concepts like *"pollution caused by humans"* and *"forest habitats losing their trees"*.
