### Question 1
According to Beebe & Beebe (2017), communication is defined as:
- [ ] A) The mechanical transfer of sound waves through a vacuum
- [x] B) The process of presenting a message to an audience and co-creating understanding through symbolic interaction
- [ ] C) One-way instruction from a superior to a subordinate
- [ ] D) The transmission of data packets across digital computer networks

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Beebe & Beebe emphasize audience-centered presentation and symbolic co-creation of meaning.

### Question 2
What is the defining characteristic of Julia T. Wood’s (2012) framework?
- [ ] A) Communication is static and unchangeable
- [x] B) Communication is a systemic process in which people interact with and through symbols to create and interpret meanings
- [ ] C) Communication only takes place when both parties speak English
- [ ] D) Communication does not depend on past relationships

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Wood's framework stresses that communication is systemic, processual, symbolic, and meaning-driven.

### Question 3
How does Schramm’s Interactive Model explain why two people from radically different cultural backgrounds might experience severe miscommunication?
- [ ] A) Their internet routers have different bandwidth limits
- [x] B) Their "Fields of Experience" do not sufficiently overlap, creating semantic and cultural noise
- [ ] C) Interactive models do not account for culture
- [ ] D) Senders cannot encode nonverbal gestures

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Wilbur Schramm demonstrated that shared understanding is possible only where the communicators' fields of experience overlap.

### Question 4
A project manager sends an email update to their team. Three members reply asking clarifying questions, while two reply with thumbs-up emojis. In the communication cycle, these responses are:
- [ ] A) Encodings without messages
- [ ] B) Semantic distractions
- [x] C) Feedback demonstrating varied reception and decoding
- [ ] D) Psychological interferences

*Answer*: **C** | *Explanation*: Receiver replies (verbal or nonverbal) serve as feedback indicating how the message was interpreted.

### Question 8: Analyzing the Rhetorical Situation
**Scenario**: The Supreme Student Government (SSG) President is delivering a speech to the student body following the sudden cancellation of the annual school sports festival due to budget cuts.
* **Task 1**: Identify the **Exigence** compelling the speech.
* **Task 2**: Identify the **Audience Adaptation** strategies the SSG President should deploy.
* **Task 3**: What **Constraints** exist in this situation?

**Model Answer**:
1. **Exigence**: The student body's widespread frustration, disappointment, and rumors regarding the cancelled festival, which demands transparent explanation and constructive alternative solutions.
2. **Audience Adaptation**: The SSG President should adopt an empathetic and respectful tone, acknowledge the students' valid disappointment, avoid bureaucratic jargon, and propose collaborative student activities.
3. **Constraints**: Fixed school administrative policies, limited available budget, school safety rules, and varying student grade levels with diverse interests.

### Question 9: Diagnosing and Resolving Noise
**Scenario**: During a virtual classroom presentation on Zoom, three separate issues occur:
1. Speaker A’s microphone crackles and drops packets every few seconds.
2. Listener B has not eaten all day and cannot concentrate due to stomach pain.
3. Listener C is offended because Speaker A used an unfamiliar, culturally insensitive colloquialism.

* **Task**: Categorize each noise type and propose an immediate strategic remedy.

**Model Answer**:
1. **Physical / Technological Noise**: Speaker A should switch to a wired headset, disable high-bandwidth background video filters, or use chat text for key bullet points.
2. **Physiological Noise**: Listener B should take a short break to hydrate and eat a light snack to restore cognitive focus.
3. **Semantic / Cultural Noise**: Speaker A should pause, respectfully clarify the intended meaning, apologize for the insensitive phrasing, and replace it with inclusive, standard vocabulary.

### Question 10: Comparative Matrix of Communication Models
**Task**: Compare Linear, Interactive, and Transactional Models across three parameters: (a) Direction of flow, (b) Role of participants, and (c) Role of feedback.

**Model Answer**:
* **Direction of Flow**:
  * *Linear*: One-way / unidirectional.
  * *Interactive*: Two-way alternating / circular.
  * *Transactional*: Multidirectional / simultaneous and continuous.
* **Role of Participants**:
  * *Linear*: Fixed sender and passive receiver.
  * *Interactive*: Alternating sender and receiver turns.
  * *Transactional*: Simultaneous transceivers (co-communicators).
* **Role of Feedback**:
  * *Linear*: Absent or ignored.
  * *Interactive*: Delayed or turn-based response.
  * *Transactional*: Immediate, continuous, ongoing verbal and nonverbal cues.
