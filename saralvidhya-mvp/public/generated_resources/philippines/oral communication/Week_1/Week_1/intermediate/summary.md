## 1. Primary Scholarly Definitions (Standard Grounding)

Academic communication study is anchored upon four established theoretical definitions:

> ### Primary Scholarly Definitions
> * **McCornack (2014) & Sipacio & Balgos (2016)**:
>   > *"Communication is a process of sharing and conveying messages or information from one person to another within and across channels, contexts, media, and cultures."*
> * **Verderber, Sellnow, & Verderber (2017)**:
>   > *"Communication is the process of creating shared meaning."*
> * **Beebe & Beebe (2017)**:
>   > *"Communication is the process of presenting a message to an audience and co-creating understanding through symbolic interaction."*
> * **Julia T. Wood (2012)**:
>   > *"Communication is a systemic process in which people interact with and through symbols to create and interpret meanings."*

## 2. Public Speaking, Civic Responsibility & The Audience-Centered Model

Verderber, Sellnow, and Verderber (2017) define public speaking as **a sustained, formal presentation by a speaker to an audience**.

Public speaking fulfills dual developmental purposes:
* **Personal & Professional Empowerment**: Former presidential speechwriter James Humes observed that public speaking is *"the language of leadership"*. Corporate surveys consistently cite oral articulateness as the foremost criterion in recruitment and leadership promotion.
* **Civic & Democratic Engagement**: Originating as a classical liberal art in ancient Greece and Rome, speech training cultivates critical analysis—teaching learners *how to think rather than what to think*.

## 3. Digital Civility, Netiquette & Ethical Standards

Communication in mediated contexts requires strict adherence to behavioral guidelines:

### Core Netiquette (Verderber et al., 2017)
1. **Decorum**: Moderating tone; refraining from typing in ALL CAPS (which represents shouting).
2. **Conciseness**: Respecting listener cognitive load and digital bandwidth.
3. **De-escalation**: Prohibiting *flaming* (hostile, emotionally charged insults).
4. **Source Verification**: Countering digital disinformation by corroborating factual claims prior to sharing.

### Ethical Commitments & The NCA Credo
Ethical discourse rests on five standards: **Honesty** (avoiding plagiarism and cyberplagiarism), **Integrity** (congruence of speech and action), **Fairness** (unbiased representation), **Respect** (inclusive language honoring diversity), and **Responsibility** (accountability for communicative outcomes).

> *"Ethical communication is fundamental to responsible thinking, decision making, and the development of relationships and communities within and across contexts, cultures, channels, and media."* — **National Communication Association (NCA)**

## 4. The 6-Step Communication Cycle & Structural Elements

Human communication proceeds through six systematic stages:

```
[1. Idea Generation] ──► [2. Encoding] ──► [3. Transmission]
  Source conceives         Converts idea into       Transmits message
  intent/content           symbolic code            via chosen channel
                                                          │
                                                          ▼
[6. Feedback Loop]   ◄── [5. Decoding] ◄─── [4. Reception]
  Receiver responds,       Interprets symbols       Sensory intake
  closing loop             & extracts meaning       (auditory/visual)
```

### The 9 Essential Elements

| Element | Operational Function | Concrete Illustration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Speaker / Source** | Originates and encodes intent. | A student delivering a research report. |
| **Message** | Symbolic content conveyed verbally/nonverbally. | Argument, evidentiary data, vocal inflection. |
| **Encoding** | Mental translation of thoughts into symbols. | Choosing technical terminology vs. layman analogies. |
| **Channel** | The vehicular medium carrying the signal. | Sound waves, videoconferencing feed, printed deck. |
| **Receiver** | Target listener who collects and decodes. | The grading panel or student audience. |
| **Decoding** | Interpreting symbols to construct meaning. | Attributing meaning based on prior vocabulary knowledge. |
| **Feedback** | Responsive cue signaling message uptake. | Raised hands, nodding, furrowed brows during Q&A. |
| **Context** | Multidimensional setting of the interaction. | Formal classroom defense vs. informal corridor chat. |
| **Noise (Interference)** | Disruptive stimuli diminishing fidelity. | Physical noise, physiological fatigue, semantic jargon. |

### Classification of Interference (Noise)
* **Physical (External)**: Environmental acoustic barriers (traffic, humming HVAC, audio stutter).
* **Internal Physiological**: Biological impairments (hunger, migraine, hearing deficiency).
* **Internal Psychological**: Affective or cognitive distractions (test anxiety, confirmation bias).
* **Semantic**: Linguistic ambiguity, unfamiliar acronyms, or mismatched cultural idioms.

### Wilbur Schramm’s Interactive Model: Field of Experience
Schramm demonstrated that communication fidelity depends directly upon the degree of overlap between the sender's and receiver's **Field of Experience** (cultural background, education, language fluency, and beliefs). Disjointed fields inevitably yield semantic noise and misunderstanding.

## 6. The 7 Cs of Effective Communication & Barrier Management

Formulated by Cutlip, Center, and Broom (2012), the 7 Cs govern communicative excellence:
1. **Completeness**: Imparting all essential facts necessary for an informed decision.
2. **Conciseness**: Communicating maximum significance using minimum verbiage.
3. **Consideration**: Framing the message from the receiver's perspective (*"you-attitude"*).
4. **Concreteness**: Supporting claims with verifiable data and clear illustrations.
5. **Courtesy**: Exhibiting diplomatic respect and positive regard.
6. **Clearness**: Maintaining focused sentence structure and accessible vocabulary.
7. **Correctness**: Ensuring grammatical precision and factual validity.

### Diagnostic Strategies for Common Barriers
* *Affective / Emotional Stress*: Implement brief conversational pause; allow rational composure before resuming discourse.
* *Jargon Overload*: Translate specialized concepts into accessible analogies.
* *Communication Apprehension*: Rehearse aloud with timing benchmarks; redirect focus onto audience value.
