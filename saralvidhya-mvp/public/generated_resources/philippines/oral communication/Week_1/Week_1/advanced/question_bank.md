### Question 1
In Julia T. Wood’s systemic framework, why is communication defined as an **unrepeatable and irreversible process**?
- [ ] A) Because recordings of past speeches are prohibited by law
- [x] B) Because every communicative act dynamically alters the participants' relational and cognitive state; identical conditions cannot be artificially replicated
- [ ] C) Because spoken sound waves degrade immediately in the atmosphere
- [ ] D) Because listeners possess perfect photographic recall

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Communication is dynamic and processual; each interaction alters participants' psychological, emotional, and relational context, making literal repetition impossible.

### Question 2
How does Lloyd Bitzer (1968) distinguish between an everyday problem and a true **Rhetorical Exigence**?
- [ ] A) An everyday problem is solved by money; an exigence is solved by silence
- [x] B) A rhetorical exigence is an imperfection characterized by urgency that can be completely or partially modified only through purposive discourse
- [ ] C) An exigence cannot be affected by human action

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: For Bitzer, an exigence is rhetorical only if discourse can assist in mitigating or resolving the underlying crisis.

### Question 3
In Harold Lasswell’s 1948 formula ($\text{Who} \to \text{Says What} \to \text{In Which Channel} \to \text{To Whom} \to \text{With What Effect}$), the underlying theoretical assumption regarding the audience is:
- [ ] A) The audience actively negotiates meaning through simultaneous feedback
- [x] B) The message has a direct, unmediated, and powerful hypodermic effect upon a relatively passive recipient
- [ ] C) Meaning is co-created through symbolic interaction

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Lasswell’s model originated in wartime propaganda and political mass media analysis, assuming linear, direct audience effects.

### Question 4
Why is Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver’s (1949) model often critiqued by contemporary humanistic communication scholars?
- [ ] A) It was written in French
- [x] B) It conflates technical signal fidelity with human semantic comprehension and treats meaning as a physical package rather than a socially negotiated construct
- [ ] C) It includes too many circular feedback loops

*Answer*: **B** | *Explanation*: Shannon-Weaver was engineered for electronic transmission; human communication involves negotiated semantics, nonverbal cues, and subjective interpretation.

## ✍️ Section B: Advanced Essay & Theoretical Synthesis Prompts

### Question 8: Theoretical Critique of Shannon-Weaver vs. Transactional Models
**Prompt**: Compare and contrast the epistemological foundations of the Shannon-Weaver Mathematical Model (1949) and Barnlund's Transactional Model (1970). In your essay, address:
1. The nature of "meaning" (transmitted entity vs. socially constructed reality).
2. The role and timing of feedback.
3. The implications for ethical responsibility in public discourse.

**Model Answer Key**:
* **Nature of Meaning**: Shannon-Weaver treats meaning as a discrete, pre-packaged electronic signal injected from information source to destination. In contrast, Barnlund’s Transactional Model posits that meaning is not pre-packaged; it is an emergent, fluid, and socially co-created phenomenon negotiated within shared cultural systems.
* **Role and Timing of Feedback**: In Shannon-Weaver, feedback is absent (or treated as an external secondary transmission cycle). In Barnlund's model, feedback is continuous, immediate, concurrent, and inseparable from transmission—communicators transceive concurrently.
* **Ethical Implications**: In a linear paradigm, ethical responsibility rests exclusively on the sender's transmission fidelity. In a transactional framework, ethical responsibility is co-distributed: listeners bear active ethical responsibilities to listen fair-mindedly, suspend hasty biases, and co-construct constructive discourse (NCA Credo).

### Question 9: Analyzing Context Collapse and Mediated Rhetoric
**Prompt**: A corporate executive's internal motivational speech to department managers is covertly recorded on a smartphone and leaked on TikTok and X (Twitter), causing public boycotts.
* **Task 1**: Analyze this event using Lloyd Bitzer’s **Rhetorical Situation** (exigence, audience, constraints).
* **Task 2**: Explain how the phenomenon of **Context Collapse** precipitated this communication breakdown.
* **Task 3**: Formulate two actionable remediation strategies for executive communication in a digital age.

**Model Answer Key**:
1. **Rhetorical Situation Analysis**: The executive's original discourse was crafted for an immediate rhetorical audience (managers) under specific internal constraints (corporate culture, internal KPIs). The leak created an unmanaged *exigence* among the general public who perceived the remarks outside their intended framing.
2. **Context Collapse**: The dissolution of spatial, institutional, and temporal boundaries stripped the discourse of its immediate context, exposing it to a vast, anonymous, heterogeneous mass audience with entirely different fields of experience and values.
3. **Remediation Strategies**:
   * *Universalized Audience Adaptation*: Assume all internal corporate discourse may become public; ensure speech adheres to public ethical standards, empathy, and organizational integrity.
   * *Transparent Crisis Address*: Immediately issue a concise, complete, and courteous statement explaining the original context, acknowledging legitimate public concerns, and committing to constructive dialogue.

### Question 10: Semiotic Dissection of Wood's Triad (Arbitrary, Ambiguous, Abstract)
**Prompt**: Using a concrete Philippine sociocultural symbol or term (e.g., *"Bayanihan"* or *"Pakikisama"*), demonstrate how its semiotic properties are simultaneously arbitrary, ambiguous, and abstract.

**Model Answer Key**:
* **Arbitrary**: The phonemes comprising *"Bayanihan"* have no physical resemblance or intrinsic natural connection to communal labor or solidarity. The connection exists purely through historical and linguistic social agreement among Filipino speakers.
* **Ambiguous**: The meaning of *"Pakikisama"* varies significantly across individuals and contexts. In a positive context, it denotes harmonious community fellowship; in a political or corporate context, it may ambiguously signify yielding to corrupt peer pressure or nepotism.
* **Abstract**: *"Bayanihan"* represents an overarching conceptual generalization (mutual civic unity and selflessness) rather than a tangible, physical object. It transcends any single act, such as carrying a nipa hut, to denote an enduring philosophical attitude.
