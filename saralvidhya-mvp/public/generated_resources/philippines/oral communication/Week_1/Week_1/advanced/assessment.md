### Question 1
Julia T. Wood (2012) articulates that human communication operates through symbols that are arbitrary, ambiguous, and abstract. What does the **arbitrary** nature of symbols fundamentally signify?
- [ ] A) Symbols have static, universal meanings that cannot change over time
- [x] B) There is no natural, intrinsic, or necessary physical correspondence between a word and the object or concept it denotes
- [ ] C) Words always refer to tangible physical objects rather than generalizations
- [ ] D) Symbols are universally understood across all linguistic cultures without translation

> **Explanation**: Words are arbitrary because there is no natural connection between the signifier (e.g., the word "dog") and the signified creature; meaning is sustained purely through social convention.

### Question 2
How does Wood’s (2012) systemic framework differentiate the **Content Level of Meaning** from the **Relationship Level of Meaning**?
- [ ] A) Content level conveys emotional tone; relationship level transmits literal facts
- [x] B) Content level transmits denotative, literal propositions; relationship level communicates power distribution, status, and affective affinity through metacommunicative cues
- [ ] C) Content level operates only in digital communication; relationship level exists only face-to-face
- [ ] D) There is no structural difference; both levels are identical

> **Explanation**: Content level refers to the literal verbal data; relationship level reflects how communicators view each other (communicating dominance, equality, warmth, or distance).

### Question 3
In Barnlund’s (1970) and Wood’s (2012) **Transactional Model**, communicators are termed **simultaneous transceivers**. Why is this distinction crucial over interactive models?
- [ ] A) Transceivers take turn-based, alternating pauses during speech delivery
- [x] B) Encoding and decoding occur concurrently; while speaking, communicators are actively receiving and interpreting nonverbal audience cues and continuously adapting their discourse
- [ ] C) Transceivers rely entirely on electronic transmission rather than human interaction
- [ ] D) Transceivers eliminate all physiological and semantic noise from the interaction

> **Explanation**: Transactional models reject the turn-taking assumption of interactive models, recognizing that communicators continuously and simultaneously encode and decode messages.

### Question 4
In Lloyd Bitzer’s formulation of the Rhetorical Situation, which condition defines a **Rhetorical Audience** as opposed to a mere body of listeners?
- [ ] A) Any group of people assembled in an auditorium
- [x] B) Persons who are capable of being influenced by discourse and of serving as mediators/agents of change to resolve the exigence
- [ ] C) Listeners who agree unconditionally with the speaker's viewpoint
- [ ] D) Consumers of broadcast mass media who remain passive

> **Explanation**: According to Bitzer (1968), a rhetorical audience consists specifically of individuals who can be moved by discourse and possess the capacity to effect change.

### Question 5
How does the phenomenon of **Context Collapse** in mediated communication (ICT) alter traditional ethical responsibilities in public speaking?
- [ ] A) It eliminates the need for fact-checking because online content is self-verifying
- [x] B) Remarks tailored for a bounded, immediate audience are permanently clipped, distributed, and analyzed by diverse, unexpected global audiences, requiring heightened ethical reflexivity
- [ ] C) It restricts speech to local geographical regions
- [ ] D) It renders the National Communication Association (NCA) Credo obsolete

> **Explanation**: Digital platforms dissolve boundaries between private/classroom settings and mass broadcast, meaning messages can be removed from their original context and scrutinized globally.

### Question 6
Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver’s Mathematical Model of Communication (1949) was conceived primarily to address:
- [ ] A) The interpersonal dynamics of romantic couples
- [x] B) The technical fidelity, capacity, and noise entropy of signal transmission across telephone and electronic channels
- [ ] C) The ethical responsibilities of political leaders in ancient Rome
- [ ] D) The social construction of cultural identities through symbolic dialogue

> **Explanation**: Shannon and Weaver designed their model for Bell Telephone Laboratories to quantify signal transmission efficiency and minimize channel noise in engineering systems.

### Question 7
Under the National Communication Association (NCA) Credo for Communication Ethics, what is the primary ethical responsibility of a listener evaluating opposing political discourse?
- [ ] A) To interrupt immediately whenever an assertion contradicts personal doctrine
- [x] B) To practice fair-minded listening, honor human worth and dignity, and critically evaluate evidence with openness and personal integrity
- [ ] C) To record and distribute unedited commentary anonymously
- [ ] D) To disengage completely from any civic deliberation that poses discomfort

> **Explanation**: The NCA Credo mandates that ethical communication enhances human dignity and mutual respect by fostering fairness, open-minded critical thinking, and responsibility.
