# Flashcards: Epistemological Foundations & Theoretical Discourse (Advanced Level)

---

### Card 1
**Front**: State Julia T. Wood’s (2012) definition of communication verbatim.  
**Back**: *"Communication is a systemic process in which people interact with and through symbols to create and interpret meanings."*

---

### Card 2
**Front**: Why are communicative symbols considered arbitrary, ambiguous, and abstract?  
**Back**: 
* **Arbitrary**: Signifiers possess no intrinsic physical correspondence to their referent.  
* **Ambiguous**: Meanings vary subjectively across individuals, subcultures, and historical contexts.  
* **Abstract**: Words represent conceptual generalizations rather than tangible material realities.

---

### Card 3
**Front**: Define Lloyd Bitzer’s concept of "Exigence" in the Rhetorical Situation.  
**Back**: An exigence is an objective or perceived crisis, urgent imperfection, or societal defect that puts pressure on people and can be mitigated or resolved through discourse.

---

### Card 4
**Front**: Explain the concept of "Simultaneous Transceiving" in Barnlund's Transactional Model.  
**Back**: Participants concurrently encode and decode verbal and nonverbal cues—while speaking, an individual continuously processes audience micro-expressions and modifies delivery in real time.

---

### Card 5
**Front**: How does "Context Collapse" affect public communication in mediated digital environments?  
**Back**: Spatial and temporal boundaries dissolve; discourse designed for an immediate, bounded audience is clipped, archived, and broadcast to global, heterogeneous, and unintended audiences.

---

### Card 6
**Front**: What is the cybernetic function of Feedback in human communication systems?  
**Back**: Feedback acts as a homeostatic regulation mechanism that enables communicators to detect semantic discrepancies and dynamically adapt communicative strategies.

---

### Card 7
**Front**: State the National Communication Association (NCA) Credo for Communication Ethics.  
**Back**: *"Ethical communication is fundamental to responsible thinking, decision making, and the development of relationships and communities within and across contexts, cultures, channels, and media. Moreover, ethical communication enhances human worth and dignity by fostering truthfulness, fairness, responsibility, personal integrity, and respect for self and others."*

---

### Card 8
**Front**: How does Schramm’s "Field of Experience" explain communicative breakdown across cultural divides?  
**Back**: When interlocutors possess disjointed fields of experience (divergent semantic networks, epistemologies, or norms), the overlap is insufficient for mutual decoding, precipitating systemic breakdown.

---

### Card 9
**Front**: Differentiate Harold Lasswell’s (1948) linear formula from Claude Shannon’s (1949) mathematical model.  
**Back**: Lasswell’s model analyzes mass political effects ("Who Says What In Which Channel To Whom With What Effect"), whereas Shannon’s model analyzes physical signal transmission, entropy, and channel noise fidelity.

---

### Card 10
**Front**: How do Cutlip, Center, and Broom’s 7 Cs operationalize communicative ethics and effectiveness?  
**Back**: They establish heuristic standards (Completeness, Conciseness, Consideration, Concreteness, Courtesy, Clearness, Correctness) that balance information entropy with audience cognitive load and moral accountability.
