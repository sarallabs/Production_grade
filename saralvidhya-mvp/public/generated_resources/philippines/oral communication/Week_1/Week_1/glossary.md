# Glossary: Oral Communication Key Terminology

---

### A
* **Audience Adaptation**: The strategic process of adjusting speech content, vocabulary, and delivery style to match the background, educational level, and expectations of listeners.
* **Audience Analysis**: The systematic study of listeners' demographic, psychological, and situational profiles prior to speaking.

### C
* **Channel**: The medium or vehicle through which an encoded message travels from the sender to the receiver (e.g., sound waves, digital fiber optic, print).
* **Cyberplagiarism**: The unethical extraction, downloading, or copying of digital text, images, or media from the internet without proper academic citation.

### D
* **Decoding**: The cognitive process wherein the receiver interprets received symbols and translates them into meaningful mental concepts.

### E
* **Encoding**: The cognitive and physical process wherein the sender translates internal ideas into shared symbols (words, gestures, vocal inflections).
* **Exigence**: In Lloyd Bitzer's rhetorical theory, an urgent imperfection, crisis, or defect that puts pressure on people and can be partially or completely modified through discourse.

### F
* **Feedback**: The verbal or nonverbal reaction transmitted back by the receiver indicating whether the message was received, understood, or rejected.
* **Field of Experience**: An individual's unique sum of personal history, cultural background, native language, education, and values (central to Schramm's interactive model).
* **Flaming**: The act of posting or sending hostile, aggressive, or insulting messages over computer networks.

### I
* **Interference (Noise)**: Any stimulus that distorts, impedes, or interrupts the transmission and comprehension of a message.
* **Interpersonal Communication**: Dyadic communication between two individuals who share an identifiable relationship.
* **Intrapersonal Communication**: Cognitive self-dialogue and internal information processing within one's own mind.

### L
* **Linear Model**: A unidirectional model depicting communication as a one-way transfer from sender to passive receiver without feedback.

### M
* **Message**: The collection of verbal utterances, visual aids, and nonverbal signals conveyed to an audience.

### N
* **Netiquette**: The social and behavioral etiquette conventions governing respectful communication over digital and computer networks.

### P
* **Plagiarism**: The act of presenting another person's ideas, expressions, or creations as one's own by omitting proper credit.
* **Public Speaking**: A sustained, formal oral presentation delivered by a speaker to an immediate audience.

### R
* **Rhetorical Situation**: The convergence of Speaker, Audience, and Occasion to resolve a central exigence through persuasive speech.

### S
* **Semantic Noise**: Confusion or misunderstanding resulting from unfamiliar jargon, ambiguous wording, or mismatched cultural idioms.

### T
* **Transactional Model**: A dynamic model wherein communicators act as simultaneous transceivers who co-create meaning within shared contexts.
* **Transceiver**: A communicator who concurrently sends and receives verbal and nonverbal messages.
