Plants are living things that need water to survive. In fact, water makes up **75–90%** of a plant's living cells! Without water, the cell's living material (protoplasm) dies. Water helps plants make food (photosynthesis), carry nutrients, and stay firm and upright.

**How water moves — Diffusion:** Molecules always move from where there are a lot of them to where there are fewer, all on their own. This is called diffusion. No energy needed!

**Osmosis:** When water moves through a thin skin (semi-permeable membrane) from a watery area to a less watery area, that's osmosis. If a cell takes in water it becomes **turgid** (swollen and firm). If it loses water it becomes **flaccid** (limp). If it loses too much water, the inside of the cell pulls away from the wall — this is called **plasmolysis**.

**How water travels up a tall tree — Ascent of Sap:** Water travels through tiny tubes called **xylem** inside the plant. The best explanation is the **Cohesion-Tension Theory**: water molecules stick together (cohesion) and stick to the tube walls (adhesion), forming a long chain. When leaves lose water vapour through tiny pores called **stomata**, it pulls the whole chain upward — like drinking through a straw.

**Transpiration:** Plants lose water as vapour mainly through stomata (~90%). This cools the plant and helps pull water and minerals up from roots.

**Guttation:** Sometimes plants release liquid water droplets from the tips of leaves at night. This happens through special pores called **hydathodes** when the roots push water up.
