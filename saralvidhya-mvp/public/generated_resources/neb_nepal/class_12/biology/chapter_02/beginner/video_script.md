## Scene 3
**Duration:** 30 seconds

**Visual Prompt:**
Clear animation of a plant cell (round, with visible cell wall and membrane). Show water molecules (blue circles) moving through the membrane into the cell — the cell visibly swells and glows (turgid). Then show a salt shaker pouring salt around the same cell — water moves OUT — cell shrinks and turns a slightly sad color (flaccid/plasmolysed). Simple arrows show direction of water movement. Labels: "Hypotonic → Turgid", "Hypertonic → Plasmolysis". Warm educational illustration style.

**Narration:**
"Water can move through the thin skin of a cell through a process called osmosis. Put the cell in dilute water — water rushes in — the cell becomes firm and turgid! Put it in salty water — water rushes out — the cell shrinks. If it shrinks a lot, the inner part pulls away from the wall. That's called plasmolysis!"

## Scene 4

**Visual Prompt:**
Animated journey of a water droplet starting at soil level being absorbed by root hairs, traveling up through a glowing blue xylem tube (shown as a transparent pipe inside a tree trunk), all the way to the leaf. At the leaf, show tiny stomata opening (animated mouth-like pores) releasing water vapour as wisps. Label each step: "Root → Xylem → Leaf → Stomata → Vapour". Bright, simple, storybook illustration style. Use a friendly cartoon tree with visible internal anatomy.

**Narration:**
"Now the big question — how does water climb 90 metres up a tree? It travels through tubes called xylem. Water molecules stick together — that's cohesion — like holding hands in a chain. When leaves lose water vapour through tiny pores called stomata, it pulls the whole chain up from the roots. Like drinking through a very long straw!"

## Scene 5
**Duration:** 20 seconds

**Visual Prompt:**
Side-by-side comparison animation. Left side — daytime: sunny, leaves releasing tiny vapour wisps through open stomata (shown as open mouths). Right side — nighttime: moonlit, tiny water drops appearing at the tips of grass leaves (guttation). Labels: "Transpiration — Water Vapour (Day)" and "Guttation — Water Droplets (Night)". Clean, simple, friendly illustration.

**Narration:**
"Plants lose water in two ways. Transpiration is when water escapes as invisible vapour through stomata — mostly during the day. Guttation is when liquid water drops appear at leaf tips at night — pushed out by root pressure. Those morning droplets you see on grass? That's guttation!"
