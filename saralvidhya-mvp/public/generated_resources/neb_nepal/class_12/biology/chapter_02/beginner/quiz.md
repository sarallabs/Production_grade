## Question 1

Where does most transpiration (water loss) in a plant occur?

A. Through the roots
B. Through the stem bark
C. Through the stomata on leaves
D. Through the flowers

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Stomata are tiny pores found on the surface of leaves. About 90% of all water that a plant loses as vapour escapes through these stomata. Guard cells on either side of the stomata can open or close the pore to control water loss. The other options account for very little water loss in comparison.

## Question 2

A plant cell is placed in salty water (hypertonic solution). What will happen?

A. The cell will swell up and burst
B. The cell will gain more minerals
C. The cell will lose water and may shrink (plasmolysis)
D. Nothing will happen

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** When a plant cell is placed in a hypertonic solution (more concentrated than the cell sap), water moves OUT of the cell through osmosis — this is called exosmosis. The cell loses its firmness, becomes flaccid, and if enough water is lost, the cell membrane shrinks away from the cell wall. This is called plasmolysis. It's the same reason vegetables shrink when sprinkled with salt!

## Question 3

What makes water molecules stay together as a continuous chain inside xylem tubes, allowing water to be pulled up a tall tree?

A. Root pressure pushing from below
B. Cohesion — the attraction between water molecules
C. The weight of the water itself
D. Photosynthesis in the leaves

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Water molecules are attracted to each other by hydrogen bonds — this is called cohesion. This strong attraction keeps water as a continuous, unbroken chain inside the xylem tubes. When water evaporates from the leaves (transpiration), it pulls the whole chain upward — like a chain of paperclips being lifted by pulling the top one. Cohesion stops the chain from breaking. This is the Cohesion-Tension Theory of ascent of sap.

## Question 4

What is diffusion?

A. Movement of water through a membrane from low concentration to high concentration
B. Movement of molecules from high concentration to low concentration without using energy
C. The process by which plants make food using sunlight
D. The movement of water upward through xylem

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Diffusion is the natural, spontaneous movement of molecules (any molecules — not just water) from an area where there are many of them (high concentration) to an area where there are fewer of them (low concentration). No energy is needed — it just happens on its own. Think of perfume spreading across a room — that's diffusion. It's different from osmosis, which specifically involves water moving through a membrane.

## Question 5

Early in the morning, you notice tiny water droplets on the tips of grass leaves. What process caused this?

A. Dew formation from the atmosphere
B. Transpiration through stomata
C. Guttation through hydathodes
D. Rain water collected on the leaf

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Those droplets are formed by guttation — a process where liquid water (not vapour) is pushed out of the plant through special pores called hydathodes, located at leaf tips and margins. This happens at night and early morning when the soil is wet, stomata are closed (so transpiration is low), and root pressure builds up, forcing liquid water out. Unlike dew (which forms from atmospheric water vapour condensing), guttation water comes from inside the plant.
