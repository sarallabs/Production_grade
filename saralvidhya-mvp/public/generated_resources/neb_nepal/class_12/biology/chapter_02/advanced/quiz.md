## Question 1

A plant cell has an osmotic pressure (OP) of 15 atm and is in a state where its DPD equals its OP. Which of the following correctly describes this cell?

A. The cell is fully turgid with maximum turgor pressure
B. The cell is flaccid with turgor pressure equal to zero
C. The cell is undergoing active plasmolysis and has lost all its water
D. The cell has a DPD of zero and is in equilibrium with pure water

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** DPD = OP − TP. If DPD = OP, then TP = 0. A turgor pressure of zero means the cell is flaccid — there is no pressure against the cell wall. This occurs when the cell is placed in a hypertonic solution and water has left via exosmosis. The protoplast is at the point of just losing contact with the wall (incipient plasmolysis). Option A is wrong because fully turgid means TP = OP → DPD = 0. Option D confuses DPD = 0 (turgid cell) with DPD = OP (flaccid cell).

## Question 2

In an experiment, a transpiring shoot is connected to a tube of mercury (Hg) and water. As the shoot transpires, the mercury column rises. When the shoot is covered with petroleum jelly (blocking all stomata), the mercury column stops rising. Which conclusion is BEST supported?

A. Root pressure is the primary driver of mercury rise
B. Transpiration pull through stomata is the main force responsible for water and mercury ascent
C. Imbibition by the xylem walls draws mercury upward
D. Cohesion of mercury molecules is responsible for the rise

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The mercury rises because transpiration through the stomata creates a negative pressure (transpiration pull) that is transmitted through the continuous water column in the xylem, pulling the mercury up. When stomata are blocked → no transpiration → no pull → mercury stops rising. This is a direct demonstration of Dixon's Cohesion-Tension theory. Root pressure would push water up from below, not be affected by blocking stomata. Mercury has very low cohesion with plant xylem walls.

## Question 3

Two gas molecules — Gas P (molecular mass = 2) and Gas Q (molecular mass = 32) — diffuse through the same medium. According to Graham's Law of Diffusion, Gas P diffuses how many times faster than Gas Q?

A. 2 times faster
B. 4 times faster
C. 8 times faster
D. 16 times faster

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Graham's Law: Rate ∝ 1/√M. Rate of P / Rate of Q = √M_Q / √M_P = √32 / √2 = √16 = 4. Gas P (e.g., H₂) diffuses 4 times faster than Gas Q (e.g., O₂). This principle is why hydrogen (lightest gas) diffuses fastest and is relevant to understanding differential gas exchange rates across stomata in plants.

## Question 4

A botanist observes that plant X shows no guttation even when placed in waterlogged soil overnight, but shows normal transpiration during the day. Plant Y shows vigorous guttation under the same waterlogged conditions. Which explanation BEST accounts for the absence of guttation in Plant X?

A. Plant X has sunken stomata that reduce water loss
B. Plant X has non-functional or absent hydathodes
C. Plant X has a higher rate of cuticular transpiration that dissipates root pressure
D. Plant X's guard cells are unable to respond to light signals

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Guttation specifically occurs through hydathodes — specialized structures connected to xylem endings at leaf margins. If Plant X has non-functional or absent hydathodes, root pressure cannot force liquid water out, even when root pressure is being generated (waterlogged soil → high root pressure). Option A (sunken stomata) affects transpiration, not guttation, since guttation does not occur through stomata. Option C is unlikely to fully dissipate root pressure in waterlogged conditions. Option D affects transpiration/gas exchange, not guttation.

## Question 5

A plant cell at incipient plasmolysis is transferred to pure water. During the process of deplasmolysis, which sequence correctly describes the changes in the cell's DPD?

A. DPD starts at 0, increases as water enters, and returns to 0 when fully turgid
B. DPD starts equal to OP, decreases as water enters (TP increases), and reaches 0 when fully turgid
C. DPD starts equal to OP, increases as water enters (due to increased osmotic pressure), and reaches maximum at full turgidity
D. DPD remains constant throughout the process since OP and TP change by equal amounts

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** At incipient plasmolysis: TP = 0, therefore DPD = OP (maximum DPD). As the cell is placed in pure water (higher water potential than cell), water enters by endosmosis → TP increases progressively → DPD = OP − TP decreases. When the cell reaches full turgidity: TP = OP → DPD = 0. So DPD goes from a maximum value (= OP) down to 0. Option A is wrong (DPD doesn't start at 0 for a flaccid cell). Option C is wrong (DPD decreases, not increases). Option D is wrong (TP increases but OP remains approximately constant for dilute solutions).
