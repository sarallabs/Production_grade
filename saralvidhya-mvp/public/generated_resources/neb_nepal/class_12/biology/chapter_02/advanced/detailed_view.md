## 1. Chapter Overview

Plant Physiology is the branch of biology concerned with the functional and metabolic activities of plants. This chapter focuses on **water relations** — how water enters, moves through, and exits the plant body. The topics span from molecular-level diffusion to whole-plant phenomena like the ascent of sap in 90-metre trees. Mastering this chapter requires understanding thermodynamic principles (water potential, chemical potential), pressure mechanics (turgor, osmotic, wall pressure), and the interplay of physical forces at organ and tissue levels.

**Key Figures:**
- **Stephan Hales** — Father of Plant Physiology
- **Sir J.C. Bose** — Father of Indian Plant Physiology
- **Jean Fernel (1552)** — coined the term "physiology" (meaning "natural knowledge")
- **Palladin (1927)** — defined physiology's goal: complete mechanistic knowledge of plant phenomena, reducible to physics and chemistry

## 2. Key Concepts

### 2.1 Importance of Water
- Constitutes **75–90%** of active protoplasm
- Functions: photosynthesis raw material, solvent for metabolites, medium for mineral transport, turgidity regulator, medium for plant movements
- Without water: protoplasm becomes inactive → cell death

### 2.2 Diffusion

**Definition:** Passive movement of molecules/ions from a region of **higher chemical potential (concentration)** to a region of **lower chemical potential**, until equilibrium is reached. No energy expenditure required.

**Key parameters:**
| Factor | Effect on diffusion rate |
|---|---|
| Concentration gradient | Higher gradient → faster diffusion |
| Temperature | Higher temp → increased kinetic energy → faster |
| Density of medium | Higher density → more resistance → slower |
| Molecular mass (Graham's Law) | Lighter molecules diffuse faster |

**States:** Gas > Liquid > Solid (in terms of diffusion speed)

**Diffusion Pressure (DP):** The pressure exerted by diffusing molecules due to their kinetic energy. Pure water has maximum DP.

**Diffusion Pressure Deficit (DPD):**
> DPD = Osmotic Pressure (OP) − Turgor Pressure (TP)

- DPD of pure water = 0
- Adding solutes → reduces DP → creates DPD
- DPD = Suction Pressure = driving force for water entry into cells
- When TP = OP → DPD = 0 → cell fully turgid → no net water movement

**Independent Diffusion:** Different molecules (CO₂, O₂, H₂O vapour) diffuse simultaneously through stomata, each along its own partial pressure gradient — this enables simultaneous photosynthesis gas exchange and transpiration.

**Significance of Diffusion:** Transpiration, gas exchange (photosynthesis/respiration), osmosis, passive salt uptake, hormone distribution.

### 2.3 Osmosis

**Definition:** Movement of water (solvent) through a **differentially permeable (semi-permeable) membrane** from a region of **higher water potential** to a region of **lower water potential**.

**Water Potential (Ψ):** Chemical potential of water. Pure water = highest Ψ. Addition of solutes lowers Ψ. Osmosis is always from high Ψ → low Ψ. Passive process (no ATP needed).

**Types of semi-permeable membranes:** Plasma membrane, tonoplast, egg membrane, fish bladder, parchment paper.

**Types of Osmosis:**
| Type | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Endosmosis | Cell in hypotonic solution | Water enters → turgidity ↑ |
| Exosmosis | Cell in hypertonic solution | Water exits → turgidity ↓ → plasmolysis |

**Solution Types:**
- **Isotonic** — same solute concentration as cell sap → no net osmosis
- **Hypotonic** — lower concentration than cell sap → endosmosis
- **Hypertonic** — higher concentration than cell sap → exosmosis

**Turgor Pressure (TP):** Hydrostatic pressure exerted by cell contents against cell wall during endosmosis.

**Wall Pressure (WP):** Equal and opposite force exerted by rigid cell wall back on protoplasm. At equilibrium: WP = TP.

**Plasmolysis:** Shrinkage of protoplast away from cell wall in hypertonic solution (exosmosis). Proves: cell wall is permeable, plasma membrane is selectively permeable.

**Deplasmolysis:** Recovery of plasmolysed cell when returned to hypotonic solution.

**Flaccid cell:** TP = 0, WP = 0, maximum DPD = OP

**Turgid cell:** TP = OP, DPD = 0, maximum WP

**Significance of Plasmolysis:** Test for living cells, food preservation (pickling — osmotic death of microbes), demonstrates membrane permeability differences.

### 2.4 Ascent of Sap

**Definition:** Upward movement of water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves through xylem.

**Path:** Xylem — tracheids and vessels (hollow, lignified, dead cells forming continuous tubes).

**Evidence for xylem pathway:**
- **Stain Test (Eosin):** Eosin solution taken up; xylem stains red
- **Ringing Test:** Removing cortex + phloem does not wilt the plant; xylem intact → sap still moves

**Theories (in order of historical development):**

#### Vital Force Theories (REJECTED)
| Theory | Proponent | Mechanism | Objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relay Pump Theory | Godlewski | Osmotic pumping by xylem parenchyma | Water rises in dead/poisoned stems (Strasburgher, MacDougal) |
| Pulsation Theory | J.C. Bose | Rhythmic pulsations of innermost cortex cells | Same as above |

#### Physical Force Theories

| Theory | Mechanism | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Root Pressure Theory (Priestly) | Osmotic hydrostatic pressure builds in roots | Max ~2 atm → only ~21 m lift; absent in gymnosperms; seasonal |
| Atmospheric Pressure Theory | Evaporation creates vacuum; atmosphere pushes up | Works only to ~10 m; no free exposed surface exists |
| Imbibition Theory | Colloidal xylem walls imbibe water | Too slow; inadequate force |
| **Cohesion-Tension Theory (Dixon)** ✓ | Transpiration pull + cohesion + adhesion | **Most accepted** |

#### Cohesion-Tension (Dixon's) Theory — MOST ACCEPTED

**Three pillars:**
1. **Continuous water column** in xylem from root to leaf
2. **Cohesion** — intermolecular H-bond attraction between water molecules (up to ~350 atm); maintains column integrity
3. **Adhesion** — attraction of water to hydrophilic xylem walls; prevents gravitational slipping
4. **Transpiration Pull** — evaporation at leaf mesophyll creates negative pressure (tension) that is transmitted through the continuous water column to roots

**Mechanism:**
- Evaporation from mesophyll cells → water potential of leaf cells drops → osmotic movement of water from xylem → tension/negative pressure transmitted downward → water pulled up from roots

**Supporting experiment:** Transpiring twig + tube of water dipped in mercury → mercury rises as twig transpires → demonstrates suction/pull force.

### 2.5 Transpiration

**Definition:** Loss of water in the form of **water vapour** from aerial parts of the plant (leaves, stems, flowers).

**Scale:** Plants absorb large volumes of water; only **1–2% used** for metabolism; **97–99% lost** via transpiration.

**Types:**
| Type | Site | % of total loss |
|---|---|---|
| Stomatal | Through stomata | ~90% |
| Cuticular | Through leaf cuticle | 3–10% |
| Lenticular | Through lenticels in bark | ~1% |

#### Stomatal Structure and Mechanism
- **Dicots:** Kidney-shaped guard cells
- **Monocots:** Dumb-bell shaped guard cells
- Surrounded by **subsidiary cells** (epidermal cells flanking guard cells)

**Opening mechanism (K⁺ ion pump theory):**
1. Light triggers K⁺ active transport INTO guard cells
2. Solute concentration ↑ → water potential of guard cells ↓
3. Water enters guard cells via osmosis from epidermal cells
4. Guard cells swell; **inner walls thicker/less elastic** → uneven stretching → cells bow outward → pore opens

**Closing:** K⁺ exits → water potential ↑ → water exits → guard cells become flaccid → pore closes

**Functions of Transpiration:**
- Cools leaf surface (latent heat of vaporisation)
- Creates transpiration pull (driving force for ascent of sap)
- Maintains cell turgidity
- Facilitates mineral translocation from soil

**Factors affecting transpiration rate:**

*External:*
- **Light:** Opens stomata → increases transpiration
- **Temperature:** Increases rate up to ~35°C; beyond that stomata may close
- **Wind:** Removes saturated air → increases rate; excessive wind → stomata close
- **Humidity:** High humidity → decreases transpiration; low humidity → increases it
- **CO₂ concentration:** High CO₂ → stomata close → reduces transpiration

*Internal (plant):*
- Cuticle thickness (thicker = less cuticular transpiration)
- Number and distribution of stomata
- Sunken stomata (xerophytic adaptation) → reduces transpiration
- Root/shoot ratio

**Experiments:**
- **Bell Jar method:** Potted plant under bell jar → condensation proves transpiration
- **Cobalt Chloride paper test:** Blue → pink on lower leaf surface faster → more stomata on abaxial surface of dorsiventral leaves
- **Ganong's Potometer:** Measures water absorption (~= transpiration) via air bubble movement in graduated tube

### 2.6 Guttation

**Definition:** Exudation of **liquid water droplets** (not vapour) from **hydathodes** at leaf tips/margins.

**Mechanism:** High soil moisture + low transpiration (night/early morning, stomata closed) → root pressure builds → forces liquid water through hydathodes.

**Hydathodes:** Specialized water-secreting structures at leaf margins; connected to xylem endings; not under stomatal regulation.

**Differs from transpiration:**
| Feature | Transpiration | Guttation |
|---|---|---|
| Form of water | Vapour | Liquid droplets |
| Driving force | Transpiration pull (negative pressure) | Root pressure (positive pressure) |
| Site | Stomata, cuticle, lenticels | Hydathodes |
| Timing | Daytime (mainly) | Night / early morning |
| Residue | None | White mineral/organic crust |

**Significance:** Helps balance water and nutrient status; evidence of root pressure.

**Risks:** Mineral salt deposits can cause leaf margin injury; excess water/nutrient loss.

## 3. Definitions and Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| DPD | Osmotic Pressure − Turgor Pressure; net driving force for water entry |
| Water Potential (Ψ) | Chemical potential of water; pure water = 0; solutions have negative Ψ |
| Osmotic Pressure | Pressure needed to prevent osmosis across a semi-permeable membrane |
| Turgor Pressure | Pressure of cell contents against cell wall |
| Wall Pressure | Equal and opposite pressure of cell wall on protoplasm |
| Plasmolysis | Shrinkage of protoplast away from wall in hypertonic solution |
| Deplasmolysis | Recovery of plasmolysed cell in hypotonic solution |
| Transpiration Pull | Negative pressure/tension in xylem created by leaf evaporation |
| Cohesion | Mutual attraction between water molecules (H-bonds) |
| Adhesion | Attraction of water molecules to xylem wall surfaces |
| Hydathodes | Specialized structures for liquid water secretion in guttation |
| Imbibition | Absorption of water by colloidal substances causing swelling |

## 4. Important Points for Competitive Exams

- DPD of pure water = 0; DPD of a fully turgid cell = 0; DPD is maximum in a flaccid cell
- Water potential of pure water = 0 (reference); solutes always make it negative
- Root pressure can lift water only ~21 m — insufficient for tall trees
- Cohesion force between water molecules can be up to 350 atm
- K⁺ ion pump is the most accepted mechanism for stomatal opening
- Stomatal transpiration = ~90% of total water loss
- Only 1–2% of absorbed water is used in metabolism
- Guttation is driven by positive root pressure; transpiration is driven by negative pressure
- Strasburgher's experiment disproved vital force theories
- Gymnosperms show little/no root pressure → depends entirely on cohesion-tension

---

## 5. Common Mistakes

- Confusing **DPD** with **osmotic pressure** — DPD is OP minus TP, not OP alone
- Thinking transpiration is wasteful — it drives the entire ascent of sap and cools leaves
- Confusing **guttation** (liquid) with **transpiration** (vapour)
- Assuming root pressure alone drives ascent of sap in tall trees — it cannot
- Misidentifying guard cell shape: kidney-shaped in **dicots**, dumb-bell in **monocots**
- Thinking osmosis requires energy — it is entirely passive (driven by water potential gradient)
- Confusing **endosmosis** (water in) with **exosmosis** (water out)
