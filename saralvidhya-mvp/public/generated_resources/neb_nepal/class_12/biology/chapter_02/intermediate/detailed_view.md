## 1. Chapter Overview

Plant physiology is the study of functional processes in plants — metabolism, growth, locomotion, and respiration. The chapter focuses on water relations: how water enters, moves through, and exits the plant.

**Key Figures:**
- **Stephan Hales** — Father of Plant Physiology
- **Sir J.C. Bose** — Father of Indian Plant Physiology
- **Jean Fernel (1552)** — coined "physiology" (natural knowledge)
- **Palladin (1927)** — goal of plant physiology: complete mechanistic knowledge reducible to physics and chemistry

## 2. Key Concepts

### 2.1 Importance of Water

Water constitutes **75–90%** of active protoplasm. Without water, protoplasm becomes inactive or dies.

Functions of water:
- Raw material for photosynthesis
- Solvent for metabolic reactants
- Medium for mineral/organic transport
- Maintains cell turgidity
- Medium for plant movements (stomatal opening, flower movement)

---

### 2.2 Diffusion

**Definition:** Passive, spontaneous movement of molecules/ions from a region of **higher concentration** to **lower concentration** until equilibrium. No energy required.

**Rate of diffusion depends on:**
- Concentration gradient (higher → faster)
- Temperature (higher → faster kinetic energy)
- Density of medium (higher → slower; gas > liquid > solid)
- Molecular mass (lighter → faster — Graham's Law)

**Experiment:** Copper sulphate crystals in water — intense blue near crystals gradually spreads to uniform blue throughout — demonstrates diffusion.

**Diffusion Pressure (DP):** Pressure exerted by diffusing molecules proportional to their concentration.

**Diffusion Pressure Deficit (DPD):**
> **DPD = Osmotic Pressure (OP) − Turgor Pressure (TP)**

- DPD of pure water = 0
- Adding solutes → DP drops → DPD increases
- Also called **Suction Pressure** or **Suction Force**
- Water always moves from low DPD → high DPD

**Independent Diffusion:** Different substances (CO₂, O₂, H₂O vapour) diffuse simultaneously through stomata, each along its own concentration gradient.

**Significance:** Transpiration, gas exchange, osmosis, passive salt uptake, hormone distribution.

---

### 2.3 Osmosis

**Definition:** Movement of water (solvent) through a **differentially permeable membrane** from a region of **higher water concentration (higher water potential)** to **lower water concentration (lower water potential)**. Passive process.

**Semi-permeable membrane:** Allows water and small solutes to pass; blocks large solute particles. Examples: plasma membrane, tonoplast, egg membrane, parchment paper.

**Types of Osmosis:**
| Type | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Endosmosis | Cell in hypotonic solution | Water enters → turgid cell |
| Exosmosis | Cell in hypertonic solution | Water exits → flaccid → plasmolysis |

**Solution Types:**
- **Isotonic:** Same concentration as cell sap → no net osmosis
- **Hypotonic:** Lower concentration → endosmosis → turgidity
- **Hypertonic:** Higher concentration → exosmosis → plasmolysis

**Turgor Pressure (TP):** Pressure of cell contents against cell wall (during endosmosis).

**Wall Pressure (WP):** Equal and opposite force of cell wall on protoplasm. At equilibrium: WP = TP.

**Plasmolysis:** Protoplast shrinks away from cell wall in hypertonic solution.
- Proves: cell wall is permeable; plasma membrane is selectively permeable
- Significance: food preservation (pickling kills bacteria by plasmolysis), test for living cells

**Deplasmolysis:** Recovery when plasmolysed cell placed in hypotonic/water solution.

**Cell States:**
| State | TP | DPD | WP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flaccid | 0 | = OP | 0 |
| Partially turgid | < OP | OP − TP | = TP |
| Fully turgid | = OP | 0 | = OP |

---

### 2.4 Ascent of Sap

**Definition:** Upward movement of water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves through xylem.

**Path:** Xylem — tracheids and vessels (hollow, dead cells forming continuous tubes).

**Evidence:**
- **Stain Test:** Eosin solution stains xylem red → confirms xylem as water pathway
- **Ringing Test:** Remove cortex + phloem; leaves stay fresh → xylem alone conducts water

**Theories:**

| Theory | Proponent | Mechanism | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relay Pump Theory | Godlewski | Living xylem parenchyma pump water osmotically | **Rejected** — water rises in dead stems (Strasburgher) |
| Pulsation Theory | J.C. Bose | Cortex cell pulsations pump water | **Rejected** — same reason |
| Root Pressure Theory | J.H. Priestly | Osmotic root pressure pushes water up | **Insufficient** — max ~21 m; absent in gymnosperms |
| Atmospheric Pressure Theory | — | Evaporation creates vacuum; atmosphere pushes up | **Insufficient** — works only to ~10 m |
| **Cohesion-Tension Theory** | **H.H. Dixon** | Transpiration pull + cohesion + adhesion | **ACCEPTED** |

**Cohesion-Tension Theory (Dixon's Theory):**

Three pillars:
1. **Continuous water column** in xylem — from roots to leaves
2. **Cohesion** — strong H-bond attraction between water molecules (~350 atm); keeps column intact
3. **Adhesion** — attraction of water to hydrophilic xylem walls; prevents gravity-induced slipping
4. **Transpiration Pull** — evaporation from leaf mesophyll creates negative pressure/tension, transmitted down through the continuous water column, pulling water from roots

**Demonstration:** Transpiring twig connected to water-mercury tube — mercury rises as twig transpires; stops when stomata blocked.

### 2.5 Transpiration

**Definition:** Loss of water in the form of **water vapour** from aerial parts (leaves, stems, flowers).

Plants absorb large amounts of water; only **1–2% used** for growth; **97–99% lost** via transpiration.

**Types:**
| Type | Site | % of Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Stomatal | Through stomata | ~90% |
| Cuticular | Through leaf cuticle | 3–10% |
| Lenticular | Through lenticels in bark | ~1% |

**Stomata and Guard Cells:**
- Stomata are minute pores bordered by **two guard cells**
- **Dicots:** Kidney-shaped guard cells
- **Monocots:** Dumb-bell shaped guard cells
- Opening: guard cells become turgid → outer walls stretch → pore opens
- Closing: guard cells lose water → become flaccid → pore closes
- Regulated by K⁺ ion movement into/out of guard cells

**Functions of Transpiration:**
- Cools leaf surface (evaporative cooling)
- Creates transpiration pull — drives ascent of sap
- Maintains cell turgidity
- Facilitates mineral translocation from soil

**Factors Affecting Transpiration:**

*External:*
- **Light:** Stomata open → increases transpiration
- **Temperature:** Increases rate (up to ~35°C)
- **Wind:** Removes saturated air → increases rate
- **Humidity:** High humidity → decreases transpiration

*Internal:*
- Cuticle thickness, stomata number, sunken stomata, leaf area

**Experiments:**
- **Bell Jar method:** Condensation inside bell jar proves transpiration
- **Cobalt Chloride paper test:** Turns pink on lower (abaxial) surface faster → more stomata on lower surface of dorsiventral leaves
- **Ganong's Potometer:** Measures water absorption (~= transpiration); air bubble moves in calibrated tube

---

### 2.6 Guttation

**Definition:** Exudation of **liquid water droplets** from **hydathodes** at leaf tips and margins.

**Mechanism:** High soil moisture + closed stomata (night/early morning) → root pressure builds → liquid water forced through hydathodes.

**Hydathodes:** Permanently open pores at leaf margins connected to xylem endings; cannot be regulated (unlike stomata).

**Comparison with Transpiration:**
| Feature | Transpiration | Guttation |
|---|---|---|
| Form of water | Vapour | Liquid droplets |
| Driving force | Transpiration pull (negative pressure) | Root pressure (positive pressure) |
| Structures | Stomata, cuticle, lenticels | Hydathodes |
| Time | Daytime | Night / early morning |
| Residue | None | White mineral/organic crust |

## 3. Important Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| DPD | OP − TP; net driving force for water entry into cells |
| Osmotic Pressure | Pressure needed to prevent osmosis |
| Turgor Pressure | Pressure of cell contents against cell wall |
| Plasmolysis | Protoplast shrinks away from wall in hypertonic solution |
| Cohesion | Mutual attraction between water molecules (H-bonds) |
| Adhesion | Attraction of water molecules to xylem walls |
| Transpiration Pull | Suction/tension in xylem created by evaporation at leaves |
| Hydathodes | Structures for guttation at leaf margins |

---

## 4. Important Points for Board Exam

- DPD = 0 in pure water AND in a fully turgid cell
- Root pressure = ~2 atm → can lift water only ~21 m
- Cohesion force = up to 350 atm
- Stomatal transpiration = ~90% of total water loss
- Only 1–2% of water absorbed is used in metabolism
- Guard cells: kidney-shaped (dicots) vs dumb-bell (monocots)
- Strasburgher's experiment disproved vital force theories

---

## 5. Common Mistakes

- DPD ≠ Osmotic Pressure alone; DPD = OP − TP
- Transpiration ≠ Guttation; different forms, driving forces, structures
- Root pressure alone CANNOT explain ascent in tall trees
- Osmosis is passive — no energy (ATP) required
- Xylem = path for water; phloem = path for food (sugar)

## 7. Quick Revision

1. Water = 75–90% of protoplasm; needed for photosynthesis, transport, turgidity
2. Diffusion = passive, high → low concentration; DPD = OP − TP = suction pressure
3. Osmosis = water through semi-permeable membrane; endosmosis → turgid; exosmosis → plasmolysis
4. Ascent of sap = through xylem; Cohesion-Tension theory (most accepted)
5. Transpiration = 97–99% of absorbed water; 90% stomatal; regulated by guard cells
6. Guttation = liquid water, hydathodes, root pressure, night/morning
