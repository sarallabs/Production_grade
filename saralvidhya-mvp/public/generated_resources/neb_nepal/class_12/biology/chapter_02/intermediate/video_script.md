## Scene 2
**Duration:** 25 seconds

**Visual Prompt:**
Clean scientific animation showing two processes side by side. Left: general diffusion — molecules spreading from high to low concentration across an open space (copper sulphate crystals dissolving in water, blue spreading outward). Right: osmosis — water molecules crossing a semi-permeable membrane (shown as dotted line) from dilute to concentrated side. Labels: "Diffusion — any molecules, no membrane" and "Osmosis — water only, through membrane". Blue and white colour scheme, textbook-style clean animation.

**Narration:**
"Diffusion is the spontaneous movement of molecules from high to low concentration — no energy needed. Osmosis is a special case: only water moves, through a selectively permeable membrane, from high water potential to low. The driving force? DPD — Diffusion Pressure Deficit — equal to Osmotic Pressure minus Turgor Pressure."

## Scene 3
**Duration:** 30 seconds

**Visual Prompt:**
Animated sequence of a plant cell in three states. State 1: Turgid — cell in hypotonic solution, water entering (blue arrows inward), cell visibly swollen, DPD = 0 label. State 2: Flaccid — water leaving, cell deflating, TP = 0 label. State 3: Plasmolysed — cell in hypertonic solution, protoplast visibly shrunk and separated from cell wall, gap clearly labeled. Then show reverse: cell placed in water → protoplast expands back → "Deplasmolysis". Clean cell diagram style with labeled membranes and walls.

**Narration:**
"Add water — endosmosis — the cell becomes turgid. Add concentrated salt — exosmosis — it becomes flaccid. Continue losing water and the protoplast pulls away from the wall — that's plasmolysis. Return it to water, and deplasmolysis restores the cell. These states are governed entirely by the balance of osmotic and turgor pressure."

Include a small insert showing the mercury-rising experiment (twig + tube + mercury). Scientific illustration, teal and brown colour palette.

**Narration:**
"How does water reach the top of a 90-metre Sequoia? Dixon's Cohesion-Tension Theory. Evaporation from leaves creates a transpiration pull — a negative pressure that travels down through an unbroken water column. Cohesion — hydrogen bonds between water molecules — keeps the column intact. Adhesion to xylem walls stops it from falling. This elegant physical mechanism requires no energy from the plant."
