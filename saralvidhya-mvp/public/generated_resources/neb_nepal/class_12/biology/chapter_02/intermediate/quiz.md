## Question 1

A plant cell has an osmotic pressure of 12 atm and a turgor pressure of 7 atm. What is its Diffusion Pressure Deficit (DPD)?

A. 19 atm
B. 7 atm
C. 12 atm
D. 5 atm

**Correct Answer: D**

**Explanation:** DPD = Osmotic Pressure − Turgor Pressure = 12 − 7 = 5 atm. DPD is also called suction pressure — it represents the net force driving water into the cell. The higher the DPD, the more water the cell will "pull in." A fully turgid cell has DPD = 0 (because TP = OP). Pure water also has DPD = 0. This formula DPD = OP − TP is important for all board exam numerical questions.

## Question 2

Which of the following correctly explains why vital force theories of ascent of sap were rejected?

A. Root pressure was found to be sufficient to lift water in tall trees
B. Water was found to rise in stems even after all living cells were killed
C. Xylem was found to be a living tissue that actively pumps water
D. Transpiration was shown to have no role in water movement

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Strasburgher (and MacDougal) killed all the living cells of a stem using picric acid or boiling, and yet water continued to rise through the xylem. This was direct proof that living cells are NOT required for ascent of sap — eliminating theories like Godlewski's relay pump and J.C. Bose's pulsation theory, which relied on living cell activity. This left purely physical mechanisms (like Cohesion-Tension Theory) as the only valid explanation.

## Question 3

Cobalt chloride paper is placed on both surfaces of a dorsiventral (dicot) leaf. The paper on the lower surface turns pink faster. What does this demonstrate?

A. The lower surface of the leaf is warmer
B. The upper surface has a thicker cuticle preventing any water loss
C. More stomata are present on the lower (abaxial) surface of a dorsiventral leaf
D. Guttation occurs through the lower surface of the leaf

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Cobalt chloride paper is blue when dry and turns pink when it absorbs water vapour (moisture). The faster colour change on the lower surface indicates greater water vapour loss there. This is because in dorsiventral (bifacial) dicot leaves, the majority of stomata are located on the lower (abaxial) surface — this reduces water loss from the sunlit upper surface while still allowing gas exchange. This experiment demonstrates both transpiration and the distribution of stomata.

## Question 4

Which of the following conditions will INCREASE the rate of transpiration?

A. High humidity, low temperature, no wind
B. Low humidity, high temperature, moderate wind
C. High humidity, high temperature, no light
D. Low temperature, high CO₂ concentration, no wind

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Transpiration rate increases when: (1) Humidity is LOW — larger vapour pressure gradient between leaf interior and outside air drives faster evaporation. (2) Temperature is HIGH — increases evaporation rate and kinetic energy of water molecules, and also keeps stomata open. (3) Moderate wind — removes the layer of saturated air around the leaf, maintaining a steep humidity gradient. Option A (high humidity, no wind) would slow transpiration. Option D (low temp, high CO₂) would cause stomatal closure.

## Question 5

In guttation, water exits the plant in liquid form, while in transpiration it exits as vapour. Which of the following correctly explains this fundamental difference?

A. Guttation occurs through stomata; transpiration through hydathodes
B. Guttation is driven by root pressure (positive); transpiration is driven by transpiration pull (negative pressure)
C. Guttation requires sunlight; transpiration occurs only at night
D. Guttation is a type of diffusion; transpiration is a type of osmosis

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The key difference is the driving force and direction of pressure. Transpiration is driven by negative pressure (tension) created by evaporation at the leaf — the transpiration pull pulls water upward and out as vapour through stomata. Guttation is driven by positive root pressure — osmotic activity of root cells builds hydrostatic pressure that physically pushes liquid water upward and out through hydathodes (which cannot close). Because it is a positive push of liquid water (not evaporation), the water exits as liquid droplets, not vapour.
