Plant physiology studies the life processes of plants — metabolism, growth, respiration, and movement. **Stephan Hales** is the father of plant physiology; **Sir J.C. Bose** is the father of Indian plant physiology. Water constitutes **75–90%** of active protoplasm and is essential for photosynthesis, mineral transport, cell turgidity, and metabolic reactions.

**Diffusion** is the passive movement of molecules from high to low concentration. The **Diffusion Pressure Deficit (DPD = OP − TP)** is the net force driving water into a cell. A pure water has DPD = 0; a fully turgid cell also has DPD = 0.

**Osmosis** is movement of water through a differentially permeable membrane from high water potential to low water potential. Endosmosis (water enters — hypotonic medium) makes cells turgid; exosmosis (water exits — hypertonic medium) leads to plasmolysis. Deplasmolysis occurs when a plasmolysed cell is returned to a hypotonic solution.

**Ascent of Sap** occurs through xylem (tracheids and vessels). The most accepted theory is **Dixon's Cohesion-Tension Theory**: evaporation from leaves creates a transpiration pull (negative pressure), transmitted through a continuous water column maintained by cohesion (H-bonds between water molecules) and adhesion (water to xylem walls). Root pressure (~2 atm) is insufficient for tall trees. Vital force theories (Godlewski, Bose) were rejected after Strasburgher showed water rises in stems with dead cells.

**Transpiration** — loss of water vapour from aerial parts — accounts for 97–99% of water absorbed. Types: stomatal (~90%), cuticular (3–10%), lenticular (~1%). Guard cells (kidney-shaped in dicots; dumb-bell in monocots) regulate stomatal opening based on turgor. Factors: light, temperature, humidity, wind. Measured using Ganong's potometer.

**Guttation** is exudation of liquid water through hydathodes at leaf margins, driven by root pressure at night/early morning. Unlike transpiration, it leaves mineral residues on leaf margins.
