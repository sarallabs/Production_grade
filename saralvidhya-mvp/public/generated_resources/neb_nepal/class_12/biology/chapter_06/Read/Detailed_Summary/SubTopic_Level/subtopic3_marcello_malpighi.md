### 1.3 Marcello Malpighi — Pioneer of Microscopic Histology

**Marcello Malpighi** (1628–1694) was an Italian physician and biologist widely celebrated as the **Pioneer and Founder of Microscopic Anatomy**. By applying newly invented light microscopes to animal tissues, Malpighi uncovered microscopic structures previously invisible to the human eye.

#### Landmark Histological Discoveries
1. **Discovery of Capillaries (1661)**: Malpighi observed the microcirculation in frog lungs, demonstrating microscopic capillary networks connecting arterial arterioles to venous venules. This discovery provided the missing physical link in William Harvey's circulatory model.
2. **Renal Malpighian Corpuscles**: Identified the renal corpuscles (composed of the glomerulus and surrounding capsule) responsible for blood filtration in the kidney nephron.
3. **Cutaneous Malpighian Layer**: Described the deep germinative layer (*stratum basale* and *stratum spinosum*) of the mammalian epidermis.
4. **Insect Excretory Tubules**: Discovered the excretory Malpighian tubules in arthropods.

Malpighi's observational breakthroughs laid the empirical foundation for modern cellular histology and microscopic pathology.
