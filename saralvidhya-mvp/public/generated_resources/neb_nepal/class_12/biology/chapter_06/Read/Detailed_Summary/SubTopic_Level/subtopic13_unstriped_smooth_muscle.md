### 4.2 Unstriped / Smooth Muscle — Visceral Non-Striated Musculature

**Smooth Muscle** (Unstriped, Non-striated, or Involuntary Muscle) lines the tunics of internal hollow visceral organs and blood vessels, executing slow, sustained involuntary contractions.

#### Structural Characteristics
- **Fusiform Cell Morphology**: Individual muscle fibers are spindle-shaped (fusiform) with broad central bodies and tapering ends.
- **Uninucleate Architecture**: Each cell contains a single, centrally located oval nucleus.
- **Absence of Sarcomeric Striations**: Actin and myosin filaments are present but do not form regular repeating sarcomeres; instead, thin actin filaments criss-cross the cytoplasm, anchoring to cytoplasmic **dense bodies** (analogous to Z-lines).

#### Functional Classification

1. **Single-Unit (Visceral) Smooth Muscle**:
   - Muscle fibers are densely interconnected by low-resistance **gap junctions**.
   - Action potentials spread rapidly from cell to cell, causing the entire muscle sheet to contract rhythmically as a single coordinated functional unit (e.g. muscular coats of stomach, intestines, bile duct, uterus).
2. **Multi-Unit Smooth Muscle**:
   - Individual muscle fibers operate independently, each receiving separate autonomic motor nerve terminals.
   - Lacks gap junctions; permits fine, incremental contractile control (e.g. ciliary body and iris muscles of the eye, arrector pili hair muscles, wall of large arteries).
