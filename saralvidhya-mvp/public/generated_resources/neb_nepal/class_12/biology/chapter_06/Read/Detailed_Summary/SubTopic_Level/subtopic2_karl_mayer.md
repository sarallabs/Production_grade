### 1.2 Karl Mayer — Formalization of Histological Nomenclature

In 1801, German anatomist **Karl Mayer** (August Franz Josef Karl Mayer) introduced the formal scientific term **Histology** to describe the emerging discipline focused on the microscopic structure of biological tissues.

#### Etymology and Anatomical Scope
- **Etymological Roots**: The word *histology* is synthesized from the Greek terms ***histos*** (ἱστός, meaning 'web', 'beam', or 'woven tissue') and ***logos*** (λόγος, meaning 'study' or 'scientific discourse').
- **Scientific Definition**: Histology is defined as the microscopic anatomical science devoted to investigating the structural organization, intercellular matrix composition, cell-to-cell junctions, and physiological functions of tissues across biological taxa.

By establishing precise terminology, Karl Mayer elevated histology from a fragmented sub-discipline of macroscopic anatomy into a rigorous, recognized scientific field.
