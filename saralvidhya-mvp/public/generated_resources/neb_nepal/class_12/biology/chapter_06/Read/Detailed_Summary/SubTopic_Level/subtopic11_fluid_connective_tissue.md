### 3.3 Fluid Connective Tissue — Blood and Lymph Microvascular Components

**Fluid Connective Tissue** (Vascular or Mobile Connective Tissue) is a specialized liquid tissue circulating continuously through closed blood vessels and lymphatic channels.

#### 1. Blood Composition and Formed Elements

```
Whole Blood (100%)
 ├── Plasma (55% Volume)
 │     ├── Water (90–92%)
 │     ├── Plasma Proteins (6–8%): Albumin | Globulins | Fibrinogen
 │     └── Dissolved Nutrients & Electrolytes (Na+, Ca2+, Cl-, Glucose)
 └── Formed Elements / Blood Cells (45% Volume)
       ├── RBCs / Erythrocytes (5–5.5 million/mm³): Biconcave, Hemoglobin, Enucleated
       ├── WBCs / Leucocytes (6,000–8,000/mm³): Nucleated Immune Cells
       │     ├── Granulocytes: Neutrophils (60%) | Eosinophils (2-3%) | Basophils (0.5-1%)
       │     └── Agranulocytes: Lymphocytes B & T (20-25%) | Monocytes (6-8%)
       └── Platelets / Thrombocytes (150,000–350,000/mm³): Clotting Cell Fragments
```

#### Detailed Leucocyte Differential (WBC Subtypes)
- **Neutrophils**: Multilobed nucleus; highly phagocytic first responders to bacterial infections.
- **Eosinophils**: Bilobed nucleus; release histaminase during allergic reactions and combat parasitic infections.
- **Basophils**: S-shaped nucleus; release histamine, heparin, and serotonin during inflammatory cascades.
- **Lymphocytes**: Large spherical nucleus; **B-lymphocytes** differentiate into antibody-producing plasma cells; **T-lymphocytes** execute cell-mediated immunity.
- **Monocytes**: Kidney-shaped nucleus; largest WBCs that exit circulation to become tissue **macrophages**.

#### 2. Lymph Architecture
**Lymph** is interstitial tissue fluid that enters lymphatic capillaries. It consists of blood plasma filtered across blood capillaries, rich in small plasma proteins and **lymphocytes**, but completely devoid of RBCs, platelets, and large clotting complexes.
