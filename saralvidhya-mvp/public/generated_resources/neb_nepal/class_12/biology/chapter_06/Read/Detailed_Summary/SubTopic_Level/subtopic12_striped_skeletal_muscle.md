### 4.1 Striped / Skeletal Muscle — Striated Syncytial Microarchitecture

**Skeletal Muscle** (Striped or Voluntary Muscle) constitutes the bulk of body somatic musculature attached to bones. It generates mechanical force for locomotion and postural stability under voluntary somatic motor control.

#### Microscopic & Ultrastructural Organization
- **Syncytial Fiber Architecture**: Skeletal muscle fibers are long, unbranched cylindrical cells formed by fusion of embryonic myoblasts, resulting in a **multinucleated syncytium** with multiple nuclei pushed to the peripheral plasma membrane (**sarcolemma**).
- **Striations & Sarcomere Unit**: Myofibrils exhibit distinct transverse striations due to repeating longitudinal arrangements of thick (**myosin**) and thin (**actin**) myofilaments:
  - **A-Band (Anisotropic Band)**: Dark central region containing overlapping thick myosin filaments.
  - **I-Band (Isotropic Band)**: Light region containing thin actin filaments bisected by a proteinaceous **Z-line (Z-disc)**.
  - **Sarcomere**: The basic structural and functional contractile unit spanning between two adjacent Z-lines.

#### Red vs White Muscle Fiber Comparison

| Functional Characteristic | Red Muscle Fibers (Slow-Twitch) | White Muscle Fibers (Fast-Twitch) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Myoglobin Content** | Abundant (Deep red appearance) | Very low / Absent (Pale white appearance) |
| **Mitochondrial Density** | Extremely high (Oxidative) | Low (Glycolytic) |
| **Capillary Supply** | Dense capillary network | Sparse capillary network |
| **Primary Metabolism** | Aerobic respiration | Anaerobic glycolysis |
| **Contraction Speed & Fatigue** | Slow sustained contractions; Highly fatigue-resistant (e.g. postural back muscles) | Fast powerful contractions; Fatigues rapidly (e.g. extraocular eye muscles) |
