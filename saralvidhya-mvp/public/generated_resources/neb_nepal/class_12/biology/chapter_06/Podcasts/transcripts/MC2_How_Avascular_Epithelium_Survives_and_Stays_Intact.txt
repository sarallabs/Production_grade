First, we've got to look at the unique physical constraints of this tissue. It's built right on a basement membrane made of white collagen, and it has a minimal matrix, meaning there's very little extracellular fluid or space.

Most importantly, it is avascular, meaning it has zero blood supply. So, you might be wondering, if this tissue has literally no blood vessels, how does it not starve to death?

Well, it relies completely on the process of diffusion to receive nutrition and gases, and to expel waste. Second, because there is minimal matrix to act as a cellular scaffolding, the cells must be held together directly.

We're talking about four exact types of cell junctions used to accomplish this. Think of these like different types of microscopic hardware like biological superglue, rivets, and tunnels that lock the cells together to form an impenetrable barrier.

You've got tight junctions, or zonula occludens, acting as that superglue. Then there's adherence junctions, or zonula adherence.

Next up are desmosomes, also called macula adherence, which function as those heavy-duty rivets. And finally, gap junctions, known as the septed junction, form the tiny tunnels.

Ultimately, just remember that epithelial tissues are avascular, minimally spaced cellular barriers held firmly intact by four specialized junctions.
