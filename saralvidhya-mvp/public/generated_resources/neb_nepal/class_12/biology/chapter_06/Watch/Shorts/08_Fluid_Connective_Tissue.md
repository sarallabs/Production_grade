# Short 08: Fluid Connective Tissue — Blood & Lymph

**Duration:** ~90 seconds | **Words:** ~240 | **Mode:** Causal & Process-Oriented

---

**[HOOK]**
What fluid flows 24/7 through 100,000 kilometres of vessels, carries oxygen to every cell, and fights every infection your body faces? Blood — your fluid connective tissue.

---

**[SCRIPT]**
Blood is a **specialised fluid connective tissue** made of two components:

**Plasma (55%):**
- ~90% water
- Plasma proteins: albumin (osmotic pressure), globulins (immune function and transport), fibrinogen (blood clotting)
- Solutes: glucose, amino acids, hormones, gases, inorganic salts

**Formed Elements (45%):**

**RBCs / Erythrocytes:**
- Biconcave discs, anucleate (in mammals)
- Contain haemoglobin — carry O₂ and CO₂
- ~4.5–5.5 million/mm³
- Lifespan: 120 days; made in red bone marrow; broken down in the spleen (the "graveyard of RBCs")

**WBCs / Leucocytes (4,000–11,000/mm³), nucleated, amoeboid:**
- **Granulocytes:**
  - Neutrophils (60–70%): phagocytise bacteria; first responders
  - Basophils (0.5–1%): release histamine and heparin; allergy and inflammation
  - Eosinophils (2–4%): anti-parasitic; moderate allergy
- **Agranulocytes:**
  - Lymphocytes (20–25%): B cells (antibodies), T cells (cell-mediated immunity)
  - Monocytes (3–8%): become macrophages; phagocytosis

**Platelets / Thrombocytes:**
- Cell fragments (no nucleus), ~2.5 lakh/mm³
- Essential for haemostasis (blood clotting)

**Lymph:** A clear fluid derived from blood plasma that filters through capillary walls into interstitial space. Collected by lymphatic capillaries → lymph nodes (filtered by immune cells) → thoracic duct → returned to blood.
