**[SCRIPT]**
Proper connective tissue is made of three components:

**Cells** — five key types:
- **Fibroblasts** — the most abundant; synthesise collagen, elastic, and reticular fibres and ground substance.
- **Mast cells** — release histamine (inflammation and vasodilation) and heparin (anti-coagulation).
- **Histocytes** — fixed macrophages; engulf bacteria and cell debris.
- **Lymphocytes** — immune surveillance cells.
- **Plasma cells** — derived from B lymphocytes; secrete antibodies (immunoglobulins).

**Matrix** — a jelly-like to gel-like ground substance composed of proteoglycans and glycoproteins.

**Fibres** — three types:
- **Collagen (white)** — high tensile strength, inextensible; most abundant; in tendons, cartilage.
- **Elastic (yellow)** — extensible, recoils; in ligaments, aortic wall, lung pleura.
- **Reticular (colourless)** — thin, branching framework fibres; in lymphoid organs, liver, spleen.

**Types:**
- **Loose CT** — Areolar tissue (body's packing material, all cell and fibre types in loose arrangement) and Adipose (white fat — one large droplet, peripheral nucleus; brown fat — multiple droplets, high mitochondria, thermogenesis).
- **Dense CT** — White fibrous CT (tendons, ligaments), Elastic CT (ligamentum nuchae), Reticular CT (lymph node framework).
