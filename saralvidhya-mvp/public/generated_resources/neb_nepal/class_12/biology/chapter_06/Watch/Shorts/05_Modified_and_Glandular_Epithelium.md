# Short 05: Modified & Glandular Epithelium

**Duration:** ~90 seconds | **Words:** ~225 | **Mode:** Causal & Process-Oriented

---

**[HOOK]**
Some epithelial cells don't just cover surfaces — they have evolved to beat, absorb, sense, secrete, and even produce eggs and sperm. These are the modified epithelia.

> [VISUAL: High-speed microscopy animation of cilia beating rhythmically on the surface of tracheal epithelium, moving mucus like a river.]

---

**[SCRIPT]**
When the free border of epithelial cells is structurally modified for a specific function, we call it **modified epithelium**. There are six types:

**1. Ciliated** — two variants:
- **Kino-ciliated**: true motile cilia with a **9+2 microtubule arrangement** (9 peripheral duplets + 2 central singlets). Moves mucus in respiratory organs, moves ova in fallopian tubes, circulates CSF in the neurocoel.
- **Stereo-ciliated**: long, non-motile modified microvilli; found in the epididymis for sperm maturation.

**2. Brush-bordered** — microvilli on the free border maximise absorption surface; in intestine and PCT of nephron.

**3. Neuro-sensory** — cells modified into receptor cells; in taste buds, olfactory epithelium, and inner ear.

**4. Pigmented** — contains melanin granules to absorb excess light; in the retinal pigment epithelium of the eye.

**5. Germinal** — produces gametes; in seminiferous tubules of testis (spermatogenesis) and germinal disc of ovary (oogenesis).

**6. Glandular** — specialised for secretion. Classified by:
- **Ducts**: exocrine (duct → surface), endocrine (no duct → bloodstream), heterocrine (both).
- **Consistency**: serous (watery), mucous (viscous), mixed.
- **Secretion mode**: apocrine (apex pinched off), merocrine (exocytosis), holocrine (whole cell ruptures).
- **Cell count**: unicellular (goblet cell) or multicellular.
- **Structure**: simple (tubular, coiled, alveolar) or compound (tubular, alveolar, tubulo-alveolar).
