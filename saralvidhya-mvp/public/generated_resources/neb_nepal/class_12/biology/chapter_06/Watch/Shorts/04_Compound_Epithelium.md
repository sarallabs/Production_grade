# Short 04: Compound Epithelium — Stratified & Transitional

**Duration:** ~90 seconds | **Words:** ~220 | **Mode:** Descriptive & Spatial

---

**[HOOK]**
What covers your skin, lines your mouth, and can stretch to hold a full urinary bladder? Three types of compound epithelium — all built for protection and stress.

> [VISUAL: Split-screen microscopy — striated skin epidermis on one side, and a bladder transitioning from empty to full on the other.]

---

**[SCRIPT]**
Compound epithelium has **many layers** of cells. Only the innermost layer touches the basement membrane. The outermost (free border) determines the type.

**I. Stratified Squamous** — the topmost 5–6 layers are squamous; inner layers are cuboidal or columnar.
- **Non-keratinised**: free surface is smooth — no keratin secreted. Found in buccal cavity, gullet, oesophagus, anus, vagina — moist surfaces that need flexibility.
- **Keratinised**: free surface is rough — keratin secreted. Found in skin and all keratin derivatives:
  - Fish: scales
  - Reptiles: dry scales, spines, scutes, claws
  - Birds: feathers, beak, claws
  - Mammals: hair, horn, hoof, nail, claw, spines

Keratin is a **scleroprotein** — hard, insoluble, waterproof — secreted only by vertebrates.

**II. Stratified Cuboidal** — rare; in ducts of salivary, sweat, and mammary glands.

**III. Stratified Columnar** — uncommon; in conjunctiva, pharynx, anus, male urethra.

**IV. Transitional (Urothelium)** — cells shift shape based on tension:
- **Relaxed**: rounded/cuboidal cells in 5–6 layers, basement membrane visible.
- **Stretched**: cells flatten to squamous, basement membrane invisible.
- Location: renal pelvis, ureter, urinary bladder, urethra.
