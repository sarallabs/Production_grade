# Short 02: Epithelial Tissue — Overview & Characteristics

**Duration:** ~90 seconds | **Words:** ~220 | **Mode:** Descriptive & Spatial

---

**[HOOK]**
Every surface you touch on your body — and every surface inside it — is covered by the same tissue type. What is it, and what makes it so effective?

> [VISUAL: Animated cross-section of human skin surface, zooming into a single layer of flat cells tightly packed like floor tiles.]

---

**[SCRIPT]**
Epithelial tissue is the body's cover, lining, and secretory interface. It covers **external surfaces** like skin, **internal surfaces** like the gut, lungs, and kidney tubules, and **glandular surfaces** like the thyroid and sweat glands.

**Four defining characteristics:**
1. **Avascular** — no blood vessels; nutrients arrive by diffusion from connective tissue beneath.
2. **Minimal matrix** — cells are tightly packed with almost no intercellular space.
3. **Rests on a basement membrane** — a non-cellular protein layer anchoring it to underlying tissue.
4. **Held by four cell junctions:**
   - **Tight junctions** — seal intercellular gaps.
   - **Adherens junctions** — link actin cytoskeletons.
   - **Desmosomes** — anchor cells against mechanical pulling.
   - **Gap junctions** — allow direct ion and small-molecule exchange.

The number of surfaces covered depends on body plan: **2 surfaces** in acoelomates, **3** in pseudocoelomates, **4** in coelomates.
