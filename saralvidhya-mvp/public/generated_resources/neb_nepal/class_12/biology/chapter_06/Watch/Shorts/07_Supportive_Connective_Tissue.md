# Short 07: Supportive Connective Tissue — Cartilage & Bone

**Duration:** ~90 seconds | **Words:** ~235 | **Mode:** Descriptive & Spatial

---

**[HOOK]**
Your skeleton is not one material — it is two: the flexible cartilage and the rigid bone. Both are connective tissues, but built for very different demands.

**[SCRIPT]**
Both cartilage and bone belong to **supportive connective tissue**.

**Cartilage** is avascular and aneural. Cells — **chondrocytes** — live in fluid-filled spaces called lacunae within the **chondrin matrix**. The outer covering is the **perichondrium**.

Four types of cartilage:
| Type | Fibre | Properties | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyaline | Fine collagen | Smooth, glassy, most common | Trachea, articular surfaces, fetal bones |
| Calcified | Calcium deposits | Rock-hard | Epiphyseal ends |
| Fibro-cartilage | Dense collagen | Strongest | Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis |
| Elastic | Elastic fibres | Flexible, resilient | Pinna, epiglottis |

**Bone** is vascular. Cells — **osteocytes** — sit in lacunae within a matrix of **ossein** (collagen) + hydroxyapatite (calcium phosphate — the mineral backbone).

Three types of bone formation:
- **Cartilaginous (Endochondral)** — replaces a cartilage template; for long bones.
- **Membranous (Intramembranous)** — forms directly from mesenchyme; for flat skull bones.
- **Sesamoid** — forms within tendons; e.g., patella.

**Long bone structure:**
- **Epiphysis** — spongy bone with red marrow (blood cell production); covered by hyaline cartilage.
- **Diaphysis** — compact bone with yellow marrow (fat); covered by periosteum; inner lining: endosteum.
- **Haversian System (Osteon)** — Haversian canal (blood vessels) + concentric lamellae + osteocytes in lacunae + canaliculi + Volkmann's transverse canals.
