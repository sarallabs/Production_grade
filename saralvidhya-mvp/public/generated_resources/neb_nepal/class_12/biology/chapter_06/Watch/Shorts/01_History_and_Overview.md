**[SCRIPT]**
Histology — the study of tissues — was born across three centuries.

**Marcello Malpighi** (1628–1694) became the **founder of histology** when he first examined tissue microstructure through early microscopes, revealing capillary beds the naked eye couldn't see.

**Xavier Bichat** (1771–1802) changed everything by dissecting organs *without* a microscope — identifying **21 elementary tissue types** purely by touch, texture, and chemistry. He coined the term *tissue*, and became the **Father of Histology** and **Founder of Animal Histology**.

In **1801**, **Karl Mayer** named the science: **histology** — from Greek *histos* (tissue) and *logos* (study).

Evolutionarily, animal tissues first appeared in **Phylum Coelenterata** — jellyfish, Hydra, sea anemones — the first phylum to reach a **tissue-grade of organisation**. Before this, animals were at cellular or cell-aggregate grades with no true coordinated tissue layers.

This was the biological turning point: organised tissues made complex organs — and complex life — possible.
