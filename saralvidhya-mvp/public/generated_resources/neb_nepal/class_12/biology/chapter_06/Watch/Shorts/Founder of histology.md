# Founder of Histology — Short

Who actually founded the study of tissues, and how did complex life first evolve them? Let's dive in!

**[CORE]**
In biology, **histology** is the study of the microscopic structure of tissues—the vital bridge between cells and organs.
> [VISUAL: 3D animated diagram illustrating the hierarchy of biological organization: Cell -> Tissue -> Organ -> Organ System -> Organism.]

Its history begins with Marcello Malpighi in the seventeenth century. By using early microscopes to study biological microstructures, Malpighi became the celebrated **founder of histology**.
> [VISUAL: On-screen text slide: "Marcello Malpighi (1628-1694) - Founder of Histology". Cut to close-up of cell structures under a vintage lens filter.]

But in the late eighteenth century, French anatomist Xavier Bichat took a different path. Working *without* a microscope, Bichat dissected organs to prove that tissues are the actual building blocks of anatomy.
> [VISUAL: On-screen text slide: "Marie Francois Xavier Bichat (1771-1802)". Transition to a historical sketch of hands dissecting organs.]

He identified **twenty-one elementary tissues**, coined the word **'tissue'**, and is recognized as the **father of histology** and **founder of animal histology**.
> [VISUAL: A stylized vertical list of "21 Elementary Tissues" scrolls down rapidly. The word "TISSUE" is highlighted in a vibrant color.]

Shortly after, in **1801**, Karl Mayer coined the term *histology* to name this growing branch of science.
> [VISUAL: On-screen text slide: "Karl Mayer (1801) - Coined the term 'Histology'". The word "HISTOLOGY" lights up letter by letter.]

But biology is also about evolution! True **tissue-grade organization** first appeared in the animal kingdom in **Phylum Coelenterata**, marking the evolutionary leap from single cells to coordinated tissues.
> [VISUAL: Close-up video of a glowing jellyfish representing Phylum Coelenterata, showing its simple translucent tissue layer contracting.]

**[PAYOFF]**
From Malpighi's early lens to the simple tissues of Coelenterata, understanding histology is the key to unlocking how all complex organisms are built!
