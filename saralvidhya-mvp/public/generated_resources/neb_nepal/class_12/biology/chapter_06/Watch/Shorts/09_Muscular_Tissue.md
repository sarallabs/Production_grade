**[SCRIPT]**
Muscular tissue is **mesodermal** in origin and specialised for contraction and relaxation — converting chemical energy into mechanical movement. The mechanism: actin (thin filaments) and myosin (thick filaments) slide over each other — the **sliding filament theory** of **Huxley and Hanson**.

**1. Striped (Skeletal / Striated) Muscle:**
- Multi-nucleated, unbranched, cylindrical.
- Alternating bands form the sarcomere: **A-band** (myosin + actin), **I-band** (actin only), **H-band** (myosin only), **M-line** (centre), **Z-lines** (sarcomere boundaries).
- During contraction: I-band shrinks, H-band disappears, A-band unchanged.
- Organised: myofibrils → myofibres (endomysium) → fascicles (perimysium) → muscle block (epimysium).
- **Red muscle** (myoglobin, fatigue-resistant) vs. **white muscle** (no myoglobin, fast-twitch).
- Voluntary; fatigues quickly; wraps bones.

**2. Unstriped (Smooth) Muscle:**
- Uninucleated, spindle-shaped myocytes.
- No sarcomere bands — actin and myosin are dispersed.
- **Single-unit**: contracts as one wave (urinary bladder, alimentary canal, uterus).
- **Multi-unit**: contracts independently (blood vessels, arrector pilli, iris).
- Involuntary; fatigues in ~10–12 hours; performs peristalsis.

**3. Cardiac Muscle:**
- Branched, connected by **intercalated discs** (desmosomes + gap junctions → functional syncytium).
- Single/double central nucleus; faint striations.
- Involuntary; **never fatigues**; only in the heart.
- Least abundant muscle type.
- Facts: total muscles = 639; largest = gluteus maximus; smallest = stapedius; longest = sartorius; strongest = masseter.
