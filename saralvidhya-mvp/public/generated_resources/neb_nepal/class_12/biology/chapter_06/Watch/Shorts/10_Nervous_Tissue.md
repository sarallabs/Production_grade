# Short 10: Nervous Tissue — Neuroglia & Neurons

Eighty-six billion neurons — and yet you can't grow a single new one after birth. The brain's architecture is fixed, but its connections are limitless.

**[SCRIPT]**
Nervous tissue is **ectodermal** in origin (except microglia — mesodermal). It **controls, coordinates, integrates, and regulates** the organism through **nerve impulse conduction**.

**Two cell types:**

**1. Neuroglia** — supportive, protective cells that CAN divide (mitosis).
- **CNS neuroglia (4 types):**
  - **Astrocyte** (star-shaped): regulates neuro-environment, forms the blood-brain barrier (BBB), heals brain injury.
  - **Oligodendrocyte**: forms and maintains the myelin sheath of CNS axons.
  - **Microglia** (mesodermal): CNS macrophages — phagocytise bacteria and dead neuron debris.
  - **Ependymal cell** (cuboidal/columnar): lines cerebral ventricles; secretes and circulates CSF.
- **PNS neuroglia (2 types):**
  - **Stellate (Satellite) cell**: surrounds soma in ganglia; regulates neuronal nutrients.
  - **Schwann cell**: forms PNS myelin sheath; enables peripheral nerve regeneration after injury.

**2. Neurons** — structural and functional units; CANNOT divide after birth.
- **Soma (cyton)**: nucleus, Nissl's granules (rough ER), neurofibrils, mitochondria.
- **Dendrites** (multiple): branch outward; carry signals TO soma; Nissl granules present.
- **Axon** (single): carries signals AWAY from soma; often myelinated; uniform diameter.
- **Myelin sheath**: formed by oligodendrocytes (CNS) and Schwann cells (PNS). Gaps = **Nodes of Ranvier** — sites of **saltatory conduction** (fast, jumping impulse).
- **Classification by structure**: apolar → pseudopolar → unipolar → bipolar → multipolar (most common in humans).
- **Classification by function**: sensory/afferent (receptors → CNS), motor/efferent (CNS → effectors), intermediate/adjustor (within CNS, most numerous).
- **Grey matter**: soma-rich, appears grey. **White matter**: myelin-rich axons, appears white.
