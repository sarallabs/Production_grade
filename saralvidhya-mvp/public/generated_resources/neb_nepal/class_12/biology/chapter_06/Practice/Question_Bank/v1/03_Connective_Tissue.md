## Q1. Define connective tissue.
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue`

**Answer:** Connective tissue is a type of animal tissue that binds, supports, protects, and connects other tissues and organs. It is characterised by scattered cells embedded in an extensive extracellular matrix consisting of fibres and ground substance.

---

## Q2. Name the five types of cells found in proper connective tissue.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Cells`

**Answer:** The five types of cells in proper connective tissue are: (1) Fibroblasts — secrete fibres and ground substance; (2) Mast cells — contain histamine; (3) Histocytes (macrophages) — phagocytic cells; (4) Lymphocytes — immune cells; (5) Plasma cells — secrete antibodies.

---

## Q3. Name the three types of fibres found in connective tissue.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Fibres`

**Answer:** The three types of fibres in connective tissue are: (1) Collagen (White) fibres — tough, inextensible, provide tensile strength; (2) Elastic (Yellow) fibres — elastic, allow stretching and recoil; (3) Reticular (Colourless) fibres — thin, form supportive networks (stroma).

---

## Q4. Distinguish between collagen, elastic, and reticular fibres.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Fibres`

**Answer:** Collagen (white) fibres provide tensile strength and are inextensible. Elastic (yellow) fibres are extensible and return to original length after stretching. Reticular (colourless) fibres form delicate supporting networks (stroma) in organs like the liver and spleen.

---

## Q5. Distinguish between areolar and adipose tissue.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Loose`

**Answer:** Areolar tissue is loose connective tissue also called packing tissue that fills spaces between organs and provides support and elasticity. Adipose tissue is specialised loose connective tissue containing fat-storing adipocytes (white for energy storage, brown for thermogenesis).

---

## Q6. Distinguish between tendon and ligament.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Dense > Fibrous`

**Answer:** Tendons connect muscle to bone; they are composed of dense collagen fibres arranged in parallel bundles, giving high tensile strength in one direction. Ligaments connect bone to bone; they contain more elastic fibres than tendons, allowing some stretch while maintaining joint stability.

---

## Q7. What is reticular tissue?
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Dense > Reticular`

**Answer:** Reticular tissue is a type of dense connective tissue composed of a network of reticular fibres and reticular cells. It forms the supportive stroma (framework) of organs such as the liver, spleen, lymph nodes, and bone marrow.

---

## Q8. Why is cartilage described as avascular?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive > Cartilage`

**Answer:** Cartilage is avascular because it lacks blood vessels. Nutrients and oxygen reach chondrocytes (cartilage cells) by diffusion through the gel-like matrix (chondrin). This slow nutrition contributes to the slow healing of cartilage after injury.

---

## Q9. Name the four types of cartilage.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive > Cartilage`

**Answer:** The four types of cartilage are: (1) Hyaline cartilage — most common, glassy appearance; (2) Calcified cartilage — hardened with calcium salts; (3) Fibrocartilage (white fibrous cartilage) — strongest type, contains collagen fibres; (4) Elastic cartilage (yellow fibrous cartilage) — flexible, contains elastic fibres.

---

## Q10. Distinguish between cartilage and bone as supportive connective tissues.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive`

**Answer:** Cartilage is avascular, flexible, and its matrix is chondrin (gel-like). Cells (chondrocytes) sit in lacunae. Bone is vascular, rigid, and its matrix is ossein (calcium phosphate). Osteocytes in lacunae are connected via canaliculi. Cartilage heals slowly; bone heals faster due to blood supply.

---

## Q11. Name the three types of bone formation.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive > Bone > Formation`

**Answer:** The three types of bone formation are: (1) Cartilaginous (Endochondral) bone — replaces a cartilage template (e.g., long bones); (2) Membranous (Dermal) bone — forms directly in connective tissue membrane (e.g., flat bones of skull); (3) Sesamoid bone — develops within tendons (e.g., patella).

---

## Q12. Distinguish between epiphysis and diaphysis of a long bone.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive > Bone > Shape and Size > Long > Epiphysis / Diaphysis`

**Answer:** Epiphysis is the expanded end of a long bone composed of spongy bone containing red bone marrow (haematopoiesis). Diaphysis is the shaft of the long bone composed of compact bone surrounding a medullary cavity with yellow bone marrow (fat storage), and contains the Haversian system.

---

## Q13. What is the Haversian system (osteon)?
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive > Bone > Shape and Size > Long > Diaphysis / Compact`

**Answer:** The Haversian system (osteon) is the structural unit of compact bone. It consists of a central Haversian canal (containing blood vessels and nerves) surrounded by concentric lamellae of bone matrix, with osteocytes in lacunae connected to each other and the canal by canaliculi.

---

## Q14. Name the components of blood as a fluid connective tissue.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Fluid > Blood`

**Answer:** Blood consists of: (1) Plasma (55%) — liquid matrix containing proteins, nutrients, and gases; (2) Blood corpuscles (45%): RBCs (erythrocytes), WBCs (leucocytes — granulocytes: neutrophils, basophils, eosinophils; agranulocytes: lymphocytes, monocytes), and Platelets (thrombocytes).

---

## Q15. Distinguish between granulocytes and agranulocytes.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Fluid > Blood > Blood cells > WBCs`

**Answer:** Granulocytes are WBCs with cytoplasmic granules, including neutrophils (phagocytic), eosinophils (anti-allergic/anti-parasitic), and basophils (release histamine/heparin). Agranulocytes lack prominent granules and include lymphocytes (B and T — immune response) and monocytes (largest WBC, phagocytic).

---

## Q16. What percentage of blood volume is plasma and what percentage is blood corpuscles?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Fluid > Blood`

**Answer:** Plasma constitutes approximately 55% of blood volume and blood corpuscles constitute approximately 45%.

## Q17. Distinguish between white adipose tissue and brown adipose tissue.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Loose > Adipose`

**Answer:** White adipose tissue (WAT) contains large adipocytes with a single large fat droplet; it is the primary site of energy storage and insulation. Brown adipose tissue (BAT) contains adipocytes with multiple small fat droplets and abundant mitochondria; it generates heat through non-shivering thermogenesis (important in newborns and hibernating animals).

---

## Q18. What is lymph?
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Fluid > Lymph`

**Answer:** Lymph is a clear, colourless fluid connective tissue derived from blood plasma that has filtered out of capillaries into tissue spaces. It is collected by lymphatic vessels and returned to the bloodstream. Lymph contains lymphocytes and lacks RBCs.

---

## Q19. True or False: Fibrocartilage is the weakest type of cartilage. Justify.
**Type:** TrueFalse  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive > Cartilage > Fibro-cartilage`

**Answer:** False. Fibrocartilage is the strongest type of cartilage. It contains large numbers of collagen (white fibrous) fibres in its matrix, giving it great tensile strength. It is found in intervertebral discs and the pubic symphysis.

## Q20. Why is blood classified as a connective tissue?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Fluid > Blood`

**Answer:** Blood is classified as a fluid connective tissue because it shares the defining characteristics: it has cells (blood corpuscles) scattered in an extracellular matrix (plasma), connects distant parts of the body, and is mesodermal in origin — just like other connective tissues. It transports substances between different tissues, 'connecting' them functionally.

**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Supportive > Bone > Shape and Size`

**Answer:** Based on shape and size, bones are classified as: (1) Flat bones — e.g., skull, scapula; (2) Irregular bones — e.g., vertebrae; (3) Short bones — e.g., carpals; (4) Long bones — e.g., femur, humerus.

## Q22. What is the role of fibroblasts in connective tissue?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Connective Tissue > Proper > Cells > Fibroblasts`

**Answer:** Fibroblasts are the most common cells of proper connective tissue. They are responsible for synthesising and secreting the extracellular matrix components — collagen fibres, elastic fibres, and the ground substance (glycosaminoglycans). They are crucial for wound healing and tissue repair.
