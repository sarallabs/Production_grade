## Q1. Classify glands based on their mode of secretion (Merocrine, Apocrine, and Holocrine) with examples.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > Modified & Glandular Epithelium`
**Recommended Diagram:** Modes of Glandular Secretion (Merocrine, Apocrine, Holocrine) Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Merocrine glands release products via exocytosis without cell loss (salivary glands). Apocrine glands pinch off their apical cytoplasm during secretion (mammary glands). Holocrine glands rupture completely, dying to release secretions (sebaceous glands).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Merocrine (Eccrine) Glands**: Secretory vesicles discharge products across the apical plasma membrane via standard exocytosis with zero cellular damage or cytoplasm loss (e.g. salivary glands, pancreas, eccrine sweat glands).
2. **Apocrine Glands**: Secretory granules accumulate in the apical cell region, which pinches off along with a portion of apical cytoplasm during discharge (e.g. mammary glands, axillary apocrine sweat glands).
3. **Holocrine Glands**: Secretory products fill the entire cytoplasm; the whole cell undergoes apoptosis, disintegrates, and dies to discharge its oily contents (e.g. cutaneous sebaceous glands).
4. *Summary*: Merocrine = zero cell loss; Apocrine = partial apical cell loss; Holocrine = total cell disintegration.
