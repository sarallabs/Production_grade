## Q1. Describe the cellular components of Proper Connective Tissue and state their functions.

**Type:** Describe  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Connective Tissue > Proper Connective Tissue`
**Recommended Diagram:** Connective Tissue Proper Cells Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Proper connective tissue contains Fibroblasts (matrix/fiber synthesis), Mast cells (histamine, heparin release), Macrophages (phagocytosis), and Plasma cells (antibody synthesis with cartwheel nucleus).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Fibroblasts**: Stellate, abundant cells that actively synthesize extracellular ground matrix and protein fiber precursors (collagen, elastin).
2. **Mast Cells**: Granular cells mediating inflammatory allergic cascades; secrete **Histamine** (vasodilator), **Heparin** (anticoagulant), and **Serotonin** (vasoconstrictor).
3. **Histiocytes / Macrophages**: Phagocytic cells derived from monocytes that engulf invading microorganisms and dead cellular debris.
4. **Plasma Cells**: Differentiated B-lymphocytes featuring a characteristic **'cartwheel' or 'clock-face'** nuclear chromatin pattern; synthesize circulating antibodies.
5. **Adipocytes**: Ring-shaped cells specialized for lipid storage and thermal insulation.

## Q2. Differentiate between Tendons and Ligaments in dense connective tissue.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Connective Tissue > Proper Connective Tissue`
**Recommended Diagram:** Tendon vs Ligament Microarchitecture Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Tendons are dense regular white fibrous tissues connecting skeletal muscle to bone with high tensile strength. Ligaments are dense elastic yellow fibrous tissues connecting bone to bone with high flexibility.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Structural Composition**: Tendons consist of parallel unbranched bundles of collagen (white) fibers. Ligaments consist of branching networks of elastic (yellow) fibers mixed with collagen.
2. **Attachments**: Tendons attach voluntary muscle to bone. Ligaments attach bone to bone across synovial joints.
3. **Flexibility vs Tensile Strength**: Tendons possess immense tensile strength with limited elasticity. Ligaments exhibit high elasticity and flexibility.
4. **Cell Distribution**: Fibroblasts in tendons lie in continuous parallel rows between collagen bundles. Fibroblasts in ligaments are scattered irregularly throughout elastic fibers.
5. **Functional Significance**: Tendons transmit muscle force to pull bones. Ligaments stabilize joint movement and prevent hyperextension.

## Q3. Compare Cartilage and Bone in terms of matrix composition, vascularity, and cell arrangement.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Connective Tissue > Supportive Connective Tissue`
**Recommended Diagram:** Cartilage vs Bone Microscopic Structure Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Cartilage has a semi-rigid avascular chondrin matrix with chondrocytes in lacunae. Bone has a rigid vascular mineralized ossein matrix with hydroxyapatite calcium salts and osteocytes in lacunae linked by canaliculi.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Matrix Mineralization**: Cartilage matrix is composed of flexible, semi-rigid **chondrin** (chondroitin sulfate). Bone matrix is composed of rigid **ossein** impregnated with inorganic hydroxyapatite calcium phosphate crystals.
2. **Vascularity**: Cartilage is entirely **avascular** (nourished by perichondrium diffusion). Bone is heavily **vascularized** via Haversian and Volkmann canals.
3. **Cellular Architecture**: Cartilage cells (**chondrocytes**) occur in lacunae in groups of 1–4 without canaliculi. Bone cells (**osteocytes**) lie singly in lacunae interconnected by fine cytoplasmic **canaliculi**.
4. **Growth Pattern**: Cartilage exhibits both appositional and interstitial growth. Bone grows exclusively by appositional deposition.
5. **Structural Roles**: Cartilage provides flexible shock absorption at joint ends. Bone forms the primary load-bearing endoskeleton.

## Q4. Describe the microscopic architecture of an Osteon (Haversian System) in compact bone.

**Type:** Mechanism  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Connective Tissue > Supportive Connective Tissue`
**Recommended Diagram:** Haversian System (Osteon) Compact Bone Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
An Osteon consists of a central Haversian canal containing blood vessels and nerves, surrounded by concentric ossein lamellae, with osteocytes housed inside lacunae interconnected by canaliculi.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Haversian Canal**: The central longitudinal canal containing nutrient artery, vein, lymphatic vessel, and nerve fibers.
2. **Concentric Lamellae**: Concentric rings of hard ossein matrix impregnated with calcium phosphate crystals arranged around the Haversian canal.
3. **Lacunae**: Fluid-filled cavities situated between adjacent lamellae, housing mature **osteocytes**.
4. **Canaliculi**: Radiating microscopic canals housing osteocyte cytoplasmic processes, allowing nutrient and gas transport between osteocytes and the central canal.
5. **Volkmann's Canals**: Transverse or oblique canals connecting adjacent Haversian canals to each other and to the periosteum.
6. *Functional Result*: The Haversian system ensures efficient nutrient delivery to osteocytes in highly mineralized bone.

## Q5. Describe the composition of human blood plasma and formed elements.

**Type:** Describe  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Connective Tissue > Fluid Connective Tissue`
**Recommended Diagram:** Blood Composition and Formed Elements Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Blood consists of 55% liquid Plasma (90-92% water, plasma proteins albumin, globulin, fibrinogen) and 45% Formed Elements (RBCs, Granulocyte/Agranulocyte WBCs, and Thrombocytes).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Blood Composition**: Whole blood is a liquid connective tissue consisting of 55% extracellular **Plasma** and 45% **Formed Elements** (corpuscles).
2. **Plasma Constituents**: Contains 90–92% water and 6–8% vital plasma proteins: **Albumin** (osmotic pressure), **Globulins** (immunoglobulins/defense), and **Fibrinogen** (blood clotting).
3. **Erythrocytes (RBCs)**: Biconcave, enucleated corpuscles (5–5.5 million/mm³) packed with hemoglobin (12-16 g/dL) for gas transport; 120-day lifespan.
4. **Leucocytes (WBCs)**: Nucleated immune defense cells (6,000–8,000/mm³). Subdivided into **Granulocytes** (Neutrophils, Eosinophils, Basophils) and **Agranulocytes** (Lymphocytes B/T, Monocytes).
5. **Thrombocytes (Platelets)**: Enucleated cell fragments (150,000–350,000/mm³) derived from bone marrow megakaryocytes; essential for blood coagulation cascades.
