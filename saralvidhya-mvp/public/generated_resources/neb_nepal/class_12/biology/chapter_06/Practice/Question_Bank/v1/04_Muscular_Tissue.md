## Q1. Define muscular tissue.
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue`

**Answer:** Muscular tissue is a specialised animal tissue composed of elongated, contractile cells (muscle fibres) that can generate force through contraction. It is responsible for all movement in the body — voluntary, involuntary, and cardiac.

---

## Q2. Name the three types of muscular tissue.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue`

**Answer:** The three types of muscular tissue are: (1) Skeletal (Striated/Voluntary) muscle; (2) Smooth (Non-striated/Involuntary) muscle; (3) Cardiac (Heart/Involuntary) muscle.

---

## Q3. Distinguish between skeletal muscle and smooth muscle.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue`

**Answer:** Skeletal muscle is striated (shows cross-striations), multinucleated, voluntary (under conscious control), and attaches to bones. Smooth muscle is non-striated, uninucleate, involuntary, and found in walls of internal organs (gut, blood vessels, bladder).

---

## Q4. Distinguish between red muscle and white muscle.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Striped > Red muscle / White muscle`

**Answer:** Red muscle fibres are rich in myoglobin (an oxygen-storing protein), contain many mitochondria, and are fatigue-resistant — suited for sustained aerobic activity. White muscle fibres have little myoglobin, fewer mitochondria, and are fast-contracting but fatigue quickly — suited for rapid, short bursts of activity.

---

## Q5. What are intercalated discs and in which type of muscle are they found?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Cardiac / Heart muscle`

**Answer:** Intercalated discs are specialised junctions (containing gap junctions and desmosomes) found at the ends of cardiac muscle cells. They electrically and mechanically couple adjacent cardiac muscle cells, allowing the heart to contract as a coordinated unit (functional syncytium).

---

## Q6. Why is cardiac muscle said to be 'involuntary and non-fatiguing'?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Cardiac / Heart muscle`

**Answer:** Cardiac muscle is involuntary because it contracts rhythmically without conscious control, governed by the sinoatrial (SA) node. It is non-fatiguing because cardiac muscle cells have a very high density of mitochondria and an abundant blood supply, enabling continuous aerobic energy production throughout life.

---

## Q7. Distinguish between single-unit and multi-unit smooth muscle.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Unstriped / Smooth`

**Answer:** Single-unit (visceral) smooth muscle has cells electrically coupled via gap junctions, causing the entire sheet to contract together as one unit (e.g., gut wall, uterus). Multi-unit smooth muscle has cells that contract independently in response to individual nerve stimulation (e.g., iris of the eye, piloerector muscles), allowing fine graded control.

---

## Q8. True or False: Skeletal muscle is involuntary. Justify.
**Type:** TrueFalse  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Striped / Striated / Skeletal`

**Answer:** False. Skeletal muscle is voluntary — it contracts under conscious control via the somatic nervous system. Smooth muscle and cardiac muscle are involuntary.

---

## Q9. True or False: Cardiac muscle is striated and involuntary. Justify.
**Type:** TrueFalse  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Cardiac / Heart muscle`

**Answer:** True. Cardiac muscle shows striations (cross-striations due to organised sarcomere arrangement) and is involuntary (controlled by the intrinsic cardiac conduction system, not conscious will).

## Q10. What is the role of myoglobin in red muscle?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Striped > Red muscle`

**Answer:** Myoglobin is an oxygen-storing protein found in red muscle fibres. It stores oxygen within the muscle cell and releases it during sustained activity, enabling continuous aerobic (oxidative) metabolism. Its high oxygen affinity ensures a steady oxygen supply to mitochondria during prolonged exercise.

---

## Q11. What is the other name for smooth muscle and where is it found?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Unstriped / Smooth`

**Answer:** Smooth muscle is also called unstriated, non-striated, or involuntary muscle. It is found in the walls of internal (visceral) organs such as the digestive tract, blood vessels, respiratory tract, urinary bladder, and uterus.

---

## Q12. Distinguish between cardiac muscle and skeletal muscle in three key ways.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue`

**Answer:** (1) Cardiac muscle cells are branched; skeletal muscle cells are unbranched. (2) Cardiac muscle cells are uninucleate (one nucleus per cell); skeletal muscle is multinucleated. (3) Cardiac muscle is involuntary with intercalated discs; skeletal muscle is voluntary with no intercalated discs.

---

## Q13. Explain the significance of striations in skeletal and cardiac muscle.
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Striped / Striated / Skeletal`

**Answer:** Striations in skeletal and cardiac muscle result from the regular, alternating arrangement of thick myosin (A-bands) and thin actin (I-bands) filaments within sarcomeres. This organised arrangement is essential for the sliding filament mechanism of contraction, enabling powerful and coordinated force generation.

**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Striped / Striated / Skeletal`

**Answer:** Skeletal muscle is also called: (1) Striped muscle; (2) Striated muscle; (3) Voluntary muscle; (4) Somatic muscle.

## Q15. Why is smooth muscle suited for sustained, slow contractions?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue > Unstriped / Smooth`

**Answer:** Smooth muscle is suited for sustained, slow contractions because: it uses much less ATP than skeletal muscle for the same force; it can maintain a contracted state (tonic contraction) for long periods without fatigue; and its actin-myosin interaction is slower but energy-efficient, making it ideal for maintaining organ tone (e.g., maintaining blood vessel diameter).

---

## Q16. Distinguish between voluntary and involuntary muscle with examples.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Muscular Tissue`

**Answer:** Voluntary muscle is under conscious control via the somatic nervous system. Example: skeletal muscle (biceps, quadriceps). Involuntary muscle contracts without conscious control, governed by the autonomic nervous system. Examples: smooth muscle (gut, blood vessels) and cardiac muscle (heart).
