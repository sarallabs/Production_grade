## Q1. Describe the structural anatomy of a Neuron, highlighting Nissl's granules and Nodes of Ranvier.

**Type:** Describe  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Nervous Tissue > Neurone`
**Recommended Diagram:** Neuron Microscopic Structure and Node of Ranvier Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
A neuron consists of a Soma (cell body containing nucleus and Nissl's granules for protein synthesis), branched centripetal Dendrites, and a long centrifugal Axon surrounded by a myelin sheath interrupted at Nodes of Ranvier for saltatory conduction.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Soma / Cyton**: Central cell body containing a spherical nucleus and prominent **Nissl's granules** (clumps of rough ER and ribosomes for active protein synthesis). Nissl's granules are present in cyton and dendrites but **absent in axon hillock and axon**.
2. **Dendrites**: Short, highly branched receptive processes that conduct nerve impulses **centripetally** toward the cyton.
3. **Axon**: Single, long cylindrical process originating from the axon hillock that conducts impulses **centrifugally** away from the cyton to terminal telodendria.
4. **Myelin Sheath & Nodes of Ranvier**: In myelinated nerves, Schwann cells (PNS) or oligodendrocytes (CNS) wrap axons in a lipid myelin sheath interrupted by periodic gaps called **Nodes of Ranvier**.
5. **Saltatory Conduction**: Action potentials jump rapidly from node to node across Nodes of Ranvier, increasing nerve impulse transmission speed up to 100-fold.
