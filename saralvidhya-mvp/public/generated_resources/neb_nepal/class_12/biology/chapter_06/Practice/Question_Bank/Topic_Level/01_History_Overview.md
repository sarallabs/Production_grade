## Q1. Who coined the term 'tissue' in animal anatomy and in which year?

**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Xavier Bichat`
**Recommended Diagram:** None

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Marie François Xavier Bichat (1771–1802) introduced the French term 'tissu' (meaning woven fabric) in 1792 to describe the distinct structural layers of the body during gross anatomical dissections.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Marie François Xavier Bichat (1771–1802) was a brilliant French anatomist and pathologist.
2. In 1792, he coined the French word 'tissu' (derived from the Latin 'texere', meaning to weave) to describe body membranes.
3. He conceptualized that organs are built from distinct, repeating elementary membrane layers.
4. Bichat performed extensive cadaveric dissections without using a microscope.
5. His work shifted anatomical science from an organ-centered perspective to a tissue-centered perspective.
6. His contribution established tissue as the fundamental structural unit of human anatomy and pathology.

## Q2. Why is Xavier Bichat universally recognized as the 'Father and Founder of Animal Histology'?

**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Xavier Bichat`
**Recommended Diagram:** Bichat's Organ-Tissue Hierarchy Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Bichat is recognized as the Father of Histology because he established tissue as the fundamental structural unit of human organs, identified 21 elementary tissue types, and postulated that disease primarily affects individual tissues rather than whole organs.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Xavier Bichat established the theoretical framework of histology long before microscopes were routinely used in biology.
2. He introduced the Organ-Tissue Theory, asserting that organs are complex assemblies of simpler elementary tissue layers.
3. He systematically identified and classified 21 distinct elementary tissues based on gross anatomical features, heat drying, and chemical treatments.
4. He revolutionized pathology by demonstrating that disease processes target specific tissue components within organs.
5. He separated anatomical study into macroscopic dissection and tissue layer classification.
6. For these groundbreaking conceptual and diagnostic contributions, he is revered as the Founder and Father of Animal Histology.

## Q3. How did Xavier Bichat classify elementary tissues without the aid of a microscope?

**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Xavier Bichat`
**Recommended Diagram:** None

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Bichat classified 21 elementary tissues solely using gross anatomical dissection, physical drying, maceration in fluids, and exposure to heat and chemical reagents, analyzing their macro-texture and physical properties.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Bichat worked prior to the widespread adoption of high-power compound light microscopes in medicine.
2. He relied on macroscopic inspection during systematic cadaveric dissections.
3. He subjected tissue samples to chemical reagents such as acids, alkalis, and alcohol to test chemical resistance.
4. He performed thermal treatments, including boiling and air-drying, to observe structural changes.
5. He used physical maceration in water to separate overlapping membrane layers.
6. Through these empirical physical-chemical tests, he successfully distinguished 21 distinct elementary tissue types.

## Q4. Define the term 'Histology' and state who coined it.

**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Karl Mayer`
**Recommended Diagram:** None

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Histology is the branch of microscopic anatomy concerned with the minute structure, matrix composition, and function of plant and animal tissues. The term was coined by German anatomist Karl Mayer in 1801.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Histology is defined as the scientific study of the microscopic structure, organization, and physiological function of biological tissues.
2. German anatomist August Franz Josef Karl Mayer formally coined the term 'Histology' in 1801.
3. It synthesizes microscopic anatomy, cell biology, and biochemistry into a unified discipline.
4. Histology examines how specialized cells interact with intercellular matrix components to form functional tissues.
5. It forms the essential bridge between cytology (study of individual cells) and organology (study of whole organs).
6. The field provides the prerequisite baseline for understanding histopathology and clinical disease diagnosis.

## Q5. Explain the Greek etymology of the word 'Histology'.

**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Karl Mayer`
**Recommended Diagram:** None

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
The word 'Histology' is derived from two classical Greek roots: 'histos' (ἱστός, meaning web, beam, or tissue) and 'logos' (λόγος, meaning study or scientific discourse).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Karl Mayer constructed the term 'Histology' in 1801 using classical Greek nomenclature.
2. The root word 'histos' (ἱστός) originally referred to a loom beam, web, or woven fabric.
3. In biological context, 'histos' denotes the intricate woven network of cells and extracellular fibers in a tissue.
4. The suffix '-logy' stems from 'logos' (λόγος), denoting systematic study, science, or discourse.
5. Thus, etymologically, Histology literally translates to 'the scientific study of biological webs or tissues'.
6. This precise nomenclature unified the growing sub-discipline of microscopic tissue anatomy.

## Q6. What key microscopic circulatory discovery was made by Marcello Malpighi in 1661?

**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Marcello Malpighi`
**Recommended Diagram:** Malpighi's Capillary Microcirculation Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
In 1661, Marcello Malpighi discovered blood capillaries in frog lungs, demonstrating the microscopic vascular link between arteries and veins and confirming William Harvey's theory of closed circulation.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Marcello Malpighi (1628–1694) was an Italian physician and pioneer microscopist.
2. In 1661, he examined the living, inflated lungs of frogs under early compound light microscopes.
3. He discovered minute, thin-walled vessels connecting terminal arterioles to venules, which he named capillaries.
4. This discovery provided the definitive physical proof of William Harvey's 1628 hypothesis of closed blood circulation.
5. He proved that blood flows from the arterial system into venous channels through microscopic tissue networks.
6. For this and other microscopic discoveries, Malpighi is celebrated as the Pioneer of Microscopic Anatomy.

## Q7. List three anatomical structures named in honor of Marcello Malpighi.

**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Marcello Malpighi`
**Recommended Diagram:** Renal Malpighian Corpuscle Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Three major eponymous structures are: 1) Malpighian corpuscles (renal glomerulus and Bowman's capsule in kidneys), 2) Malpighian layer (stratum basale of skin epidermis), and 3) Malpighian tubules (excretory organs in insects).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Renal Malpighian Corpuscles**: The spherical blood-filtering units in mammalian kidney cortex consisting of a capillary glomerulus surrounded by Bowman's capsule.
2. **Cutaneous Malpighian Layer**: The deep germinative layer (*stratum basale* and *stratum spinosum*) of the epidermis responsible for continuous mitotic skin cell renewal.
3. **Splenic Malpighian Corpuscles**: White pulp lymphoid nodules surrounding central arterioles inside the spleen.
4. **Arthropod Malpighian Tubules**: Branching excretory tubules extending from the alimentary canal in insects and arachnids.
5. These diverse eponymous terms highlight Malpighi's extensive comparative microscopic investigations across animal phyla.

## Q8. Explain why Phylum Coelenterata (Cnidaria) represents a landmark evolutionary milestone in tissue development.

**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > History & Overview > Phylum Coelenterata`
**Recommended Diagram:** Diploblastic Body Wall of Coelenterata Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Phylum Coelenterata is the earliest animal phylum to evolve a true 'tissue grade of body organization', transitioning from loose cellular aggregates (Porifera) to integrated epithelial tissue layers with cellular division of labor.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Unicellular protozoans and cellular-level sponges (Porifera) lack true integrated tissues.
2. Phylum Coelenterata (Cnidaria, e.g. *Hydra*, jellyfish) represents the phylogenetic origin of true animal tissues.
3. Coelenterates display a **diploblastic** body wall derived from two embryonic germ layers: outer ectoderm and inner endoderm.
4. The ectoderm forms a functional protective **epidermis**, while the endoderm forms a digestive **gastrodermis**.
5. These cell layers are separated by a gelatinous acellular matrix called the **mesoglea**.
6. Coelenterates also exhibit the earliest specialized neural tissue, organized as a diffuse nerve net.
7. This tissue-grade organization established the blueprint for organ system development in higher Metazoa.
