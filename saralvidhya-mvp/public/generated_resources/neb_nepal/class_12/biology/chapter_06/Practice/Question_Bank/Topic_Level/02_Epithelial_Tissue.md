## Q1. Describe the general structural characteristics of epithelial tissue.

**Type:** Describe  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > General Features & Surfaces`
**Recommended Diagram:** General Epithelial Tissue Architecture Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Epithelial tissue consists of closely packed cells with minimal intercellular matrix, rests on a non-cellular basement membrane, lacks direct blood vessels (avascular), and covers internal and external body surfaces.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **High Cellularity**: Composed almost entirely of tightly packed cells with negligible intercellular space (*substantia intercellularis*).
2. **Basement Membrane Attachment**: Basal cell surfaces rest on a specialized non-cellular **basement membrane** (basal lamina + reticular lamina).
3. **Avascularity**: Absolutely devoid of capillary blood vessels; nutrients and oxygen diffuse from underlying vascular connective tissue.
4. **Cell Junctions**: Cells are firmly connected via tight junctions, adherens belts, desmosomes, and gap junctions.
5. **High Regeneration Capacity**: Displays rapid mitotic renewal to repair surface damage caused by friction or chemical wear.
6. **Germ Layer Origins**: Uniquely derived from all three embryonic germ layers: ectoderm (skin epidermis), endoderm (GI tract lining), and mesoderm (vascular endothelium).

## Q2. Compare the structure and functional roles of tight junctions, desmosomes, and gap junctions.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > General Features & Surfaces`
**Recommended Diagram:** Cell Junctions in Epithelial Tissue Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Tight junctions form impermeable fluid seals between cells; desmosomes provide strong mechanical anchoring under shear stress; gap junctions contain connexon channels that allow direct intercellular ion and signal passage.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Tight Junctions (Zonula Occludens)**: Transmembrane occludin and claudin proteins fuse adjacent cell membranes, preventing paracellular diffusion of fluids and solutes across epithelial barriers.
2. **Desmosomes (Macula Adherens)**: Button-like protein plaques containing cadherins linked to keratin intermediate filaments; provide high mechanical tensile strength under shearing forces.
3. **Gap Junctions (Nexus)**: Hexameric **connexon** channel complexes forming hydrophilic tunnels between cells; permit rapid movement of small ions (Ca2+, Na+) and signaling molecules (cAMP).
4. *Functional Summary*: Tight junctions create barriers, desmosomes provide structural cohesion, and gap junctions enable direct metabolic and electrical communication.

## Q3. Differentiate between Endothelium and Mesothelium in terms of germ origin and anatomical locations.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > Simple Epithelium`
**Recommended Diagram:** Simple Squamous Endothelium vs Mesothelium Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Endothelium (endodermal origin) lines the internal lumen of blood vessels, lymphatics, and heart chambers. Mesothelium (mesodermal origin) lines coelomic body cavities such as pleura, pericardium, and peritoneum.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Both Endothelium and Mesothelium are structural specializations of simple squamous epithelium.
2. **Endothelium**: Derived from embryonic endoderm/mesoderm; forms a smooth, ultra-thin non-thrombogenic lining inside blood vessels, capillary beds, heart chambers, and lymphatic channels.
3. **Mesothelium**: Derived exclusively from embryonic mesoderm; lines closed coelomic body cavities (pleural cavity around lungs, pericardial cavity around heart, peritoneal cavity around viscera).
4. Endothelium facilitates rapid gas and nutrient exchange while preventing intravascular blood clotting.
5. Mesothelium secretes lubricating serous fluid to reduce friction between visceral organs during respiratory and cardiac movements.

## Q4. Describe the structural features and functions of Simple Cuboidal Epithelium, highlighting brush-bordered PCT cells.

**Type:** Describe  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > Simple Epithelium`
**Recommended Diagram:** Simple Cuboidal Brush-Bordered PCT Epithelium Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Simple cuboidal epithelium consists of single-layered cube-shaped cells with central round nuclei. In the Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT) of nephrons, apical microvilli form a brush border to maximize solute reabsorption.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Simple cuboidal epithelium features single-layered cells appearing square in section with central, spherical nuclei.
2. Found in excretory gland ducts (thyroid follicles, salivary gland ducts, pancreatic ducts) and kidney tubules.
3. Serves dual physiological functions of active cellular secretion and selective solute absorption.
4. **PCT Brush-Border Modification**: In renal Proximal Convoluted Tubules, the apical cell membrane forms thousands of microvilli.
5. Microvilli expand the functional absorptive surface area over 20-fold, enabling massive reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, and Na+ from renal filtrate.

## Q5. Distinguish between Keratinized and Non-Keratinized Stratified Squamous Epithelium with examples.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > Compound Epithelium`
**Recommended Diagram:** Keratinized vs Non-Keratinized Stratified Epithelium Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Keratinized stratified squamous epithelium has dead surface cells packed with insoluble keratin protein, forming a dry waterproof barrier (skin epidermis). Non-keratinized epithelium retains nucleated surface cells on moist surfaces (esophagus, oral cavity).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Stratified Architecture**: Both contain multiple cell layers (basal cuboidal germinative layer stacking into flattened apical squamous cells).
2. **Keratinization Process**: In keratinized epithelium, surface cells undergo cornification, lose nuclei, and become filled with tough, insoluble **keratin** protein.
3. **Dehydration Protection**: Keratinized epithelium forms an impermeable, waterproof barrier preventing desiccation (found on human skin epidermis).
4. **Non-Keratinized Epithelium**: Surface cells remain living and nucleated, requiring a moist fluid environment to prevent cell drying.
5. **Locations**: Non-keratinized lines mucous membranes subject to mechanical friction (oral cavity, pharynx, esophagus, vagina, cornea).

## Q6. Explain the unique structural adaptations of Transitional Epithelium (Urothelium) during bladder distension.

**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > Compound Epithelium`
**Recommended Diagram:** Transitional Epithelium (Relaxed vs Stretched) Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Transitional epithelium (urothelium) consists of 4–6 layers of elastic cells with surface umbrella cells containing rigid protein plaques. When the urinary bladder fills, umbrella cells flatten and slide laterally without rupturing tissue integrity.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Transitional epithelium (Urothelium) is a specialized plastic compound epithelium restricted to the urinary system (renal pelvis, ureter, urinary bladder).
2. In an empty organ, it appears 4–6 cell layers thick with large, dome-shaped apical **umbrella cells**.
3. Apical membranes of umbrella cells feature rigid protein plaques (*uroplakins*) separated by flexible membrane hinges.
4. As urine accumulates, umbrella cells unfold their membrane plaques and stretch flat from cuboidal into squamous morphology.
5. Intercellular junctions remain intact while cell layers slide laterally, expanding organ volume without tearing the lining.
6. Uroplakin plaques also act as a chemical barrier preventing hypertonic urine from pulling water out of blood capillaries.

## Q7. Classify glands based on their mode of secretion (Merocrine, Apocrine, and Holocrine) with examples.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Epithelial Tissue > Modified & Glandular Epithelium`
**Recommended Diagram:** Modes of Glandular Secretion (Merocrine, Apocrine, Holocrine) Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Merocrine glands release products via exocytosis without cell loss (salivary glands). Apocrine glands pinch off their apical cytoplasm during secretion (mammary glands). Holocrine glands rupture completely, dying to release secretions (sebaceous glands).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Merocrine (Eccrine) Glands**: Secretory vesicles discharge products across the apical plasma membrane via standard exocytosis with zero cellular damage or cytoplasm loss (e.g. salivary glands, pancreas, eccrine sweat glands).
2. **Apocrine Glands**: Secretory granules accumulate in the apical cell region, which pinches off along with a portion of apical cytoplasm during discharge (e.g. mammary glands, axillary apocrine sweat glands).
3. **Holocrine Glands**: Secretory products fill the entire cytoplasm; the whole cell undergoes apoptosis, disintegrates, and dies to discharge its oily contents (e.g. cutaneous sebaceous glands).
4. *Summary*: Merocrine = zero cell loss; Apocrine = partial apical cell loss; Holocrine = total cell disintegration.
