## Q16. Distinguish between tissue grade and cellular grade of organisation.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > History > Phylum Coelenterata`

**Answer:** In cellular grade (e.g., Protozoa), individual cells perform all functions independently. In tissue grade (e.g., Coelenterata), similar cells are grouped into tissues, each performing specialised functions, enabling division of labour among cell groups.

## Q17. What are 'elementary tissues' as described by Bichat?
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > History > Xavier Bichat`

**Answer:** Elementary tissues, as described by Bichat, are the basic, irreducible tissue types from which all organs of the human body are constructed. He identified 21 such types, proposing that organs are formed by different combinations of these elementary tissues.

## Q18. Explain why Phylum Coelenterata is the first phylum with animal tissue.
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > History > Phylum Coelenterata`

**Answer:** Phylum Coelenterata is the first phylum to have cells organised into distinct tissue layers (ectoderm and endoderm), allowing for functional specialisation. Prior to Coelenterata, animals such as sponges had cells without true tissue formation, making Coelenterata the evolutionary starting point for tissue-level organisation.

## Q21. How did Xavier Bichat change the focus of anatomy from organs to tissues?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > History > Xavier Bichat`

**Answer:** Bichat argued that organs should not be studied as single units but as combinations of different tissues. By classifying 21 elementary tissue types and showing that the same tissues appear in different organs, he shifted anatomical analysis from the organ level to the tissue level, making tissues the primary object of anatomical and physiological investigation.

## Q22. What does the term 'animal histology' specifically study?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > History`

**Answer:** Animal histology studies the microscopic structure of tissues in animals. It examines how different cell types are arranged and organised to form tissues, and how these tissues combine to form organs in animals.
