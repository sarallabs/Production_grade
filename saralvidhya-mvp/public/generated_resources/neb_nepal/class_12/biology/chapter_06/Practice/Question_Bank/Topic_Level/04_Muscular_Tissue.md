## Q1. Describe the ultrastructure of a Sarcomere in skeletal muscle.

**Type:** Describe  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Muscular Tissue > Striped / Striated / Skeletal Muscle`
**Recommended Diagram:** Sarcomere Ultrastructure Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
A Sarcomere is the functional contractile unit between two Z-lines, comprising a dark central A-band (myosin thick filaments) and light I-bands (actin thin filaments).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Sarcomere Definition**: The basic structural and functional unit of myofibrillar contraction extending between two successive **Z-lines** (Z-discs).
2. **A-Band (Anisotropic)**: Dark central zone containing thick **myosin** filaments overlapping with thin actin filaments.
3. **I-Band (Isotropic)**: Light zone containing only thin **actin** filaments, bisected vertically by the proteinaceous Z-line.
4. **H-Zone**: Lighter central region of the A-band containing thick myosin filaments only (no actin overlap).
5. **M-Line**: Dark transverse line in the center of the H-zone that anchors thick myosin filaments.
6. **Contraction Mechanism**: During cross-bridge sliding, thin actin filaments slide inward over thick myosin filaments, shortening the I-band and H-zone while A-band length remains constant.

## Q2. Compare Single-Unit and Multi-Unit Smooth Muscle.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Muscular Tissue > Unstriped / Smooth Muscle`
**Recommended Diagram:** Single-Unit vs Multi-Unit Smooth Muscle Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Single-unit smooth muscle fibers are interconnected by gap junctions, contracting together as a functional syncytium (digestive tract). Multi-unit smooth muscle fibers lack gap junctions, operating independently under direct autonomic control (iris of eye).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Single-Unit (Visceral) Smooth Muscle**: Muscle fibers are coupled electrically via low-resistance **gap junctions**.
2. Excitation spreads rapidly across adjacent cells, causing the entire muscle sheet to contract rhythmically as a single coordinated syncytium.
3. Found in walls of hollow visceral organs (stomach, intestines, bile duct, uterus).
4. **Multi-Unit Smooth Muscle**: Individual muscle fibers operate independently, each receiving separate autonomic motor nerve terminals.
5. Lacks gap junctions; permits fine, incremental graded contractile control.
6. Found in ciliary body and iris of the eye, arrector pili hair muscles, and walls of large muscular arteries.

## Q3. Explain the structure and physiological role of Intercalated Discs in cardiac muscle.

**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Muscular Tissue > Cardiac / Heart Muscle`
**Recommended Diagram:** Intercalated Disc Microarchitecture Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Intercalated discs are specialized junctional complexes between cardiac cells containing Desmosomes (mechanical adhesion during contraction) and Gap Junctions (rapid ion passage for synchronized cardiac contraction).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. Intercalated discs are pathognomonic transverse cell boundaries connecting adjacent branching cardiac muscle cells.
2. **Desmosomes (Macula Adherens)**: Provide strong mechanical cell-to-cell adhesion, preventing cardiac fibers from pulling apart under intense systolic pressures.
3. **Fascia Adherens**: Transmembrane cadherin belts anchoring actin filaments at terminal Z-discs.
4. **Gap Junctions (Nexus)**: Provide low-resistance electrical tunnels for rapid ionic passage (Na+, Ca2+) between cardiocytes.
5. Electrical action potentials generated by the SA node sweep across gap junctions, enabling the myocardium to contract synchronously as a functional electrical syncytium.
6. This combination of mechanical strength and electrical continuity prevents heart wall rupture while maintaining autorhythmic cardiac pumping.
