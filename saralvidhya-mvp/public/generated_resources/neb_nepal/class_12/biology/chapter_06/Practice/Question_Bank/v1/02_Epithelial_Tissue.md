## Q1. Define epithelial tissue.
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue`

**Answer:** Epithelial tissue is a type of animal tissue that lines all free body surfaces, covers organs, and forms glands. It is characterised by closely packed cells with minimal intercellular matrix, avascular nature, and is held together by specialised cell junctions.

---

## Q2. Why is epithelial tissue described as avascular?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Characteristics`

**Answer:** Epithelial tissue is avascular because it lacks blood vessels. Nutrition and gas exchange occur by diffusion from the underlying connective tissue through the basement membrane.

---

## Q3. Name the four types of cell junctions found in epithelial tissue.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Characteristics > Cell junctions`

**Answer:** The four types of cell junctions in epithelial tissue are: (1) Tight junction — seals the space between cells; (2) Adherens junction — links cell membranes; (3) Desmosome — provides mechanical strength; (4) Gap junction — allows direct communication between cells.

---

## Q4. Distinguish between simple epithelium and compound epithelium.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers`

**Answer:** Simple epithelium consists of a single layer of cells resting on the basement membrane. Compound (stratified) epithelium consists of multiple layers of cells, with only the basal layer touching the basement membrane. Simple epithelium functions mainly in secretion and absorption, while compound epithelium mainly provides protection.

---

## Q5. Name the types of simple epithelium.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers > Simple Epithelium`

**Answer:** The types of simple epithelium are: (1) Simple squamous; (2) Simple cuboidal; (3) Simple columnar; (4) Pseudostratified.

---

## Q6. Distinguish between endothelium and mesothelium.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers > Simple Epithelium > Squamous`

**Answer:** Endothelium is a simple squamous epithelium of endodermal origin that lines blood vessels (capillaries, endocardium, tunica interna). Mesothelium is of mesodermal origin and lines the body cavities (parietal and visceral/splanchnic layers).

---

## Q7. What is pseudostratified epithelium?
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers > Simple Epithelium > Pseudostratified`

**Answer:** Pseudostratified epithelium is a type of simple epithelium that appears stratified because the nuclei of its cells are at different levels, but all cells rest on the basement membrane. It is typically ciliated and contains goblet cells, and lines the respiratory tract.

---

## Q8. Distinguish between kino-ciliated and stereo-ciliated epithelium.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Ciliated`

**Answer:** Kino-ciliated epithelium has true motile cilia with a 9+2 microtubule arrangement (axoneme), which actively beat to move substances. Stereo-ciliated epithelium has non-motile stereocilia with a 0+0 (no central tubules) arrangement, which function mainly in absorption.

---

## Q9. List three types of modified epithelium.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium`

**Answer:** Three types of modified epithelium are: (1) Ciliated epithelium (with kino-cilia or stereo-cilia); (2) Glandular epithelium (secretory); (3) Neuro-sensory epithelium (sensory receptor cells).

---

## Q10. What is brush-bordered epithelium and where is it found?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Brush-bordered`

**Answer:** Brush-bordered epithelium has microvilli on the free surface, which increase the surface area for absorption. It is found in the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) of nephrons (cuboidal type) and in the intestinal mucosa (columnar type).

---

## Q11. Distinguish between exocrine, endocrine, and heterocrine glands.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Glandular > By Ducts`

**Answer:** Exocrine glands have ducts and secrete onto body surfaces or into body cavities. Endocrine glands are ductless and secrete hormones directly into the bloodstream. Heterocrine (mixed) glands have both exocrine and endocrine components, e.g., the pancreas.

---

## Q12. Distinguish between apocrine, merocrine, and holocrine glands based on secretion mode.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Glandular > By Quantity`

**Answer:** Merocrine glands secrete by exocytosis without loss of cytoplasm (e.g., salivary glands). Apocrine glands lose part of their apical cytoplasm during secretion (e.g., mammary glands). Holocrine glands release their entire cellular content upon disintegration (e.g., sebaceous glands).

---

## Q13. Distinguish between serous and mucous glands.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Glandular > By Consistency`

**Answer:** Serous glands secrete a watery, enzyme-rich fluid (e.g., parotid salivary gland). Mucous glands secrete a thick, viscous mucus rich in glycoproteins (e.g., mucous cells of the stomach). Mixed glands produce both serous and mucous secretions.

---

## Q14. How many free surfaces does epithelium have in coelomates, pseudocoelomates, and acoelomates?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Surfaces`

**Answer:** Coelomates have 4 free surfaces; pseudocoelomates have 3 free surfaces; acoelomates have 2 free surfaces.

## Q15. Name the types of compound epithelium.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers > Compound Epithelium`

**Answer:** The types of compound (stratified) epithelium are: (1) Stratified squamous (keratinised and non-keratinised); (2) Stratified cuboidal (rare); (3) Stratified columnar (uncommon); (4) Transitional (urothelium/plastic epithelium).

---

## Q16. What is transitional epithelium (urothelium)?
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers > Compound Epithelium > Transitional`

**Answer:** Transitional epithelium, also called urothelium or plastic epithelium, is a type of stratified epithelium found lining the urinary bladder and ureters. Its cells can change shape — squamous when stretched (bladder full) and cuboidal when relaxed (bladder empty).

---

## Q17. Distinguish between keratinised and non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium.
**Type:** Difference  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers > Compound Epithelium > Stratified > Squamous`

**Answer:** Keratinised stratified squamous epithelium has dead, keratin-filled surface cells (e.g., skin/epidermis) providing a waterproof barrier. Non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium has living surface cells (e.g., oral mucosa, oesophagus, vagina) providing protection against abrasion while remaining moist.

---

## Q18. What is germinal epithelium?
**Type:** Definition  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Germinal`

**Answer:** Germinal epithelium is a type of modified epithelium that gives rise to germ cells (gametes). It lines the gonads — the germinal epithelium of the ovary produces oocytes and that of the testis produces spermatogonia.

---

## Q19. True or False: Epithelial tissue contains a rich blood supply. Justify.
**Type:** TrueFalse  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Easy
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Characteristics`

**Answer:** False. Epithelial tissue is avascular — it has no blood vessels. Nutrients and oxygen reach epithelial cells by diffusion from the underlying connective tissue through the basement membrane.

---

## Q20. What is the role of cell junctions in epithelial tissue?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Characteristics > Cell junctions`

**Answer:** Cell junctions in epithelial tissue serve to: Tight junctions — seal the intercellular space and prevent leakage; Adherens junctions — link adjacent cell membranes for structural integrity; Desmosomes — provide mechanical strength to resist shear forces; Gap junctions — allow direct communication and exchange of ions/molecules between adjacent cells.

---

## Q21. Classify glandular epithelium by complexity, naming all types.
**Type:** List  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Hard
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Glandular > By Complexity`

**Answer:** By complexity, glands are classified as: Simple glands — Simple tubular, Simple branched tubular, Simple coiled tubular, Simple alveolar, Simple branched alveolar. Compound glands — Compound tubular, Compound alveolar, Compound tubulo-alveolar.

---

## Q22. What type of epithelium lines the PCT (proximal convoluted tubule) of nephrons?
**Type:** Fact  |  **Bloom's:** Remember  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Layers > Simple Epithelium > Cuboidal > Brush-bordered`

**Answer:** Simple cuboidal brush-bordered epithelium lines the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) of nephrons. The microvilli (brush border) greatly increase the surface area for reabsorption.

---

## Q23. What is pigmented epithelium and where is it found?
**Type:** Explain  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `Animal Tissue > Epithelial Tissue > Modified Epithelium > Pigmented`

**Answer:** Pigmented epithelium contains melanin or other pigment granules within the epithelial cells. A key example is the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) of the eye, which absorbs stray light to improve visual acuity and supports the photoreceptor cells.
