## Q1. Describe the cellular components of Proper Connective Tissue and state their functions.

**Type:** Describe  |  **Bloom's:** Understand  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Connective Tissue > Proper Connective Tissue`
**Recommended Diagram:** Connective Tissue Proper Cells Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Proper connective tissue contains Fibroblasts (matrix/fiber synthesis), Mast cells (histamine, heparin release), Macrophages (phagocytosis), and Plasma cells (antibody synthesis with cartwheel nucleus).

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Fibroblasts**: Stellate, abundant cells that actively synthesize extracellular ground matrix and protein fiber precursors (collagen, elastin).
2. **Mast Cells**: Granular cells mediating inflammatory allergic cascades; secrete **Histamine** (vasodilator), **Heparin** (anticoagulant), and **Serotonin** (vasoconstrictor).
3. **Histiocytes / Macrophages**: Phagocytic cells derived from monocytes that engulf invading microorganisms and dead cellular debris.
4. **Plasma Cells**: Differentiated B-lymphocytes featuring a characteristic **'cartwheel' or 'clock-face'** nuclear chromatin pattern; synthesize circulating antibodies.
5. **Adipocytes**: Ring-shaped cells specialized for lipid storage and thermal insulation.

## Q2. Differentiate between Tendons and Ligaments in dense connective tissue.

**Type:** Compare  |  **Bloom's:** Analyze  |  **Difficulty:** Medium
**Mindmap Path:** `UNIT 6: ANIMAL TISSUE > Connective Tissue > Proper Connective Tissue`
**Recommended Diagram:** Tendon vs Ligament Microarchitecture Diagram

### 📝 2-Mark Short Answer
Tendons are dense regular white fibrous tissues connecting skeletal muscle to bone with high tensile strength. Ligaments are dense elastic yellow fibrous tissues connecting bone to bone with high flexibility.

### 📜 5-Mark Detailed Answer
1. **Structural Composition**: Tendons consist of parallel unbranched bundles of collagen (white) fibers. Ligaments consist of branching networks of elastic (yellow) fibers mixed with collagen.
2. **Attachments**: Tendons attach voluntary muscle to bone. Ligaments attach bone to bone across synovial joints.
3. **Flexibility vs Tensile Strength**: Tendons possess immense tensile strength with limited elasticity. Ligaments exhibit high elasticity and flexibility.
4. **Cell Distribution**: Fibroblasts in tendons lie in continuous parallel rows between collagen bundles. Fibroblasts in ligaments are scattered irregularly throughout elastic fibers.
5. **Functional Significance**: Tendons transmit muscle force to pull bones. Ligaments stabilize joint movement and prevent hyperextension.
