### Section 2: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

1.  **What is a tissue? State the two main types of plant tissues based on their dividing capacity.**
    *   **Model Answer:** A tissue is a group of cells that have a common origin and perform one or more specific functions. Based on their capacity to divide, plant tissues are broadly classified into two main types: Meristematic tissue and Permanent tissue.

2.  **Describe any three characteristic features of meristematic cells.**
    *   **Model Answer:** Three characteristic features of meristematic cells are:
        *   They are living, small, and have thin, elastic, cellulosic cell walls.
        *   They are compactly arranged without intercellular spaces.
        *   Each cell contains dense cytoplasm with a large, prominent nucleus, and vacuoles are either absent or very small.

3.  **Differentiate between apical meristem and intercalary meristem with respect to their position and function.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Apical meristem:** Located at the tips of roots and shoots. Their function is to increase the length of the plant body.
        *   **Intercalary meristem:** Found at the base of internodes or leaf bases. They are considered a part of apical meristem that gets separated and are responsible for increasing the length of the plant at specific regions (e.g., in grasses).

4.  **What is the role of the root cap? From which part of the apical meristem does it originate?**
    *   **Model Answer:** The root cap is a protective layer of cells covering the root apical meristem. Its role is to protect the delicate meristematic tissue as the root grows through the soil. In the Histogen theory, it originates from the calyptrogen, which is formed from the dermatogen.

5.  **Explain the difference between promereistem and primary meristem.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Promeristem:** This is the earliest embryonic meristematic tissue found at the tips of developing organs. It is the source from which primary meristems arise.
        *   **Primary meristem:** This tissue is derived from the promeristem during early development. It is located at the tips of roots and shoots and retains its power of division throughout the plant's life, forming the primary structure of the plant body.

6.  **Define lateral meristems. Give two examples and mention their function.**
    *   **Model Answer:** Lateral meristems are meristematic tissues that lie along the lateral sides of roots and stems. They are responsible for increasing the girth or thickness of the plant organ. Examples include vascular cambium and cork cambium.

7.  **What is the significance of the Tunica-Corpus theory in understanding shoot apex organization?**
    *   **Model Answer:** The Tunica-Corpus theory, proposed by Schmidt, explains the organization of the shoot apex into two distinct regions: Tunica (outer layers responsible for epidermis and leaf primordia) and Corpus (central mass responsible for cortex, vascular tissue, and pith). This theory helps differentiate the contributions of different meristematic zones to the shoot structure.

8.  **Distinguish between vascular cambium and cork cambium.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Vascular Cambium:** Primarily a primary meristem (though can be secondary in origin) located between xylem and phloem, responsible for secondary growth by producing secondary xylem and secondary phloem.
        *   **Cork Cambium:** A secondary meristem arising from the cortex, responsible for producing cork (phellem) on the outside and secondary cortex (phelloderm) on the inside, contributing to the bark formation.

9.  **What is the primary function of the ground meristem? Name two tissues it differentiates into.**
    *   **Model Answer:** The primary function of the ground meristem is to differentiate and form the ground tissue system of the plant body. It differentiates into tissues such as hypodermis, cortex, endodermis, pericycle, and pith in dicots.

10. **Briefly explain the difference between endogenous and exogenous branch origins.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Exogenous:** Branches originate from the outer layers of the plant body, typically from the shoot apical meristem giving rise to shoot branches.
        *   **Endogenous:** Branches originate from internal tissues, such as the pericycle, which is the origin of root branches.

### Section 3: Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)

1.  **Describe the classification of meristematic tissues based on their position in the plant body. Include specific examples for each type.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   Introduction: Define meristematic tissues and their role in growth.
        *   Classification based on position:
            *   **Apical Meristems:**
                *   Location: Tips of shoot and root.
                *   Function: Increase in length.
                *   Examples: Shoot apex, root apex.
            *   **Intercalary Meristems:**
                *   Location: At the base of internodes, leaf bases (e.g., grasses, Pinus, Mentha).
                *   Function: Increase in length at specific regions.
            *   **Lateral Meristems:**
                *   Location: Along the lateral sides of roots and stems (e.g., vascular cambium, cork cambium).
                *   Function: Increase in girth/thickness (secondary growth).
        *   Conclusion: Summarize the importance of these meristems for plant growth.

2.  **Explain the Histogen Theory of root apical meristem organization. Name the three distinct regions and their respective derivatives.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   Introduction: State that the Histogen Theory explains the structural organization of root and shoot apices.
        *   Focus on Root Apex:
            *   Proposed by Hanstein (1870).
            *   Three distinct regions of meristematic tissue:
                *   **Dermatogen:** Outermost layer, forms epidermis. Mentions calyptrogen for root cap formation.
                *   **Periblem:** Middle region, forms the cortex.
                *   **Plerome:** Central region, forms the stele (vascular tissues, pericycle, pith).
        *   Limitations/Further observations: Briefly mention that strict boundaries are not always clear in higher plants.
        *   Conclusion: Highlight the contribution of this theory.

3.  **Elaborate on the Tunica-Corpus theory of shoot apical meristem organization. Describe the components and their roles in differentiating plant structures.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   Introduction: State the theory and its proposer (Schmidt, 1924) for shoot apex.
        *   Two distinct zones:
            *   **Tunica:**
                *   Structure: One or more peripheral layers of cells.
                *   Cell characteristics: Smaller than corpus.
                *   Function: Outer layer forms epidermis; other layers contribute to leaf primordia and hypodermal cells.
                *   Mention variation in number of layers in monocots and dicots.
            *   **Corpus:**
                *   Structure: Central, undifferentiated, multi-layered mass of cells.
                *   Cell characteristics: Comparatively larger, divides irregularly.
                *   Function: Forms cortex, vascular tissues, and pith.
        *   Conclusion: Emphasize how this theory clarifies the development of shoot organs.

4.  **What are permanent tissues? Describe the process of differentiation and dedifferentiation in the formation of permanent tissues, using examples.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   Definition of Permanent Tissues: Derived from meristematic tissues that have lost their power to divide and are specialized for specific functions.
        *   **Differentiation:** The process by which meristematic cells mature and specialize into specific types of permanent tissues with distinct structures and functions.
            *   Example: Cells of the procambium differentiate into xylem and phloem. Ground meristem cells differentiate into cortex, endodermis, etc.
        *   **Dedifferentiation:** The process by which specialized permanent tissue cells regain their meristematic capacity and lose their specific characteristics, becoming meristematic again.
            *   Example: Cells of parenchyma in the medullary rays dedifferentiate to form interfascicular cambium (secondary meristem).
        *   **Redifferentiation:** The process by which dedifferentiated cells once again differentiate into specialized permanent tissues, potentially different from their original type.
        *   Conclusion: Explain how these processes are crucial for plant growth, repair, and secondary growth.

5.  **Classify meristematic tissues based on their origin and development. Describe each type with its characteristics and significance.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   Introduction: Briefly state the basis of classification (origin and development).
        *   **Promeristem:**
            *   Origin: Earliest embryonic meristematic cells.
            *   Location: Small area at tips of stem and root.
            *   Significance: Gives rise to primary meristems.
        *   **Primary Meristem:**
            *   Origin: Derived from promeristem.
            *   Location: Tips of root, stem, and appendages.
            *   Characteristics: Retain division capacity, form primary plant body structure.
            *   Mention vascular cambium in dicot stems as a primary meristem dividing in one plane.
        *   **Secondary Meristem:**
            *   Origin: Formed from primary permanent tissues that become meristematic.
            *   Location: Always lateral (vascular cambium of root, interfascicular cambium, cork cambium).
            *   Significance: Responsible for secondary growth.
        *   Conclusion: Summarize their roles in continuous growth and thickening.
