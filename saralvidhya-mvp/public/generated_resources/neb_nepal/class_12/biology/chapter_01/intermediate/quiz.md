### Question 1
Which of the following characteristics is *NOT* typical of meristematic cells?

A. Presence of large, distinct vacuoles
B. Compact arrangement with no intercellular spaces
C. Thin, elastic, and cellulosic cell walls
D. Dense cytoplasm with a prominent nucleus

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Meristematic cells are characterized by very small or absent vacuoles, not large and distinct ones. Large vacuoles are typically found in mature, differentiated cells.

### Question 2
According to the Histogen Theory, which region of the apical meristem in a root is responsible for forming the central stele (vascular tissues, pericycle, pith)?

A. Dermatogen
B. Periblem
C. Plerome
D. Calyptrogen

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Histogen Theory describes three regions of the apical meristem. The Plerome is the central meristematic region that forms the stele. The Dermatogen forms the epidermis, the Periblem forms the cortex, and the Calyptrogen forms the root cap.

### Question 3
Secondary meristems are responsible for secondary growth, leading to an increase in girth. Which of the following is an example of a secondary meristem?

A. Apical meristem of a root
B. Intercalary meristem in grasses
C. Vascular cambium in a dicot stem
D. Protoderm

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Vascular cambium in dicot stems and cork cambium are examples of secondary meristems that arise from dedifferentiation of permanent tissues and are responsible for secondary growth (increase in girth). Apical and intercalary meristems are primary meristems, and protoderm is a functional classification of meristems.

### Question 4
The Tunica-Corpus Theory is used to explain the organization of the shoot apex. What is the primary role of the 'Corpus' in this theory?

A. To form the epidermis of the shoot.
B. To develop leaf primordia.
C. To form the central core of cortex, vascular tissue, and pith.
D. To contribute to the formation of the leaf primordia and hypodermal cells.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Corpus is the central, multilayered mass of cells in the shoot apex. It divides by irregular divisions and forms the bulk of the stem, including the cortex, vascular tissues, and pith. The Tunica, on the other hand, forms the epidermis and contributes to leaf primordia.

### Question 5
Intercalary meristems are found in specific locations and contribute to growth. Which of the following plant parts typically possesses intercalary meristems, enabling them to regrow rapidly after being grazed?

A. The tips of the roots of a carrot plant.
B. The base of the internodes in grasses.
C. The lateral sides of a woody stem.
D. The shoot apex of a young sunflower seedling.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Intercalary meristems are found at the base of internodes in grasses and Equisetum. This allows them to elongate and regrow after being cut or grazed, which is crucial for their survival. Apical meristems are at the tips (roots and shoots), lateral meristems are on the sides (increasing girth), and while shoot apices are meristematic, intercalary growth is specifically associated with the base of internodes in grasses.
