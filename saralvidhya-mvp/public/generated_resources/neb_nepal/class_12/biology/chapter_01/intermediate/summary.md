Plant anatomy, the study of plant structure, begins with understanding tissues. A tissue is a group of cells with a common origin and function. In higher plants, tissues are specialized to perform specific roles, leading to a division of labor.

Plant tissues are broadly classified into two types based on their ability to divide:

1.  **Meristematic Tissue:** These are immature, actively dividing cells responsible for plant growth. They are small, thin-walled, and compactly arranged with no intercellular spaces. Meristematic tissues can be classified based on their origin (promeristem, primary meristem, secondary meristem), position (apical, intercalary, lateral), and function (protoderm, procambium, ground meristem). Apical and intercalary meristems contribute to the plant's length, while lateral meristems increase its thickness.

2.  **Permanent Tissue:** (Not fully detailed in this excerpt, but it's the counterpart to meristematic tissue) These cells have lost their ability to divide and have differentiated to perform specific functions. They can be simple (made of one type of cell) or complex (made of different types of cells).
