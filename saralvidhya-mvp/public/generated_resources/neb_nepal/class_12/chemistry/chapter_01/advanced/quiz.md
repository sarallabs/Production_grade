**Question 1**
In an ideal volumetric titration, the "equivalence point" and the "end point" are often treated as synonymous. However, in rigorous analytical practice, a "titration error" is defined by the discrepancy between them. Which of the following conditions would most likely minimize this error?
A. Using an indicator that changes color exactly at the equivalence point, regardless of the reaction kinetics.
B. Performing the titration at a very slow speed to ensure equilibrium is maintained throughout.
C. Selecting an indicator whose pH transition range falls strictly within the steep vertical portion of the titration curve.
D. Using a highly concentrated titrant to ensure that the volume added at the end point is as small as possible.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The titration error is minimized when the indicator's color change occurs exactly at the point where chemically equivalent amounts have reacted (equivalence point). For acid-base titrations, the pH changes most rapidly near the equivalence point. An indicator whose transition range coincides with this rapid change ensures the observed end point is closest to the theoretical equivalence point.

**Question 2**
In the redox titration of oxalic acid ($H_2C_2O_4$) against potassium permanganate ($KMnO_4$) in an acidic medium, $KMnO_4$ acts as a self-indicator. If the titration were performed in a highly alkaline medium instead, what would be the likely visual outcome?
A. The pink color would appear even earlier due to the formation of $MnO_2$.
B. The reaction would not proceed, as $KMnO_4$ is only an oxidizing agent in acidic conditions.
C. The titration would fail because $KMnO_4$ would be reduced to brown $MnO_2$ (a precipitate), masking the end point.
D. The color change would be more distinct, shifting from purple to colorless.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** In acidic medium, $MnO_4^-$ is reduced to $Mn^{2+}$ (colorless), allowing the persistent pink of excess $KMnO_4$ to serve as the end point. In alkaline or neutral media, $MnO_4^-$ is reduced to $MnO_2$, which is a brown/black precipitate. This precipitate obscures the view and prevents the detection of the faint pink color, rendering the titration useless for accurate volumetric analysis.

**Question 3**
Consider the reaction: $CuSO_4 + 4KI \rightarrow 2K_2SO_4 + Cu_2I_2 + I_2$. This is a classic example of iodometry. Why is it necessary to add sodium thiosulphate ($Na_2S_2O_3$) to the resulting mixture to determine the concentration of $CuSO_4$?
A. Because $Cu_2I_2$ is unstable and must be neutralized by thiosulphate.
B. Because $I_2$ is the product representing the stoichiometry of the initial $CuSO_4$, and $I_2$ can be easily titrated with $Na_2S_2O_3$.
C. Because $CuSO_4$ cannot be titrated directly with an indicator.
D. Because the reaction between $CuSO_4$ and $KI$ is incomplete without the presence of thiosulphate.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Iodometry involves titrating the *liberated* iodine. In the given reaction, the amount of $I_2$ produced is chemically equivalent to the amount of $Cu^{2+}$ ions. By titrating this liberated $I_2$ against a standard solution of $Na_2S_2O_3$, we can indirectly but accurately calculate the initial concentration of the $CuSO_4$ solution.

**Question 4**
Which of the following statements regarding the selection of a reaction for volumetric analysis is INCORRECT?
A. The reaction must be stoichiometric and simple.
B. The reaction should ideally be slow to allow for precise volume measurements.
C. The reaction must proceed to completion.
D. A prominent physical change (like color change) must be detectable at the end point.

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** One of the primary conditions for a successful volumetric analysis (as per the provided text) is that the reaction must take place at a **very fast speed**. If the reaction is slow, it would take an impractical amount of time to reach the end point, and the accuracy would be compromised due to the lack of a sharp transition.

**Question 5**
In Argentometric titration (Precipitation titration), the reaction $AgNO_3 + NaCl \rightarrow AgCl\downarrow + NaNO_3$ is used. If a student mistakenly uses a solution containing $Fe^{3+}$ ions as an impurity, how does this affect the titration process?
A. It acts as an indicator, changing color when all $Cl^-$ ions are consumed.
B. It will precipitate as $FeCl_3$, consuming more $AgNO_3$ and leading to a positive titration error.
C. It will interfere with the visual detection of the end point or compete for the titrant.
D. It has no effect, as only $Ag^+$ and $Cl^-$ react.

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** In precipitation titrations, selectivity is key. Impurities like $Fe^{3+}$ can form their own precipitates with reagents or interfere with the specific indicators used for argento-metric titrations (like the Volhard or Mohr methods). Competitive precipitation or complex formation directly interferes with the accuracy of detecting the equivalence point.
