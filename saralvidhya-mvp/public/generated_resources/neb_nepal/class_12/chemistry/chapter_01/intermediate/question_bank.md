### Part 2: Short Answer Questions (2-3 Marks)

1.  **Differentiate between qualitative and quantitative analysis.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Qualitative analysis** involves the detection and identification of the constituents present in an inorganic substance. It answers "what is present?". (1.5 marks)
        *   **Quantitative analysis** involves the estimation of the accurate amounts of the constituents identified. It answers "how much is present?". (1.5 marks)

2.  **Define volumetric analysis and state its primary goal.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Volumetric analysis** involves the determination of the volume of a solution of accurately known concentration (standard solution) required to react quantitatively with the solution of the substance being determined. (2 marks)
        *   Its primary goal is to determine the amount of constituents in the given unknown solution by measuring volumes. (1 mark)

3.  **Explain the terms 'titrant' and 'titrate'.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Titrant:** It is the reagent of known concentration (standard solution) that is added from the burette during a titration. (1.5 marks)
        *   **Titrate:** It is the substance being titrated, typically a solution of unknown strength, taken in the conical flask. (1.5 marks)

4.  **What is an indicator in titration? Why is it crucial?**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   An **indicator** is an auxiliary reagent (often an organic substance) that gives a sharp, unmistakable colour change at or near the end point of a titration. (1.5 marks)
        *   It is crucial because it visually signals the completion of the reaction (end point), allowing the analyst to stop adding the titrant and record the volume used. Without an indicator (or a self-indicating substance), it would be impossible to determine the end point accurately. (1.5 marks)

5.  **List three essential conditions for a chemical reaction to be suitable for volumetric analysis.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        1.  The reaction should proceed to completion under easily attainable experimental conditions and take place at a very fast speed.
        2.  The reaction involved must be simple, following a clear stoichiometry.
        3.  The reaction must involve a prominent change in colour at the end point, or a suitable indicator should be available to show the end point.
        (Any three, 1 mark each)

6.  **Briefly describe acidimetry and alkalimetry with an example.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Acidimetry and Alkalimetry** is a type of volumetric analysis where a known strength of an acid or a base is titrated with a requisite volume of a base or an acid of unknown strength, or vice-versa. These are neutralization titrations. (1.5 marks)
        *   **Example:** Titration of hydrochloric acid (HCl) against sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution. (1.5 marks)
            HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

7.  **Why is potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) considered a self-indicator in certain redox titrations?**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   KMnO₄ is a strong oxidizing agent and its solution has a distinct purple colour due to the MnO₄⁻ ion. (1 mark)
        *   In acidic medium, it is reduced to colourless Mn²⁺ ions (e.g., in titration with oxalic acid). (1 mark)
        *   Once all the reducing agent (e.g., oxalic acid) has reacted, the very next drop of KMnO₄ added will impart a faint but permanent pink colour to the solution, indicating the end point without the need for an external indicator. (1 mark)

8.  **Distinguish between iodimetry and iodometry.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Iodimetry:** This type of redox titration involves using a standard solution of iodine (I₂) directly as an oxidizing agent to titrate a reducing substance. (1.5 marks)
        *   **Iodometry:** In this type, an oxidizing agent (of unknown strength) reacts with an excess of iodide ions (KI) to liberate iodine. The liberated iodine is then titrated with a standard solution of sodium thiosulphate (Na₂S₂O₃). The amount of liberated iodine corresponds to the amount of the original oxidizing agent. (1.5 marks)

9.  **What are precipitation titrations? Give an example.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Precipitation titrations** are a type of volumetric analysis where the strength of a solution is determined by its complete precipitation upon reaction with a known (standard) solution of another substance. The end point is indicated by the formation or disappearance of a precipitate. (2 marks)
        *   **Example:** Titration of silver nitrate (AgNO₃) solution with sodium chloride (NaCl) solution, forming a precipitate of silver chloride (AgCl).
            AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃ (1 mark)

10. **Define 'equivalent weight' as introduced in the context of chemical analysis.**
    *   **Model Answer:**
        *   **Equivalent weight** is the definite combining weight of an element. It refers to the weight of an element that combines with a fixed weight of any one chosen standard element. (3 marks)
        *(Note: For Class 10, typically a conceptual understanding is expected, not calculation.)*

### Part 3: Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)

1.  **Describe the process of a typical volumetric titration, explaining key terms like standard solution, titrant, titrate, end point, and equivalence point.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Introduction:** Volumetric analysis determines the concentration of an unknown solution by reacting it with a known concentration solution.
        *   **Titration Process:**
            *   A known volume of the solution of unknown concentration (titrate) is accurately measured using a pipette and transferred to a conical flask. An appropriate indicator is added.
            *   The standard solution (titrant) is filled into a burette and its initial reading is noted.
            *   The titrant is slowly added drop by drop from the burette into the conical flask, with continuous swirling, until the indicator changes colour.
            *   The point at which the indicator changes colour is the **end point**.
            *   The final burette reading is noted, and the volume of titrant used is calculated.
        *   **Key Terms Explanation:**
            *   **Standard Solution:** Solution of accurately known concentration.
            *   **Titrant:** The standard solution added from the burette.
            *   **Titrate:** The solution of unknown concentration in the conical flask.
            *   **End Point:** The point where the indicator shows a visible colour change, signaling the completion of the reaction.
            *   **Equivalence Point:** The theoretical point where chemically equivalent amounts of the titrant and titrate have reacted completely. (Mention titration error).
        *   **Calculation:** From the volume of standard solution used and the stoichiometry of the reaction, the concentration of the unknown solution can be calculated.

2.  **Explain the principle of Acidimetry and Alkalimetry. Describe the experimental setup and reaction involved in the titration of hydrochloric acid (HCl) against sodium hydroxide (NaOH), including the choice of indicator.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Principle (Acidimetry & Alkalimetry):** These are neutralization titrations. An acid reacts quantitatively with a base to form salt and water. The concentration of an unknown acid (or base) is determined by reacting it with a standard solution of a base (or acid).
        *   **Reaction Involved:** HCl (acid) + NaOH (base) → NaCl (salt) + H₂O (water)
            *   Ionic representation: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)
        *   **Experimental Setup:**
            *   **Burette:** Filled with standard NaOH solution (titrant).
            *   **Conical Flask:** Contains a known volume of HCl solution (titrate).
            *   **Indicator:** Methyl orange or phenolphthalein.
        *   **Procedure (brief):**
            *   Pipette 10 ml of HCl into a conical flask. Add 2-3 drops of indicator (e.g., phenolphthalein).
            *   Fill burette with NaOH solution, adjust to zero.
            *   Add NaOH dropwise to HCl, swirling continuously, until the indicator changes colour (e.g., colourless to pink for phenolphthalein).
            *   Note the volume of NaOH used.
        *   **Indicator Choice:**
            *   Phenolphthalein: Colourless in acidic medium, pink in basic medium (pH range 8.2-10). Suitable for strong acid-strong base titrations.
            *   Methyl orange: Red in acidic medium, yellow in basic medium (pH range 3.1-4.4). Also suitable for strong acid-strong base titrations.
        *   **Conclusion:** The volume of NaOH used helps calculate the concentration of HCl.

3.  **Discuss Oxidation-Reduction (Redox) titrations. Explain the principle and elaborate on the titration of oxalic acid solution against potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) solution in acidic medium, highlighting the role of KMnO₄.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Principle of Redox Titrations:** These titrations involve a redox reaction where electron transfer or change in oxidation number occurs between the reacting substances. The concentration of an unknown oxidizing or reducing agent is determined by reacting it with a standard solution of a reducing or oxidizing agent, respectively.
        *   **Example:** Titration of oxalic acid (reducing agent) against KMnO₄ (oxidizing agent) in acidic medium (usually H₂SO₄).
        *   **Reactions:**
            *   KMnO₄ (oxidizing agent): 2KMnO₄ + 3H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2MnSO₄ + 3H₂O + 5[O] (Mn changes from +7 to +2)
            *   Oxalic acid (reducing agent): 5C₂H₂O₄ + 5[O] → 10CO₂ + 5H₂O (Carbon changes from +3 to +4)
            *   Overall reaction: 2KMnO₄ + 5C₂H₂O₄ + 3H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2MnSO₄ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O
        *   **Role of KMnO₄ as Self-Indicator:**
            *   KMnO₄ solution is purple due to MnO₄⁻ ions (Mn in +7 oxidation state).
            *   During titration, KMnO₄ oxidizes oxalic acid and gets reduced to colourless Mn²⁺ ions (Mn in +2 oxidation state).
            *   As long as oxalic acid is present, the added KMnO₄ is decolorized.
            *   Once all the oxalic acid has reacted, the very next drop of KMnO₄ added to the solution will not be decolorized and will impart a faint but permanent pink colour, indicating the end point. This eliminates the need for an external indicator.
        *   **Conditions:** The reaction is carried out in an acidic medium (H₂SO₄) to ensure complete reduction of MnO₄⁻ to Mn²⁺.

4.  **Compare and contrast Volumetric analysis and Gravimetric analysis as types of quantitative analysis.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Definition/Principle (Comparison):**
            *   **Volumetric Analysis:** Determines the amount of a substance by measuring the *volume* of a standard solution required to react quantitatively with it. (Focus on volume measurements).
            *   **Gravimetric Analysis:** Determines the amount of a substance by measuring the *mass* of a pure compound to which the analyte is chemically converted (e.g., precipitation, then weighing). (Focus on mass/weight measurements).
        *   **Advantages of Volumetric Analysis (Contrast):**
            *   Rapid and faster process.
            *   Does not involve laborious operations like drying and weighing precipitates.
            *   Generally more accurate for certain types of reactions.
        *   **Disadvantages of Volumetric Analysis (Contrast):**
            *   Requires a prominent colour change or a suitable indicator.
            *   Applicable only to reactions that are fast, complete, and simple.
        *   **Scope (Contrast):**
            *   Volumetric analysis is versatile for acid-base, redox, precipitation reactions, etc., where clear end points are achievable.
            *   Gravimetric analysis is preferred for determining substances that can be isolated as a pure, stable, and weighable precipitate.
        *   **Conclusion:** Both are quantitative methods, but differ in the type of measurement used (volume vs. mass) and have different applicability based on reaction characteristics and desired precision.

5.  **Enumerate and briefly explain the different types of volumetric analysis discussed, giving one characteristic example for each.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        1.  **Acidimetry and Alkalimetry (Neutralization Titrations):**
            *   **Explanation:** Involves the titration of an acid with a base or vice-versa to determine their concentrations. The reaction is a neutralization reaction.
            *   **Example:** Titration of HCl with NaOH using phenolphthalein indicator. (HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O)
        2.  **Oxidation and Reduction Titration (Redox Titrations):**
            *   **Explanation:** Involves reactions where there is a change in oxidation number or transfer of electrons between reacting substances.
            *   **Example:** Titration of oxalic acid solution against potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) solution in acidic medium, where KMnO₄ acts as a self-indicator.
        3.  **Iodometry and Iodimetry:**
            *   **Explanation:** These are specific types of redox titrations involving iodine.
                *   **Iodimetry:** Direct titration using a standard solution of iodine as an oxidizing agent.
                *   **Iodometry:** Indirect titration where iodine is liberated from iodide by an oxidizing agent, and the liberated iodine is then titrated with a standard sodium thiosulphate solution.
            *   **Example (Iodimetry):** Titration of Na₂S₂O₃ with I₂ (I₂ + 2Na₂S₂O₃ → 2NaI + Na₂S₄O₆)
            *   **Example (Iodometry):** Estimation of CuSO₄ by reacting it with KI to liberate I₂, followed by titration of I₂ with Na₂S₂O₃. (CuSO₄ + 4KI → 2K₂SO₄ + Cu₂I₂ + I₂)
        4.  **Precipitation Titration:**
            *   **Explanation:** The strength of a solution is determined by its complete precipitation upon reaction with a known (standard) solution of another substance.
            *   **Example:** Titration of silver nitrate (AgNO₃) with sodium chloride (NaCl) where AgCl precipitates. (AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃). These are often called argentometric titrations.

### Part 4: Higher Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) Questions

1.  **A student is performing an acid-base titration to determine the concentration of an unknown HCl solution using a standard NaOH solution. However, they mistakenly forget to add the indicator to the conical flask containing the HCl. What would be the consequence of this error on their observation and the calculated concentration?**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Consequence on Observation:** The student would not observe any visible colour change in the conical flask, even after adding the NaOH solution from the burette. They would keep adding the titrant without knowing when the reaction is complete, likely overshooting the equivalence point significantly or indefinitely.
        *   **Consequence on Calculated Concentration:** Since the end point cannot be identified, the student would record an arbitrarily high volume of NaOH consumed. This artificially high volume would lead to a calculated concentration of HCl that is significantly higher than its actual concentration (assuming direct calculation based on volume). The result would be inaccurate and unreliable.

2.  **During an experiment to standardize a sodium hydroxide solution using a standard solution of oxalic acid, a student washes the burette with distilled water but forgets to rinse it with the NaOH solution before filling it. How will this procedural error affect the calculated concentration of the NaOH solution? Justify your answer.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Procedural Error:** Not rinsing the burette with the titrant (NaOH solution) after washing with distilled water.
        *   **Effect:** The distilled water droplets remaining inside the burette will dilute the NaOH solution filled in it.
        *   **Impact on Titration:** This diluted NaOH solution will appear to have a lower concentration than its actual value. Therefore, a larger volume of this diluted NaOH solution will be required to react completely with the fixed amount of oxalic acid in the conical flask.
        *   **Effect on Calculated Concentration:** Since the calculated volume of NaOH used (from the burette reading) will be higher than it should have been for the actual concentration, the *calculated* concentration of NaOH solution will appear to be lower than its true concentration.

3.  **Consider the reaction: Fe (s) + CuSO₄ (aq) → FeSO₄ (aq) + Cu (s). A student proposes to use this reaction for a volumetric analysis to determine the concentration of CuSO₄ solution. Critically evaluate whether this reaction is suitable for volumetric analysis based on the conditions discussed in the chapter.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Conditions for Volumetric Analysis:**
            1.  Reaction should proceed to completion rapidly.
            2.  Reaction must be simple and follow clear stoichiometry.
            3.  There must be a prominent colour change at the end point or a suitable indicator available.
        *   **Evaluation of the proposed reaction:**
            1.  **Completion & Speed:** This is a displacement reaction. While it can go to completion, it might not be instantaneous, especially as solid iron reacts with the solution. The speed might be a limiting factor.
            2.  **Simplicity & Stoichiometry:** The reaction is simple and has clear stoichiometry.
            3.  **Prominent Colour Change/Indicator:**
                *   CuSO₄ solution is blue, and FeSO₄ solution is pale green. The change from blue to pale green is not a sharp, distinct colour change that occurs precisely at the equivalence point. It's a gradual fading of blue and appearance of green.
                *   There isn't a readily available external indicator that would give a sharp colour change for this specific reaction at the equivalence point.
                *   Solid copper is also formed, which can interfere with visual observation and potentially coat the iron surface, slowing the reaction further.
        *   **Conclusion:** This reaction is **not suitable** for volumetric analysis. The primary reason is the lack of a sharp, distinct end point signal (either inherent or via an indicator) and potentially the slow reaction rate due to the involvement of a solid reactant. Volumetric analysis typically involves reactions between two solutions.
