**Question 1**
Which of the following is the primary measurement involved in volumetric analysis for determining the amount of constituents in an unknown solution?
A. Weight of the precipitate formed
B. Change in the temperature of the solution
C. Volume of solutions involved in the chemical reaction
D. Electrical conductivity of the solution

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The text states, "The volumetric analysis deals with measurements of the volumes of solutions involved in the chemical reactions, which ultimately leads to the determination of the amount of the constituents in the given unknown solutions."

**Question 2**
For a chemical reaction to be suitable for volumetric analysis, which of the following conditions is NOT necessarily true?
A. The reaction should proceed to completion rapidly.
B. The reaction involved must be simple and reproducible.
C. The reaction must produce a visible precipitate at the end point.
D. A suitable indicator should be available to show the end point of the reaction.

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The text lists conditions for volumetric analysis: "a) The reaction should proceed to completion under easily attainable experimental conditions and takes place at a very fast speed. b) The reaction involved must be simple. c) The reaction must involve a prominent change in colour at the end point of the reaction or a suitable indicator should be available to show the end point of the reaction." While precipitation titrations exist, it is not a *universal* condition that *all* volumetric reactions must produce a precipitate; many use colour change (e.g., acid-base titrations, redox titrations).

**Question 3**
In the process of titration, the solution of accurately known concentration is referred to as the:
A. Titrate
B. Analyte
C. Standard solution
D. Unknown solution

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: As per the text, "The solution of accurately known strength is called the standard solution." The titrant is the reagent of known concentration, which is a standard solution. The titrate is the substance being titrated, usually of unknown concentration.

**Question 4**
The titration involving oxalic acid solution against KMnO₄ solution in acidic medium, where KMnO₄ acts as a self-indicator, is an example of which type of volumetric analysis?
A. Acidimetry and alkalimetry
B. Precipitation titration
C. Oxidation and reduction titration
D. Iodometry

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The text explicitly mentions, "For example, titration of oxalic acid solution against KMnO4 solution in acidic medium. In this titration KMnO4 acts as self indicator and gives pink colour at the end point. The reaction involved is a redox reaction..." This falls under the "Oxidation and reduction titration" category.

**Question 5**
In a volumetric titration, the point at which the indicator gives a clear visual change is called the:
A. Equivalence point
B. Titration error point
C. End point
D. Completion point

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The text defines this term: "The point at which the indicator gives a clear visual change gives the end point of the titration." The equivalence point is when chemically equivalent amounts have reacted, which ideally coincides with the end point.
