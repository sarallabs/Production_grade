Chemical analysis is broadly divided into **qualitative analysis** (identifying substances) and **quantitative analysis** (determining their amounts). **Volumetric analysis** is a quantitative technique that involves measuring the volumes of solutions involved in chemical reactions to find the unknown concentration of a substance.

In volumetric analysis, a solution of accurately known concentration, called a **standard solution**, is reacted with a solution of unknown concentration. This process is known as **titration**. The standard solution is referred to as the **titrant**, and the solution being analyzed is the **titrate**.

The completion of the reaction during titration is detected by a sharp, observable colour change, usually produced by an added substance called an **indicator**. The point at which the indicator changes colour is the **end point**. Ideally, the end point should coincide with the **equivalence point**, where chemically equivalent amounts of reactants have completely reacted.

For a reaction to be suitable for volumetric analysis, it must be fast, proceed to completion under easily attainable conditions, be simple, and have a suitable indicator available to signal the end point. Volumetric analysis is generally rapid, accurate, and less laborious compared to other quantitative methods.

Common types of volumetric analysis include **acidimetry and alkalimetry** (neutralization titrations), oxidation-reduction (redox) titrations, and precipitation titrations.
