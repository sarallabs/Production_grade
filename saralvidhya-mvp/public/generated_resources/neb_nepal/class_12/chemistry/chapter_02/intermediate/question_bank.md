### Short Answer Questions (2–3 marks each)

1.  **Define 'ionization' and 'degree of ionization'. What is the relationship between them?**
    *   **Model Answer:** Ionization is the process by which a substance, usually an electrolyte, dissociates into ions when dissolved in a solvent, typically water. The degree of ionization (α) is the fraction of the total number of molecules of an electrolyte that dissociates into ions in a solution. It is expressed as:
        α = (Number of molecules ionized into ions) / (Total number of molecules dissolved)
        A higher degree of ionization means more ions are formed.

2.  **State Arrhenius theory of ionization. What are cations and anions?**
    *   **Model Answer:** Arrhenius theory states that when an electrolyte is dissolved in water, it dissociates into positively charged ions (cations) and negatively charged ions (anions). Acids are substances that produce H⁺ ions, bases produce OH⁻ ions, and salts produce both cations and anions in solution. Cations are positively charged ions (e.g., Na⁺, K⁺), and anions are negatively charged ions (e.g., Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻).

3.  **Explain why the ionization of an electrolyte increases with dilution.**
    *   **Model Answer:** According to Ostwald's Dilution Law, for weak electrolytes, the degree of ionization (α) is approximately proportional to the square root of dilution (√V). As dilution increases, the concentration of the electrolyte decreases. With fewer solute molecules in a larger volume of solvent, there are more solvent molecules available to interact with and separate the solute molecules, leading to increased ionization.

4.  **What is meant by the term 'electrolytic solution'? Give one example.**
    *   **Model Answer:** An electrolytic solution is a solution that conducts electricity. This is because the substance dissolved in the solvent (usually water) dissociates into ions, which are free to move and carry electric charge when a potential difference is applied.
        Example: An aqueous solution of sodium chloride (NaCl) or an aqueous solution of hydrochloric acid (HCl).

5.  **How does the nature of the solvent affect the degree of ionization of an electrolyte?**
    *   **Model Answer:** The nature of the solvent significantly affects ionization. Solvents with high dielectric constants are better at weakening the electrostatic forces of attraction between ions in the electrolyte. This allows the ions to separate more easily, leading to a higher degree of ionization. Water, with its high dielectric constant, is an excellent solvent for ionization.

6.  **Differentiate between strong and weak electrolytes.**
    *   **Model Answer:** Strong electrolytes are substances that ionize almost completely in solution, meaning a very high percentage of their molecules dissociate into ions. They conduct electricity very well. Weak electrolytes ionize only to a small extent, with a significant portion of their molecules remaining undissociated. They conduct electricity poorly.

7.  **State Ostwald's Dilution Law for a binary electrolyte. What is its limitation?**
    *   **Model Answer:** Ostwald's Dilution Law states that for a weak binary electrolyte, the degree of dissociation (α) is directly proportional to the square root of the dilution (V) at a given temperature, i.e., α ∝ √V, or Kc = α²/((1-α)V).
        Limitation: The law is strictly applicable only to weak electrolytes where the degree of dissociation 'α' is very small, allowing the approximation (1-α) ≈ 1. For strong electrolytes, where 'α' is large, the law does not hold accurately.

8.  **Explain the concept of dynamic equilibrium in the context of ionic dissociation.**
    *   **Model Answer:** In the dissociation of a weak electrolyte, say AB ⇌ A⁺ + B⁻, a dynamic equilibrium is established between the undissociated molecules of AB and the ions A⁺ and B⁻. This means that the rate at which AB dissociates into ions is equal to the rate at which the ions A⁺ and B⁻ recombine to form AB. The equilibrium is dynamic because both forward and reverse processes are continuously occurring.

9.  **What is the role of temperature in the ionization of an electrolyte?**
    *   **Model Answer:** The ionization of an electrolyte generally increases with an increase in temperature. Higher temperatures provide more kinetic energy to the molecules, leading to increased molecular motion. This increased motion helps to overcome the interionic forces of attraction between ions, promoting further dissociation and thus increasing the degree of ionization.

10. **Why is it important for a solution to be electrically neutral, even though it contains ions?**
    *   **Model Answer:** An electrolytic solution is electrically neutral because the total positive charge carried by all the cations is always equal to the total negative charge carried by all the anions. While ions are present and mobile, their charges balance each other out, resulting in an overall neutral solution.

### Long Answer Questions (5 marks each)

1.  **Discuss the postulates of Arrhenius theory of ionization. Explain how this theory accounts for the electrical conductivity of solutions.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Introduction:** Briefly introduce Arrhenius and his theory.
        *   **Postulates:**
            *   Electrolytes dissociate into ions (cations and anions) in polar solvents like water.
            *   Solid electrolytes are already composed of ions.
            *   Dynamic equilibrium exists between ions and undissociated molecules.
            *   Total positive charge = Total negative charge (electrical neutrality).
            *   Movement of ions towards electrodes causes electrical conductivity.
            *   Properties of the solution are due to ions.
            *   Ions behave osmotically like molecules.
        *   **Electrical Conductivity:** Explain that the presence of mobile ions in an electrolytic solution allows for the flow of electric current when a potential difference is applied. Ions move towards oppositely charged electrodes (cations to cathode, anions to anode), thus carrying the charge and enabling conduction. Substances that do not ionize will not conduct electricity.

2.  **Derive Ostwald's Dilution Law for a binary electrolyte. Explain its significance for weak electrolytes.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Introduction:** State that Ostwald's Dilution Law is an application of the law of mass action to ionic equilibrium.
        *   **Derivation:**
            *   Consider a binary electrolyte AB ⇌ A⁺ + B⁻.
            *   Define initial state (1 mole AB, 0 ions).
            *   Define equilibrium state (1-α moles AB, α moles A⁺, α moles B⁻ in volume V).
            *   Calculate equilibrium concentrations: [AB] = (1-α)/V, [A⁺] = α/V, [B⁻] = α/V.
            *   Apply the law of mass action: Kc = ([A⁺][B⁻]) / [AB].
            *   Substitute concentrations and simplify to get Kc = α² / ((1-α)V).
        *   **Significance for Weak Electrolytes:**
            *   For weak electrolytes, α is very small, so (1-α) ≈ 1.
            *   The law simplifies to Kc = α²/V or α = √(Kc × V).
            *   This shows that α ∝ √V, meaning the degree of dissociation increases with dilution.

3.  **What factors affect the degree of ionization of an electrolyte? Discuss any two in detail.**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Introduction:** List the factors affecting ionization (nature of electrolyte, nature of solvent, concentration, temperature).
        *   **Detailed Discussion (Choose any two):**
            *   **Nature of Solvent:** Explain the role of dielectric constant in weakening interionic forces and facilitating dissociation. Give examples of solvents with high and low dielectric constants.
            *   **Concentration:** Explain the inverse relationship. Higher concentration means more inter-molecular interactions, hindering dissociation. Dilution provides more space and solvent molecules, promoting ionization (referencing Ostwald's Law for weak electrolytes).
            *   **Nature of Electrolyte:** Differentiate between strong and weak electrolytes and how their inherent nature leads to different extents of ionization.
            *   **Temperature:** Explain how increased kinetic energy at higher temperatures overcomes interionic forces, leading to increased ionization.

4.  **Explain the concept of ionization constant (Kc). How is it related to Ostwald's Dilution Law?**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Definition of Ionization Constant (Kc):** Define Kc as the equilibrium constant for the ionization process. For a reversible reaction A ⇌ B + C, Kc = ([B][C]) / [A]. It represents the ratio of products of concentrations of ions to the concentration of undissociated molecules at equilibrium.
        *   **Relationship with Ostwald's Dilution Law:**
            *   Start with the equilibrium AB ⇌ A⁺ + B⁻.
            *   State the expression for Kc: Kc = ([A⁺][B⁻]) / [AB].
            *   Show how substituting the equilibrium concentrations from Ostwald's Law (derived from initial moles and degree of dissociation) into the Kc expression leads to the Ostwald's equation: Kc = α² / ((1-α)V).
        *   **Significance:** Kc is a characteristic constant for a given electrolyte at a specific temperature. It helps determine the extent of ionization.

5.  **Discuss the limitations of Arrhenius theory of ionization. How was it extended by others (briefly)?**
    *   **Outline Answer:**
        *   **Limitations:**
            *   Only applicable to aqueous solutions or polar solvents.
            *   Does not explain the acidic and basic properties of substances that do not contain hydrogen or hydroxyl ions (e.g., NH₃ as a base, BF₃ as an acid).
            *   The concepts of "acid" and "base" are defined solely by the production of H⁺ and OH⁻ ions, lacking broader context.
            *   Doesn't explain the ionization of non-electrolytes like gases in certain reactions.
        *   **Extensions (Briefly):** Mention the Brønsted-Lowry theory (proton donor/acceptor) and Lewis theory (electron pair acceptor/donor) as broader and more comprehensive theories explaining acidic and basic behaviour, overcoming some of Arrhenius's limitations.
