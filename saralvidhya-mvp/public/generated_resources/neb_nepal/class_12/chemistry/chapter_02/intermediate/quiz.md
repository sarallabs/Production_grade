Question 1
According to Arrhenius theory, when an electrolyte dissolves in a polar solvent like water, it yields:
A. Neutral atoms and molecules
B. Electrically neutral particles only
C. Electrically charged particles called ions
D. Undissociated polar molecules

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The Arrhenius theory states that electrolytes dissociate in water to form positively charged cations and negatively charged anions, which are electrically charged particles.

Question 2
Which of the following factors will *decrease* the degree of ionization of a weak electrolyte in a solution?
A. Increasing the temperature
B. Increasing the concentration of the solution
C. Dissolving it in a solvent with a higher dielectric constant
D. Adding more solvent (dilution)

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The degree of ionization is inversely proportional to the concentration of the solution. As concentration increases, the solution becomes more crowded with solute particles, leading to less ionization. Dilution, higher temperature, and solvents with higher dielectric constants generally increase ionization.

Question 3
Ostwald's Dilution Law is an application of the law of mass action to:
A. Chemical equilibrium in gaseous reactions
B. Ionic equilibrium in solutions
C. Solid-state reactions
D. Equilibrium involving colloids

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Ostwald's Dilution Law specifically deals with the equilibrium between ionized and unionized molecules of a weak electrolyte in a solution, which is a type of ionic equilibrium.

Question 4
For a weak binary electrolyte AB, undergoing ionization as AB ⇌ A⁺ + B⁻, if the degree of ionization is 'α' and the volume of the solution is 'V', what is the correct expression for the ionization constant (Kc) according to Ostwald's Dilution Law, assuming 'α' is small?
A. Kc = α² / V
B. Kc = α / V
C. Kc = α² / (1 - α)V
D. Kc = V / α²

Correct Answer: A
Explanation: For weak electrolytes where 'α' is very small, Ostwald's law simplifies from Kc = α² / [(1 - α)V] to Kc = α² / V, as (1 - α) is approximately equal to 1.

Question 5
A substance that allows electric current to pass through its solution is called an:
A. Non-electrolyte
B. Electrolyte
C. Strong acid
D. Weak base

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Electrolytes are substances whose aqueous solutions conduct electricity because they dissociate into ions, allowing for the movement of charge. Non-electrolytes do not ionize and hence do not conduct electricity.
