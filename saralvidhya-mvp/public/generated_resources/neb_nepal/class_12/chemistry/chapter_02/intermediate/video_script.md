**Narration:**

For instance, dilution generally increases ionization. This relationship is elegantly described by Ostwald's Dilution Law, which states that for weak electrolytes, the degree of dissociation, alpha, is proportional to the square root of dilution (α ∝ √V)."

### Scene 5

**Duration:** 40 seconds

**Visual Prompt:**
**(Equation Animation & Explanation):**
*   **Frame 1:** Display the equilibrium reaction: `AB ⇌ A⁺ + B⁻`.
*   **Frame 2:** Show the setup of the equilibrium:
    `Initial: 1 0 0`
    `At equilibrium: (1-α) α α`
*   **Frame 3:** Introduce the equilibrium constant expression for Ostwald's Law:
    `Kc = [A⁺][B⁻] / [AB]`
    Substitute the equilibrium concentrations to show the derivation visually:
    `Kc = (α/V) * (α/V) / ((1-α)/V)`
    Simplify to `Kc = α² / [(1-α)V]`
    Highlight `Kc` as the ionization constant.
*   **Frame 4:** For weak electrolytes, show the approximation: `(1-α) ≈ 1`, leading to `Kc = α²/V`.
*   **Overlay Text:** "Ostwald's Dilution Law Derivation", "Ionization Constant (Kc)"

**Narration:**
"Ostwald's Dilution Law applies the law of mass action to this ionic equilibrium. For a binary electrolyte AB, at equilibrium, if 'α' is the degree of dissociation and 'V' is the volume, we derive the ionization constant, Kc. For weak electrolytes, where alpha is very small, the equation simplifies beautifully to Kc equals alpha squared divided by V, highlighting the direct relationship between dissociation and dilution."
