This chapter introduces **ionic equilibrium**, which studies the dynamic equilibrium between ions and undissociated molecules in aqueous solutions. These solutions, called electrolytic solutions, conduct electricity due to the presence of ions.

The **Arrhenius theory of ionization** explains that when electrolytes (acids, bases, salts) dissolve in water, they dissociate into positively charged **cations** and negatively charged **anions**. An equilibrium is established, represented by an **ionization constant (K)**. The total positive and negative charges in a solution are always equal, making it electrically neutral.

The **degree of ionization (α)** is the fraction of molecules that ionize. This degree is influenced by:
*   **Nature of electrolyte:** Strong electrolytes ionize almost completely, while weak ones ionize partially.
*   **Nature of solvent:** Solvents with higher dielectric constants weaken attractive forces between ions, promoting ionization.
*   **Concentration:** Ionization increases with dilution (lower concentration).
*   **Temperature:** Higher temperatures generally increase ionization.
