### Scene 3

**Duration:** 30 seconds

**Visual Prompt:**
**(Equation board animation):** Display $\alpha = \frac{\text{Number of ionized molecules}}{\text{Total dissolved molecules}}$. Show weak electrolyte with $\alpha \ll 1$ vs strong electrolyte with $\alpha \approx 1$. Overlay: "$K_c$ for $\text{AB} \rightleftharpoons \text{A}^+ + \text{B}^-$" and $K_c = \frac{[\text{A}^+][\text{B}^-]}{[\text{AB}]}$.

**Narration:**
"The **degree of ionization**, $\alpha$, is the fraction of solute molecules that exist as ions at equilibrium. The **ionization constant** $K_c$ quantifies how far ionization proceeds at a given temperature—larger $K_c$ means a stronger tendency to ionize for that equilibrium setup. Weak electrolytes sit where **$\alpha$ is materially below 1**; strong electrolytes behave as **almost fully ionized** under typical introductory treatments."

### Scene 5

**Duration:** 40 seconds

**Visual Prompt:**
**(Derivation animation):** ICE-style layout for $\text{AB} \rightleftharpoons \text{A}^+ + \text{B}^-$ with molarity $c$: equilibrium concentrations $(1-\alpha)c$, $\alpha c$, $\alpha c$. Derive $K_c = \frac{\alpha^2 c}{1-\alpha}$. Show approximation $1-\alpha \approx 1$ for very weak electrolytes $\Rightarrow K_c \approx \alpha^2 c$ and $\alpha \approx \sqrt{K_c/c}$, hence $\alpha \propto \sqrt{V}$ with $V \propto 1/c$. Red stamp: "**Not for strong electrolytes** — assumption breaks."

**Narration:**
"**Ostwald's dilution law** applies the law of mass action to weak binary ionization: you should be able to move from $K_c = \frac{\alpha^2 c}{1-\alpha}$ to the standard weak-electrolyte approximation $K_c \approx \alpha^2 c$, giving $\alpha \approx \sqrt{K_c/c}$ and the proportionalities $\alpha \propto \sqrt{V}$ and $\alpha \propto 1/\sqrt{c}$. The crucial expert checkpoint is the assumption **$\alpha \ll 1$**—which fails for **strong electrolytes**, where conductivity trends reflect mobility and interionic effects more than incomplete ionization."
