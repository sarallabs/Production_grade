Indeed! For the water autoionization equilibrium, H2O ⇌ H+ + OH-, we have an equilibrium constant. Since water is barely ionized, the concentration of un-dissociated water molecules is essentially constant. That's super useful! So, a pH of 7 is neutral. Below 7 is acidic, and above 7 is basic. Strong acids have low pH values, like 0-2, and strong bases have high pH values, like 12-14.

Let's move on to dissociation equilibria of weak electrolytes. We know weak acids and bases only partially ionize.

Right. For a weak acid, HA, dissociating in water, we can write an acid dissociation constant, Ka. It's given by Ka = [H+][A-]/[HA]. The larger the Ka, the stronger the acid.

So, we can compare the strength of two weak acids by looking at the square root of the ratio of their Ka values.

Exactly. The same logic applies to weak bases. For a weak base, BOH, we have a base dissociation constant, Kb = [B+][OH-]/[BOH]. Again, a larger Kb means a stronger base.

So, water breaks apart (hydro-lysis) the salt components.

Precisely. We can categorize salts based on the strengths of the acid and base they come from:

* Salts of strong acid and strong base (like NaCl): These don't hydrolyze. Their ions (Na+, Cl-) don't react with water to a significant extent, so the solution remains neutral.

* Salts of weak acid and strong base (like sodium acetate, CH3COONa): The anion (CH3COO-) reacts with H+ from water to form weak acetic acid. This removes H+ ions, so the OH- concentration increases, making the solution alkaline. This is anionic hydrolysis.

The cation (NH4+) reacts with OH- from water to form weak ammonium hydroxide. This removes OH- ions, so the H+ concentration increases, making the solution acidic. This is cationic hydrolysis.

And finally, salts of weak acid and weak base (like ammonium acetate, CH3COONH4)?

Both the cation and anion hydrolyze. The resulting solution's pH depends on the relative strengths of the weak acid and weak base formed. It could be slightly acidic, slightly alkaline, or even neutral if they're equally strong.
