### Scene 1: Futures vs. Options (The Core Difference)

**Visual Prompt:**
A high-tech digital laboratory setup. In the center, a large virtual scale balances two glowing blocks representing a buyer and a seller. The scale is perfectly balanced, labeled "Futures Contract - Symmetrical Risk". The scale then morphs into an asymmetrical layout where one side (the option buyer) has a protective shield that blocks falling red arrows, while the other side (the option seller) has an open field exposed to the elements. Text pops up: "Options - Asymmetrical Risk". The camera zooms in on the word "Right vs. Obligation".

**Narration:**
In derivatives, futures impose symmetric obligations on buyers and sellers; standard options give the buyer a right but not an obligation, so payoffs are asymmetric and the premium is paid upfront.

### Scene 2: Call Options - The Right to Buy

**Visual Prompt:**
A stock chart showing a green candle pattern trending upward. A digital hand purchases a document labeled "Call Option (Right to Buy)". A horizontal line labeled "Strike Price: ₹100" is established on the chart. As the stock price rises above this line to ₹120, a glowing green bar fills the space between ₹100 and ₹120, representing the payoff. A smaller red block at the bottom represents the premium paid.

**Narration:**
A call option is the right to buy an asset at a set strike price. When you are bullish on a stock, you buy a call. If the stock price rises above your strike price, you exercise your right to buy the stock cheap, pocketing the difference.

### Scene 3: Put Options - The Right to Sell

**Visual Prompt:**
A stock chart showing a red candle pattern trending downward. A digital hand purchases a document labeled "Put Option (Right to Sell)". A horizontal line labeled "Strike Price: ₹100" is established. As the stock price crashes down to ₹80, a bright green arrow extends downward from the ₹100 line to the ₹80 stock price, showing the profit generated. A protective safety net is shown below the seller's assets.

**Narration:**
A put option is the right to sell an asset at a set strike price. If you expect a stock price to fall, you buy a put. If the price crashes, the put option acts as a safety net, allowing you to sell at the higher strike price and secure your profits.

### Scene 4: The Option Premium and Breakeven

**Visual Prompt:**
A digital wallet transfers a small stack of gold coins labeled "Option Premium" to another wallet. A horizontal timeline shows the stock price passing the Strike Price of ₹100, then passing another line at ₹110 labeled "Breakeven Price = Strike Price + Premium". An animation shows that below ₹110, the buyer is still recouping their premium cost. Above ₹110, the net profit starts to accumulate.

**Narration:**
To acquire this right, the option buyer pays a non-refundable upfront fee called the premium. Because of this cost, you only break even on a call option when the stock price rises above the strike price plus the premium. For a put option, the breakeven is the strike price minus the premium.

### Scene 5: Understanding Moneyness

**Visual Prompt:**
A vertical thermometer or gauge divided into three colored zones. For a Call option: the top zone is bright green and labeled "In-the-Money (ITM) - Spot > Strike"; the middle pointer is yellow and labeled "At-the-Money (ATM) - Spot = Strike"; the bottom zone is light blue and labeled "Out-of-the-Money (OTM) - Spot < Strike". The screen splits to show the inverse zones for a Put option.

**Narration:**
Moneyness describes the relationship between the stock price and the strike price. An option is In-the-Money when exercising it immediately creates a positive cash flow. It is At-the-Money when the stock price equals the strike price, and Out-of-the-Money when exercising it would make no financial sense.

### Scene 6: Intrinsic Value vs. Time Value

**Visual Prompt:**
A 3D glass bar chart showing a total option premium of ₹35. The bottom section of the bar is solid blue, representing "Intrinsic Value: ₹20". The top section is a translucent orange cloud, representing "Time Value: ₹15". An hourglass appears next to the chart. As sand falls, the orange cloud slowly evaporates, shrinking to zero as the hourglass runs out. The solid blue bar remains unchanged.

**Narration:**
An option's premium has two parts: intrinsic value, which is the option's value if exercised immediately, and time value, which represents the probability of favorable price movements before expiry. As the expiration date approaches, time value decays to zero, a concept known as time decay.

### Scene 7: The Option Greeks Dashboard

**Visual Prompt:**
A high-tech cockpit dashboard with five circular dials, each glowing with neon lights. 
- Dial 1: "Delta" moving between 0 and +1.
- Dial 2: "Gamma" showing the acceleration of Delta.
- Dial 3: "Theta" showing a clock face ticking away, labeled "Time Decay".
- Dial 4: "Vega" reacting to a soundwave pattern labeled "Volatility".
- Dial 5: "Rho" shifting slightly with percentage signs representing interest rates.

**Narration:**
Options traders manage their positions using the Option Greeks. Delta measures sensitivity to stock price changes. Gamma tracks the stability of Delta. Theta measures the daily impact of time decay. Vega monitors sensitivity to changes in volatility, and Rho tracks the influence of interest rates.
