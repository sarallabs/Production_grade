Question 1
Which of the following statements correctly describes the position of a Put option buyer?
A. The buyer has the obligation to buy the underlying asset at the strike price.
B. The buyer has the right to buy the underlying asset at the strike price.
C. The buyer has the obligation to sell the underlying asset at the strike price.
D. The buyer has the right to sell the underlying asset at the strike price.

Correct Answer: D
Explanation: A put option gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell the underlying asset at the pre-determined strike price. The buyer pays a premium for this right.

Question 2
If the current market price of a stock is ₹435, and a Call option on the stock has a strike price of ₹410 and a premium of ₹35, what is the intrinsic value and the time value of the option?
A. Intrinsic Value = ₹25, Time Value = ₹10
B. Intrinsic Value = ₹10, Time Value = ₹25
C. Intrinsic Value = ₹35, Time Value = ₹0
D. Intrinsic Value = ₹0, Time Value = ₹35

Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Intrinsic Value of a Call = max(0, Spot Price - Strike Price) = max(0, 435 - 410) = ₹25. The Time Value = Premium - Intrinsic Value = ₹35 - ₹25 = ₹10.

Question 3
Which Option Greek measures the sensitivity of the option premium to the passage of time (time decay)?
A. Delta
B. Gamma
C. Theta
D. Vega

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Theta measures the rate at which the option premium decreases as time to expiration decreases by one day, assuming all other variables remain constant.

Question 4
An increase in the volatility of the underlying asset's price has what impact on Call and Put option premiums?
A. Call premiums increase; Put premiums decrease.
B. Both Call and Put premiums increase.
C. Call premiums decrease; Put premiums increase.
D. Both Call and Put premiums decrease.

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Since options have limited downside risk (capped at the premium paid) and unlimited or high upside risk, an increase in volatility increases the probability of extreme price movements in either direction, making both call and put options more valuable.

Question 5
If an investor buys a Put option with a strike price of ₹500 for a premium of ₹45, what is the breakeven price of this position at expiration?
A. ₹545
B. ₹500
C. ₹455
D. ₹45

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The breakeven price for a Put option buyer (Long Put) is calculated as Strike Price - Premium Paid = ₹500 - ₹45 = ₹455. At this price, the buyer exercises the option to sell at ₹500, yielding a payoff of ₹45, which exactly offsets the ₹45 premium paid.
