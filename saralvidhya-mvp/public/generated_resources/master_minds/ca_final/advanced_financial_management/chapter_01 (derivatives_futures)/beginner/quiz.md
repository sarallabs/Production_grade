Question 1
Which of the following represents a key difference between Forward and Futures contracts?
A. Forward contracts are standardized, while futures contracts are customized.
B. Futures contracts are traded on an organized exchange, whereas forward contracts are traded Over-the-Counter (OTC).
C. Forward contracts require daily marking-to-market, while futures contracts are settled only at maturity.
D. Futures contracts carry significantly higher counterparty risk than forward contracts.

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Forward contracts are customized agreements negotiated privately between two parties (OTC), which exposes them to counterparty default risk. Futures contracts are standardized agreements traded on organized stock exchanges and are cleared through a clearing house that guarantees settlement, reducing default risk to near zero.

Question 2
A trader enters into a long stock futures contract at ₹1,500. The lot size is 100 shares. The broker requires an initial margin of 12% and a maintenance margin of 80% of the initial margin. What is the Maintenance Margin amount for this position?
A. ₹18,000
B. ₹14,400
C. ₹12,000
D. ₹15,000

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: 
First, calculate the total contract value:
$$\text{Contract Value} = \text{Futures Price} \times \text{Lot Size} = 1,500 \times 100 = ₹1,50,000$$
Second, calculate the Initial Margin:
$$\text{Initial Margin} = 12\% \text{ of } 1,50,000 = ₹18,000$$
Third, calculate the Maintenance Margin:
$$\text{Maintenance Margin} = 80\% \text{ of } 18,000 = ₹14,400$$

Question 3
If the spot price of Stock XYZ is ₹600, the risk-free interest rate is 12% per annum, and a 2-month futures contract is available, what is the theoretical futures price assuming no dividends are paid before expiry? (Use Simple Interest).
A. ₹612
B. ₹606
C. ₹672
D. ₹610

Correct Answer: A
Explanation: 
Using the Cost of Carry formula with simple interest:
$$F = S \times \left(1 + \frac{r \times T}{1200}\right)$$
Given $S = 600$, $r = 12\%$, and $T = 2 \text{ months}$:
$$F = 600 \times \left(1 + \frac{12 \times 2}{1200}\right)$$
$$F = 600 \times \left(1 + \frac{24}{1200}\right)$$
$$F = 600 \times (1 + 0.02) = 600 \times 1.02 = ₹612$$

Question 4
Which of the following describes the market condition known as 'Contango'?
A. The futures price is equal to the spot price ($F = S$).
B. The futures price is lower than the spot price ($F < S$).
C. The futures price is higher than the spot price ($F > S$).
D. The futures price is independent of the spot price.

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Contango is a market condition where the futures price of an asset trades at a premium over its current spot price ($F > S$). This premium represents the positive cost of carry (such as storage costs, financing costs, or interest rates) required to hold the asset until maturity.

Question 5
An investor has a long position in a futures contract with a lot size of 200 shares, entered at a futures price of ₹250. At the end of Day 1, the closing futures price is ₹242. If the initial margin deposited was ₹8,000, what is the closing balance in the margin account after the daily Mark-to-Market (MTM) adjustment?
A. ₹6,400
B. ₹9,600
C. ₹8,000
D. ₹7,600

Correct Answer: A
Explanation: 
For a long position (buyer), the daily Mark-to-Market (MTM) profit or loss is calculated as:
$$\text{MTM Profit/Loss} = (\text{Closing Price} - \text{Entry Price}) \times \text{Lot Size}$$
$$\text{MTM Profit/Loss} = (242 - 250) \times 200 = -8 \times 200 = -₹1,600 \text{ (Loss)}$$
The ending margin account balance is updated by deducting the MTM loss:
$$\text{Closing Balance} = \text{Initial Margin} - \text{MTM Loss} = 8,000 - 1,600 = ₹6,400$$
