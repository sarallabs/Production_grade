### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** Futures contracts benefit from high secondary market liquidity, while forward contracts are generally illiquid due to their customized, non-transferable nature.
- **Formula/Rule:** Liquidity: Forwards (Less liquid) vs. Futures (More liquid).
- **Application Context:** Traded on Indian exchanges (NSE/BSE/MCX) with standard contract specifications (Nifty lot size of 25, Bank Nifty lot size of 15).

### 🔍 Detailed View
Liquidity refers to the ability to transact quickly with minimal price impact. Futures contracts are highly fungible, allowing traders to close a position by executing an equal and opposite trade (e.g., buying 1 lot of Nifty futures to offset a short position of 1 lot). This keeps bid-ask spreads very tight. Forwards cannot be easily offset because there is no public secondary market. To exit, a party must write a canceling contract or pay a settlement fee to the counterparty.
