### 🔍 Detailed View

#### 🔸 ETF Creation Process
The creation process does not involve cash transactions with the fund:
1. **Index Composition:** The AP (typically a large institutional broker) checks the index weightings.
2. **Stock Purchasing:** The AP buys the underlying shares in the exact weightings on the stock exchange.
3. **In-Kind Swap:** The AP delivers this basket of shares to the ETF Custodian.
4. **Unit Issuance:** The ETF trust issues "Creation Units" (large blocks of ETF units, e.g., 50,000 units) to the AP.
5. **Retail Listing:** The AP sells these individual ETF units to retail investors on the NSE/BSE.

#### 🔸 ETF Custodian
The custodian holds the shares/bonds delivered by the APs. They ensure that for every ETF unit trading on the exchange, there is a corresponding asset held in the custody account. They also handle corporate actions (like stock splits or mergers) and collect dividends, which are either reinvested or paid out to ETF holders.

#### 🔸 ETF vs. Mutual Fund (Pros)
**Intraday Trading:** Investors can buy/sell at fluctuating market prices throughout the day.
**Lower Expenses:** Passive ETFs do not pay distributor commissions or active management fees. Expense ratios are often $<0.20\%$.
**Trading Flexibility:** Supports limit orders, stop-losses, and short-selling.

#### 🔸 ETF vs. Mutual Fund (Cons)
**Bid-Ask Spread:** If an ETF has low trading volume, the selling price (bid) might be significantly lower than the buying price (ask), creating an implicit cost.
**Premium/Discount to NAV:** In volatile markets, the market price of an ETF on the exchange can deviate from its actual NAV.
**Demat Charges:** Annual maintenance charges (AMC) for demat accounts and transaction taxes (STT) add to the cost.
