# Premium

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Options involve an upfront premium payment that represents the market price of the option's time and volatility values. Futures require no premium, only margin collateral.
**Key Takeaway:** Option premium is a sunk cost for buyers; future margin is a refundable deposit.
**Analogy:** A non-refundable airline ticket booking fee (Option Premium) vs. a refundable security deposit for renting a car (Future Margin).
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** The option premium ($C$ or $P$) is the equilibrium price of the contract determined by the market. Futures do not require premium payments because the contract is initially entered at a fair price where its present value is zero.
**Worked Example:** To buy a stock option, you pay the premium (e.g., ₹8,000) directly to the seller's account. This money cannot be recovered unless you sell the option. To trade a stock future, you deposit ₹1,50,000 in margin collateral with the exchange, which is returned when you close the position.
