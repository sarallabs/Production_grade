### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

Open-ended funds have no cap on the number of units. The AMC must manage daily inflows and outflows. To manage redemption risk, managers must match portfolio liquidity with potential outflows. This requires maintaining a portion of assets in cash, cash equivalents (TREPS), or highly liquid blue-chip stocks. Large redemptions during panic periods can force the liquidation of liquid assets first, increasing the concentration of illiquid assets in the remaining portfolio.

In deep dive mode, we explore the institutional application and regulatory background of this concept. Under SEBI rules, Indian derivatives are subject to standard margins (SPAN + Exposure) and are tightly regulated to ensure systemic safety.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

**Macaulay Duration ($D_{mac}$):** The weighted average time to receive all cash flows from a bond:
$$D_{mac} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^n \frac{t \cdot C_t}{(1+y)^t}}{\text{Bond Price}}$$
Where $C_t$ is the cash flow at time $t$, and $y$ is the yield to maturity.
**Modified Duration ($D_{mod}$):** Measures price sensitivity to interest rate changes:
$$D_{mod} = \frac{D_{mac}}{1 + \frac{y}{m}}$$
$$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D_{mod} \cdot \Delta y$$
**Tax Regulation (Section 50AA - April 2023):** Debt mutual funds (with $\le 35\%$ equity exposure) no longer receive indexation benefits or long-term capital gains tax rates (formerly 20% with indexation). All gains, regardless of holding period, are classified as short-term capital gains and taxed at the investor's marginal slab rate.

Gilt funds focus on maturity profiles ranging from 3 to 15+ years. Because they have no credit risk, their volatility is purely driven by interest rate movements (duration risk). In deep dive mode, we explore the institutional application and regulatory background of this concept. Under SEBI rules, Indian derivatives are subject to standard margins (SPAN + Exposure) and are tightly regulated to ensure systemic safety.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

BAFs use mathematical models to adjust net equity exposure (typically between 30% and 80%). The models analyze indicators like:
1. **Historical P/E Ratio:** Reducing equity exposure when P/E exceeds historical standard deviations.
2. **P/B Ratio:** Using book value to determine asset bubble conditions.
3. **Yield-Gap Ratio:** Comparing equity earnings yield ($1/\text{PE}$) to 10-year G-Sec yield:
$$\text{Yield Gap} = \text{10Y G-Sec Yield} - \frac{1}{\text{PE}}$$
To maintain the tax status of an equity fund (requiring $\ge 65\%$ gross equity exposure), BAFs use equity derivatives (arbitrage). The net asset allocation is structured as:
$$\text{Total Assets} = \text{Net Long Equity} + \text{Arbitrage (Equity Futures Shorts)} + \text{Debt Securities}$$
Where $\text{Net Long Equity} + \text{Arbitrage} \ge 65\%$ to qualify for equity taxation, while actual equity market exposure is limited to $\text{Net Long Equity}$.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

Let the active return at day $t$ be $R_{A,t} = R_{P,t} - R_{B,t}$.
The Tracking Error (TE) is the annualized standard deviation of $R_{A,t}$:
$$\text{TE} = \sigma(R_A) \times \sqrt{252}$$
$$\text{TE} = \sqrt{252 \times \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{t=1}^N (R_{A,t} - \bar{R}_A)^2}$$
Where $\bar{R}_A$ is the mean active return over the period. Minimizing TE requires optimizing transaction execution, managing cash flows efficiently, and utilizing corporate actions (like dividends) immediately.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

The fund buys stock $S$ in the spot market and simultaneously sells futures contract $F$ at a premium. The locked-in return is the basis:
$$\text{Basis} = F_0 - S_0$$
At expiry, the futures price converges to the spot price ($F_T = S_T$). The return is earned as the basis converges to zero. Because the fund holds physical shares hedged with derivatives, its gross equity exposure exceeds 65%, qualifying it for equity taxation (lower tax rate) despite having the risk profile of a debt liquid fund.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

**REITs and InvITs Structure:**
**SPV Structure:** The trust holds shares in Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) that own income-generating real estate or infrastructure.
**Mandatory Distribution:** Under SEBI rules, REITs/InvITs must distribute at least 90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unit-holders as dividends or interest.
**Taxation:** The distribution consists of:
1. **Interest Income:** Taxable at the investor's slab rate.
2. **Dividend Income:** Taxable if the SPV opted for lower tax regime, otherwise exempt.
3. **Repayment of Debt (Amortization):** Subject to complex tax tracking.
FOFs wrapper: FOFs holding REITs/InvITs are taxed as debt funds, simplifying the tax administration for retail investors.
