# Sub-Topic 3.3.1: ETF Mechanics

> **Reference:** Page 5

---

### 📋 Summary View

#### 🔸 ETF Creation Process
- **Concept:** The process of creating ETFs where large financial institutions (Authorized Participants) exchange blocks of actual shares for ETF units.
- **Analogy:** A dairy cooperative: farmers bring actual milk (underlying shares) to the dairy plant, and the dairy issues packets of branded milk (ETF units) in return.
- **Key Takeaway:** ETFs are created in bulk through institutional swap deals, not individual cash purchases.

#### 🔸 ETF Custodian
- **Concept:** An independent financial institution that holds and safeguards the actual stocks that back the ETF units.
- **Analogy:** A bank locker keeper who holds the gold bars, while you carry a certificate saying you own the gold.
- **Key Takeaway:** The custodian ensures the ETF units are backed by real assets.

#### 🔸 ETF vs. Mutual Fund (Pros)
- **Concept:** ETFs trade live on stock exchanges just like individual shares, offering real-time pricing and lower expenses.
- **Analogy:** Buying gold live from a jewelry market with live price updates versus placing an order at a fixed yesterday's price.
- **Key Takeaway:** Faster trading and cheaper fees than traditional mutual funds.

#### 🔸 ETF vs. Mutual Fund (Cons)
- **Concept:** ETFs require a demat and trading account, brokerage fees, and can suffer from low liquidity (hard to sell).
- **Analogy:** Buying a car is cheaper than renting daily, but you must pay for a garage, insurance, and broker commission.
- **Key Takeaway:** Higher setup requirements and transaction commissions can eat into savings.

---

### 🔍 Detailed View

#### 🔸 ETF Creation Process
Unlike mutual funds, where investors send cash directly to the AMC, ETFs rely on Authorized Participants (APs). The AP buys the shares of all companies in the index from the open market and delivers them to the ETF trust. In return, the trust issues large blocks of ETF units (called Creation Units) to the AP. The AP then sells these units to retail investors on the stock exchange.

#### 🔸 ETF Custodian
The Custodian holds physical custody of the shares, bonds, or gold representing the ETF. They make sure the ETF does not issue units without having the actual underlying assets in their vaults. They also collect dividends from the companies and transfer them to the ETF scheme.

#### 🔸 ETF vs. Mutual Fund (Pros)
**Real-Time Trading:** You can buy or sell ETFs anytime during market hours (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM) at fluctuating prices. Mutual funds only buy/sell at the end of the day NAV.
**Lower Cost:** ETFs have lower management fees because they are passive.
**No Minimum Hold:** No exit loads or lock-in periods for standard ETFs.

#### 🔸 ETF vs. Mutual Fund (Cons)
**Brokerage Costs:** You must pay commission/brokerage to your broker every time you buy or sell an ETF.
**Account Requirement:** You cannot buy ETFs without opening a Demat and Trading account.
**Liquidity Risk:** If there are no buyers on the stock exchange when you want to sell, you might be forced to sell your ETF units at a heavy loss (below actual NAV).
