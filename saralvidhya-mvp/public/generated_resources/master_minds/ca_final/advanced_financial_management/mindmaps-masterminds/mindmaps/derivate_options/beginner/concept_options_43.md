### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** For a Put Buyer, the breakeven point is the strike price minus the premium paid. The stock must fall below this point for you to make a net profit.
- **Analogy:** If you spend ₹100 on shipping to return a defective ₹1,000 item, your net recovery is only ₹900.
- **Key Takeaway:** The stock must crash enough to cover the cost of the insurance premium.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** For a Put option, the breakeven point is calculated by subtracting the premium from the strike price. If the stock falls below this level, the put buyer is in net profit.
**Worked Example:** You buy a SBI ₹800 Put option for a ₹40 premium.
Your Breakeven Price is ₹800 - ₹40 = ₹760.
If SBI share falls to ₹780, you exercise the option to sell at ₹800 (making ₹20 payoff), but you still lose ₹20 net because of the ₹40 premium.
If SBI falls to ₹700, your payoff is ₹100, giving you a net profit of ₹60.
