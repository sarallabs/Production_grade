# Volatility Must Be Estimated

> **Sub-Topic 2.5.2:** Limitations | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Advanced

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Volatility must be estimated, not observed.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Volatility cannot be directly observed in the market; it must be estimated from historical data or implied from market prices, leading to estimation errors.
**Key Takeaway:** Option pricing is highly sensitive to an unobservable parameter.
**Analogy:** Trying to price a second-hand car based on a guess of how well the previous owner maintained the engine.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** While stock price, strike, interest rate, and time are known facts, volatility is a forecast. If a trader estimates historical volatility is 20% but future volatility turns out to be 35%, their options will be mispriced.
**Worked Example:** A trader uses a 15-day historical volatility average to price a Nifty option. Suddenly, the RBI announces an unscheduled interest rate meeting. Volatility spikes, making the trader's pricing model obsolete.
