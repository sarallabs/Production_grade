# Obligation

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.3:** Futures vs. Options | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1
> **Content:** Obligation: Futures (Both parties obligated) vs. Options (Buyer has right, no obligation).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** In a Futures contract, both the buyer and seller are obligated (forced) to complete the deal. In Options, only the buyer has a choice, while the seller is obligated.
- **Analogy:** Futures is like a signed court marriage (both must comply); Options is like an engagement where the bride/groom can back out but the caterer cannot.
- **Key Takeaway:** Futures = Mutual obligation. Options = Buyer has choice, seller has obligation.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Futures leave no room for change of mind. If you buy Nifty futures and the market falls, you must absorb the losses till the end. In options, if the market falls, as a call buyer, you can simply say "I don't want to buy" and walk away.
**Worked Example:** If you buy Nifty futures at ₹22,000 and it falls to ₹21,000, you lose ₹1,000. If you buy a Nifty Call option at strike ₹22,000 for a ₹150 premium and Nifty falls to ₹21,000, you choose not to exercise and lose only your ₹150 premium.
