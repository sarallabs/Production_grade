### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** The model assumes stock prices move in a smooth, predictable bell-curve pattern (normal distribution), with no sudden extreme jumps.
- **Analogy:** Expecting daily rainfall to always be average, ignoring the possibility of a sudden cloudburst or drought.
- **Key Takeaway:** BSM assumes stock movements are gentle and follow a standard statistical curve.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** In reality, stock markets experience sudden crashes (like during Covid in 2020) or massive jumps (like during election results). BSM assumes extreme events are virtually impossible, which makes it underestimate the price of deep Out-of-the-Money options that protect against crashes.
**Worked Example:** If a stock is at ₹100, BSM assumes the chance of it falling to ₹50 in a week is 0.0001%. But in real life, bad news can cause a 50% crash in a day. Therefore, real-world crash-protection Put options are always more expensive than BSM predicts.
