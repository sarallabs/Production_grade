### 🔍 Detailed View

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Plan Options

#### 🔸 Growth Option
In the growth plan, the NAV reflects the total value of the assets. No payouts are made. Tax efficiency: Capital gains are only taxed when you redeem the units.
**Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG):** Held < 12 months (Equity) = 15% tax.
**Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG):** Held > 12 months (Equity) = 10% tax on gains exceeding ₹1 Lakh. (Note: standard pre-July 2024 rates for academic context).

#### 🔸 Dividend Payout (IDCW Payout) Option
Under current tax rules, mutual fund dividends (IDCW payouts) are added to the investor's individual income and taxed at their marginal income tax slab rate (which can be up to 30% + surcharge). Additionally, the AMC deducts 10% TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) on dividend payouts exceeding ₹5,000 in a financial year. The NAV of the fund drops by the exact amount of the dividend distributed.

#### 🔸 Dividend Reinvestment (IDCW Reinvestment) Option
Even though the investor does not receive cash in hand, the reinvested dividend is treated as distributed income. It is taxed at the investor's tax slab rate, and TDS applies. The reinvested net-of-tax amount is used to buy new units at the ex-dividend NAV. This increases the unit balance but creates a complex tax-tracking process for capital gains calculation later.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: SIP & SWP

#### 🔸 SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)
Rupee Cost Averaging works because the investment amount is fixed:
When markets are low, the NAV is low, and the SIP purchases **more units**.
When markets are high, the NAV is high, and the SIP purchases **fewer units**.
Mathematically, the average cost per unit of the investor will be lower than the simple average of NAVs over the period. This is the harmonic mean of the NAVs.

#### 🔸 SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan)
Tax advantage of SWP over IDCW or Debt FD interest:
When you withdraw ₹10,000 via SWP, it is not treated as pure income. It is treated as a redemption of units. The tax is calculated only on the capital gains portion of that ₹10,000.
For example: If you redeem 100 units at NAV ₹100 (Total ₹10,000) that you bought at NAV ₹90, the cost price was ₹9,000. The taxable capital gain is only ₹1,000. The remaining ₹9,000 is tax-free return of capital.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: SEBI Regulations

#### 🔸 Side-Pocketing
When a debt issuer defaults or is downgraded below investment grade:
1. **Split:** The AMC splits the fund's units into a Main portfolio (healthy assets) and a Segregated portfolio (bad debt).
2. **Valuation:** The segregated portfolio is valued at zero or heavily written down.
3. **Redemptions:** No new subscriptions or redemptions are allowed in the segregated portfolio.
4. **Recovery:** If the defaulted company repays in the future, the recovered money is paid back to the original unit holders.

#### 🔸 Total Expense Ratio (TER) & Direct vs. Regular Plans
SEBI limits the Total Expense Ratio (TER) for equity and debt schemes under Regulation 52. The caps for Equity schemes are:
**First ₹500 Cr of AUM:** Max 2.25%
**Next ₹250 Cr:** Max 2.00%
**Next ₹1,250 Cr:** Max 1.75%
**Next ₹3,000 Cr:** Max 1.60%
**Next ₹5,000 Cr:** Max 1.50%
**On the next ₹40,000 Cr:** reduction of 0.05% for every increase of ₹5,000 Cr.
Additional expenses (up to 0.30%) can be charged for inflows from B30 (Beyond Top 30) cities. Direct plans must have a lower expense ratio reflecting zero distributor commissions.

#### 🔸 Valuation of Non-Traded Securities
**Non-Traded Equities:** Valued using capitalization of earnings (PE ratio of industry with discount) or book value method, applying a mandatory illiquidity discount (typically 15%).
**Non-Traded Debt:** For securities maturing in > 30 days, valuation must be based on yield matrices provided by AMFI-approved agencies (CRISIL and ICRA) using Yield-to-Maturity (YTM) grids that factor in credit rating and sector spreads.
