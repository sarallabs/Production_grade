# Option

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.1:** Core Definitions | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1
> **Content:** Option: Contract giving holder right, but not obligation, to buy/sell underlying at specific price for specific period.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** An option is a legal financial contract between a buyer and a seller. It gives the buyer the right (the choice) to buy or sell a stock at a fixed price, but they are under no obligation to do so.
- **Analogy:** A refundable booking token for a banquet hall.
- **Key Takeaway:** You pay a small fee to have the final decision.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** In the stock market, an option is an agreement where you pay an upfront fee (called the "premium") to lock in the price of a stock (like Reliance or Infosys) for a set period. You have the power to decide whether to execute the trade. If the stock price goes where you wanted, you complete the deal. If it goes the wrong way, you let the contract expire and walk away.
**Worked Example:** You pay ₹50 to lock in a share of Tata Motors at ₹1,000 for one month. If the share rises to ₹1,200, you exercise your right, buy it at ₹1,000, and make a profit. If it drops to ₹800, you don't buy it; you just lose your ₹50 token.
