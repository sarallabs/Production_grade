# American Option

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.2:** Exercise Styles | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Advanced

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 2
> **Content:** American Option: Can be exercised any time between purchase and expiration.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** An option contract that can be exercised at the discretion of the holder on any trading day prior to and including the expiration date.
**Key Takeaway:** Incorporates an early exercise premium, making it more expensive than equivalent European options.
**Analogy:** A corporate warrant that can be exercised at any point during its multi-year term.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** The pricing of American options requires solving a free boundary problem because the optimal exercise boundary ($S^*(t)$) is unknown and must be calculated dynamically. Early exercise of an American Call is only optimal on dividend-paying stocks immediately prior to the ex-dividend date, or when the stock is deep in-the-money and interest rates are exceptionally high.
**Worked Example:** A trader holds an American Call option on a stock trading at ₹1,200 with a strike of ₹800. The stock is paying a ₹50 dividend tomorrow. If the trader does not exercise today, the stock price will drop to ₹1,150 tomorrow, destroying option value. The trader exercises the option today to acquire the shares and capture the ₹50 dividend.
