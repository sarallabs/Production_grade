# Overvalues Deep OTM Calls / Undervalues Deep ITM Calls

> **Sub-Topic 2.5.2:** Limitations | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Tends to overvalue deep OTM calls and undervalue deep ITM calls.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** BSM tends to give inaccurate prices for options that are very far away from the current stock price.
- **Analogy:** A weather model that is good at predicting light rain but fails to predict extreme heatwaves or blizzards.
- **Key Takeaway:** The model is less reliable for extreme strike prices.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Because BSM assumes stock returns follow a standard bell curve, it underplays the chance of extreme moves. This causes it to misprice options that are deep Out-of-the-Money (OTM) or deep In-the-Money (ITM).
**Worked Example:** For a stock at ₹100, a Call option with a strike of ₹150 (deep OTM) should be extremely cheap. BSM might price it at ₹0.01, but in reality, speculative demand on the NSE might keep its price at ₹1.00, meaning BSM significantly undervalued it.
