### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** The cash market serves long-term investment and capital appreciation goals, while the derivatives market is used for risk hedging, directional speculation, and arbitrage.
- **Formula/Rule:** Investment Objective: Cash (Investment/Consumption) vs. Derivatives (Speculation/Hedging/Arbitrage).
- **Application Context:** Traded on Indian exchanges (NSE/BSE/MCX) with standard contract specifications (Nifty lot size of 25, Bank Nifty lot size of 15).

### 🔍 Detailed View
The two markets cater to different financial objectives:
**Cash Market**: Designed for capital allocation. Investors buy stocks to share in the growth of companies over time. It is a long-term wealth creation tool.
**Derivatives Market**: Designed for risk transfer and short-term trading. It allows:
**Hedging**: Mitigating risk.
**Speculation**: Expressing a short-term market view with leverage.
**Arbitrage**: Exploiting price discrepancies between markets to secure low-risk profits.
