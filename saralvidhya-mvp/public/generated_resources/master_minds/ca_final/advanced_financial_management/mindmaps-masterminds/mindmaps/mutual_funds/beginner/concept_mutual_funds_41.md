# Side-Pocketing

> **Sub-Topic 3.4.3:** SEBI Regulations | **Chapter:** Mutual Funds | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 7
> **Content:** Side-Pocketing: Isolating risky/defaulted assets from rest of portfolio. Protects new investors and allows existing investors to choose retention/sale of bad assets.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** A mechanism that allows mutual funds to separate bad/defaulted bonds from healthy assets in a debt fund.
- **Analogy:** A basket of apples where one apple starts rotting. You put the rotten apple in a separate plastic bag (side pocket) so it does not spoil the healthy apples.
- **Key Takeaway:** Prevents a single bad debt from ruining the entire mutual fund.

### 🔍 Detailed View
When a company defaults on its loan payments, its bond value crashes to zero. To protect investors, the AMC splits the fund into two:
1. **Main Portfolio:** Holds all the healthy, safe investments. Trading and withdrawals continue normally here.
2. **Side-Pocket Portfolio:** Holds the bad debt. This is locked. If the company pays back the money in the future, the recovered cash is distributed to the old investors. New investors entering the fund do not get a share in this bad asset.
