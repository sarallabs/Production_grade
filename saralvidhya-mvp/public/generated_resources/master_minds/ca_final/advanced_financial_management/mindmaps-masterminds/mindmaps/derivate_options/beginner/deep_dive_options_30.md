## 🔬 Advanced Context & Institutional Insights

In deep dive mode, we explore the institutional application and regulatory background of this concept. Under SEBI rules, Indian derivatives are subject to standard margins (SPAN + Exposure) and are tightly regulated to ensure systemic safety.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

**Explanation:** In India, trading options involves brokerage (e.g., ₹20 per trade), Securities Transaction Tax (STT), Goods and Services Tax (GST), SEBI turnover fees, and stamp duty. These transaction costs can turn theoretical arbitrage profits into net losses.
**Worked Example:** BSM shows an arbitrage opportunity to buy a call and sell a future for a ₹1.50 profit. However, transaction taxes and brokerages cost ₹2.00 per share, making the trade unprofitable in the real world.
