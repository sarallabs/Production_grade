# Closed-End Funds

> **Sub-Topic 3.2.1:** By Function | **Chapter:** Mutual Funds | **Level:** Advanced

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Closed-End: Fixed maturity (3-15 years), open only during NFO, traded on exchange thereafter.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Schemes with a fixed unit capital and maturity date, trading on exchanges to provide liquidity while keeping the fund assets locked.
**Key Takeaway:** Capital size is static; shields the portfolio from redemption-induced asset liquidations.
**Analogy:** A venture capital fund or private equity closed structure with a fixed investment horizon.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
Closed-ended funds lock in investor capital. The fund manager can remain 100% invested in illiquid or high-yield papers (e.g., lower-rated corporate bonds) without keeping cash reserves. However, the secondary market trading price of closed-end units on the exchange often trades at a significant discount to the NAV (often 5% to 15%), reflecting liquidity premiums and lack of market market-makers.
