# Obligation

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1
> **Content:** Obligation: Futures (Both parties obligated) vs. Options (Buyer has right, no obligation).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Futures contracts impose a mutual, linear obligation on both parties. Options create an asymmetric structure where the buyer holds a right (choice) and the seller holds an obligation.
**Key Takeaway:** Futures represent linear risk transfer; options represent asymmetric volatility and price risk transfer.
**Analogy:** A forward power purchase agreement (Futures) vs. buying backup diesel generators to use only during power outages (Options).
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** In futures, both the long and short positions have a linear payoff ($S_T - F_0$ and $F_0 - S_T$ respectively). In options, the buyer's payoff is non-linear ($\max(S_T - X, 0)$ for Calls), which is purchased by paying a premium to the seller.
**Worked Example:** A portfolio manager hedges ₹1 Crore of equity. If they sell Nifty Futures, they lock in the portfolio value, but they will not benefit if the market rises. If they buy Nifty Puts instead, they protect their downside while retaining the ability to profit if the market surges.
