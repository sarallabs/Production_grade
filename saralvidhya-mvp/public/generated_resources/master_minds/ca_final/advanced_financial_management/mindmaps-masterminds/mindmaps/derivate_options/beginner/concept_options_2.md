# Call Option

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.1:** Core Definitions | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 2
> **Content:** Call Option: Right to buy underlying security.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** A Call Option is a contract that gives you the right to *buy* a stock at a locked price. You buy a Call Option when you expect the stock price to rise.
- **Analogy:** Locking in the price of gold jewelry at a local shop before a wedding season, hoping prices will go up.
- **Key Takeaway:** Call = Right to Buy (expecting prices to go up).

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Buying a Call Option is like buying a "price freeze" ticket for buying a stock. If the market value of the stock shoots up above your locked price (Strike Price), your ticket becomes highly valuable because you can buy a expensive stock at a cheap locked price.
**Worked Example:** Nifty is at ₹22,000. You pay a ₹200 premium for a Call Option with a strike price of ₹22,000. If Nifty jumps to ₹22,500, your option allows you to buy Nifty at ₹22,000. The option is now worth at least ₹500, yielding a net profit of ₹300 (₹500 value - ₹200 premium paid).
