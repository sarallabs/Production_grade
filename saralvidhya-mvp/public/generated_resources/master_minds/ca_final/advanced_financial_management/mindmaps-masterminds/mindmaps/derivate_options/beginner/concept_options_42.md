# Call Breakeven: Strike Price + Premium

---

> **Content:** Call Breakeven: Strike Price + Premium.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** For a Call Buyer, the breakeven point is the strike price plus the premium paid. The stock must rise above this point for you to make a net profit.
- **Analogy:** If you buy a concert ticket for resale for ₹1,000 and spent ₹200 on travel, you only make a profit if you sell the ticket for more than ₹1,200.
- **Key Takeaway:** Just crossing the strike price is not enough; you must cover the premium cost too.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** At the breakeven stock price, your trading profit is exactly zero. Below this price (but above strike), you recover some premium. Above this price, you make pure profit.
**Worked Example:** You buy a Nifty ₹22,000 Call option at a ₹200 premium.
Your Breakeven Price is ₹22,000 + ₹200 = ₹22,200.
If Nifty ends at ₹22,100, you exercise the option (saving ₹100), but since you paid ₹200 premium, you still lose ₹100 net.
If Nifty ends at ₹22,300, you make ₹300 payoff, leaving you with ₹100 net profit.
