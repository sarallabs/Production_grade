--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Big players like mutual funds, insurance companies, and foreign pension funds use derivatives to protect massive portfolios, generate steady extra returns, and profit from pricing mismatches (arbitrage).
- **Analogy:** Like a large logistics firm. They lock in diesel prices in advance (hedging), optimize truck routes to save fuel (arbitrage), and rent out extra warehouse space (yield enhancement).
- **Key Takeaway:** Institutions trade in large volumes to manage risk, increase returns, and exploit tiny price differences.

### 🔍 Detailed View
Institutional investors cannot easily sell all their stocks if they expect a market drop because selling billions of rupees of shares would crash the stock price. Instead, they sell futures or buy put options in large quantities to hedge their risk. They also look for tiny price discrepancies between the cash market and futures market to make risk-free profit, which is called arbitrage.
