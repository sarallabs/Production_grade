# Sub-Topic 3.2: Arbitrage Strategies

> **Reference:** Page 12

---

### 📋 Summary View

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Concept
- **Concept:** Arbitrage is a trading strategy where you make a guaranteed, risk-free profit without using any of your own money, by exploiting a price difference between two markets.
- **Analogy:** If a shop is selling a book for ₹100, and another shop next door is buying that same book for ₹120, you borrow ₹100 from a friend, buy the book, sell it next door, return the ₹100, and keep ₹20. You used zero personal capital and made a risk-free profit.
- **Key Takeaway:** Arbitrage is a risk-free, zero-net-investment strategy that exploits pricing errors.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Execution Rule
- **Concept:** The main rule of arbitrage is simple: buy the asset in the cheaper market and sell it in the more expensive market at the exact same time.
- **Analogy:** Like buy-one-get-one-free deals. You buy the cheap bundle and immediately sell the individual items at full price to make a profit.
- **Key Takeaway:** Arbitrage requires simultaneous execution in both markets to avoid price risk.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Transaction Costs
- **Concept:** Arbitrage is not free. You must pay brokerage fees, government taxes (like STT), margin costs, and stock borrowing fees, which reduce your profits.
- **Analogy:** If you buy tomatoes at ₹40 and sell them at ₹45, but you have to pay ₹6 for transport, you actually lose ₹1. The transport cost (transaction cost) ruined the trade.
- **Key Takeaway:** Transaction costs reduce the profitability of arbitrage and create pricing boundaries.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Feasibility Rule
- **Concept:** You should only execute an arbitrage trade if the price difference is large enough to cover all transaction costs and still leave a clear profit.
- **Analogy:** If you can buy a toy online for ₹100 and sell it to a friend for ₹120, but the shipping cost is ₹25, you do not do it because the shipping cost is larger than the ₹20 profit.
- **Key Takeaway:** Arbitrage is only feasible if the pricing mismatch exceeds the total transaction costs.

---

### 🔍 Detailed View

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Concept
In financial markets, arbitrageurs look for cases where a futures contract is trading away from its fair theoretical price. They execute offsetting trades in the cash and futures markets simultaneously. By locking in the prices, they guarantee a profit regardless of whether the market goes up or down.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Execution Rule
If you buy the cheap asset first, and wait a few minutes to sell it in the expensive market, the price might change, and your profit could disappear. To avoid this risk, arbitrageurs use computer programs that execute both the buy and sell orders in milliseconds.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Transaction Costs
In India, every trade incurs charges: brokerage fees, Securities Transaction Tax (STT), exchange transaction charges, GST, and SEBI fees. If you are short-selling stock for a reverse cash-and-carry trade, you must also pay a fee to borrow those shares through the Stock Lending and Borrowing (SLB) scheme. These costs must be subtracted from the price difference to find the net profit.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Feasibility Rule
If Nifty futures are trading only ₹10 above their fair price, and the total brokerage and taxes for the trade equal ₹12, an arbitrageur will ignore the opportunity. Executing the trade would result in a net loss of ₹2. Institutional traders have automated systems that continuously calculate these costs before entering any trade.
