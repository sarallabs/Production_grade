# Interest Rate (r)

> **Sub-Topic 2.3.1:** Five Variables | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Interest Rate (r): Rate rises -> Call Value rises, Put Value falls.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Interest rates have a minor effect on options. Rising interest rates slightly increase Call premiums and decrease Put premiums.
- **Analogy:** When bank interest rates rise, buying things on credit gets costlier, making option lock-ins slightly more attractive.
- **Key Takeaway:** Interest rates are the least influential variable for retail options traders.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** When interest rates are high, buying a stock requires carrying costs (opportunity cost of cash). Buying a Call option instead allows you to delay payment, saving interest. This makes Call options more desirable and thus slightly more expensive.
**Worked Example:** If the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) hikes interest rates by 0.5%, you will notice a tiny upward shift in Call option premiums and a tiny downward shift in Put option premiums, assuming all other factors remain constant.
