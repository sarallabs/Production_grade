# Topic 3.5: Performance Measurement

---

### 📋 Summary View

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Ratios

#### 🔸 Sharpe Ratio
- **Concept:** A ratio that measures how much extra return a fund generates for every unit of risk it takes.
- **Analogy:** Driving a car: reaching 100 km/h smoothly (high Sharpe) versus reaching 100 km/h with dangerous, erratic driving (low Sharpe).
- **Key Takeaway:** A higher Sharpe ratio means the fund manager is smart and generates high returns without taking wild risks.

#### 🔸 Treynor Ratio
- **Concept:** A ratio that measures the excess returns earned by a fund per unit of market-related risk (Beta).
- **Analogy:** Comparing two surfers; who rides the giant ocean waves (market waves) more steadily and wins.
- **Key Takeaway:** Used to evaluate well-diversified funds against overall stock market volatility.

#### 🔸 Jensen's Alpha
- **Concept:** A measure of how much a fund has outperformed its expected return based on its risk level.
- **Analogy:** A student scoring 95% when the exam difficulty predicted they would score 80%; that extra 15% is the student's personal talent (Alpha).
- **Key Takeaway:** A positive Alpha represents the pure value added by the fund manager's skill.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Hedge Funds vs. Mutual Funds

#### 🔸 Regulation Differences
- **Concept:** Mutual funds are strictly regulated by SEBI to protect small savers, while Hedge Funds are lightly regulated and meant only for wealthy investors.
- **Analogy:** A public bus with speed limits and safety checks (Mutual Fund) versus a private racing car with no speed governor (Hedge Fund).
- **Key Takeaway:** Mutual funds offer high safety; Hedge Funds carry high risk.

#### 🔸 Investment Strategy
- **Concept:** Mutual funds generally buy assets and hold them (long-only), whereas Hedge Funds use complex strategies like short-selling, leverage, and derivatives.
- **Analogy:** A regular farmer who only profits when crops grow (Mutual Fund) versus a trader who bets on crop prices rising or falling (Hedge Fund).
- **Key Takeaway:** Mutual funds make money when the market rises; Hedge Funds can make money even when the market crashes.

#### 🔸 Fee Structure
- **Concept:** Mutual funds charge a simple annual management fee (TER), while Hedge Funds charge both a management fee and a performance fee (a share of the profits).
- **Analogy:** A taxi charging a flat distance rate (Mutual Fund) versus a business consultant charging a retainer fee plus 20% of your business profits (Hedge Fund).
- **Key Takeaway:** Hedge Funds are much more expensive and take a cut of your earnings.

#### 🔸 Information Disclosure
- **Concept:** Mutual funds must disclose their portfolios and NAV regularly, while Hedge Funds keep their portfolios secret to protect their strategies.
- **Analogy:** A public company publishing audited accounts versus a private family business keeping its books hidden.
- **Key Takeaway:** Mutual funds are fully transparent; Hedge Funds are secretive.

### 🔍 Detailed View

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Ratios

#### 🔸 Sharpe Ratio
The Sharpe Ratio compares the fund's return against a risk-free return (like bank FD) and divides it by the volatility (Standard Deviation). In simple terms, it tells you if the extra returns are due to smart investing or just lucky, high-risk gambling. Always compare the Sharpe ratio of similar funds before buying.

#### 🔸 Treynor Ratio
Similar to Sharpe, but it uses **Beta** (market risk) instead of Standard Deviation (total risk). It assumes that the fund is fully diversified, so only market-related risk matters. A higher Treynor ratio indicates that the fund manager is successfully outperforming the general market fluctuations.

#### 🔸 Jensen's Alpha
Alpha measures the "extra" return. If a fund's risk level suggests it should return 10%, but the fund returns 12%, the Alpha is +2%. If the fund returns 8%, the Alpha is -2% (poor management). Beginners should look for funds with a consistent, positive Alpha over 3-5 years.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Hedge Funds vs. Mutual Funds

#### 🔸 Regulation Differences
Mutual funds are open to the general public and must publish their NAV daily and portfolio monthly. They have strict limits on leverage (borrowing money). Hedge Funds (called Alternative Investment Funds Category III in India) require a minimum investment of ₹1 Crore and have relaxed rules, allowing them to take huge risks.

#### 🔸 Investment Strategy
Mutual funds are directional: they buy stocks hoping they go up. Hedge Funds use "hedging" techniques. They might buy Stock A and short-sell Stock B simultaneously. They also use leverage (borrowing money to trade larger volumes), which can multiply profits or wipe out capital.

#### 🔸 Fee Structure
Mutual funds are cheap: they charge a flat fee of 0.5% to 2.25% per year. Hedge Funds usually follow a "2 and 20" model: a 2% annual management fee plus a 20% performance fee on any profits generated above a certain threshold (hurdle rate).

#### 🔸 Information Disclosure
SEBI rules force mutual funds to be transparent: you can check exactly where every rupee of your money is invested at the end of each month. Hedge Funds disclose very little to the public to prevent other traders from copying their trades, making it downstream harder for investors to monitor their daily risk.
