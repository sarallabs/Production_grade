# Seller (Writer) of Put

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.1:** Core Definitions | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 2
> **Content:** Seller (Writer) of Put: Obligation to buy if buyer exercises.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** The Seller (Writer) of a Put option collects the premium upfront but is *obligated* to buy the stock if the buyer decides to exercise their right.
- **Analogy:** An insurance company that collects your car insurance premium but is obligated to buy your damaged car if it crashes.
- **Key Takeaway:** Put Seller = Takes fee, must buy if buyer asks.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** You write a put option if you expect a stock's price to stay flat or rise. You collect the premium as profit. However, if the stock price crashes, the buyer will force you to buy the stock from them at the high strike price, causing you a major loss.
**Worked Example:** You sell a Put Option of Tata Motors at a strike price of ₹900 and collect a ₹40 premium. If the share stays above ₹900, you keep the ₹40. If the share crashes to ₹800, you are forced to buy it at ₹900, losing ₹60 net (₹100 loss on buy - ₹40 premium collected).
