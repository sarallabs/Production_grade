# Loss Visibility

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.3:** Futures vs. Options | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Advanced

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1
> **Content:** Loss Visibility: Futures (Unknown in advance) vs. Options (Known in advance for buyer).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Option buyers have complete loss visibility at trade entry (limited to the premium paid). Futures traders face unlimited risk due to gap risks and slippage.
**Key Takeaway:** Options buying allows precise risk budgeting; futures require active stop-loss orders.
**Analogy:** Purchasing a limited-liability insurance policy vs. signing a contract agreeing to cover all future liabilities of a partnership.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** The maximum loss of a long option is mathematically capped: $\text{Max Loss} = \text{Premium Paid}$. Futures carry tail risk because overnight news can cause the market to gap down below a trader's stop-loss order, resulting in large execution slippage.
**Worked Example:** A trader holds Nifty futures overnight. An international crisis causes Nifty to open 4% down the next morning. The trader's stop-loss is bypassed, and they lose ₹22,000 per lot. If they had bought a Call option instead, their loss would be strictly capped at the premium paid (e.g., ₹4,000).
