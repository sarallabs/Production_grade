# Margin Requirements

> **Sub-Topic 1.2:** Market Types | **Chapter:** Derivates Futures | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 2, Table
> **Content:** Margin: Forwards (No margin) vs. Futures (Requires margin).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** In futures trading, you must deposit security money (called margin) with your broker before you can trade. In forward contracts, you usually do not pay any margin upfront; the entire payment is made at the very end.
- **Analogy:** Futures are like booking a hotel room online where you must pay a security deposit. Forwards are like booking a local taxi where you pay the driver only after you reach your destination.
- **Key Takeaway:** Futures require security money upfront and daily adjustments; Forwards are based on trust and pay at the end.

### 🔍 Detailed View
The margin in futures acts as a safety net. Since prices move daily, the exchange uses your margin to cover any losses you make. If your losses eat into your margin, you will get a 'margin call' asking you to deposit more cash. Forwards don't have this daily check, which means no cash changes hands until the final day.
