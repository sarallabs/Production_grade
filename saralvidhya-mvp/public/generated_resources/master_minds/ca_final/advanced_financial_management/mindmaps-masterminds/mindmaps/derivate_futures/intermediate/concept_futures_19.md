# Ownership and Voting Rights

> **Sub-Topic 1.2:** Market Types | **Chapter:** Derivates Futures | **Level:** Intermediate

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Ownership: Cash (Results in ownership) vs. Derivatives (Need not result in ownership).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** Purchasing shares in the cash market grants equity ownership and voting rights, whereas derivative contracts convey no ownership claims or corporate governance participation.
- **Formula/Rule:** Ownership: Cash (Results in ownership) vs. Derivatives (Need not result in ownership).
- **Application Context:** Traded on Indian exchanges (NSE/BSE/MCX) with standard contract specifications (Nifty lot size of 25, Bank Nifty lot size of 15).

### 🔍 Detailed View
This distinction is critical for corporate actions:
**Cash Market**: Shares are held in the investor's Demat account (managed by NSDL or CDSL in India). The investor is registered in the company's register of members, granting them voting rights and the right to receive corporate communications.
**Derivatives Market**: The trader holds a contract. The company's registrar has no record of the derivative trader's position. Consequently, the trader has no voting rights and cannot participate in corporate governance.
