## 🔬 Advanced Context & Institutional Insights

In deep dive mode, we explore the institutional application and regulatory background of this concept. Under SEBI rules, Indian derivatives are subject to standard margins (SPAN + Exposure) and are tightly regulated to ensure systemic safety.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

**Explanation:** When interest rates are high, buying a Call option is a cash-efficient alternative to buying the stock outright. The cash saved can earn interest in a bank. Thus, call premiums rise. Conversely, put options become less valuable because selling the stock immediately to collect cash is more attractive.
**Worked Example:** A 1-year Call option on a stock trading at ₹1,000 has a Rho of 4.5. If the RBI raises repo rates by 1% (100 basis points), the call option premium will rise by ₹4.50, assuming all other pricing factors remain constant.
