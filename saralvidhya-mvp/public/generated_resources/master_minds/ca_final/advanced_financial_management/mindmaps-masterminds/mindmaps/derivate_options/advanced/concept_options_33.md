# Assumes Constant Risk-Free Rate and Volatility

> **Sub-Topic 2.5.2:** Limitations | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Advanced

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Assumes constant risk-free rate and volatility (incorrect in reality).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Volatility and interest rates fluctuate constantly in real markets, which directly violates BSM's assumption of constant values.
**Key Takeaway:** BSM cannot dynamically adjust to shifting market fear (volatility) and monetary policy.
**Analogy:** Navigating a winding mountain road using a map that only shows straight lines.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Volatility is dynamic. When markets fall, volatility typically spikes, changing the pricing environment. Since BSM assumes constant volatility, it must be continuously fed with updated Implied Volatility (IV) estimates to remain useful.
**Worked Example:** During a geopolitical conflict, the Nifty VIX (volatility index) jumps from 14% to 25% in one day. Options prices shoot up. A trader using a static BSM model with 14% volatility will significantly underprice the options.
