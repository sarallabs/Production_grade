# Topic 3.2: Types of Funds

---

### 📋 Summary View

### 🔹 Sub-topic: By Function

#### 🔸 Open-End Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Schemes with variable capital structures where units are continuously created and destroyed based on investor cash flows, priced at daily NAV.
**Key Takeaway:** Capital size is dynamic; asset-liability management (ALM) is critical due to daily redemption risk.
**Analogy:** A corporate bank account with instant-access credit facilities.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Closed-End Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Schemes with a fixed unit capital and maturity date, trading on exchanges to provide liquidity while keeping the fund assets locked.
**Key Takeaway:** Capital size is static; shields the portfolio from redemption-induced asset liquidations.
**Analogy:** A venture capital fund or private equity closed structure with a fixed investment horizon.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Interval Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Hybrid structures that remain closed-ended for specified durations and open for transaction windows at predetermined intervals.
**Key Takeaway:** Balances long-term asset positioning with scheduled investor exit windows.
**Analogy:** Private credit funds that offer quarterly redemption gates with strict limits.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Equity Funds

#### 🔸 Growth Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity portfolios targeting capital appreciation by selecting growth companies characterized by high earnings growth rates, high return on equity (ROE), and high retention ratios.
**Key Takeaway:** Maximizes exposure to the Growth factor ($g = \text{Retention Ratio} \times \text{ROE}$).
**Analogy:** A technology-focused VC fund targeting high-growth sectors with no immediate dividend distributions.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Aggressive Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** High-beta equity portfolios utilizing momentum, sector concentration, and thematic strategies to capture maximum market upside.
**Key Takeaway:** Leverages high active risk and tracking error to maximize potential outperformance.
**Analogy:** A leveraged long equity strategy or thematic sector trading desk.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Income Funds (Equity)
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity portfolios targeting defensive companies with high dividend payout ratios and stable, non-cyclical cash flows.
**Key Takeaway:** Low-beta, value-tilted equity strategy designed to harvest dividend yield premiums.
**Analogy:** A real estate portfolio generating stable rental yield with minimal capital gains.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Diversified Equity Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Multi-sector equity funds designed to eliminate idiosyncratic (stock-specific) risk through broad diversification.
**Key Takeaway:** A core portfolio allocation tool designed to capture the broad equity risk premium.
**Analogy:** A global multi-sector index portfolio like the MSCI World Index.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Multi-Cap Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity funds with a mandatory allocation of at least 25% each in Large-cap, Mid-cap, and Small-cap stocks.
**Key Takeaway:** Structured capitalization diversification, limiting the manager's ability to run style-drift portfolios.
**Analogy:** A multi-factor portfolio with static risk-weight constraints across capitalization buckets.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Flexi-Cap Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity portfolios with a minimum 65% allocation to equity, but with no capitalization constraints, allowing dynamic reallocation across large, mid, and small-caps.
**Key Takeaway:** Gives the fund manager unconstrained freedom to navigate the capitalization cycle.
**Analogy:** An unconstrained global macro equity fund.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Contra Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Portfolios executing a contrarian value strategy, targeting companies trading at deep discounts due to temporary operational or sectoral stress.
**Key Takeaway:** Harvests the Value factor by exploiting cognitive biases and market overreaction.
**Analogy:** Distressed asset investing or purchasing deep value special situations.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Dividend Yield Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Portfolios mandated to invest at least 65% of assets in dividend-yielding stocks.
**Key Takeaway:** Systematic tilt toward high dividend yield and value factor characteristics.
**Analogy:** A cash-generative real estate portfolio or public infrastructure trust (InvIT).
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Debt & Other Funds

#### 🔸 Bond Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Fixed income portfolios managing credit risk and interest rate risk (Macaulay Duration) to generate yield and capital gains.
**Key Takeaway:** Net returns are driven by interest accrual (YTM) and capital gains/losses from duration exposure.
**Analogy:** A corporate debt portfolio managed by a commercial bank treasury desk.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Gilt Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Debt funds investing a minimum of 80% of assets in sovereign government securities (G-Secs), characterized by high Macaulay duration.
**Key Takeaway:** Zero credit default risk, but highly sensitive to interest rate policy and yield curve shifts.
**Analogy:** A sovereign debt portfolio matching long-term government liabilities.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Money Market Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Debt portfolios investing in money market papers with maturities up to 1 year, prioritizing capital preservation and liquidity.
**Key Takeaway:** Minimizes duration risk and credit risk; acts as an institutional liquidity management tool.
**Analogy:** A commercial bank treasury liquidity buffer held in short-term central bank reserves.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Capital Protection Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Hybrid funds that dynamically adjust equity and debt exposure using quantitative valuation models to manage downside risk and capture market upside.
**Key Takeaway:** Dynamic asset allocation utilizing valuation ratios (PE, PB, and Yield-gap ratios).
**Analogy:** A quantitative asset allocation overlay model that dynamically hedges equity exposure using index futures.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Gold Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Feeder funds investing in physical gold ETFs to capture the price movements of gold bullion.
**Key Takeaway:** Offers a non-correlated asset class allocation to hedge inflation and systemic crisis risk.
**Analogy:** A commodity trading account holding physical gold certificates.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Quant Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Quantitative funds selecting securities based on systematic, rules-based mathematical models that target specific style factors.
**Key Takeaway:** Eliminates human active management risk; harvests systematic style premiums.
**Analogy:** A systematic equity long-short hedge fund running factor models.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity funds with a 3-year lock-in offering tax deductions under Section 80C.
**Key Takeaway:** Shortest lock-in under Section 80C; permits the manager to take long-term small/mid-cap exposures.
**Analogy:** A closed-end equity fund that converts into an open-ended fund after 3 years.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Specialized Strategies

#### 🔸 Index Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Passive funds mimicking a benchmark index, including Smart Beta indices that weight stocks based on factors rather than market cap.
**Key Takeaway:** Passive implementation of active factor strategies at low cost.
**Analogy:** A passive index tracker that tracks a value-weighted or equal-weighted index rather than a market-cap weighted index.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Tracking Error
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** The standard deviation of the daily active returns of a passive fund relative to its benchmark index.
**Key Takeaway:** A key metric of tracking efficiency; indicates how well the passive fund replicates the index.
**Analogy:** The root-mean-square error (RMSE) in statistical regression models.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Low-risk funds exploiting pricing anomalies between spot and derivative markets, capturing the basis spread.
**Key Takeaway:** Risk-free returns (basis arbitrage) with equity-like taxation benefits.
**Analogy:** Cash-and-carry arbitrage in commodity markets.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Sector Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Concentrated portfolios investing at least 80% of assets in a single economic sector.
**Key Takeaway:** High idiosyncratic risk; returns are driven by sectoral business cycles.
**Analogy:** A focused venture capital fund targeting only semiconductor manufacturers.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Thematic Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Portfolios targeting a broad economic theme across multiple sectors (e.g., ESG, Infrastructure).
**Key Takeaway:** Broader than sector funds, but still vulnerable to thematic cycle downturns.
**Analogy:** An thematic investment vehicle targeting the global transition to renewable energy.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Fund of Funds (FOF)
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Mutual funds investing in other funds or specialized assets like REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) and InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts).
**Key Takeaway:** Offers access to specialized asset classes with mandatory cash distribution rules.
**Analogy:** A master-feeder fund structure.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Offshore Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Funds registered outside India (e.g., Mauritius, Singapore) to pool foreign capital for investment in Indian securities.
**Key Takeaway:** Optimizes tax treaty benefits and currency conversions for foreign portfolio investors (FPIs).
**Analogy:** A feeder fund registered in the Cayman Islands feeding into an onshore US master fund.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Indian funds investing in foreign markets directly or via offshore feeder structures.
**Key Takeaway:** Provides global diversification and a currency hedge, but taxed as a debt fund.

### 🔍 Detailed View

### 🔹 Sub-topic: By Function

#### 🔸 Open-End Funds
Open-ended funds have no cap on the number of units. The AMC must manage daily inflows and outflows. To manage redemption risk, managers must match portfolio liquidity with potential outflows. This requires maintaining a portion of assets in cash, cash equivalents (TREPS), or highly liquid blue-chip stocks. Large redemptions during panic periods can force the liquidation of liquid assets first, increasing the concentration of illiquid assets in the remaining portfolio.

#### 🔸 Closed-End Funds
Closed-ended funds lock in investor capital. The fund manager can remain 100% invested in illiquid or high-yield papers (e.g., lower-rated corporate bonds) without keeping cash reserves. However, the secondary market trading price of closed-end units on the exchange often trades at a significant discount to the NAV (often 5% to 15%), reflecting liquidity premiums and lack of market market-makers.

#### 🔸 Interval Funds
The interval periods (e.g., 3 days every quarter) are defined in the Scheme Information Document. During these windows, investors can buy or redeem units at NAV directly from the AMC. The fund manager structures the portfolio's debt maturities to align with these windows, utilizing instruments like commercial papers and short-term deposits that mature just before the interval date to provide liquidity.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Equity Funds

#### 🔸 Growth Funds
Growth funds typically buy stocks with high P/E and P/B ratios. The portfolio is positioned in sectors with high expansion rates (e.g., tech, consumption). These portfolios have high systematic sensitivity (Beta > 1) and are priced based on long-duration cash flow expectations. Thus, they are highly sensitive to changes in long-term interest rates (discount rates).

#### 🔸 Aggressive Funds
Aggressive funds systematically tilt towards high-volatility, small-cap, or cyclical companies. The portfolio beta ($\beta_p$) is maintained significantly above 1.0. These funds may have high portfolio turnover ratios ($>150\%$), indicating active tactical trading. They face high maximum drawdowns (MDD) and require a long holding period to recover from market corrections.

#### 🔸 Income Funds (Equity)
These funds target companies with mature business models, low reinvestment needs, and high free cash flow conversion. The portfolio is constructed to provide a yield cushion. During bear markets, the dividend yield acts as a floor for the stock prices, resulting in a low portfolio downside capture ratio.

#### 🔸 Diversified Equity Funds
The manager uses bottom-up stock selection combined with top-down sectoral allocations. The portfolio tracking error is typically kept moderate (3% to 5%) relative to a broad index like the Nifty 500. This ensures that the portfolio return is dominated by systematic market exposure rather than extreme sector bets.

#### 🔸 Multi-Cap Funds
Under SEBI's 2020 mandate, Multi-Cap funds must allocate:
Large-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
Mid-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
Small-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
The remaining 25% is tactical. This structure forces the fund to maintain a high allocation to smaller, illiquid companies (minimum 50% combined in mid and small-caps), which increases the portfolio's systematic risk and liquidity risk compared to large-cap portfolios.

#### 🔸 Flexi-Cap Funds
The manager adjusts allocations based on the valuation spreads between capitalization tiers (e.g., shifting to large-caps when small-cap valuations are at a historical premium). This category is highly popular among institutional managers because it avoids the forced illiquidity of the multi-cap mandate. The performance is heavily dependent on the manager's tactical asset allocation skill.

#### 🔸 Contra Funds
The manager targets stocks where current market sentiment is negative but fundamentals remain intact. This involves analyzing debt levels, asset backing, and cyclical recovery timelines. The primary risks are:
1. **Value Trap:** A stock that appears cheap but continues to deteriorate.
2. **Timing Risk:** The contrarian theme may take several years to play out, leading to prolonged underperformance against growth benchmarks.

#### 🔸 Dividend Yield Funds
The manager targets stocks with a dividend yield greater than the benchmark Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 average. The portfolio is typically defensive, exhibiting low drawdowns and lower volatility. The returns are driven by cash dividends and capital gains from undervalued companies, making these funds a useful tool for conservative equity allocators.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Debt & Other Funds

#### 🔸 Bond Funds
**Macaulay Duration ($D_{mac}$):** The weighted average time to receive all cash flows from a bond:
$$D_{mac} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^n \frac{t \cdot C_t}{(1+y)^t}}{\text{Bond Price}}$$
Where $C_t$ is the cash flow at time $t$, and $y$ is the yield to maturity.
**Modified Duration ($D_{mod}$):** Measures price sensitivity to interest rate changes:
$$D_{mod} = \frac{D_{mac}}{1 + \frac{y}{m}}$$
$$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D_{mod} \cdot \Delta y$$
**Tax Regulation (Section 50AA - April 2023):** Debt mutual funds (with $\le 35\%$ equity exposure) no longer receive indexation benefits or long-term capital gains tax rates (formerly 20% with indexation). All gains, regardless of holding period, are classified as short-term capital gains and taxed at the investor's marginal slab rate.

#### 🔸 Gilt Funds
Gilt funds focus on maturity profiles ranging from 3 to 15+ years. Because they have no credit risk, their volatility is purely driven by interest rate movements (duration risk). If a Gilt fund has a Macaulay duration of 8 years and the yield curve shifts upward by 100 bps, the fund's NAV will fall by approximately 8%. Managers use interest rate swaps (IRS) to hedge duration risk or take tactical bets on the direction of RBI repo rate cuts/hikes.

#### 🔸 Money Market Funds
These funds invest in Commercial Paper (CP), Certificates of Deposit (CD), Treasury Bills, and Tri-party Repo (TREPS) maturing in $\le 365$ days. Because maturity is short, modified duration is low ($D_{mod} < 1$). The fund's return is driven by accrual income (coupon yields). It is highly liquid and exhibits minimal NAV volatility.

#### 🔸 Capital Protection Funds
BAFs use mathematical models to adjust net equity exposure (typically between 30% and 80%). The models analyze indicators like:
1. **Historical P/E Ratio:** Reducing equity exposure when P/E exceeds historical standard deviations.
2. **P/B Ratio:** Using book value to determine asset bubble conditions.
3. **Yield-Gap Ratio:** Comparing equity earnings yield ($1/\text{PE}$) to 10-year G-Sec yield:
$$\text{Yield Gap} = \text{10Y G-Sec Yield} - \frac{1}{\text{PE}}$$
To maintain the tax status of an equity fund (requiring $\ge 65\%$ gross equity exposure), BAFs use equity derivatives (arbitrage). The net asset allocation is structured as:
$$\text{Total Assets} = \text{Net Long Equity} + \text{Arbitrage (Equity Futures Shorts)} + \text{Debt Securities}$$
Where $\text{Net Long Equity} + \text{Arbitrage} \ge 65\%$ to qualify for equity taxation, while actual equity market exposure is limited to $\text{Net Long Equity}$.

#### 🔸 Gold Funds
Gold funds operate as a wrapper. The fund's asset-liability match is tied to the underlying Gold ETF price. The ETF must buy 99.5% pure physical gold bullion. Since gold is denominated in USD internationally, the fund's return is also driven by the USD-INR exchange rate. Under current tax laws, gold mutual funds are taxed as debt mutual funds (marginal slab rate).

#### 🔸 Quant Funds
Quant funds utilize multi-factor pricing models. The models screen stocks based on factors like:
**Value:** Low P/E, Low P/B, High Enterprise Value / EBITDA.
**Momentum:** High relative strength index (RSI), price acceleration over 6 and 12 months.
**Quality:** High ROE, low debt-to-equity, high cash flow conversion.
**Low Volatility:** Low historical standard deviation or low Beta.
The portfolio is rebalanced systematically (e.g., monthly or quarterly) without human intervention, maintaining fixed risk factor exposures.

#### 🔸 ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)
Under SEBI guidelines, ELSS must maintain a minimum 80% equity exposure. The 3-year lock-in period creates a stable capital base. The fund manager does not need to maintain cash reserves for daily redemptions, allowing them to invest in relatively illiquid, high-conviction mid and small-cap stocks. This structure is highly efficient for long-term wealth creation.

### 🔹 Sub-topic: Specialized Strategies

#### 🔸 Index Funds
While standard index funds replicate market-cap weighted indices (e.g., Nifty 50), Smart Beta index funds track factor-based indices (e.g., Nifty 50 Value 20, Nifty Alpha Low-Volatility 30). This allows investors to target specific factor exposures (Value, Momentum, Quality) without paying active management fees. The tracking error is monitored relative to the specific factor index.

#### 🔸 Tracking Error
Let the active return at day $t$ be $R_{A,t} = R_{P,t} - R_{B,t}$.
The Tracking Error (TE) is the annualized standard deviation of $R_{A,t}$:
$$\text{TE} = \sigma(R_A) \times \sqrt{252}$$
$$\text{TE} = \sqrt{252 \times \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{t=1}^N (R_{A,t} - \bar{R}_A)^2}$$
Where $\bar{R}_A$ is the mean active return over the period. Minimizing TE requires optimizing transaction execution, managing cash flows efficiently, and utilizing corporate actions (like dividends) immediately.

#### 🔸 Arbitrage Funds
The fund buys stock $S$ in the spot market and simultaneously sells futures contract $F$ at a premium. The locked-in return is the basis:
$$\text{Basis} = F_0 - S_0$$
At expiry, the futures price converges to the spot price ($F_T = S_T$). The return is earned as the basis converges to zero. Because the fund holds physical shares hedged with derivatives, its gross equity exposure exceeds 65%, qualifying it for equity taxation (lower tax rate) despite having the risk profile of a debt liquid fund.

#### 🔸 Sector Funds
Sector funds exhibit high sector concentration, making them highly volatile. They are characterized by a high active share (deviating from a broad market index). Portfolio managers cannot diversify sector-specific risks (e.g., changes in government policy, raw material shortages), requiring investors to actively time entry and exit.

#### 🔸 Thematic Funds
Thematic funds invest in a collection of sectors linked by a theme. For example, a "Defense and Aerospace" theme will invest in private defense manufacturers, public sector defense firms, component suppliers, and logistics firms. It requires deep analysis of thematic trends and regulatory catalysts.

#### 🔸 Fund of Funds (FOF)
**REITs and InvITs Structure:**
**SPV Structure:** The trust holds shares in Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) that own income-generating real estate or infrastructure.
**Mandatory Distribution:** Under SEBI rules, REITs/InvITs must distribute at least 90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unit-holders as dividends or interest.
**Taxation:** The distribution consists of:
1. **Interest Income:** Taxable at the investor's slab rate.
2. **Dividend Income:** Taxable if the SPV opted for lower tax regime, otherwise exempt.
3. **Repayment of Debt (Amortization):** Subject to complex tax tracking.
FOFs wrapper: FOFs holding REITs/InvITs are taxed as debt funds, simplifying the tax administration for retail investors.

#### 🔸 Offshore Funds
These funds are structured to minimize double taxation under treaties (DTAA). They must register with SEBI as FPIs and comply with strict Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) guidelines. The assets are held by local domestic custodians in India.

#### 🔸 International Funds
These funds expose investors to international asset classes. When the Indian Rupee depreciates against the USD, the NAV of the international fund rises proportionally. However, under Indian tax laws, these funds are classified as debt mutual funds for tax purposes, meaning all capital gains are taxed at the investor's marginal slab rate (Section 50AA).
