# Call Buyer Payoff: Max(S - X, 0)

> **Sub-Topic 2.6.1:** Formulas | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 2
> **Content:** Call Buyer Payoff: Max(S - X, 0).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** The payoff for a Call Buyer is the stock price minus the strike price (or zero, whichever is higher).
- **Analogy:** Your profit is the discount you get at a store minus the cost of the discount coupon you bought.
- **Key Takeaway:** You only make profit if the stock rises above your strike price.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** The formula is $Payoff = \max(S - X, 0)$. S is the final stock price, and X is the strike price. If S is less than X, the option is worthless (0). To find your net profit, you must subtract the premium you paid from this payoff.
**Worked Example:** You buy a ₹2,200 Call option for a ₹100 premium.
If the stock ends at ₹2,400: Payoff is $\max(2400 - 2200, 0) = \text{₹}200$. Net Profit = ₹200 - ₹100 premium = +₹100.
If the stock ends at ₹2,100: Payoff is $\max(2100 - 2200, 0) = 0$. Net Profit = 0 - ₹100 premium = -₹100 (maximum loss).
