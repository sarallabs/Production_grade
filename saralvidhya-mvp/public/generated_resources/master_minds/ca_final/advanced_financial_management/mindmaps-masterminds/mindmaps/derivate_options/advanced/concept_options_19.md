# Time to Expiry (T)

> **Sub-Topic 2.3.1:** Five Variables | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Advanced

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Time to Expiry (T): Time passes -> Time value decreases for both Call and Put.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Option premiums consist of time value, which decays daily. This decay is non-linear and accelerates as expiration approaches.
**Key Takeaway:** Options are wasting assets; holding them long-term hurts buyers due to Theta decay.
**Analogy:** A block of ice melting under the sun. The rate of melting increases as the block gets smaller.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Time value represents the probability that the option will expire in-the-money. The mathematical decay of time value is denoted by Theta ($\Theta = \frac{\partial V}{\partial T}$). The decay curve is relatively flat with 90 days left, but turns into a steep downward cliff in the final 30 days, especially the last 7 days.
**Worked Example:** You buy an At-the-Money Nifty option with 30 days left. Daily decay (Theta) is ₹3. With 3 days left, if Nifty remains at the exact same spot, the Theta decay accelerates to ₹25 per day, wiping out the option value rapidly.
