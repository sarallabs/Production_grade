# Sub-Topic 3.2.2: Equity Funds

### 📋 Summary View

#### 🔸 Growth Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity portfolios targeting capital appreciation by selecting growth companies characterized by high earnings growth rates, high return on equity (ROE), and high retention ratios.
**Key Takeaway:** Maximizes exposure to the Growth factor ($g = \text{Retention Ratio} \times \text{ROE}$).
**Analogy:** A technology-focused VC fund targeting high-growth sectors with no immediate dividend distributions.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Aggressive Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** High-beta equity portfolios utilizing momentum, sector concentration, and thematic strategies to capture maximum market upside.
**Key Takeaway:** Leverages high active risk and tracking error to maximize potential outperformance.
**Analogy:** A leveraged long equity strategy or thematic sector trading desk.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Income Funds (Equity)
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity portfolios targeting defensive companies with high dividend payout ratios and stable, non-cyclical cash flows.
**Key Takeaway:** Low-beta, value-tilted equity strategy designed to harvest dividend yield premiums.
**Analogy:** A real estate portfolio generating stable rental yield with minimal capital gains.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Diversified Equity Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Multi-sector equity funds designed to eliminate idiosyncratic (stock-specific) risk through broad diversification.
**Key Takeaway:** A core portfolio allocation tool designed to capture the broad equity risk premium.
**Analogy:** A global multi-sector index portfolio like the MSCI World Index.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Multi-Cap Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity funds with a mandatory allocation of at least 25% each in Large-cap, Mid-cap, and Small-cap stocks.
**Key Takeaway:** Structured capitalization diversification, limiting the manager's ability to run style-drift portfolios.
**Analogy:** A multi-factor portfolio with static risk-weight constraints across capitalization buckets.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Flexi-Cap Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Equity portfolios with a minimum 65% allocation to equity, but with no capitalization constraints, allowing dynamic reallocation across large, mid, and small-caps.
**Key Takeaway:** Gives the fund manager unconstrained freedom to navigate the capitalization cycle.
**Analogy:** An unconstrained global macro equity fund.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Contra Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Portfolios executing a contrarian value strategy, targeting companies trading at deep discounts due to temporary operational or sectoral stress.
**Key Takeaway:** Harvests the Value factor by exploiting cognitive biases and market overreaction.
**Analogy:** Distressed asset investing or purchasing deep value special situations.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Dividend Yield Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Portfolios mandated to invest at least 65% of assets in dividend-yielding stocks.
**Key Takeaway:** Systematic tilt toward high dividend yield and value factor characteristics.
**Analogy:** A cash-generative real estate portfolio or public infrastructure trust (InvIT).
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View

#### 🔸 Growth Funds
Growth funds typically buy stocks with high P/E and P/B ratios. The portfolio is positioned in sectors with high expansion rates (e.g., tech, consumption). These portfolios have high systematic sensitivity (Beta > 1) and are priced based on long-duration cash flow expectations. Thus, they are highly sensitive to changes in long-term interest rates (discount rates).

#### 🔸 Aggressive Funds
Aggressive funds systematically tilt towards high-volatility, small-cap, or cyclical companies. The portfolio beta ($\beta_p$) is maintained significantly above 1.0. These funds may have high portfolio turnover ratios ($>150\%$), indicating active tactical trading. They face high maximum drawdowns (MDD) and require a long holding period to recover from market corrections.

#### 🔸 Income Funds (Equity)
These funds target companies with mature business models, low reinvestment needs, and high free cash flow conversion. The portfolio is constructed to provide a yield cushion. During bear markets, the dividend yield acts as a floor for the stock prices, resulting in a low portfolio downside capture ratio.

#### 🔸 Diversified Equity Funds
The manager uses bottom-up stock selection combined with top-down sectoral allocations. The portfolio tracking error is typically kept moderate (3% to 5%) relative to a broad index like the Nifty 500. This ensures that the portfolio return is dominated by systematic market exposure rather than extreme sector bets.

#### 🔸 Multi-Cap Funds
Under SEBI's 2020 mandate, Multi-Cap funds must allocate:
Large-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
Mid-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
Small-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
The remaining 25% is tactical. This structure forces the fund to maintain a high allocation to smaller, illiquid companies (minimum 50% combined in mid and small-caps), which increases the portfolio's systematic risk and liquidity risk compared to large-cap portfolios.

#### 🔸 Flexi-Cap Funds
The manager adjusts allocations based on the valuation spreads between capitalization tiers (e.g., shifting to large-caps when small-cap valuations are at a historical premium). This category is highly popular among institutional managers because it avoids the forced illiquidity of the multi-cap mandate. The performance is heavily dependent on the manager's tactical asset allocation skill.

#### 🔸 Contra Funds
The manager targets stocks where current market sentiment is negative but fundamentals remain intact. This involves analyzing debt levels, asset backing, and cyclical recovery timelines. The primary risks are:
1. **Value Trap:** A stock that appears cheap but continues to deteriorate.
2. **Timing Risk:** The contrarian theme may take several years to play out, leading to prolonged underperformance against growth benchmarks.

#### 🔸 Dividend Yield Funds
The manager targets stocks with a dividend yield greater than the benchmark Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 average. The portfolio is typically defensive, exhibiting low drawdowns and lower volatility. The returns are driven by cash dividends and capital gains from undervalued companies, making these funds a useful tool for conservative equity allocators.
