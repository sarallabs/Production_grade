# Assumes Constant Risk-Free Rate and Volatility

> **Sub-Topic 2.5.2:** Limitations | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Assumes constant risk-free rate and volatility (incorrect in reality).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** In reality, volatility and interest rates change constantly, making the BSM assumption of constant values incorrect.
- **Analogy:** Designing a car's cruise control assuming the road will always be flat, ignoring hills and valleys.
- **Key Takeaway:** Real-world markets are dynamic; BSM assumes they are static.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Volatility changes every second based on market news. Since BSM uses a single fixed number for volatility, it cannot keep up with changing market moods.
**Worked Example:** If Nifty volatility jumps from 12% to 20% due to global tensions, options prices will shoot up. BSM will output outdated, cheap option prices if it is not manually updated with the new volatility numbers.
