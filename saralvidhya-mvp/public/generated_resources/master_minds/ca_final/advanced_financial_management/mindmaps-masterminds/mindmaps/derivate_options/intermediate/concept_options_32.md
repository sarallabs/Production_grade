### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** BSM assumes stock price returns follow a normal log-normal distribution (bell curve), meaning price changes are smooth and extreme moves are statistically impossible.
- **Formula/Rule:** Standard guidelines apply.
- **Application Context:** No specific application context available.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Real asset returns show "fat tails" (extreme price moves occur far more often than a bell curve predicts) and are skewed. BSM underprices deep out-of-the-money options because it underestimates the probability of extreme market crashes.
**Worked Example:** A stock trades at ₹500. BSM predicts the probability of the stock dropping to ₹350 in a week is 0.001%. In reality, due to corporate fraud or market panic, such drops happen more frequently. Thus, real out-of-the-money put options trade at premiums higher than BSM's theoretical price.
