### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** A Put Option gives the holder the right to sell the underlying asset at the strike price. The buyer is bearish on the stock.
- **Formula/Rule:** Standard guidelines apply.
- **Application Context:** No specific application context available.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Puts are hedging instruments. Buying a put allows an investor to lock in a floor price for their equity portfolio. It provides a synthetic short position without the margin call risks of shorting physical shares or futures.
**Worked Example:** An investor owns 1,000 shares of Reliance at ₹3,000. To hedge against market correction, they buy a ₹2,900 Put Option (PE) for a ₹60 premium. If Reliance crashes to ₹2,600, the investor exercises the option to sell at ₹2,900, limiting their net loss to ₹160 per share (₹100 drop to strike + ₹60 premium paid) instead of ₹400.
