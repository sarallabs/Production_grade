# Assumes Continuous Stock Prices (No Jumps)

> **Sub-Topic 2.5.2:** Limitations | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Assumes continuous stock prices (no large jumps).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** BSM assumes stock prices glide smoothly from one price to another, ignoring overnight gaps or sudden news-driven price jumps.
- **Analogy:** Assuming a train travels smoothly without ever stopping or skipping stations.
- **Key Takeaway:** The model fails during sudden market gaps (opening much higher or lower).

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** In India, companies release earnings after market hours. The next morning, a stock might open 10% higher or lower without trading at any price in between. BSM assumes this "gapping" cannot happen.
**Worked Example:** You own a Put option to protect your stock. The stock closes at ₹100. Overnight, a major scam is revealed, and the stock opens at ₹60. The price skipped all levels between ₹100 and ₹60. BSM models cannot model this risk-profile transition accurately.
