# Investment Strategy

> **Sub-Topic 3.5.2:** Hedge Funds vs. Mutual Funds | **Chapter:** Mutual Funds | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 8
> **Content:** Strategy: MF (Directional) vs. HF (Directional, Arbitrage, Spread).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Mutual funds generally buy assets and hold them (long-only), whereas Hedge Funds use complex strategies like short-selling, leverage, and derivatives.
- **Analogy:** A regular farmer who only profits when crops grow (Mutual Fund) versus a trader who bets on crop prices rising or falling (Hedge Fund).
- **Key Takeaway:** Mutual funds make money when the market rises; Hedge Funds can make money even when the market crashes.

### 🔍 Detailed View
Mutual funds are directional: they buy stocks hoping they go up. Hedge Funds use "hedging" techniques. They might buy Stock A and short-sell Stock B simultaneously. They also use leverage (borrowing money to trade larger volumes), which can multiply profits or wipe out capital.
