# Hedging and Risk Management

> **Sub-Topic 3.1:** Hedging & Price Discovery | **Chapter:** Derivates Futures | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 4
> **Content:** Hedging: Mitigate risk by entering a contract moving in opposite direction to underlying.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Hedging is like buying insurance for your investments. You enter into a derivatives contract that will make a profit if your actual investments lose money, balancing out your overall position.
- **Analogy:** Think of an umbrella shop owner. If it rains, they make money from umbrella sales. If it is sunny, they lose money. To protect their income, they might also sell sunglasses. The two products move in opposite directions, protecting their overall business.
- **Key Takeaway:** Hedging protects your portfolio from market falls by offsetting losses with derivative gains.

### 🔍 Detailed View
Suppose you own ₹1 lakh worth of shares in a company. You are worried that the stock market will crash next week because of a global event. Instead of selling all your shares (which might trigger taxes and fees), you buy a 'Put option' or sell 'Futures' for the same amount. If the market crashes, your shares will lose value, but your futures contract will make an equal profit, protecting your capital.
