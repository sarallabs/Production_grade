# Misprices Options on High-Dividend Stocks

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** The standard BSM formula does not account for high dividend yields, causing significant pricing distortions for high-payout stocks.
**Key Takeaway:** Do not use basic BSM for high-dividend PSU stocks like ONGC or Coal India.
**Analogy:** Valuing an investment bond without accounting for the regular coupon payments it distributes.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** High-dividend-paying stocks drop in value repeatedly throughout the year. If the dividend payout is not subtracted from the stock price in the formula, the call options will be overpriced and put options will be underpriced.
**Worked Example:** Coal India trades at ₹450 and pays a ₹25 dividend. Standard BSM will show the ₹450 Call option is worth ₹35. In reality, because the stock drops to ₹425 ex-dividend, the call is only worth ₹15. The model overpriced the option by 133%.
