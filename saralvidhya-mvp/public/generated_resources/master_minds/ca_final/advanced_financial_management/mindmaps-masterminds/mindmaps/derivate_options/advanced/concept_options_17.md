> **Content:** Underlying Price (S): S rises -> Call Value rises, Put Value falls.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** The price of the underlying asset is the primary driver of option values. Call values move in the same direction as the stock; Put values move in the opposite direction.
**Key Takeaway:** Asset price changes directly alter the option's intrinsic value.
**Analogy:** The height of a building determines the shadow lengthcast on the street.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** This relationship is measured by Delta ($\Delta$). For a Call option, the derivative of option price ($V$) with respect to stock price ($S$) is positive ($\frac{\partial V}{\partial S} > 0$). For Puts, it is negative ($\frac{\partial V}{\partial S} < 0$).
**Worked Example:** A Call option has a Delta of 0.60. If the stock price rises by ₹20, the call option premium will increase by approximately ₹12 (₹20 * 0.60), assuming other variables remain unchanged.
