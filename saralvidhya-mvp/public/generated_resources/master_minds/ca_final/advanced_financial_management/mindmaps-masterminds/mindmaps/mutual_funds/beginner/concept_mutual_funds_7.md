# Closed-End Funds

> **Sub-Topic 3.2.1:** By Function | **Chapter:** Mutual Funds | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Closed-End: Fixed maturity (3-15 years), open only during NFO, traded on exchange thereafter.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Funds where you can only invest during the initial launch period (New Fund Offer or NFO). The units are locked for a fixed time (e.g., 3 to 5 years).
- **Analogy:** A chartered flight where you must buy tickets beforehand, and you cannot get off until the plane lands at the destination.
- **Key Takeaway:** Your money is locked in, and you can only redeem it when the term ends.

### 🔍 Detailed View
Closed-ended funds have a fixed maturity period. Once the NFO closes, you cannot buy units directly from the AMC. To provide liquidity, these units are listed on stock exchanges (like NSE or BSE) where you can buy or sell them from other investors, but the trading volume is usually very low, making them hard to sell before maturity.
