### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** Shareholders in the cash market are entitled to corporate dividends, while derivative traders do not receive dividend distributions, though derivative prices adjust to reflect them.
- **Formula/Rule:** Dividends: Cash (Entitled to dividends) vs. Derivatives (Not entitled).
- **Application Context:** Traded on Indian exchanges (NSE/BSE/MCX) with standard contract specifications (Nifty lot size of 25, Bank Nifty lot size of 15).

### 🔍 Detailed View
Dividends are corporate actions that affect stock valuation:
**Cash Market**: Investors who hold the stock on the record date receive the dividend payment directly in their bank accounts.
**Derivatives Market**: Derivative contracts do not receive dividend payments. However, the expected dividend is priced into the futures contract. The futures price is:
$$F = S - D + \text{Cost of Carry}$$
where $D$ is the present value of the dividend. On the ex-dividend date, the stock price drops by the dividend amount, and the futures price adjusts to prevent arbitrage.
