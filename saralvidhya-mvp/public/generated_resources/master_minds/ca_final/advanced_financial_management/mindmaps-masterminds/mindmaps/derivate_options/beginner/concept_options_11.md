# Premium

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.3:** Futures vs. Options | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1
> **Content:** Premium: Futures (None) vs. Options (Upfront payment/receipt).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Options require an upfront non-refundable fee called a premium. Futures require no premium, only a security margin deposit.
- **Analogy:** Option premium is like paying a non-refundable insurance premium. Future margin is like a refundable rental deposit for a house.
- **Key Takeaway:** Options = Cost of buying the contract (Premium). Futures = Security deposit (Margin).

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** An option premium is the price of the contract. Once you pay it to the seller, it is gone. In futures, you pay a margin (security deposit) to ensure you will honor the contract. You get your margin back when you close the trade.
**Worked Example:** To buy a Nifty option, you might pay a ₹5,000 premium to the seller. This money is spent. To buy Nifty futures, you deposit ₹1,00,000 margin with your broker. When you sell the futures contract, you get your ₹1,00,000 back (plus or minus your trading profit/loss).
