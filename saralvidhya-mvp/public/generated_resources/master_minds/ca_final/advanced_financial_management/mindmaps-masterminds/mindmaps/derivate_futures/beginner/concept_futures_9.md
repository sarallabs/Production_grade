# Corporate Users

> **Sub-Topic 1.1:** Core Definitions | **Chapter:** Derivates Futures | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1, User (c)
> **Content:** Companies: Hedge inventory risk and currency risk (receivables/payables).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Companies use derivatives to protect their businesses from sudden price shocks, like changes in the cost of raw materials (inventory risk) or fluctuations in foreign exchange rates (currency risk).
- **Analogy:** Think of an Indian IT company like Infosys that gets paid in US Dollars. If the dollar rate drops suddenly, they lose money. So they sign a contract with a bank to lock in the dollar rate today, ensuring their rupee income remains safe.
- **Key Takeaway:** Corporates use derivatives as a shield to keep their costs and revenues stable, not for gambling.

### 🔍 Detailed View
A manufacturing company that needs copper will buy copper futures on the commodity exchange. This locks in the price, protecting the company if copper prices spike. Similarly, an Indian exporter expecting a payment of $1 million in three months will sell USD/INR futures to lock in the exchange rate today, protecting their profit margin from a strengthening rupee.
