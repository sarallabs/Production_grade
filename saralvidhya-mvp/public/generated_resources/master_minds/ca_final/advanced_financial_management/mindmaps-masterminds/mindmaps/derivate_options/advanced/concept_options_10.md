# MTM (Mark-to-Market)

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.3:** Futures vs. Options | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Advanced

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Futures require daily MTM cash settlement of profits and losses. Option buyers do not face MTM adjustments, but option sellers are subject to margin requirements.
**Key Takeaway:** Option buying avoids daily cash outflow risks; futures require continuous liquidity management.
**Analogy:** A daily invoice for actual energy usage (Futures) vs. a prepaid license fee with no subsequent charges (Options Buying).
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Under SEBI risk management rules, futures positions are marked-to-market daily at the closing price, and cash is debited or credited. Option buyers pay the premium upfront, and their maximum loss is capped, so they face no MTM cash calls. Option sellers must maintain margin and face MTM calls because their risk is uncapped.
**Worked Example:** A proprietary trading desk buys 10 lots of Nifty Futures. If Nifty drops 200 points in a day, the desk must settle a ₹50,000 cash debit by the next morning. If they had bought 10 lots of Nifty Calls instead, they would face zero MTM cash debits, even if the option premium dropped significantly on paper.
