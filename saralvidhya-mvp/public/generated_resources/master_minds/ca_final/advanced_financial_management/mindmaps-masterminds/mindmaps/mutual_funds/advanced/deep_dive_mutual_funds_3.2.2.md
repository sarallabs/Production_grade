### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

Growth funds typically buy stocks with high P/E and P/B ratios. The portfolio is positioned in sectors with high expansion rates (e.g., tech, consumption). These portfolios have high systematic sensitivity (Beta > 1) and are priced based on long-duration cash flow expectations. Thus, they are highly sensitive to changes in long-term interest rates (discount rates).

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

Aggressive funds systematically tilt towards high-volatility, small-cap, or cyclical companies. The portfolio beta ($\beta_p$) is maintained significantly above 1.0. These funds may have high portfolio turnover ratios ($>150\%$), indicating active tactical trading. They face high maximum drawdowns (MDD) and require a long holding period to recover from market corrections.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

Under SEBI's 2020 mandate, Multi-Cap funds must allocate:
Large-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
Mid-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
Small-cap: Minimum 25% of total assets.
The remaining 25% is tactical. This structure forces the fund to maintain a high allocation to smaller, illiquid companies (minimum 50% combined in mid and small-caps), which increases the portfolio's systematic risk and liquidity risk compared to large-cap portfolios.

### Quantitative Modeling & Mathematical Context

> **Model Parameters:** No specific quantitative model details.

The manager targets stocks where current market sentiment is negative but fundamentals remain intact. This involves analyzing debt levels, asset backing, and cyclical recovery timelines. The primary risks are:
1. **Value Trap:** A stock that appears cheap but continues to deteriorate.
2. **Timing Risk:** The contrarian theme may take several years to play out, leading to prolonged underperformance against growth benchmarks.
