### 📋 Summary View

#### 🔸 Open-End Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Schemes with variable capital structures where units are continuously created and destroyed based on investor cash flows, priced at daily NAV.
**Key Takeaway:** Capital size is dynamic; asset-liability management (ALM) is critical due to daily redemption risk.
**Analogy:** A corporate bank account with instant-access credit facilities.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Closed-End Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Schemes with a fixed unit capital and maturity date, trading on exchanges to provide liquidity while keeping the fund assets locked.
**Key Takeaway:** Capital size is static; shields the portfolio from redemption-induced asset liquidations.
**Analogy:** A venture capital fund or private equity closed structure with a fixed investment horizon.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

#### 🔸 Interval Funds
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Hybrid structures that remain closed-ended for specified durations and open for transaction windows at predetermined intervals.
**Key Takeaway:** Balances long-term asset positioning with scheduled investor exit windows.
**Analogy:** Private credit funds that offer quarterly redemption gates with strict limits.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View

#### 🔸 Open-End Funds
Open-ended funds have no cap on the number of units. The AMC must manage daily inflows and outflows. To manage redemption risk, managers must match portfolio liquidity with potential outflows. This requires maintaining a portion of assets in cash, cash equivalents (TREPS), or highly liquid blue-chip stocks. Large redemptions during panic periods can force the liquidation of liquid assets first, increasing the concentration of illiquid assets in the remaining portfolio.

#### 🔸 Closed-End Funds
Closed-ended funds lock in investor capital. The fund manager can remain 100% invested in illiquid or high-yield papers (e.g., lower-rated corporate bonds) without keeping cash reserves. However, the secondary market trading price of closed-end units on the exchange often trades at a significant discount to the NAV (often 5% to 15%), reflecting liquidity premiums and lack of market market-makers.

#### 🔸 Interval Funds
The interval periods (e.g., 3 days every quarter) are defined in the Scheme Information Document. During these windows, investors can buy or redeem units at NAV directly from the AMC. The fund manager structures the portfolio's debt maturities to align with these windows, utilizing instruments like commercial papers and short-term deposits that mature just before the interval date to provide liquidity.
