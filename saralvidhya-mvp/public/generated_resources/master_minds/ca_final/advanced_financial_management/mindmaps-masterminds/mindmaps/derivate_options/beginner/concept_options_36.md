# Volatility Must Be Estimated

> **Sub-Topic 2.5.2:** Limitations | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Volatility must be estimated, not observed.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Volatility cannot be looked up like a stock price; it has to be guessed or calculated based on past data, leading to errors.
- **Analogy:** Trying to guess how fast a cricketer will bowl his next ball based on his past matches.
- **Key Takeaway:** Option pricing relies on estimation, not exact observation.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** You can see the current stock price, strike price, interest rate, and time left. But volatility must be estimated. If your estimate is wrong, the BSM option price will be wrong.
**Worked Example:** If you estimate a stock's volatility will be 15% but it turns out to be 30%, you will price the option too low and lose money to other traders who estimated volatility better.
