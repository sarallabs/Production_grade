# Put Buyer Payoff: Max(X - S, 0)

> **Sub-Topic 2.6.1:** Formulas | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 2
> **Content:** Put Buyer Payoff: Max(X - S, 0).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** The payoff for a Put Buyer is the strike price minus the stock price (or zero, whichever is higher).
- **Analogy:** An insurance payout that pays you the difference between your insured value and the current damaged value of your item.
- **Key Takeaway:** You only make profit if the stock falls below your strike price.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** The formula is $Payoff = \max(X - S, 0)$. S is the final stock price, and X is the strike price. If the stock price S is higher than the strike price X, you don't use the option, and the payoff is 0.
**Worked Example:** You buy a ₹800 Put option for a ₹50 premium.
If the stock ends at ₹700: Payoff is $\max(800 - 700, 0) = \text{₹}100$. Net Profit = ₹100 - ₹50 premium = +₹50.
If the stock ends at ₹850: Payoff is $\max(800 - 850, 0) = 0$. Net Profit = 0 - ₹50 premium = -₹50.
