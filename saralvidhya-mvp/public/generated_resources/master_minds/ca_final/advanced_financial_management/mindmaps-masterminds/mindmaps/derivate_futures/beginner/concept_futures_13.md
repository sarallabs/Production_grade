--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 2, Table
> **Content:** Liquidity: Forwards (Less liquid) vs. Futures (More liquid).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Futures are highly liquid, meaning it is very easy to buy or sell them instantly because thousands of people are trading them. Forwards are illiquid; once you enter a contract, it is very hard to exit or sell it to someone else before the contract ends.
- **Analogy:** Futures are like trading gold coins in a busy market—anyone will buy them from you instantly. Forwards are like selling a second-hand custom wooden wardrobe—you have to search for a specific buyer who wants that exact size.
- **Key Takeaway:** Futures can be entered or exited in seconds; Forwards trap you until the contract ends.

### 🔍 Detailed View
Because futures are standardized and traded on an exchange, you don't need to know who the buyer or seller is. You just click 'Sell' on your broker's app and it is sold. In a forward contract, if you want to back out, you must negotiate with the original partner, who might demand a heavy penalty to let you go.
