# Volatility (σ)

> **Sub-Topic 2.3.1:** Five Variables | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Volatility (σ): Volatility rises -> Both Call and Put values rise.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** Volatility measures how wildly the stock price swings. Higher volatility increases the price of both Call and Put options.
- **Analogy:** Insurance premiums shoot up during a severe storm or hurricane season because the risk of damage is higher.
- **Key Takeaway:** Higher stock price swings make options more expensive.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** When a stock is highly volatile (like during corporate results or elections), it has a higher chance of making a massive move. Because options have capped downside but unlimited upside, this uncertainty makes the option contracts much more valuable to buyers, so sellers demand higher premiums.
**Worked Example:** Before India's Union Budget announcement, Nifty options become highly expensive because everyone expects huge swings. Once the budget is announced and the market calms down, option premiums crash rapidly (called an IV crush).
