# Lower Investment

> **Sub-Topic 2.2.1:** Key Benefits | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Advanced

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1
> **Content:** Lower investment compared to futures.

### 📋 Summary View
- **Strategic Context:** **Concept Summary:** Option buying requires significantly less capital than futures because the buyer only pays the premium, not the exchange margin.
**Key Takeaway:** High capital efficiency for directional and volatility trades.
**Analogy:** Buying a ticket to a luxury concert vs. sponsoring the entire stage setup.
- **Quantitative Model:** No specific quantitative model details.
- **Risk/Opportunity:** No specific risk/opportunity details.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Trading futures requires maintaining a margin deposit (SPAN + Exposure margins, usually 15-25% of the contract value). Option buyers only pay the premium, which is a small fraction of the contract value, allowing high capital efficiency.
**Worked Example:** 1 lot of TCS Futures (175 shares) at ₹4,000 requires a margin of ₹1,40,000. An At-the-Money TCS Call option with a premium of ₹80 costs only ₹14,000 (₹80 * 175 shares) to buy, representing a 90% reduction in capital layout.
