### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** Cash market transactions require full capital outlay, while derivatives use leverage, requiring only a fraction of the contract value as margin.
- **Formula/Rule:** Payment: Cash (Full value) vs. Derivatives (Margin/Part of value).
- **Application Context:** Traded on Indian exchanges (NSE/BSE/MCX) with standard contract specifications (Nifty lot size of 25, Bank Nifty lot size of 15).

### 🔍 Detailed View
Leverage ratio is calculated as:
$$\text{Leverage} = \frac{\text{Contract Value}}{\text{Margin Paid}}$$
**Worked Example:**
A trader buys 1 lot of Nifty futures at 22,000.
Contract Value = $22,000 \times 25 = \text{₹}5,50,000$.
Margin Required (15%) = $\text{₹}82,500$.
Leverage = $\frac{5,50,000}{82,500} \approx 6.67$ times.
If Nifty rises by 2%, the contract value becomes $22,440 \times 25 = \text{₹}5,61,000$. The profit is $\text{₹}11,000$.
Return on Capital (Derivatives) = $\frac{11,000}{82,500} \times 100 = 13.33\%$.
Return on Capital (Cash Market) = $2\%$.
However, if Nifty falls by 2%, the loss is also ₹11,000, representing a 13.33% loss of capital.
