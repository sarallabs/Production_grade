# Sub-Topic 3.4.1: Plan Options

> **Reference:** Page 8

---

### 📋 Summary View

#### 🔸 Growth Option
- **Concept:** A plan where all profits earned by the mutual fund are reinvested back into the scheme.
- **Analogy:** Reinvesting the interest earned from a fixed deposit to earn interest-on-interest, rather than spending it.
- **Key Takeaway:** Best for compounding and long-term wealth building.

#### 🔸 Dividend Payout (IDCW Payout) Option
- **Concept:** A plan where the mutual fund pays out part of its profits to investors in cash at regular intervals.
- **Analogy:** An apple tree where you pluck and eat the apples as soon as they grow, rather than planting their seeds to grow more trees.
- **Key Takeaway:** Good for investors who need regular pocket money or income.

#### 🔸 Dividend Reinvestment (IDCW Reinvestment) Option
- **Concept:** A plan where dividends declared by the fund are automatically used to buy more units of the same fund.
- **Analogy:** Getting bonus points at a supermarket that are immediately converted into more products, instead of getting cashback.
- **Key Takeaway:** You get more units instead of cash in your bank.

---

### 🔍 Detailed View

#### 🔸 Growth Option
In the Growth Option, you do not receive any intermediate payments. The value of your investment grows over time. The NAV of a growth option is always higher than the dividend options of the same fund. It is highly tax-efficient because you only pay tax when you sell the units.

#### 🔸 Dividend Payout (IDCW Payout) Option
Under SEBI rules, this is now officially called the **Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal (IDCW) Payout** option. Dividends are paid out of the fund's realized profits. However, dividends are not guaranteed and are taxed at your slab rate (up to 30%+), making this plan less tax-efficient than the Growth option.

#### 🔸 Dividend Reinvestment (IDCW Reinvestment) Option
Officially called the **IDCW Reinvestment** option. Instead of sending cash to your bank account, the AMC uses the dividend money to buy additional units at the current NAV. Your total unit count increases, but the NAV per unit drops proportionally. The transaction is still taxed as dividend income.
