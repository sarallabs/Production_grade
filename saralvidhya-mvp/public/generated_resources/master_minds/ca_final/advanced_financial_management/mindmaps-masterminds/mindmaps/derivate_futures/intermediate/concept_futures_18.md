# Minimum Transaction Quantities

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Quantity: Cash (Single share possible) vs. Derivatives (Minimum one lot).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** The cash market permits trading of single units, whereas the derivatives market enforces standardized lot sizes defined by the exchange.
- **Formula/Rule:** Quantity: Cash (Single share possible) vs. Derivatives (Minimum one lot).
- **Application Context:** Traded on Indian exchanges (NSE/BSE/MCX) with standard contract specifications (Nifty lot size of 25, Bank Nifty lot size of 15).

### 🔍 Detailed View
This difference in transaction quantities affects the capital required to trade:
**Cash Market**: Investors can purchase a single share (e.g., 1 share of Tata Motors). This makes it accessible to retail investors with limited capital.
**Derivatives Market**: Trading is restricted to lot sizes. For example:
**Nifty 50 Futures**: Lot size is 25. At a spot price of 22,000, the contract value is $22,000 \times 25 = \text{₹}5,50,000$.
**Bank Nifty Futures**: Lot size is 15. At a spot price of 48,000, the contract value is $48,000 \times 15 = \text{₹}7,20,000$.
Traders must deposit the required margin to trade one lot, and cannot trade fractional lots.
