# Strike Price (X)

> **Sub-Topic 2.3.1:** Five Variables | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Beginner

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 3
> **Content:** Strike Price (X): X rises -> Call Value falls, Put Value rises.

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept:** The Strike Price is the pre-agreed locked price of the option. For calls, lower strike options are more expensive. For puts, higher strike options are more expensive.
- **Analogy:** A discount coupon for ₹500 off is much more expensive/valuable than a coupon for ₹50 off.
- **Key Takeaway:** The target locked price determines the starting value of the option.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** The strike price is fixed in the contract. A Call option that lets you buy Reliance at ₹2,800 is much more valuable than a Call option that lets you buy Reliance at ₹3,000 when the market price is ₹2,900.
**Worked Example:** If Reliance is trading at ₹2,900:
A ₹2,800 Call option (allowing you to buy cheap) will have a high premium (e.g., ₹120).
A ₹3,000 Call option (allowing you to buy at a higher price than market) will have a low premium (e.g., ₹20).
