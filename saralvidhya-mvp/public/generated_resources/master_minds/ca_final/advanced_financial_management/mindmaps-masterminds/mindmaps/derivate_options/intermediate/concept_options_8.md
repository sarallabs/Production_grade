# Obligation

> **Sub-Topic 2.1.3:** Futures vs. Options | **Chapter:** Derivatives Options | **Level:** Intermediate

--- 

### 📝 Source Institutional Content
> **Reference:** Page 1
> **Content:** Obligation: Futures (Both parties obligated) vs. Options (Buyer has right, no obligation).

--- 

### 📋 Summary View
- **Concept Summary:** Futures bind both parties to execute the trade at maturity. Options bind only the seller, while the buyer retains complete discretion.
- **Formula/Rule:** Standard guidelines apply.
- **Application Context:** No specific application context available.

### 🔍 Detailed View
**Explanation:** Futures are linear instruments; there is no choice. Options are non-linear, introducing choice. The buyer pays for this choice via the premium. The seller receives the premium as compensation for taking on the obligation.
**Worked Example:** In a futures contract, if Nifty moves against you by 500 points, you must pay the ₹12,500 loss. In a Nifty Call option bought for ₹150 (₹3,750 total cost), if Nifty drops 500 points, you simply let the option expire and lose only ₹3,750.
