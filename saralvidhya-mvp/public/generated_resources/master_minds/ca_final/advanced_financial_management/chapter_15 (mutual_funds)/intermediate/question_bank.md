### Question 1: Net Asset Value (NAV) Calculation with Accrued Expenses and Liabilities
**Cindrella Mutual Fund** has the following assets in **Scheme Rudolf** at the close of business on 31st March, 2026:

| Security / Asset | No. of Shares | Market Price per Share (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Share A Ltd | 5,00,000 | 120 |
| Share B Ltd | 3,00,000 | 350 |
| Share C Ltd | 2,00,000 | 450 |
| Share D Ltd | 1,50,000 | 600 |

**Additional Information:**
* Total number of units outstanding of Scheme Rudolf: 10,00,000 units.
* Accrued expenses of the scheme: ₹2,50,000.
* Other liabilities of the scheme: ₹2,00,000.
* Cash and bank balance held: ₹15,00,000.

**Required:**
Calculate the Net Asset Value (NAV) per unit of Scheme Rudolf as of 31st March, 2026.

#### Solution:
**Step 1: Calculate the Total Market Value of Portfolio Investments**
$$\text{Market Value of Share A Ltd} = 5,00,000 \times ₹120 = ₹6,00,00,000$$
$$\text{Market Value of Share B Ltd} = 3,00,000 \times ₹350 = ₹10,50,00,000$$
$$\text{Market Value of Share C Ltd} = 2,00,000 \times ₹450 = ₹9,00,00,000$$
$$\text{Market Value of Share D Ltd} = 1,50,000 \times ₹600 = ₹9,00,00,000$$
$$\text{Total Value of Investments} = 6,00,00,000 + 10,50,00,000 + 9,00,00,000 + 9,00,00,000 = ₹34,50,00,000$$

**Step 2: Calculate the Net Assets of the Scheme**
$$\text{Net Assets} = \text{Total Value of Investments} + \text{Cash \& Bank Balance} - \text{Accrued Expenses} - \text{Other Liabilities}$$
$$\text{Net Assets} = ₹34,50,00,000 + ₹15,00,00,000 - ₹2,50,000 - ₹2,00,000$$
$$\text{Net Assets} = ₹34,62,50,000 - ₹4,50,000 = ₹34,58,00,000$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Net Asset Value (NAV) per Unit**
$$\text{NAV per Unit} = $\frac{\text{Net Assets of the Scheme}}{\text{Total Number of Outstanding Units}}$$
$$\text{NAV per Unit} = $\frac{₹34,58,00,000}{10,00,00,000 \text{ units}} = ₹345.80$$

**Answer:** The NAV per unit of Scheme Rudolf as of 31st March, 2026 is **₹345.80**.

### Question 2: Front-End and Back-End Load Calculations
An investor wants to invest in the Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) of a mutual fund. The face value of a unit is ₹10. You are given the following prices:
* Public Offer Price (POP) / Subscription Price: ₹10.204 per unit
* Net Asset Value (NAV): ₹10.000 per unit
* Redemption / Repurchase Price: ₹9.800 per unit

**Required:**
1. Calculate the Front-end Load percentage.
2. Calculate the Back-end (Exit) Load percentage.

#### Solution:
**1. Calculation of Front-end Load**
The Public Offer Price (POP) is the price paid by the investor to buy units, which includes the front-end load.
$$\text{Public Offer Price (POP)} = $\frac{\text{NAV}}{1 - \text{Front-end Load Fraction}}$$
Alternatively, the standard industry formula is:
$$\text{POP} = \text{NAV} \times (1 + \text{Front-end Load \%})$$
Using the latter standard formulation:
$$\text{Front-end Load amount} = \text{POP} - \text{NAV} = ₹10.204 - ₹10.00 = ₹0.204$$
$$\text{Front-end Load \%} = $\frac{\text{Front-end Load amount}}{\text{NAV}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Front-end Load \%} = $\frac{₹0.204}{₹10.00} \times 100 = 2.04\%$$

**2. Calculation of Back-end (Exit) Load**
The Redemption Price is the net amount received by the investor upon selling the units, which is after deducting the back-end (exit) load.
$$\text{Redemption Price} = \text{NAV} \times (1 - \text{Back-end Load \%})$$
$$\text{Back-end Load amount} = \text{NAV} - \text{Redemption Price} = ₹10.00 - ₹9.80 = ₹0.200$$
$$\text{Back-end Load \%} = $\frac{\text{Back-end Load amount}}{\text{NAV}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Back-end Load \%} = $\frac{₹0.200}{₹10.00} \times 100 = 2.00\%$$

**Answer:**
* The Front-end Load is **2.04%**.
* The Back-end (Exit) Load is **2.00%**.

### Question 3: Plan Evaluation (Dividend Plan vs. Bonus Plan)
On 1st April, 2021, an AMC floated two schemes: **Dividend Plan** and **Bonus Plan**. The face value of each unit is ₹10. Mr. R invested ₹5,00,000 in the Dividend Plan and ₹10,00,000 in the Bonus Plan. The purchase NAV of the Dividend Plan was ₹46, and for the Bonus Plan, it was ₹42. Both plans matured on 31st March, 2026. The particulars of distributions over the 5-year period are as follows:

| Date | Dividend Plan (Dividend declared on FV of ₹10) | Bonus Plan (Bonus Ratio) | NAV of Dividend Plan (₹) | NAV of Bonus Plan (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1-04-2021 | *Investment Date* | *Investment Date* | 46.00 | 42.00 |
| 31-03-2022 | 12% | - | 47.50 | 45.00 |
| 31-03-2023 | 15% | 1:5 | 48.20 | 40.00 |
| 31-03-2024 | 18% | - | 49.00 | 43.50 |
| 31-03-2025 | 20% | 1:4 | 47.80 | 38.00 |
| 31-03-2026 | *Maturity Date* | *Maturity Date* | 49.00 | 44.00 |

*Note: Dividends under the Dividend Plan are paid out in cash. Under the Bonus Plan, bonus units are issued and added to the unit balance.*

**Required:**
Calculate the effective simple average yield per annum and the compounded annualized growth rate (CAGR) for both plans.

#### Solution:

#### **Part 1: Dividend Plan (Investment: ₹5,00,000; Entry NAV: ₹46.00)**
* **Initial Units Allotted:**
  $$\text{Units} = \frac{₹5,00,00,00}{₹46.00} = 10,869.565 \text{ units}$$

* **Cash Dividend Received (Dividends are declared on Face Value of ₹10):**
  * **Year 1 (31-03-2022):** $12\% \text{ of } ₹10 \text{ per unit} = ₹1.20 \text{ per unit}$
    $$\text{Total Dividend} = 10,869.565 \times ₹1.20 = ₹13,043.48$$
  * **Year 2 (31-03-2023):** $15\% \text{ of } ₹10 \text{ per unit} = ₹1.50 \text{ per unit}$
    $$\text{Total Dividend} = 10,869.565 \times ₹1.50 = ₹16,304.35$$
  * **Year 3 (31-03-2024):** $18\% \text{ of } ₹10 \text{ per unit} = ₹1.80 \text{ per unit}$
    $$\text{Total Dividend} = 10,869.565 \times ₹1.80 = ₹19,565.22$$
  * **Year 4 (31-03-2025):** $20\% \text{ of } ₹10 \text{ per unit} = ₹2.00 \text{ per unit}$
    $$\text{Total Dividend} = 10,869.565 \times ₹2.00 = ₹21,739.13$$
  * **Total Cash Dividends Received over 5 years:**
    $$\text{Total Cash Dividends} = 13,043.48 + 16,304.35 + 19,565.22 + 21,739.13 = ₹70,652.18$$

* **Value at Maturity (31-03-2026):**
  $$\text{Value} = 10,869.565 \text{ units} \times ₹49.00 = ₹5,32,608.69$$

* **Total Return (Capital Gains + Dividends):**
  $$\text{Total Return} = (₹5,32,608.69 - ₹5,00,000) + ₹70,652.18 = ₹32,608.69 + ₹70,652.18 = ₹1,03,260.87$$

* **Effective Simple Annual Yield:**
  $$\text{Simple Annual Yield} = $\frac{\text{Total Return}}{\text{Initial Investment} \times \text{No. of Years}} \times 100$$
  $$\text{Simple Annual Yield} = $\frac{₹1,03,260.87}{₹5,00,000 \times 5} \times 100 = 4.13\% \text{ p.a.}$$

---

#### **Part 2: Bonus Plan (Investment: ₹10,00,000; Entry NAV: ₹42.00)**
* **Initial Units Allotted:**
  $$\text{Units} = \frac{₹10,00,000}{₹42.00} = 23,809.524 \text{ units}$$

* **Bonus Units Reconciliation:**

    $$\text{Bonus Units} = 23,809.524 \times \frac{1}{5} = 4,761.905 \text{ units}$$

* **Value at Maturity (31-03-2026):**

* **Total Return (Capital Growth only, no cash dividends):**
  $$\text{Total Return} = ₹15,71,428.58 - ₹10,00,000 = ₹5,71,428.58$$

* **Effective Simple Annual Yield:**
  $$\text{Simple Annual Yield} = $\frac{₹5,71,428.58}{₹10,00,000 \times 5} \times 100 = 11.43\% \text{ p.a.}$$

**Summary Table of Results:**

### Question 4: Dividend Equalization in Open-Ended Schemes
On 1st January, 2026, an open-ended mutual fund scheme had 300 lakh outstanding units with a Net Asset Value (NAV) of ₹20.25. 
* At the end of January, the scheme issued 5 lakh units at the opening NAV plus a front-end load of 2%, adjusted for dividend equalization.
* At the end of February, the scheme repurchased 2.5 lakh units at the opening NAV less a 2% exit load, adjusted for dividend equalization.
* At the end of March, the scheme distributed 70% of its available income.

The operating results for the quarter (January to March) are:
* Net Income for January: ₹24,00,000
* Net Income for February: ₹36,40,000
* Net Income for March: ₹42,00,000

**Required:**
1. Calculate the income available for distribution.
2. Determine the issue price of units at the end of January.
3. Determine the repurchase price of units at the end of February.
4. Calculate the closing Net Assets of the scheme at the end of March.

#### Solution:

#### **1. Dividend Equalization Concept**
When new units are issued, the new investor must contribute a proportional share of the income accumulated in the scheme up to the entry date. This is called the "Dividend Equalization Credit." Similarly, when units are repurchased, the outgoing investor is paid their share of accumulated income, which is the "Dividend Equalization Debit." This ensures all unit holders receive the same dividend rate.

#### **2. Calculation of Issue Price at the end of January**
* **Opening NAV:** ₹20.25
* **Accumulated Income for January:** ₹24,00,000
* **Outstanding Units in January:** 300 lakh units
* **Dividend Equalization Rate (DER) for January:**
  $$\text{DER}_{\text{Jan}} = \frac{₹24,00,000}{300,00,000 \text{ units}} = ₹0.08 \text{ per unit}$$
* **Front-end Load (2% of NAV):**
  $$\text{Load} = 2\% \text{ of } ₹20.25 = ₹0.405$$
* **Issue Price:**
  $$\text{Issue Price} = \text{NAV} + \text{Load} + \text{DER}_{\text{Jan}} = ₹20.25 + ₹0.405 + ₹0.08 = ₹20.735 \text{ per unit}$$

#### **3. Calculation of Repurchase Price at the end of February**
* **Total Outstanding Units in February:** $300 + 5 = 305 \text{ lakh units}$
* **Accumulated Income for February:**
  $$\text{Opening Income Balance} = ₹24,00,000$$
  $$\text{Add: Equalization received on 5 lakh units issued} = 5,00,000 \times ₹0.08 = ₹40,000$$
  $$\text{Add: Net Income for February} = ₹36,40,000$$
  $$\text{Total Accumulated Income by end of Feb} = 24,00,000 + 40,000 + 36,40,000 = ₹60,80,000$$
* **Dividend Equalization Rate (DER) for February:**
  $$\text{DER}_{\text{Feb}} = \frac{₹60,80,000}{305,00,000 \text{ units}} = ₹0.1993 \text{ per unit}$$
* **Exit Load (2% of NAV):**
  $$\text{Load} = 2\% \text{ of } ₹20.25 = ₹0.405$$
* **Repurchase Price:**
  $$\text{Repurchase Price} = \text{NAV} - \text{Exit Load} + \text{DER}_{\text{Feb}} = ₹20.25 - ₹0.405 + ₹0.1993 = ₹20.0443 \text{ per unit}$$

#### **4. Income Available for Distribution at the end of March**
* **Outstanding Units in March:** $305 - 2.5 = 302.5 \text{ lakh units}$
* **Income Balance Reconciliation:**
  * Accumulated Income up to February: ₹60,80,000
  * Less: Equalization paid on 2.5 lakh units repurchased: $2,50,000 \times ₹0.1993 = ₹49,825$
  * Add: Net Income for March: ₹42,00,000
  * **Total Income Available for Distribution:**
    $$\text{Income Available} = ₹60,80,000 - ₹49,825 + ₹42,00,000 = ₹1,02,30,175$$
* **Dividend Distributed (70% of Available Income):**
  $$\text{Dividend} = 70\% \text{ of } ₹1,02,30,175 = ₹71,61,122.50$$
* **Retained Income in Scheme:**
  $$\text{Retained Income} = 30\% \text{ of } ₹1,02,30,175 = ₹30,69,052.50$$

#### **5. Closing Net Assets at the end of March**
* **Capital Account:**
  * Opening Value ($300 \text{ lakh} \times ₹20.25$): ₹6,075.00 lakh
  * Add: Proceeds from 5 lakh units issued (excluding equalization): $5 \text{ lakh} \times (₹20.25 + ₹0.405) = ₹103.275 \text{ lakh}$
  * Less: Payment for 2.5 lakh units repurchased (excluding equalization): $2.5 \text{ lakh} \times (₹20.25 - ₹0.405) = ₹49.6125 \text{ lakh}$
  * **Net Capital Value:** $6,075.00 + 103.275 - 49.6125 = ₹6,128.6625 \text{ lakh}$
* **Income Account:**
  * Add: Retained Income = ₹30,69,052.50 = ₹30.6905 lakh
* **Total Closing Net Assets:**
  $$\text{Total Net Assets} = 6,128.6625 + 30.6905 = ₹6,159.353 \text{ lakh (or } ₹61,59,35,300)$$

### Question 5: Performance Evaluation and Ranking (Sharpe, Treynor, and Jensen's Alpha)
The following performance details are available for 5 mutual fund portfolios over the last year. The risk-free rate of return ($R_f$) is 7%.

| Portfolio | Return ($R_p$) (%) | Standard Deviation ($\sigma_p$) (%) | Beta ($\beta_p$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fund A | 15.0 | 18.0 | 1.10 |
| Fund B | 13.0 | 12.0 | 0.90 |
| Fund C | 18.0 | 22.0 | 1.30 |
| Fund D | 11.0 | 8.0 | 0.60 |
| Fund E (Market) | 12.0 | 15.0 | 1.00 |

**Required:**
Calculate the Sharpe Ratio, Treynor Ratio, and Jensen's Alpha for each portfolio, and rank them. Work strictly with two decimals.

#### Solution:
* **Risk-free Rate ($R_f$):** 7%
* **Market Return ($R_m$):** 12%

#### **Formulas:**
1. **Sharpe Ratio** = $\frac{R_p - R_f}{\sigma_p}$ (Measures excess return per unit of total risk)
2. **Treynor Ratio** = $\frac{R_p - R_f}{\beta_p}$ (Measures excess return per unit of systematic risk)
3. **Jensen's Alpha ($\alpha$)** = $R_p - [R_f + \beta_p(R_m - R_f)]$ (Measures abnormal return compared to CAPM prediction)

#### **Calculations:**

* **Fund A:**
  $$\text{Sharpe} = \frac{15 - 7}{18} = 0.44$$
  $$\text{Treynor} = \frac{15 - 7}{1.10} = 7.27$$
  $$\text{Alpha} = 15 - [7 + 1.10(12 - 7)] = 15 - [7 + 5.50] = +2.50\%$$

* **Fund B:**
  $$\text{Sharpe} = \frac{13 - 7}{12} = 0.50$$
  $$\text{Treynor} = \frac{13 - 7}{0.90} = 6.67$$
  $$\text{Alpha} = 13 - [7 + 0.90(12 - 7)] = 13 - [7 + 4.50] = +1.50\%$$

* **Fund C:**
  $$\text{Sharpe} = \frac{18 - 7}{22} = 0.50$$
  $$\text{Treynor} = \frac{18 - 7}{1.30} = 8.46$$
  $$\text{Alpha} = 18 - [7 + 1.30(12 - 7)] = 18 - [7 + 6.50] = +4.50\%$$

* **Fund D:**
  $$\text{Sharpe} = \frac{11 - 7}{8} = 0.50$$
  $$\text{Treynor} = \frac{11 - 7}{0.60} = 6.67$$
  $$\text{Alpha} = 11 - [7 + 0.60(12 - 7)] = 11 - [7 + 3.00] = +1.00\%$$

* **Fund E (Market):**
  $$\text{Sharpe} = \frac{12 - 7}{15} = 0.33$$
  $$\text{Treynor} = \frac{12 - 7}{1.00} = 5.00$$
  $$\text{Alpha} = 12 - [7 + 1.00(12 - 7)] = 0.00\%$$

#### **Ranking Summary Table:**

| Fund | Sharpe Ratio | Sharpe Rank | Treynor Ratio | Treynor Rank | Jensen's Alpha (%) | Alpha Rank |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **A** | 0.44 | V | 7.27 | II | +2.50% | II |
| **B** | 0.50 | I (tie) | 6.67 | III (tie) | +1.50% | III |
| **C** | 0.50 | I (tie) | 8.46 | I | +4.50% | I |
| **D** | 0.50 | I (tie) | 6.67 | III (tie) | +1.00% | IV |
| **E (Market)**| 0.33 | VI | 5.00 | V | 0.00% | V |

**Critical Observation:** 
Funds B, C, and D all share the same Sharpe Ratio (0.50), indicating identical risk-adjusted performance based on *total risk*. However, when ranked by Treynor Ratio (systematic risk) and Jensen's Alpha, Fund C emerges as the clear winner. This divergence suggests that Fund C is better diversified, eliminating more unsystematic risk.

### Question 6: Capital Protection Oriented Fund Allocation
A Mutual Fund house plans to launch a 3-year **Capital Protection Oriented Fund (CPOF)**.
* The face value of the units is ₹10.
* The prevailing interest rate on AAA-rated corporate bonds with a 3-year maturity is 9% per annum (compounded annually).
* The remaining corpus of the fund will be invested in equity growth instruments to generate additional returns.

**Required:**
1. Determine the proportion of the initial fund corpus that must be invested in AAA-rated corporate bonds to ensure complete protection of the principal at maturity.
2. Determine the equity-to-debt asset allocation ratio.
3. If the equity portion of the portfolio grows by 45% over the 3-year period, and the bond portion behaves exactly as expected, what will be the final NAV per unit at maturity? (Assume zero terminal value for any expenses).

#### Solution:
**1. Proportion to be invested in AAA-rated bonds**
To protect the initial capital of ₹10 per unit, the bond component must grow to ₹10 in 3 years at a yield of 9% p.a.
Let $P$ be the present value of the bond component.
$$P \times (1 + r)^n = \text{Protected Capital}$$
$$P \times (1 + 0.09)^3 = ₹10$$
$$P \times 1.2950 = ₹10$$
$$P = \frac{₹10}{1.295029} = ₹7.7218 \text{ per unit}$$

$$\text{Bond Allocation Percentage} = $\frac{₹7.7218}{₹10} \times 100 = 77.22\%$$

**2. Equity-to-Debt Asset Allocation**
$$\text{Equity Component per Unit} = \text{Total Issue Price} - \text{Bond Component} = ₹10.0000 - ₹7.7218 = ₹2.2782$$
$$\text{Equity Allocation Percentage} = $\frac{₹2.2782}{₹10} \times 100 = 22.78\%$$
* **Asset Allocation Ratio (Equity : Debt)** = $22.78\% : 77.22\%$ or **22.78 : 77.22** (approximately **1 : 3.39**).

**3. Value of Portfolio at Maturity (NAV per unit)**
* **Bond Component Value at Maturity:**
  $$\text{Value} = ₹7.7218 \times (1.09)^3 = ₹10.00$$
* **Equity Component Value at Maturity (growing by 45%):**
  $$\text{Value} = ₹2.2782 \times (1 + 0.45) = ₹2.2782 \times 1.45 = ₹3.3034$$
* **Total Value of Portfolio per unit at Maturity:**
  $$\text{NAV at Maturity} = \text{Bond Value} + \text{Equity Value} = ₹10.00 + ₹3.3034 = ₹13.3034$$

**Answer:**
* Bond allocation required: **77.22%** (₹7.7218 per unit).
* Equity allocation: **22.78%** (₹2.2782 per unit).
* The final NAV at maturity will be **₹13.30** per unit.

### Question 7: Back-Solving for Original Investment and Entry Load
During the year 2025, an investor made an investment in a mutual fund scheme.
* The investor received dividends and capital gains totaling ₹3.00 per unit during the year. These distributions were entirely re-invested in the same scheme at the year-end NAV of ₹23.75.
* At the end of the year, the investor held a total of 26,750 units.
* The NAV of the scheme had appreciated by 18.75% during the year.
* An entry load of ₹0.05 per unit was charged at the time of the original investment.
* The investor has lost the original transaction records.

**Required:**
Determine:
1. The opening NAV of the scheme at the beginning of the year.
2. The number of units originally allotted.
3. The total amount of initial investment made.
4. The total entry load paid by the investor.

#### Solution:
**Step 1: Calculate the Opening NAV**
The NAV appreciated by 18.75% during the year, ending at ₹23.75.
$$\text{Ending NAV} = \text{Opening NAV} \times (1 + \text{NAV Appreciation \%})$$
$$₹23.75 = \text{Opening NAV} \times (1 + 0.1875)$$
$$₹23.75 = \text{Opening NAV} \times 1.1875$$
$$\text{Opening NAV} = $\frac{₹23.75}{1.1875} = ₹20.00$$

**Step 2: Set up equations to find units allotted**
Let $X$ be the number of units originally purchased.
* The total dividend and capital gains received:
  $$\text{Total Distribution Received} = X \times ₹3.00$$
* These distributions are reinvested at the year-end NAV of ₹23.75.
  $$\text{Additional Units Allotted from Reinvestment} = $\frac{X \times ₹3.00}{₹23.75} = 0.1263158 \times X \text{ units}$$
* Total units at the end of the year:
  $$\text{Total Units} = \text{Original Units} + \text{Reinvested Units}$$
  $$26,750 = X + 0.1263158 \times X$$
  $$26,750 = X \times (1 + 0.1263158) = 1.1263158 \times X$$
  $$X = \frac{26,750}{1.1263158} = 23,750 \text{ units}$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Purchase Price and Total Investment**
* **Purchase Price per Unit** = $\text{Opening NAV} + \text{Entry Load}$
  $$\text{Purchase Price} = ₹20.00 + ₹0.05 = ₹20.05$$
* **Total Investment Made:**
  $$\text{Total Investment} = \text{Original Units} \times \text{Purchase Price per Unit}$$
  $$\text{Total Investment} = 23,750 \text{ units} \times ₹20.05 = ₹4,76,187.50$$

**Step 4: Calculate the Total Entry Load Paid**
$$\text{Total Entry Load} = \text{Original Units} \times \text{Entry Load per Unit}$$
$$\text{Total Entry Load} = 23,750 \times ₹0.05 = ₹1,187.50$$

**Answer:**
1. Opening NAV: **₹20.00**
2. Original units allotted: **23,750 units**
3. Initial investment amount: **₹4,76,187.50**
4. Total entry load paid: **₹1,187.50**

### Question 8: Analysis of Side-Pocketing in Mutual Funds
During a sudden liquidity crisis, a prominent commercial paper issuer defaulted on its repayment obligations. A debt mutual fund had 8% exposure to this paper, causing panic redemptions. The fund house implemented "side-pocketing" for the scheme.

**Required:**
1. What is the mechanism of side-pocketing in mutual funds?
2. How does side-pocketing benefit existing, incoming, and outgoing investors?
3. Discuss the potential disadvantages or moral hazards associated with this practice.

#### Solution:
**1. Mechanism of Side-Pocketing**
Side-pocketing is the segregation of risky or distressed assets (junk/default grade) from the healthy, liquid assets in a mutual fund scheme. 
* **The Split:** The fund's portfolio is divided into a "good pocket" (liquid securities and cash) and a "bad/segregated pocket" (risky/defaulted security).
* **Subscriptions & Redemptions:** The "good pocket" continues normal operations. The "segregated pocket" is locked—no new subscriptions or redemptions are allowed. 
* **Realization:** When any recovery is made from the bad asset, the proceeds are distributed to the investors who held units at the time of segregation.

**2. Stakeholder Benefits**
* **Existing Investors:** It prevents a fire sale of liquid assets to meet redemptions, which would otherwise leave remaining investors holding only junk assets. It also allows them to trade the healthy part of their holding.
* **Incoming Investors:** New investors can buy into the "good pocket" without inheriting the losses of the defaulted asset or paying for legacy bad debt.
* **Outgoing (Redeeming) Investors:** Investors who want to exit can redeem their units in the healthy fund immediately, while retaining their right to future recovery proceeds from the segregated pool.

**3. Disadvantages and Moral Hazards**
* **Fund Manager Laxity (Moral Hazard):** Knowing they can isolate bad assets might encourage fund managers to take excessive risks to chase higher yields.
* **Complex Accounting & Cost:** Maintaining two separate NAVs and administering two distinct portfolios increases operational overhead and can confuse retail investors.
* **Illiquidity of Segregated Units:** Investors' money in the bad pocket is locked up indefinitely with no secondary market for exit.

### Question 9: Tracking Error in Index Mutual Funds
An index fund tracking the Nifty 50 Index has reported an annualized tracking error of 1.45%, while its peer group average tracking error is 0.25%.

**Required:**
1. Define tracking error and outline the mathematical basis of its computation.
2. List four common reasons why an index fund experiences tracking error.
3. As a financial advisor, what conclusions would you draw about this fund's management?

#### Solution:
**1. Definition and Mathematical Basis**
Tracking error measures the volatility of the difference in returns between a mutual fund and its benchmark index. 
Mathematically, it is the standard deviation of the difference in returns:
$$\text{Differential Return } (D_t) = R_{\text{fund}, t} - R_{\text{benchmark}, t}$$
$$\text{Average Differential Return } (\bar{D}) = $\frac{\sum D_t}{n}$$
$$\text{Tracking Error} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (D_t - \bar{D})^2}{n - 1}}$$
A lower tracking error indicates that the fund replicates its benchmark index closely.

**2. Reasons for Tracking Error**
* **Transaction Costs:** The fund incurs brokerage and impact costs when buying/selling stocks to align with the index, whereas index returns assume zero transaction cost.
* **Cash Holding:** The fund must hold cash to facilitate daily redemption requests, which creates a drag in a rising market.
* **Fees and Expenses (TER):** Asset Management Fees and administrative expenses reduce the fund's NAV, while the benchmark has no expenses.
* **Corporate Actions & Timing Lags:** Lags in reinvesting dividends paid by constituent companies or delays in executing trades when index constituents change.

**3. Advisor's Analysis**
A tracking error of 1.45% is significantly higher than the peer average of 0.25%. This indicates:
* **Inefficient Replication:** The fund manager is failing to execute trades in line with index changes efficiently.
* **High Transaction Costs/Impact Cost:** The fund might be trading in illiquid hours or paying excessive brokerage.
* **High Cash Drag:** The fund might be holding unnecessarily large cash balances.
* **Recommendation:** Investors should avoid this index fund, as they are paying for passive management but receiving returns that diverge significantly from the index.
