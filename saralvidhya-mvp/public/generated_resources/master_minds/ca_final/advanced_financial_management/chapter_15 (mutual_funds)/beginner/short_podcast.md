Welcome to our quick finance study session. Today, we are breaking down Mutual Funds section of the Advanced Financial Management syllabus, which is all about Mutual Funds. If you are a beginner, this is the perfect place to start.

I am glad we are covering this. I always hear about mutual funds, but I want to understand how they actually work. In simple terms, what is a mutual fund?

Think of a mutual fund as a collective investment vehicle. It is a trust that pools together the savings of a large number of investors who share a common financial goal. Instead of you trying to buy twenty different stocks with a small amount of money, you pool your money with thousands of other investors. 

Right, and that pooled money is managed by a professional, correct?

Exactly. The pooled corpus is managed by a professional fund manager who belongs to an Asset Management Company, or AMC. They invest this money in various securities like stocks, bonds, or short-term debt instruments, depending on the specific objective of the scheme. The profits and capital growth generated are then passed back to the unit holders in proportion to the units they hold, after deducting management fees.

That sounds very structured. Who governs all of this to make sure the investors' money is safe?

In India, the entire structure is strictly regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India, or SEBI. The mutual fund itself is set up as a trust. There are four main entities you need to remember for the exam: the Sponsor, the Board of Trustees, the Asset Management Company, and the Custodian.

Let us break those down. What is the Sponsor?

The Sponsor is the promoter who conceives the idea of the mutual fund. They establish the trust and get it registered with SEBI. They also must contribute at least forty percent of the minimum net worth of the AMC.

Okay, so the Sponsor sets it up. Then what do the Trustees do?

The Trustees hold the assets of the fund in fiduciary trust. Fiduciary means they have a legal duty to act in the best interests of the unit holders. They ensure the AMC manages the schemes according to SEBI regulations and the Trust Deed.

And the AMC is the actual investment manager, right?

Spot on. The AMC employs the research analysts and fund managers who make the daily decisions to buy and sell stocks or bonds. They are the brains behind the portfolio.

What about the Custodian? Why do we need them?

The Custodian is an independent agency that takes safe custody of the physical and electronic securities bought by the fund. Because the AMC does not physically hold the securities, the risk of fraud is minimized.

That is a very clean separation of duties. Now, when I invest in a mutual fund, I buy units. How do we know the value of each unit?

That brings us to Net Asset Value, or NAV. NAV is the market value of a single unit of the scheme. It is calculated at the end of every business day. The formula is simple: you take the total market value of all investments, add any accrued income and cash, subtract all liabilities and accrued expenses, and then divide that net assets figure by the total number of outstanding units of the scheme.

So if the net assets are ten crore rupees and there are one crore units, the NAV is ten rupees per unit?

That is correct. NAV represents the fair value of a unit. If you buy or redeem units in an open-ended fund, you do it at NAV-based prices.

What is an open-ended fund compared to a closed-ended fund?

An open-ended fund has no fixed maturity date. You can subscribe to it or redeem your units directly with the fund house on any business day at the prevailing NAV. A closed-ended fund has a fixed maturity, like three or five years. You can only buy units during the initial New Fund Offer, and after that, the units are listed on a stock exchange where you buy or sell them like shares.

That makes perfect sense. In closed-ended funds, the price on the exchange might be different from the NAV, right?

Yes, because the trading price on the stock exchange is driven by supply and demand, whereas the NAV is the actual value of the underlying assets.
