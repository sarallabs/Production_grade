## 1. Introduction to Mutual Funds

A **Mutual Fund** is a financial intermediary that pools the investible savings of multiple investors who share a common investment objective. The pooled funds are managed by professional fund managers who invest them in a diversified portfolio of securities (like stocks, bonds, and money market instruments) to achieve the scheme’s stated investment objective.

### Key Concepts:
* **The Pool:** Investors contribute capital, and in return, they receive "units." The value of the pool increases or decreases based on the market performance of the underlying securities.
* **Proportional Ownership:** Investors are called unit holders. They share the profits, losses, dividends, and interest income generated by the fund in direct proportion to the number of units they hold.
* **Professional Management:** Instead of individuals spending hours analyzing company balance sheets, a professional fund manager backed by a team of analysts makes the investment decisions.

## 2. Structure of Mutual Funds in India

In India, mutual funds are structured as **Trusts** under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. This structure is mandated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) to protect the interests of retail investors. The operations are carried out by four distinct entities:

```
                  ┌──────────────────────┐
                  │       SPONSOR        │
                  │ (Establishes Trust)  │
                  └──────────┬───────────┘
                             │
                             ▼
                  ┌──────────────────────┐
                  │ BOARD OF TRUSTEES    │
                  │(Fiduciary Custody)   │
                  └──────────┬───────────┘
                             │
              ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
              ▼                             ▼
┌───────────────────────────┐ ┌───────────────────────────┐
│ ASSET MANAGEMENT CO (AMC) │ │        CUSTODIAN          │
│   (Investment Manager)    │ │   (Securities Safety)     │
└─────────────┬─────────────┘ └───────────────────────────┘
              │
              ▼
┌───────────────────────────┐
│ REGISTRAR & AGENT (RTA)   │
│  (Investor Recordkeeper)  │
└───────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Sponsor:** 
   * The Sponsor acts like the promoter of a company. It sets up the mutual fund trust and registers it with SEBI.
   * The sponsor must have a track record of financial soundness (e.g., at least 5 years of experience in financial services with positive net worth) and must contribute at least 40% of the net worth of the AMC.

2. **Board of Trustees:**
   * The mutual fund is constituted as a trust, and the trustees hold the assets of the fund in trust for the benefit of the unit holders.
   * The Trustees monitor the AMC’s activities to ensure strict adherence to SEBI regulations and the Trust Deed. At least two-thirds of the trustees must be independent (not associated with the sponsor).

3. **Asset Management Company (AMC):**
   * The AMC is the operational arm of the mutual fund. Appointed by the Trustees and approved by SEBI, it is responsible for launching schemes, pooling funds, and managing the investment portfolio.
   * It employs professional fund managers and analysts to make buy/sell decisions in accordance with the scheme's objectives.

4. **Custodian:**
   * The Custodian is an independent entity (usually a bank or financial institution) that holds physical and electronic custody of the securities (shares, bonds) purchased by the fund.
   * This separation ensures that the AMC does not have physical access to the securities, minimizing the risk of fraud.

5. **Registrar and Transfer Agents (RTA):**
   * RTAs are responsible for back-office investor service operations, including processing application forms, issuing/redeeming units, keeping track of investor details, and distributing dividends or account statements.

## 3. Classification of Mutual Funds

Mutual funds can be categorized based on their structure (function) and their asset class (investment objective).

### A. Classification by Structure (Function)
1. **Open-ended Funds:**
   * These funds have no fixed maturity date.
   * Investors can buy (subscribe) or sell (redeem) units directly from/to the mutual fund house on any business day.
   * The sale and redemption prices are directly tied to the daily Net Asset Value (NAV). The corpus of the fund fluctuates daily based on subscriptions and redemptions.
2. **Closed-ended Funds:**
   * These funds have a stipulated maturity period (e.g., 3 to 7 years).
   * Subscription is open only during the initial New Fund Offer (NFO). Once the NFO closes, new investors cannot buy units directly from the fund, nor can existing investors redeem units before maturity.
   * To provide liquidity, the units are listed and traded on stock exchanges. The market trading price may differ from the fund's NAV due to supply-demand dynamics.
3. **Interval Funds:**
   * These funds combine features of both open-ended and closed-ended funds.
   * They are closed-ended for most of the year but open up for subscription and redemption at pre-specified intervals (e.g., for 5 days every quarter) at NAV-based prices.

### B. Classification by Asset Class (Objective)
1. **Equity Funds:** Invest primarily in stocks of listed companies. They aim for long-term capital appreciation but carry higher risk.
   * *Growth Funds:* Focus on capital growth by investing in high-growth companies.
   * *Income/Dividend Yield Funds:* Invest in companies with a track record of paying regular and stable dividends.
   * *Multi-Cap & Flexi-Cap Funds:* Invest across large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap stocks. Multi-cap has fixed mandatory limits (minimum 25% in each cap), whereas Flexi-cap gives the fund manager full flexibility to change allocations dynamically.
   * *Contra Funds:* Follow a contrarian approach, investing in underperforming stocks or sectors currently out of favor but expected to recover.
   * *Sectoral / Thematic Funds:* Focus on a specific sector (e.g., Banking, IT) or a broader theme (e.g., Infrastructure, Manufacturing). They carry concentrated, high risk.
2. **Debt Funds:** Invest in fixed-income securities like government bonds, treasury bills, and corporate debentures. They focus on preserving capital and generating steady income.
   * *Gilt Funds:* Invest exclusively in government securities (G-Secs) and T-bills, eliminating credit (default) risk, though they remain subject to interest rate risk.
   * *Bond Funds:* Invest in corporate bonds of varying maturities and ratings.
3. **Money Market (Liquid) Funds:** 
   * Invest in short-term debt instruments with maturities of up to 91 days (e.g., Certificates of Deposit, Commercial Papers, Treasury Bills).
   * They offer high liquidity, very low volatility, and are used by corporate and retail investors to park idle cash.
4. **Hybrid Funds:**
   * Invest in a mix of equity and debt to balance risk and return.
   * *Capital Protection Oriented Funds:* Closed-ended hybrid funds structured such that the debt portion grows to equal the original principal at maturity (protecting capital), while the equity portion generates upside returns.

## 4. Special and Modern Mutual Fund Schemes

1. **Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs):**
   * Passive funds structured like index funds but traded live on stock exchanges throughout the trading day (unlike traditional mutual funds which are priced only once at the end of the day).
   * A Sponsor works with **Authorized Participants** (institutional market makers) who trade baskets of underlying stocks in exchange for "creation units" of the ETF.
2. **Gold Mutual Funds:**
   * Open-ended schemes that invest in units of Gold ETFs. 
   * They allow retail investors to gain exposure to gold prices without holding physical gold or needing a demat account.
3. **Quant Funds:**
   * Schemes where stock selection is done purely using mathematical models and computer algorithms rather than human research. This cuts down human bias and emotional trading.
4. **Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS):**
   * Diversified equity funds that qualify for tax deductions up to ₹1.5 lakhs under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act.
   * They carry a mandatory **lock-in period of 3 years** from the date of allotment, which is the shortest lock-in among all tax-saving instruments.
5. **Fund of Funds (FoF):**
   * A mutual fund scheme that does not invest in stocks or bonds directly, but instead buys units of other mutual fund schemes. It offers extreme diversification but generally carries a higher expense ratio.
6. **Hedge Funds:**
   * Unregistered, private investment pools aimed at high-net-worth individuals (HNIs).
   * Unlike mutual funds, they are lightly regulated, use high leverage, take both long and short positions, trade derivatives, and charge performance-linked fees.

## 5. Operations and Calculations

### A. Net Asset Value (NAV)
The Net Asset Value is the market value of a single unit of a mutual fund scheme. It is calculated at the close of every business day.

$$\text{NAV per unit} = $\frac{\text{Market Value of Investments} + \text{Receivables & Accrued Income} + \text{Cash Balances} - \text{Accrued Expenses & Liabilities}}{\text{Total Number of Outstanding Units}}$$

### B. Loads (Transaction Costs)
* **Entry Load:** A charge levied when an investor buys units (adds to purchase price). Currently, entry load is banned by SEBI.
  $$\text{Public Offer Price (Purchase Price)} = \text{NAV} \times (1 + \text{Entry Load \%})$$
* **Exit Load:** A charge levied when an investor redeems units before a specified period (deducted from redemption price).
  $$\text{Redemption Price} = \text{NAV} \times (1 - \text{Exit Load \%})$$

### C. Total Expense Ratio (TER)
The annual operating expenses incurred by the AMC to manage the fund are charged to the scheme and expressed as a percentage of the fund’s average daily net assets.
$$\text{Total Expense Ratio (TER)} = $\frac{\text{Annual Recurring Expenses}}{\text{Average Daily Net Assets}} \times 100$$
*Under SEBI guidelines, the maximum TER for equity open-ended funds is capped at 2.50% of the daily average net assets (scaled down as the fund size grows).*

### D. Return Calculations
The return on a mutual fund is composed of capital appreciation and distribution gains:
$$\text{Simple Annual Return (\%)} = $\frac{(NAV_{\text{End}} - NAV_{\text{Start}}) + \text{Dividends Received} + \text{Capital Distributions}}{NAV_{\text{Start}}} \times 100$$

## 6. Mutual Fund Transaction Options

When investing, an investor can choose one of three options, which share the same underlying portfolio but have different accounting and payout structures:
1. **Growth Option:** 
   * Profits are not paid out. They are reinvested back into the fund, leading to an increase in the NAV over time. Ideal for long-term wealth creation.
2. **Dividend Payout Option:**
   * Realized profits are distributed to investors periodically in the form of dividends. When a dividend is paid out, the NAV of the scheme drops by an equivalent amount.
3. **Dividend Reinvestment Option:**
   * Dividends are declared but not paid in cash. Instead, they are automatically used to purchase additional units of the scheme at the prevailing NAV.

## 7. Systematic Investment and Withdrawal Plans

* **Systematic Investment Plan (SIP):**
  * A disciplined method where an investor invests a fixed sum of money at regular intervals (monthly/quarterly) in a mutual fund scheme.
  * *Benefit of Rupee Cost Averaging:* Since the investment amount is fixed, the investor buys more units when prices (NAV) are low and fewer units when prices are high. This averages out the cost of acquisition over time and eliminates the need to time the market.
* **Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP):**
  * Allows an investor to withdraw a fixed amount of money at regular intervals from their accumulated mutual fund corpus.
  * It provides a steady cash flow (like a pension) while the remaining balance continues to grow.

## 8. Valuation of Non-Traded Securities

If a security is not traded on any stock exchange for a period of **60 days** prior to the valuation date, it is classified as a "non-traded security." The AMC must value it "in good faith" using methods approved by its Board and validated by auditors.

### Basic Valuation Principles:
1. **Equity Instruments:** Valued based on the capitalization of earnings or net asset value. P/E ratios of comparable traded securities are used, applying an appropriate discount for lower liquidity.
2. **Debt Instruments:** Valued on a **Yield to Maturity (YTM)** basis, matching comparable traded debt securities with liquidity discounts.
3. **Government Securities (G-Secs):** Valued at YTM based on prevailing market rates.
4. **Call Money & Short-term Bank Deposits:** Valued at cost plus accrued interest (cost + accrual basis).

## 9. Rights and Obligations of Mutual Fund Investors

### Investor Rights:
* **Proportional Beneficial Ownership:** Rights to share in the assets and income of the scheme.
* **Dividend Payout Timelines:** Right to receive dividend warrants within 42 days of declaration.
* **AMC Termination:** 75% of unit holders can vote to terminate the AMC or approve changes to close-ended schemes.
* **Information Access:** Right to receive annual reports, monthly portfolio disclosures, and inspect material contracts/Trust deeds.

### Investor Obligations:
* **Due Diligence:** Carefully read the Scheme Information Document (SID) and Key Information Memorandum (KIM) before investing. Failure to read does not relieve the investor of liability.
* **Monitoring:** Regularly track the NAV and performance of the scheme.

## 10. Performance Indicators (Conceptual Overview)

It is important to know the key performance ratios used to evaluate mutual funds:

1. **Sharpe Ratio:**
   * Measures the excess return earned by a fund per unit of *total risk* (measured by standard deviation $\sigma$).
   * Formula: $\text{Sharpe} = \frac{R_p - R_f}{\sigma_p}$
   * *Higher is better.*

2. **Treynor Ratio:**
   * Measures the excess return earned by a fund per unit of *systematic risk* (measured by Beta $\beta$).
   * Formula: $\text{Treynor} = \frac{R_p - R_f}{\beta_p}$
   * *Higher is better.*

3. **Jensen's Alpha:**
   * Measures the absolute excess return generated by the fund manager compared to the return expected under the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM).
   * Formula: $\text{Alpha} = R_p - [R_f + \beta_p(R_m - R_f)]$
   * *Positive Alpha indicates outperformance; negative Alpha indicates underperformance.*

4. **Tracking Error:**
   * Standard deviation of the difference in returns between a passive fund and its benchmark index.
   * *For passive/index funds, a tracking error near zero is desired.*
