# Mutual Funds — Video Script
---

| Scene | Visual Description | Narration / Voiceover |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scene 1: Introduction** | *Illustration: many small contributions flow into a single pooled portfolio graphic.* | "A mutual fund is a trust that pools savings from investors sharing a common mandate. The pool is invested in a diversified basket of securities under professional management; each investor holds units proportional to contributed capital." |
| **Scene 2: The Core Structure** | *A 3D architectural blueprint of a modern glass skyscraper. Four main floors light up sequentially: the ground floor is labeled 'Sponsor', the second is 'Trustees', the third is 'Asset Management Company (AMC)', and a secure vault in the basement is labeled 'Custodian'. Sleek, clean infographics, high contrast.* | "To protect your money, SEBI mandates a secure three-tier structure. First, the Sponsor establishes the trust. Second, the Trustees safeguard unit holders' interests. Third, the Asset Management Company manages the investments. Finally, an independent Custodian holds safe physical custody of the securities." |
| **Scene 3: Open-ended vs. Closed-ended** | *A split-screen animation. On the left side, a revolving glass door of a busy bank with people constantly entering and exiting (Open-ended). On the right side, a locked metal gate that only opens at specific scheduled times on a calendar (Closed-ended). Flat vector design style, bright corporate colors.* | "Mutual funds differ by structure. Open-ended funds allow you to enter and exit at any time, with units bought or redeemed daily at NAV-related prices. Closed-ended funds lock your investment for a fixed tenure, with units trading on stock exchanges like shares." |
| **Scene 4: Net Asset Value (NAV)** | *A close-up of a digital screen showing a ledger. In real-time, green bar charts rise (Total Assets) while a smaller red bar drops (Liabilities). A mathematical division symbol appears, dividing the net assets by a growing pile of paper units, resulting in a single highlighted price tag. High-tech HUD interface.* | "How do we value a fund unit? Through the Net Asset Value, or NAV. Every business day, the fund calculates its total assets, subtracts liabilities and expenses, and divides the result by the total outstanding units. The NAV is the fair price of a single unit." |
| **Scene 5: Entry and Exit Loads** | *A clean, minimalist 3D animation showing a transaction. A gold coin entering a slot has a tiny piece shaved off (Entry Load). Later, when the coin is withdrawn, another small piece is shaved off (Exit Load). The remaining coin is handed back to the investor. High-quality render, soft clay style.* | "When transactions occur, loads may apply. An entry load increases your purchase price, while an exit load reduces your redemption proceeds. In India, entry loads are zero, but exit loads remain common to discourage short-term redemptions." |
| **Scene 6: Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)** | *Illustration: equal instalments at fixed dates accumulating into a growing position.* | "A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount on a schedule. Rupee cost averaging means more units are acquired when NAV is lower and fewer when NAV is higher, for the same instalment amount." |
| **Scene 7: Closing summary** | *On-screen checklist: structure, NAV, loads, SIP.* | "Close with: memorise sponsor–trustee–AMC–custodian roles; NAV formula; current load rules per study material; SIP averaging concept. Practise short numericals from the ICAI kit." |
