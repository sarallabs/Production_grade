## Part A: Conceptual Questions & Answers

### Question 1: What is a Mutual Fund? Explain its basic operational mechanism.
**Answer:**
A **Mutual Fund** is a financial vehicle established as a trust that pools the savings of numerous investors who share a common financial goal. The money collected is invested by a professional fund manager in a diversified portfolio of securities, which may include equity shares, debentures, bonds, money market instruments, or other assets, depending on the scheme's investment objective.

**Operational Mechanism:**
1. **Pooling of Funds:** Investors with investible surpluses contribute money to the fund.
2. **Allotment of Units:** In return for their investment, investors are allotted "units" of the scheme proportional to the amount they invest. The value of each unit is represented by the Net Asset Value (NAV).
3. **Portfolio Investment:** The pooled money is managed by a professional Asset Management Company (AMC) which invests it in various securities.
4. **Distribution of Returns:** The income generated (dividends, interest) and capital appreciation realized from these investments are passed back to the unit holders in proportion to the number of units they own, after deducting management fees and other recurring expenses.

### Question 2: Highlight the key benefits of investing through Mutual Funds instead of buying individual stocks.
**Answer:**
Investing through mutual funds offers several distinct advantages, especially for retail and beginner investors, compared to direct equity investing:
1. **Professional Management:** Mutual funds are managed by experienced and skilled fund managers backed by dedicated research teams. They analyze company financial statements, industry trends, and economic factors before investing.
2. **Diversification:** A mutual fund invests in a wide array of stocks and sectors. This spreads the risk across multiple assets, reducing the impact of poor performance by any single stock. Achieving a similar level of diversification individually would require a large capital outlay.
3. **Affordability:** Investors can start investing in mutual funds with small amounts (as low as ₹500 via Systematic Investment Plans), whereas buying high-priced individual stocks may require significant capital.
4. **Liquidity:** For open-ended schemes, investors can redeem their units directly with the mutual fund house at the prevailing NAV on any business day, ensuring quick access to cash.
5. **Low Transaction Costs:** Due to the large volume of transactions, mutual funds enjoy economies of scale, resulting in lower brokerage, custodial, and transaction fees per investor.
6. **Regulatory Protection:** In India, mutual funds are strictly regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) and overseen by a Board of Trustees, providing high transparency and protection to investors.

### Question 3: Distinguish between Open-ended and Closed-ended Mutual Funds.
**Answer:**
The table below contrasts Open-ended and Closed-ended mutual fund schemes:

| Feature | Open-ended Funds | Closed-ended Funds |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subscription Period** | Available for subscription and redemption continuously on all business days throughout the year. | Subscription is open only during the New Fund Offer (NFO) period. |
| **Maturity Period** | No fixed maturity date; the scheme exists indefinitely. | Stipulated maturity period (typically ranging from 3 to 15 years). |
| **Liquidity & Exit Route** | Direct redemption/repurchase by the mutual fund at NAV-based prices. | Traded on stock exchanges after listing; or redeemed via periodic repurchase windows at NAV. |
| **Fund Size (Corpus)** | Flexes constantly as new units are created or old units are redeemed. | Remains constant after the NFO closes. |
| **Pricing** | Units are bought and sold directly at the daily Net Asset Value (NAV). | Units trade on the exchange at market prices, which may be at a premium or discount to the NAV depending on demand and supply. |

### Question 4: Describe the organizational structure of a Mutual Fund in India. Who are the key entities?
**Answer:**
Under SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, a mutual fund in India is structured as a three-tier system comprising four key entities:
1. **Sponsor:** The Sponsor is the promoter who conceives and establishes the mutual fund trust. The sponsor must obtain registration from SEBI and contribute at least 40% of the net worth of the Asset Management Company (AMC).
2. **Mutual Fund Trust & Board of Trustees:** The mutual fund is constituted as a trust under the Indian Registration Act, 1908. The Board of Trustees holds the trust property in fiduciary custody for the benefit of the unit holders. They ensure the AMC manages the funds in compliance with SEBI guidelines and the Trust Deed.
3. **Asset Management Company (AMC):** The AMC is the company appointed by the Trustees to act as the investment manager of the mutual fund. It designs schemes, floats them, and manages the day-to-day investment of the fund's corpus in accordance with the investment objectives.
4. **Custodian:** The Custodian is an independent SEBI-registered entity that takes physical and electronic custody of the securities bought by the mutual fund. It handles the delivery and receipt of securities and tracks corporate actions like dividends and bonuses.
5. **Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA):** The RTA maintains investor records, processes applications, handles unit transactions (allotment and redemption), and sends statements and dividend warrants to the unit holders.

### Question 5: What are Index Funds, and what is meant by "tracking error"?
**Answer:**
**Index Funds** are passive mutual fund schemes that aim to replicate the performance of a specific stock market index (such as Nifty 50 or BSE Sensex). Instead of actively researching and picking stocks, the fund manager simply buys all the stocks that comprise the target index in the exact same proportion (weightage) as they have in the index.

**Tracking Error:**
Ideally, an index fund should deliver the exact same return as the index it tracks. However, in reality, there is a divergence between the fund's returns and the index's returns. This divergence or standard deviation of the difference in returns is called the **Tracking Error**.

*Reasons for Tracking Error:*
* **Transaction Costs:** The fund incurs brokerage and statutory charges when buying and selling index stocks, whereas the index itself is a theoretical number with zero transaction costs.
* **Cash Holdings:** The fund must keep some cash balance to handle daily redemption requests, which does not earn index returns.
* **AMC Management Fees:** Operating expenses and management fees charged by the AMC reduce the fund's NAV.
* **Corporate Actions Lag:** Time delays in receiving and reinvesting dividends received from portfolio companies.

## Part B: Practical/Numerical Problems

### Problem 1: Basic NAV Calculation
**Scenario:**
At the close of business on 31st March 2026, the Cinderella Mutual Fund has the following details for its Scheme Rudolf:
* Value of listed shares (at market price): ₹1,80,00,000
* Value of government securities: ₹60,00,000
* Cash and bank balances: ₹15,00,000
* Accrued expenses: ₹2,50,000
* Other liabilities: ₹2,00,000
* Total number of outstanding units: 10,00,000

Calculate the Net Asset Value (NAV) per unit of Scheme Rudolf.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Calculate the Total Assets of the Scheme**
$$\text{Total Assets} = \text{Value of Listed Shares} + \text{Value of Govt. Securities} + \text{Cash & Bank Balances}$$
$$\text{Total Assets} = ₹1,80,00,000 + ₹60,00,000 + ₹15,00,000 = ₹2,55,00,000$$

**Step 2: Calculate the Total Liabilities of the Scheme**
$$\text{Total Liabilities} = \text{Accrued Expenses} + \text{Other Liabilities}$$
$$\text{Total Liabilities} = ₹2,50,000 + ₹2,00,000 = ₹4,50,000$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Net Assets of the Scheme**
$$\text{Net Assets} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$$
$$\text{Net Assets} = ₹2,55,00,000 - ₹4,50,000 = ₹2,50,50,000$$

**Step 4: Calculate the Net Asset Value (NAV) per unit**
$$\text{NAV per unit} = $\frac{\text{Net Assets}}{\text{Total Number of Outstanding Units}}$$
$$\text{NAV per unit} = $\frac{₹2,50,50,000}{10,00,000 \text{ units}} = ₹25.05$$

**Conclusion:**
The NAV per unit of Scheme Rudolf is **₹25.05**.

### Problem 2: Load Calculations (Entry and Exit Loads)
**Scenario:**
An Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) of a mutual fund has a Net Asset Value (NAV) of ₹40.00. 
1. If the mutual fund charges an Entry Load of 2.25%, calculate the Public Offer Price (POP) or purchase price.
2. If the mutual fund charges an Exit Load of 1.00%, calculate the Redemption Price (the price at which an investor can sell units back to the fund).

*(Note: Although entry loads are currently banned by SEBI in India, they remain a part of historical and academic calculations).*

**Solution:**

**1. Calculation of Public Offer Price (Purchase Price):**
$$\text{Public Offer Price (POP)} = \text{NAV} \times (1 + \text{Entry Load Percentage})$$
$$\text{POP} = ₹40.00 \times (1 + 0.0225)$$
$$\text{POP} = ₹40.00 \times 1.0225 = ₹40.90$$

**2. Calculation of Redemption Price:**
$$\text{Redemption Price} = \text{NAV} \times (1 - \text{Exit Load Percentage})$$
$$\text{Redemption Price} = ₹40.00 \times (1 - 0.01)$$
$$\text{Redemption Price} = ₹40.00 \times 0.99 = ₹39.60$$

**Conclusion:**
* The purchase price per unit is **₹40.90**.
* The redemption price per unit is **₹39.60**.

### Problem 3: Simple Return on a Mutual Fund Scheme
**Scenario:**
On 1st April 2025, Mr. Rohan invested in a mutual fund scheme at a Net Asset Value (NAV) of ₹20.00. During the financial year, the fund distributed:
* Dividend: ₹1.20 per unit
* Capital Gains distribution: ₹0.80 per unit

On 31st March 2026, the NAV of the scheme stood at ₹22.50. Calculate the simple annual return earned by Mr. Rohan.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Identify the values**
* Initial NAV ($NAV_0$) = ₹20.00
* Ending NAV ($NAV_1$) = ₹22.50
* Dividend ($D$) = ₹1.20
* Capital Gains Distribution ($CG$) = ₹0.80

**Step 2: Calculate the Total Income and Capital Appreciation**
$$\text{Total Distributions} = D + CG = ₹1.20 + ₹0.80 = ₹2.00$$
$$\text{Capital Appreciation} = NAV_1 - NAV_0 = ₹22.50 - ₹20.00 = ₹2.50$$
$$\text{Total Gain per unit} = \text{Total Distributions} + \text{Capital Appreciation} = ₹2.00 + ₹2.50 = ₹4.50$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Simple Annual Return**
$$\text{Return (\%)} = $\frac{(NAV_1 - NAV_0) + D + CG}{NAV_0} \times 100$$
$$\text{Return (\%)} = $\frac{₹4.50}{₹20.00} \times 100 = 22.50\%$$

**Conclusion:**
The simple annual return earned by the investor is **22.50%**.

### Problem 4: Total Expense Ratio (TER) Calculation
**Scenario:**
A mutual fund scheme has an average daily net assets value of ₹80 crores. The Asset Management Company (AMC) incurs the following recurring expenses during the year:
* Fund management and advisory fees: ₹85,00,000
* Custodian and trustee fees: ₹10,00,000
* RTA fees and investor relations expenses: ₹15,00,000
* Marketing and selling expenses: ₹30,00,000

Calculate the Total Expense Ratio (TER) of the scheme. 

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Calculate the Total Recurring Expenses**
$$\text{Total Recurring Expenses} = \text{Management Fees} + \text{Custodian/Trustee Fees} + \text{RTA Fees} + \text{Marketing Expenses}$$
$$\text{Total Recurring Expenses} = ₹85,00,000 + ₹10,00,000 + ₹15,00,000 + ₹30,00,000 = ₹1,40,00,000 \text{ (or ₹1.40 Crores)}$$

**Step 2: Identify the Average Daily Net Assets**
$$\text{Average Daily Net Assets} = ₹80,00,000,000 \text{ (or ₹80 Crores)}$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Total Expense Ratio (TER)**
$$\text{Total Expense Ratio (TER)} = $\frac{Total\ Recurring\ Expenses}{Average\ Daily\ Net\ Assets} \times 100$$
$$\text{TER} = \frac{₹1.40\text{ Crores}}{₹80.00\text{ Crores}} \times 100$$
$$\text{TER} = 0.0175 \times 100 = 1.75\%$$

**Conclusion:**
The Total Expense Ratio (TER) of the scheme is **1.75%**.

### Problem 5: Units Allotted under Dividend Reinvestment Option
**Scenario:**
An investor, Ms. Priya, owns 8,000 units of a mutual fund scheme. The face value of each unit is ₹10.00. The fund declares a dividend of 12% on the face value. 
Instead of taking a cash payout, Ms. Priya is enrolled in the Dividend Reinvestment Option. If the closing NAV of the scheme on the day of reinvestment is ₹24.00, calculate:
1. The total dividend amount earned.
2. The number of new units allotted to her.
3. Her total outstanding unit balance after reinvestment.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Calculate the Dividend Earned per unit**
$$\text{Dividend per unit} = \text{Face Value} \times \text{Dividend Percentage}$$
$$\text{Dividend per unit} = ₹10.00 \times 12\% = ₹1.20$$

**Step 2: Calculate the Total Dividend Earned**
$$\text{Total Dividend} = \text{Number of Units Held} \times \text{Dividend per unit}$$
$$\text{Total Dividend} = 8,000\text{ units} \times ₹1.20 = ₹9,600$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Number of New Units Allotted**
Under the dividend reinvestment plan, the dividend amount is used to purchase additional units at the prevailing NAV.
$$\text{New Units Allotted} = $\frac{\text{Total Dividend Earned}}{\text{Reinvestment NAV}}$$
$$\text{New Units Allotted} = $\frac{₹9,600}{₹24.00} = 400\text{ units}$$

**Step 4: Calculate Total Outstanding Units**
$$\text{Total Units} = \text{Original Units} + \text{New Units Allotted}$$
$$\text{Total Units} = 8,000 + 400 = 8,400\text{ units}$$

**Conclusion:**
1. The total dividend earned is **₹9,600**.
2. The number of new units allotted is **400 units**.
3. Her total outstanding unit balance is **8,400 units**.
