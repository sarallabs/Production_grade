## Problem 1: Advanced Portfolio Valuation with Debt Diminution, Money Market Instruments, and Liabilities

### Question
The Zenith Advanced Return Scheme (ZARS), an open-ended mutual fund, has the following assets and liabilities in its portfolio as of March 31, 2026:

| Security Type | Asset / Security Description | Acquisition Cost (₹ in lakhs) | Current Market Quote / Value (₹ in lakhs) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Equity | Listed Blue-chip Equities | 2,500.00 | 3,200.00 |
| Equity | Unlisted Equities (at cost) | 800.00 | Valuation Needed |
| Debt | Listed 9% Corporate Bonds (Face Value ₹1,000) | 1,200.00 | Quoted at 5% premium |
| Debt | Unlisted 11% Corporate Debentures | 1,000.00 | Diminished by 25% |
| Debt | Government Securities (G-Secs) | 1,500.00 | Quoted at 3% discount |
| Cash Equivalent | Call Money | 300.00 | Interest accrued: ₹12.50 lakhs |
| Cash Equivalent | Commercial Paper (valued at cost + accrual) | 500.00 | Accrued interest: ₹18.00 lakhs |

**Additional Information:**
1. The total number of units outstanding under the scheme is 50,00,000 units.
2. The fund has accrued the following expenses:
   - Management Fees payable to AMC: ₹35.00 lakhs
   - Custodian Fees in arrears: ₹8.50 lakhs
   - Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA) fees in arrears: ₹6.00 lakhs
3. Other liabilities of the scheme total ₹42.00 lakhs.
4. For the unlisted equities, the Board of Directors of the AMC has approved a capitalization of earnings method. The average earnings of these companies are ₹96.00 lakhs, and the capitalization rate of comparable listed companies is 12%. An appropriate illiquidity discount of 20% is to be applied.

**Required:**
Calculate the Net Asset Value (NAV) per unit of ZARS as of March 31, 2026.

---

### Solution

#### Step 1: Valuation of Portfolio Assets

1. **Listed Blue-chip Equities:**
   Valued at the current market quote.
   $$\text{Market Value} = \text{₹3,200.00 lakhs}$$

2. **Unlisted Equities:**
   Valued using the Capitalization of Earnings method:
   $$\text{Value before discount} = $\frac{\text{Average Earnings}}{\text{Capitalization Rate}} = $\frac{\text{₹96.00 lakhs}}{12\%} = \text{₹800.00 lakhs}$$
   Applying the illiquidity discount of 20%:
   $$\text{Valuation of Unlisted Equities} = ₹800.00 \text{ lakhs} \times (1 - 0.20) = \text{₹640.00 lakhs}$$

3. **Listed 9% Corporate Bonds:**
   Quoted at a 5% premium over acquisition cost (since specific par value quotes are not provided, we apply the premium to the carrying value/cost as per standard practice, or assume cost represents par). Here, we apply it to the acquisition cost:
   $$\text{Market Value} = ₹1,200.00 \text{ lakhs} \times 1.05 = \text{₹1,260.00 lakhs}$$

4. **Unlisted 11% Corporate Debentures:**
   Valued after applying a 25% diminution:
   $$\text{Market Value} = ₹1,000.00 \text{ lakhs} \times (1 - 0.25) = \text{₹750.00 lakhs}$$

5. **Government Securities (G-Secs):**
   Quoted at a 3% discount:
   $$\text{Market Value} = ₹1,500.00 \text{ lakhs} \times (1 - 0.03) = \text{₹1,455.00 lakhs}$$

6. **Call Money:**
   Valued at cost plus accrued interest:
   $$\text{Value} = \text{Cost} + \text{Accrued Interest} = 300.00 + 12.50 = \text{₹312.50 lakhs}$$

7. **Commercial Paper:**
   Valued at cost plus accrued interest:
   $$\text{Value} = \text{Cost} + \text{Accrued Interest} = 500.00 + 18.00 = \text{₹518.00 lakhs}$$

#### Total Value of Assets (A)
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Total Assets} &= 3,200.00 + 640.00 + 1,260.00 + 750.00 + 1,455.00 + 312.50 + 518.00 \\
&= \text{₹8,135.50 lakhs}
\end{aligned}$$

---

#### Step 2: Identification of Portfolio Liabilities

1. **Accrued Management Fees:** ₹35.00 lakhs
2. **Custodian Fees in arrears:** ₹8.50 lakhs
3. **RTA Fees in arrears:** ₹6.00 lakhs
4. **Other Liabilities:** ₹42.00 lakhs

#### Total Liabilities (B)
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Total Liabilities} &= 35.00 + 8.50 + 6.00 + 42.00 \\
&= \text{₹91.50 lakhs}
\end{aligned}$$

---

#### Step 3: Calculation of Net Assets & NAV per unit

$$\text{Net Assets of the Scheme} = \text{Total Assets (A)} - \text{Total Liabilities (B)}$$
$$\text{Net Assets} = 8,135.50 \text{ lakhs} - 91.50 \text{ lakhs} = \text{₹8,044.00 lakhs}$$

$$\text{Number of Units Outstanding} = 50.00 \text{ lakhs units}$$

$$\text{NAV per unit} = $\frac{\text{Net Assets of the Scheme}}{\text{Number of Outstanding Units}} = $\frac{₹8,044.00 \text{ lakhs}}{50.00 \text{ lakhs units}} = \text{₹160.88 per unit}$$

## Problem 2: Dynamic NAV Adjustments and Dividend Equalization Reserve (DER)

### Question
On January 1, 2025, an open-ended mutual fund scheme has 100 lakh units outstanding with an opening Net Asset Value (NAV) of ₹30.00 per unit. 
During the first quarter of the year, the following activities take place:
*   **January:** The fund earns an income of ₹15.00 lakhs. At the end of January, the fund issues 8 lakh units at the opening NAV plus an entry load of 1.5%, adjusted for dividend equalization.
*   **February:** The fund earns an income of ₹22.00 lakhs. At the end of February, the fund repurchases 4 lakh units at the opening NAV less an exit load of 1.0%, adjusted for dividend equalization.
*   **March:** The fund earns an income of ₹30.00 lakhs. At the end of March, the fund distributes 80% of its available income.
*   **Quarter-End Valuation:** The overall market value appreciation of the portfolio (capital gains) for the entire quarter is ₹120.00 lakhs.

**Required:**
1. Calculate the issue price per unit at the end of January.
2. Calculate the repurchase price per unit at the end of February.
3. Determine the income available for distribution at the end of March and the total dividend paid.
4. Reconcile the Closing Net Assets and calculate the closing NAV per unit of the scheme as of March 31, 2025 (assuming loads are retained by the Asset Management Company).

---

### Solution

#### Part 1: Issue Price at the end of January
To ensure fairness, new unit holders must pay for the accumulated income per unit (Dividend Equalization component) so they do not dilute the earnings of the existing unit holders.

1. **Outstanding units before issue:** 100 lakhs
2. **Income earned in January:** ₹15.00 lakhs
3. **Dividend Equalization Reserve (DER) component per unit for January:**
   $$\text{DER component} = $\frac{\text{January Income}}{\text{Outstanding Units}} = $\frac{₹15.00 \text{ lakhs}}{100 \text{ lakhs units}} = \text{₹0.1500 per unit}$$
4. **Entry Load:** 1.5% of the opening NAV.
   $$\text{Entry Load} = 1.5\% \text{ of } ₹30.00 = \text{₹0.4500 per unit}$$
5. **Issue Price per unit:**
   $$\text{Issue Price} = \text{Opening NAV} + \text{Entry Load} + \text{DER component}$$
   $$\text{Issue Price} = 30.00 + 0.4500 + 0.1500 = \text{₹30.6000 per unit}$$

*Note: Out of ₹30.60 per unit collected, ₹30.00 goes to Unit Capital, ₹0.15 goes to the Dividend Equalization Reserve, and ₹0.45 is paid to the AMC as entry load.*

---

#### Part 2: Repurchase Price at the end of February

1. **Outstanding units before repurchase:** $100 + 8 = 108$ lakhs
2. **Accumulated Income before repurchase:**
   We aggregate the actual income earned plus the dividend equalization collected from the January issue.
   $$\text{January Income} = \text{₹15.00 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Add: Equalization collected on January issue} = 8 \text{ lakhs units} \times ₹0.15 = \text{₹1.20 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Add: February Income} = \text{₹22.00 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Accumulated Income} = 15.00 + 1.20 + 22.00 = \text{₹38.20 lakhs}$$
   
   *Alternative ICAI Practice:* In some exam solutions, the accumulated income is calculated by summing only the actual income earned and dividing by units (ignoring the previous equalization addition in the denominator/numerator flow because the premium balances out). Let's follow the standard, rigorous ledger method where all accumulated income in the pool is divided by the units:
   $$\text{DER component per unit for February} = $\frac{\text{Accumulated Income}}{\text{Outstanding Units}} = $\frac{₹38.20 \text{ lakhs}}{108 \text{ lakhs units}} = \text{₹0.3537 per unit}$$
3. **Exit Load:** 1.0% of the opening NAV.
   $$\text{Exit Load} = 1.0\% \text{ of } ₹30.00 = \text{₹0.3000 per unit}$$
4. **Repurchase Price per unit:**
   $$\text{Repurchase Price} = \text{Opening NAV} - \text{Exit Load} + \text{DER component}$$
   $$\text{Repurchase Price} = 30.00 - 0.3000 + 0.3537 = \text{₹30.0537 per unit}$$

*Note: The exiting investor receives ₹30.0537. The fund's assets are reduced by ₹30.3537 (NAV + DER), and the ₹0.30 exit load is deducted and paid to the AMC.*

---

#### Part 3: Income Available for Distribution and Dividend Paid (End of March)

To find the final available income, we track the ledger of the Dividend Equalization Reserve and Income Account:

| Transaction / Flow Description | Income / Reserve Amount (₹ in lakhs) |
| :--- | :---: |
| January Income earned | +15.0000 |
| Add: Equalization received on January issue ($8 \text{ lakhs} \times ₹0.15$) | +1.2000 |
| February Income earned | +22.0000 |
| Less: Equalization paid on February repurchase ($4 \text{ lakhs} \times ₹0.3537$) | -1.4148 |
| March Income earned | +30.0000 |
| **Total Income available for distribution** | **₹66.7852 lakhs** |

$$\text{Dividend Distributed (80% of available income)} = 80\% \text{ of } ₹66.7852 \text{ lakhs} = \text{₹53.4282 lakhs}$$
$$\text{Retained Income in the scheme} = 66.7852 - 53.4282 = \text{₹13.3570 lakhs}$$

---

#### Part 4: Reconcile Closing Net Assets and Calculate Closing NAV

Let's track the outstanding units and Net Assets:
*   **Closing Units outstanding:** $100 + 8 - 4 = 104$ lakhs units.
*   **Initial Net Assets (1-Jan-2025):** $100 \text{ lakhs} \times ₹30.00 = \text{₹3,000.00 lakhs}$

##### Receipts and Payments of the Fund (excl. Loads retained by AMC):
1.  **Inflow from January unit issue (Capital + DER portion):**
    $$\text{Inflow} = 8 \text{ lakhs units} \times (₹30.00 \text{ Capital} + ₹0.15 \text{ DER}) = 8 \times 30.15 = \text{₹241.20 lakhs}$$
2.  **Outflow from February unit repurchase (Capital + DER portion):**
    $$\text{Outflow} = 4 \text{ lakhs units} \times (₹30.00 \text{ Capital} + ₹0.3537 \text{ DER}) = 4 \times 30.3537 = \text{₹121.4148 lakhs}$$
3.  **Income earned during the quarter:**
    $$\text{Income} = 15.00 \text{ (Jan)} + 22.00 \text{ (Feb)} + 30.00 \text{ (Mar)} = \text{₹67.00 lakhs}$$
4.  **Portfolio Value Appreciation:**
    $$\text{Appreciation} = \text{₹120.00 lakhs}$$
5.  **Dividend Paid out:**
    $$\text{Dividend Paid} = \text{₹53.4282 lakhs}$$

##### Closing Net Assets:
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Closing Net Assets} &= \text{Opening Net Assets} + \text{Jan Issue Inflow} - \text{Feb Repurchase Outflow} + \text{Income Earned} + \text{Portfolio Appreciation} - \text{Dividend Paid} \\
&= 3,000.00 + 241.20 - 121.4148 + 67.00 + 120.00 - 53.4282 \\
&= \text{₹3,253.3570 lakhs}
\end{aligned}$$

##### Closing NAV per unit as of March 31, 2025:
$$\text{Closing NAV} = $\frac{\text{Closing Net Assets}}{\text{Closing Units}} = $\frac{₹3,253.3570 \text{ lakhs}}{104 \text{ lakhs units}} = \text{₹31.2823 per unit}$$

##### Verification via Balance Sheet Ledger Method:
*   **Capital Component Net Assets:**
    $$\text{Opening Capital} + \text{Jan Issue Capital} - \text{Feb Repurchase Capital} + \text{Appreciation} = 3000.00 + 240.00 - 120.00 + 120.00 = \text{₹3,240.00 lakhs}$$
*   **Income/Reserve Component Net Assets:**
    $$\text{Retained Income} = \text{₹13.3570 lakhs}$$
*   **Total Net Assets:**
    $$3,240.00 + 13.3570 = \text{₹3,253.3570 lakhs}$$
*   **NAV per unit:**
    $$\frac{3,253.3570}{104} = \text{₹31.2823 per unit}$$
    The calculations are perfectly reconciled.

## Problem 3: Performance Evaluation and Ranking Conflicts

### Question
An analyst is evaluating four open-ended equity mutual funds against the market index. The following performance data is compiled over a 10-year period:

| Portfolio / Index | Average Annual Return ($R_p$) | Standard Deviation ($\sigma_p$) | Systematic Risk / Beta ($\beta$) | Correlation with Market ($r_{pm}$) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Fund Alpha** | 16.50% | 14.00% | 1.10 | 0.85 |
| **Fund Beta** | 14.80% | 11.20% | 0.85 | 0.82 |
| **Fund Gamma** | 18.20% | 18.50% | 1.45 | 0.90 |
| **Fund Delta** | 13.50% | 9.00% | 0.60 | 0.75 |
| **Market Index** | 12.00% | 10.00% | 1.00 | 1.00 |

*Note: The risk-free rate of return ($R_f$) during this period is 6.50%.*

**Required:**
1. Compute the **Sharpe Ratio**, **Treynor Ratio**, and **Jensen's Alpha** for all four funds and rank them.
2. Calculate the **Systematic Risk** (in variance terms) and the **Unsystematic Risk** (Residual Variance) for each fund.
3. Identify and explain any ranking conflicts observed between the Sharpe and Treynor methods, focusing on the role of diversification.

---

### Solution

#### Part 1: Performance Ratios and Rankings

##### Sharpe Ratio Formula:
$$\text{Sharpe Ratio} = $\frac{R_p - R_f}{\sigma_p}$$

##### Treynor Ratio Formula:
$$\text{Treynor Ratio} = $\frac{R_p - R_f}{\beta_p}$$

##### Jensen's Alpha Formula:
$$\text{Jensen's Alpha } (\alpha) = R_p - [R_f + \beta_p(R_m - R_f)]$$
$$\text{Market Risk Premium } (R_m - R_f) = 12.00\% - 6.50\% = 5.50\%$$

##### Calculations:

*   **Fund Alpha:**
    - Sharpe: $\frac{16.50\% - 6.50\%}{14.00\%} = $\frac{10.00}{14.00} = \mathbf{0.7143}$
    - Treynor: $\frac{16.50\% - 6.50\%}{1.10} = $\frac{10.00}{1.10} = \mathbf{9.0909\%}$
    - CAPM Return: $6.50\% + 1.10(5.50\%) = 12.55\%$
    - Jensen's Alpha: $16.50\% - 12.55\% = \mathbf{+3.95\%}$

*   **Fund Beta:**
    - Sharpe: $\frac{14.80\% - 6.50\%}{11.20\%} = $\frac{8.30}{11.20} = \mathbf{0.7411}$
    - Treynor: $\frac{14.80\% - 6.50\%}{0.85} = $\frac{8.30}{0.85} = \mathbf{9.7647\%}$
    - CAPM Return: $6.50\% + 0.85(5.50\%) = 11.175\%$
    - Jensen's Alpha: $14.80\% - 11.175\% = \mathbf{+3.625\%}$

*   **Fund Gamma:**
    - Sharpe: $\frac{18.20\% - 6.50\%}{18.50\%} = $\frac{11.70}{18.50} = \mathbf{0.6324}$
    - Treynor: $\frac{18.20\% - 6.50\%}{1.45} = $\frac{11.70}{1.45} = \mathbf{8.0690\%}$
    - CAPM Return: $6.50\% + 1.45(5.50\%) = 14.475\%$
    - Jensen's Alpha: $18.20\% - 14.475\% = \mathbf{+3.725\%}$

*   **Fund Delta:**
    - Sharpe: $\frac{13.50\% - 6.50\%}{9.00\%} = $\frac{7.00}{9.00} = \mathbf{0.7778}$
    - Treynor: $\frac{13.50\% - 6.50\%}{0.60} = $\frac{7.00}{0.60} = \mathbf{11.6667\%}$
    - CAPM Return: $6.50\% + 0.60(5.50\%) = 9.80\%$
    - Jensen's Alpha: $13.50\% - 9.80\% = \mathbf{+3.70\%}$

##### Rankings Table:

| Fund | Sharpe Ratio | Sharpe Rank | Treynor Ratio | Treynor Rank | Jensen's Alpha | Alpha Rank |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Alpha** | 0.7143 | III | 9.0909% | III | +3.950% | I |
| **Beta** | 0.7411 | II | 9.7647% | II | +3.625% | IV |
| **Gamma** | 0.6324 | IV | 8.0690% | IV | +3.725% | II |
| **Delta** | 0.7778 | I | 11.6667% | I | +3.700% | III |

---

#### Part 2: Decomposition of Total Risk (Variance Terms)

Total risk (variance, $\sigma_p^2$) can be decomposed into systematic risk and unsystematic (residual) risk.
$$\text{Total Variance } (\sigma_p^2) = \text{Systematic Risk } (\beta_p^2 \times \sigma_m^2) + \text{Unsystematic Risk } (\sigma_{ep}^2)$$
$$\text{Market Variance } (\sigma_m^2) = 10^2 = 100.00\%$$

*   **Fund Alpha:**
    - Total Variance: $14.00^2 = 196.00$
    - Systematic Risk: $1.10^2 \times 100 = 1.21 \times 100 = 121.00$ (61.73% of total risk)
    - Unsystematic Risk: $196.00 - 121.00 = \mathbf{75.00}$ (38.27% of total risk)

*   **Fund Beta:**
    - Total Variance: $11.20^2 = 125.44$
    - Systematic Risk: $0.85^2 \times 100 = 0.7225 \times 100 = 72.25$ (57.60% of total risk)
    - Unsystematic Risk: $125.44 - 72.25 = \mathbf{53.19}$ (42.40% of total risk)

*   **Fund Gamma:**
    - Total Variance: $18.50^2 = 342.25$
    - Systematic Risk: $1.45^2 \times 100 = 2.1025 \times 100 = 210.25$ (61.43% of total risk)
    - Unsystematic Risk: $342.25 - 210.25 = \mathbf{132.00}$ (38.57% of total risk)

*   **Fund Delta:**
    - Total Variance: $9.00^2 = 81.00$
    - Systematic Risk: $0.60^2 \times 100 = 0.36 \times 100 = 36.00$ (44.44% of total risk)
    - Unsystematic Risk: $81.00 - 36.00 = \mathbf{45.00}$ (55.56% of total risk)

---

#### Part 3: Explanation of Ranking Conflicts
1. **Consistency:** Sharpe and Treynor methods yield consistent rankings for all four funds in this specific scenario (Delta > Beta > Alpha > Gamma). This indicates that although the portfolios have varying degrees of diversification, the relative reward per unit of systematic risk is aligned with the relative reward per unit of total risk.
2. **Conflict with Jensen's Alpha:** A significant conflict is observed when comparing Sharpe/Treynor rankings with Jensen's Alpha.
   - **Fund Alpha** ranks **3rd** in Sharpe and Treynor but ranks **1st** in Jensen's Alpha (+3.95%).
   - **Fund Delta** ranks **1st** in Sharpe and Treynor but ranks **3rd** in Jensen's Alpha (+3.70%).
3. **The Reason:** Jensen's Alpha is an absolute measure of return premium and does not scale the excess return by the portfolio's standard deviation or beta. Fund Alpha generates a higher absolute outperformance relative to its CAPM benchmark, but because it takes on a much higher total risk ($\sigma = 14.00\%$) and systematic risk ($\beta = 1.10$) than Fund Delta ($\sigma = 9.00\%$, $\beta = 0.60$), its risk-adjusted ratio measures (Sharpe and Treynor) are lower.
4. **Diversification Analysis:** The coefficient of determination ($R^2 = r_{pm}^2$) indicates how well-diversified a fund is:
   - Alpha: $0.85^2 = 72.25\%$ (27.75% unsystematic risk)
   - Beta: $0.82^2 = 67.24\%$ (32.76% unsystematic risk)
   - Gamma: $0.90^2 = 81.00\%$ (19.00% unsystematic risk)
   - Delta: $0.75^2 = 56.25\%$ (43.75% unsystematic risk)
   Delta is the least diversified (has the highest proportion of unsystematic risk at 43.75%). If an investor adds Delta to an already diversified market portfolio, the unsystematic risk will be diversified away, making the Treynor ratio the most appropriate measure. However, as a standalone portfolio, Fund Delta's high unsystematic risk is a concern, and the Sharpe ratio would be more representative of the total risk experienced.

## Problem 4: Hedge Fund Fee Structures: Hurdle Rates, High-Water Marks, and Manager Payouts

### Question
The Obsidian Capital Hedge Fund has a managed corpus of ₹10,000 lakhs at the beginning of Year 1. The fund manager operates under the following fee agreement:
*   **Management Fee:** 1.5% per annum, calculated on the opening asset value of the fund at the beginning of each year.
*   **Incentive Fee:** 20% of net profits above a hurdle rate of 8.0% per annum (net of management fee).
*   **High-Water Mark Rule:** Applied continuously. No incentive fee is payable in any year unless the net asset value of the fund (before incentive fees but after management fees) exceeds the highest year-end value previously achieved (the high-water mark).

The performance of the fund before any fees is as follows:
*   **Year 1:** Returns +18.00%
*   **Year 2:** Returns -6.00%
*   **Year 3:** Returns +15.00%

**Required:**
Calculate the total fees paid to the manager (management fee and incentive fee) and the closing net assets of the fund for each of the three years.

---

### Solution

#### Year 1

1. **Opening Fund Value:** ₹10,000.00 lakhs
2. **Management Fee:**
   $$\text{Mgmt Fee} = 1.5\% \text{ of } ₹10,000.00 = \text{₹150.00 lakhs}$$
3. **Gross Fund Value before Incentive Fee:**
   $$\text{Gross Return} = +18\% \text{ of } ₹10,000.00 = \text{₹1,800.00 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Fund Value before incentive fee} = 10,000.00 + 1,800.00 - 150.00 (\text{Mgmt Fee}) = \text{₹11,650.00 lakhs}$$
4. **Hurdle Rate Value:**
   The hurdle return is 8.0% on the opening assets.
   $$\text{Hurdle return amount} = 8.0\% \text{ of } ₹10,000.00 = \text{₹800.00 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Hurdle Threshold Value} = \text{Opening Assets} + \text{Hurdle return} = 10,000.00 + 800.00 = \text{₹10,800.00 lakhs}$$
5. **Incentive Fee Calculation:**
   Since the fund value before incentive fee (₹11,650.00 lakhs) exceeds the Hurdle Threshold (₹10,800.00 lakhs), an incentive fee is payable.
   $$\text{Eligible Profit for Incentive Fee} = 11,650.00 - 10,800.00 = \text{₹850.00 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Incentive Fee} = 20\% \text{ of } ₹850.00 = \text{₹170.00 lakhs}$$
6. **Closing Net Assets (Year 1):**
   $$\text{Closing Net Assets} = 11,650.00 - 170.00 = \text{₹11,480.00 lakhs}$$
7. **High-Water Mark established at end of Year 1:**
   $$\text{HWM}_1 = \text{₹11,480.00 lakhs}$$
8. **Total Manager Payout (Year 1):**
   $$\text{Total Fee} = 150.00 (\text{Mgmt}) + 170.00 (\text{Incentive}) = \text{₹320.00 lakhs}$$

---

#### Year 2

1. **Opening Fund Value:** ₹11,480.00 lakhs (Closing of Year 1)
2. **Management Fee:**
   $$\text{Mgmt Fee} = 1.5\% \text{ of } ₹11,480.00 = \text{₹172.20 lakhs}$$
3. **Gross Fund Value before Incentive Fee:**
   $$\text{Gross Loss} = -6\% \text{ of } ₹11,480.00 = -\text{₹688.80 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Fund Value before incentive fee} = 11,480.00 - 688.80 - 172.20 (\text{Mgmt Fee}) = \text{₹10,619.00 lakhs}$$
4. **Incentive Fee Calculation:**
   Since the gross fund value (₹10,619.00 lakhs) is below the High-Water Mark ($\text{HWM}_1 = ₹11,480.00$ lakhs), no incentive fee is payable.
   $$\text{Incentive Fee} = \text{₹0.00 lakhs}$$
5. **Closing Net Assets (Year 2):**
   $$\text{Closing Net Assets} = \text{₹10,619.00 lakhs}$$
6. **High-Water Mark (remains unchanged):**
   $$\text{HWM}_2 = \text{₹11,480.00 lakhs}$$
7. **Total Manager Payout (Year 2):**
   $$\text{Total Fee} = 172.20 (\text{Mgmt}) + 0.00 = \text{₹172.20 lakhs}$$

---

#### Year 3

1. **Opening Fund Value:** ₹10,619.00 lakhs (Closing of Year 2)
2. **Management Fee:**
   $$\text{Mgmt Fee} = 1.5\% \text{ of } ₹10,619.00 = \text{₹159.285 lakhs}$$
3. **Gross Fund Value before Incentive Fee:**
   $$\text{Gross Return} = +15\% \text{ of } ₹10,619.00 = \text{₹1,592.850 lakhs}$$
   $$\text{Fund Value before incentive fee} = 10,619.00 + 1,592.85 - 159.285 = \text{₹12,052.565 lakhs}$$
4. **Incentive Fee and High-Water Mark Check:**
   - Current high-water mark to beat is $\text{HWM}_2 = ₹11,480.00$ lakhs.
   - The gross fund value before incentive fee is ₹12,052.565 lakhs, which exceeds the HWM.
   - Net profit to be considered for incentive fee must beat both the HWM and the hurdle rate.
   - The hurdle return for Year 3 is calculated on Year 3 opening assets:
     $$\text{Hurdle return amount} = 8.0\% \text{ of } ₹10,619.00 = \text{₹849.52 lakhs}$$
     $$\text{Hurdle Threshold Value} = \text{Opening Assets} + \text{Hurdle return} = 10,619.00 + 849.52 = \text{₹11,468.52 lakhs}$$
   - Since the High-Water Mark (₹11,480.00 lakhs) is higher than the Hurdle Threshold Value (₹11,468.52 lakhs), the HWM acts as the binding constraint to beat.
   - Therefore, the eligible profit above the high-water mark is:
     $$\text{Eligible Profit} = 12,052.565 - 11,480.00 = \text{₹572.565 lakhs}$$
     $$\text{Incentive Fee} = 20\% \text{ of } ₹572.565 = \text{₹114.513 lakhs}$$
5. **Closing Net Assets (Year 3):**
   $$\text{Closing Net Assets} = 12,052.565 - 114.513 = \text{₹11,938.052 lakhs}$$
6. **New High-Water Mark established at end of Year 3:**
   $$\text{HWM}_3 = \text{₹11,938.052 lakhs}$$
7. **Total Manager Payout (Year 3):**
   $$\text{Total Fee} = 159.285 (\text{Mgmt}) + 114.513 (\text{Incentive}) = \text{₹273.798 lakhs}$$

## Problem 5: Arbitrage Funds — Stock-Futures Arbitrage and Post-Tax Yield Comparison

### Question
An arbitrage fund manager identifies a mispricing in the shares of Zenith Ltd. and its futures contract. The following market details are available on April 1, 2026:
*   **Spot Price of Zenith Ltd. ($S_0$):** ₹500.00 per share
*   **3-Month Futures Price of Zenith Ltd. ($F_0$):** ₹512.00 per share
*   **Quarterly borrowing/opportunity cost rate:** 8.0% per annum (continuously compounded)
*   **Transaction Costs:**
    *   Brokerage on spot purchase: 0.10%
    *   Brokerage on futures trade: 0.05%
    *   STT on spot purchase: 0.10%
    *   STT on futures sale: 0.01%
*   **Dividend expected:** ₹3.50 per share, payable on June 15, 2026.

An investor is considering investing ₹50.00 lakhs in either:
*   **Option A: The Arbitrage Fund** (which will replicate this stock-futures arbitrage. The fund has a holding period of 3 months, and the returns will be taxed as Equity-Oriented Mutual Fund Short-Term Capital Gains at 15.0% flat).
*   **Option B: A Debt Mutual Fund** (which yields an annualized pre-tax return of 8.50%. The returns will be taxed at the investor's marginal tax rate of 30.0%).

**Required:**
1. Formulate the arbitrage strategy and calculate the absolute arbitrage profit per share if the futures contract is held till maturity and closed at the spot price.
2. Determine the net annualized return of Option A (Arbitrage Fund) after transaction costs and tax.
3. Compare the post-tax annualized yields of Option A and Option B, and advise the investor.

---

### Solution

#### Part 1: Arbitrage Strategy and Profit Calculation

##### Strategy Formulation:
The actual futures price ($F_0 = ₹512.00$) is higher than the spot price ($S_0 = ₹500.00$). The arbitrageur should execute a **Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage**:
1. Buy physical stock in the spot market at $S_0 = ₹500.00$.
2. Simultaneously sell (short) the 3-Month futures contract at $F_0 = ₹512.00$.
3. Collect the dividend of ₹3.50 per share during the holding period.
4. On maturity, deliver the stock to settle the futures contract at the spot price $S_T$, closing the short futures position and realizing the profit.

##### Calculation of Transaction Costs per Share:
1. **Spot Purchase Costs:**
   - Brokerage: $0.10\% \text{ of } ₹500.00 = ₹0.50$
   - STT: $0.10\% \text{ of } ₹500.00 = ₹0.50$
2. **Futures Entry Costs:**
   - Brokerage: $0.05\% \text{ of } ₹512.00 = ₹0.256$
   - STT: $0.01\% \text{ of } ₹512.00 = ₹0.0512$
3. **Maturity / Settlement Costs:**
   - Brokerage on cash settlement: $0.10\% \text{ of } F_0 = ₹0.512$
   - STT on delivery: Nil (covered under cash delivery or included in transaction norms).
   $$\text{Total Transaction Costs} = 0.50 + 0.50 + 0.256 + 0.0512 + 0.512 = \text{₹1.8192 per share}$$

##### Cash Flow Analysis per Share:
- **T = 0 Cash Outflow (Purchase of Stock + Transaction Costs):**
  $$\text{Outflow} = 500.00 + 1.8192 = \text{₹501.8192}$$
- **During the Period (Dividend Received):**
  $$\text{Inflow} = \text{₹3.50}$$
- **T = 3 Months Cash Inflow (Settlement of Futures + Sale of Stock):**
  $$\text{Inflow} = \text{₹512.00}$$
- **Net Cash Flow (Net Profit) per Share:**
  $$\text{Net Profit} = \text{Inflows} - \text{Outflows} = (512.00 + 3.50) - 501.8192 = \text{₹13.6808 per share}$$

---

#### Part 2: Net Annualized Return of Option A (Arbitrage Fund)

1. **Quarterly Return:**
   $$\text{Holding Period Return (HPR)} = $\frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Effective Investment}} = $\frac{₹13.6808}{₹501.8192} = 2.7262\% \text{ for 3 months}$$
2. **Annualized Pre-Tax Return:**
   $$\text{Annualized Pre-Tax Return} = 2.7262\% \times 4 = 10.9048\% \text{ p.a.}$$
3. **Post-Tax Return (Short-Term Capital Gains Tax of 15%):**
   $$\text{Post-Tax Annualized Return} = \text{Pre-Tax Return} \times (1 - \text{Tax Rate})$$
   $$\text{Post-Tax Annualized Return} = 10.9048\% \times (1 - 0.15) = \mathbf{9.2691\% \text{ p.a.}}$$

---

#### Part 3: Yield Comparison and Investment Advice

##### Option B: Debt Mutual Fund Post-Tax Return:
1. **Annualized Pre-Tax Return:** 8.50% p.a.
2. **Marginal Tax Rate:** 30.0%
3. **Post-Tax Annualized Return:**
   $$\text{Post-Tax Return} = 8.50\% \times (1 - 0.30) = \mathbf{5.95\% \text{ p.a.}}$$

##### Comparison:
- **Option A (Arbitrage Fund Post-Tax Yield):** 9.2691% p.a.
- **Option B (Debt Fund Post-Tax Yield):** 5.9500% p.a.

##### Advice:
The investor should select **Option A (Arbitrage Fund)**.
*   **Reasoning:** The arbitrage fund exploits mispricing to lock in a risk-free return of 10.90% pre-tax. Due to the tax classification of arbitrage funds as equity funds under Indian income tax laws, they attract a much lower tax rate of 15% (for holding periods less than 12 months) compared to debt funds which are taxed at the investor's marginal rate of 30%.
*   This tax asymmetry increases the post-tax yield of Option A by **3.3191% per annum** over the debt fund, making it a significantly superior choice for risk-averse capital allocation.
