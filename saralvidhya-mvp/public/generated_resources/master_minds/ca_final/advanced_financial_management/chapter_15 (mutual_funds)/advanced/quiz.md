Question 1
An analyst evaluates two equity portfolios: Portfolio X has an average annual return of 18%, standard deviation of 16%, Beta of 1.2, and correlation with the market of 0.90. Portfolio Y has an average annual return of 15%, standard deviation of 10%, Beta of 0.6, and correlation with the market of 0.60. The risk-free rate is 6% and the market return is 12%. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the Sharpe and Treynor rankings of the portfolios?
A. Portfolio X is superior under both Sharpe and Treynor metrics because it has a higher annual return.
B. Portfolio Y is superior under Sharpe, but Portfolio X is superior under Treynor because Portfolio Y is poorly diversified.
C. Portfolio Y is superior under both Sharpe and Treynor metrics because it has a higher reward per unit of total risk and systematic risk.
D. Portfolio X is superior under Sharpe, but Portfolio Y is superior under Treynor because Portfolio X has a higher Beta.

Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
To determine the superior portfolio, we calculate both the Sharpe and Treynor ratios:
1. **Sharpe Ratios:**
   - Sharpe Portfolio X = (18% - 6%) / 16% = 12 / 16 = 0.75
   - Sharpe Portfolio Y = (15% - 6%) / 10% = 9 / 10 = 0.90
   - Ranking: Portfolio Y (0.90) > Portfolio X (0.75).
2. **Treynor Ratios:**
   - Treynor Portfolio X = (18% - 6%) / 1.2 = 12% / 1.2 = 10.00%
   - Treynor Portfolio Y = (15% - 6%) / 0.6 = 9% / 0.6 = 15.00%
   - Ranking: Portfolio Y (15.00%) > Portfolio X (10.00%).
Portfolio Y has both a higher Sharpe ratio and a higher Treynor ratio, making it superior under both metrics. Portfolio Y's lower Systematic Risk (Beta = 0.6) and lower Total Risk (SD = 10%) yield a higher risk-adjusted return, despite having a lower absolute return than Portfolio X.

Question 2
A hedge fund starts the year with assets under management (AUM) of ₹50.00 crores. The fund manager charges a 2% management fee on opening assets and a 20% incentive fee based on a hard hurdle rate of 10% (net of management fee) with a high-water mark rule. The high-water mark at the start of the year is ₹52.00 crores. The fund generates a gross return of 20% for the year before any fees. What is the total fee payable to the fund manager at the end of the year?
A. ₹1.00 crore
B. ₹1.72 crores
C. ₹1.40 crores
D. ₹1.80 crores

Correct Answer: D
Explanation:
We calculate the fees step-by-step:
1. **Management Fee:**
   - Management Fee = 2% of opening AUM (₹50.00 crores) = ₹1.00 crore.
2. **Fund Value before Incentive Fee:**
   - Gross Return = 20% of ₹50.00 crores = ₹10.00 crores.
   - Fund value before incentive fee = ₹50.00 crores + ₹10.00 crores - ₹1.00 crore (Mgmt Fee) = ₹59.00 crores.
3. **Hard Hurdle Threshold Value:**
   - Hurdle Return Amount = 10% of ₹50.00 crores = ₹5.00 crores.
   - Hurdle Threshold Value = ₹50.00 crores + ₹5.00 crores = ₹55.00 crores.
4. **Binding Constraint Check (High-Water Mark vs. Hurdle Value):**
   - High-Water Mark (HWM) = ₹52.00 crores.
   - Hurdle Value = ₹55.00 crores.
   - Since the Hurdle Value (₹55.00 crores) is higher than the HWM (₹52.00 crores), it acts as the binding constraint to beat.
5. **Incentive Fee Calculation:**
   - Eligible Profit above Hurdle Value = ₹59.00 crores - ₹55.00 crores = ₹4.00 crores.
   - Incentive Fee = 20% of ₹4.00 crores = ₹0.80 crore.
6. **Total Fee:**
   - Total Fee = Management Fee + Incentive Fee = ₹1.00 crore + ₹0.80 crore = ₹1.80 crores.

Question 3
An open-ended mutual fund scheme has 200 lakh units outstanding with an opening NAV of ₹15.00 on January 1st. In January, the fund earns ₹10.00 lakhs of net income. At the end of January, the fund issues 10 lakh units at the opening NAV plus an entry load of 2.0%, adjusted for dividend equalization. What is the total cash received by the scheme from the new unit issue?
A. ₹150.00 lakhs
B. ₹153.50 lakhs
C. ₹150.50 lakhs
D. ₹155.00 lakhs

Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
To calculate the total cash received, we find the issue price per unit:
1. **Dividend Equalization (DER) Component for January:**
   - January Income = ₹10.00 lakhs
   - Outstanding Units before issue = 200 lakhs
   - DER component per unit = ₹10.00 lakhs / 200 lakhs units = ₹0.05 per unit.
2. **Entry Load:**
   - Entry Load = 2.0% of Opening NAV (₹15.00) = ₹0.30 per unit.
3. **Issue Price per unit:**
   - Issue Price = Opening NAV + Entry Load + DER component
   - Issue Price = 15.00 + 0.30 + 0.05 = ₹15.35 per unit.
4. **Total Cash Received:**
   - Total Cash = 10 lakh units * ₹15.35 = ₹153.50 lakhs.
(Out of this, ₹150.00 lakhs goes to Unit Capital, ₹0.50 lakhs goes to the Dividend Equalization Reserve, and ₹3.00 lakhs is the entry load).

Question 4
On March 31, 2026, a mutual fund scheme has listed corporate bonds with an acquisition cost of ₹800.00 lakhs. The bonds are quoted at a 4% discount in the market. The scheme also holds unlisted equities with an acquisition cost of ₹200.00 lakhs, which are valued at cost. The accrued management fee is ₹12.00 lakhs. The scheme has 50 lakh units outstanding. What is the NAV per unit of the scheme, assuming the initial net assets were solely comprised of the acquisition costs of these investments?
A. ₹20.00
B. ₹18.40
C. ₹19.12
D. ₹18.16

Correct Answer: C
Explanation:
We value the portfolio assets and liabilities:
1. **Bonds Market Value:**
   - Bonds Cost = ₹800.00 lakhs
   - Market Quote = 4% discount
   - Market Value = ₹800.00 * 0.96 = ₹768.00 lakhs.
2. **Unlisted Equities Value:**
   - Equities Cost = ₹200.00 lakhs
   - Valuation = Valued at cost = ₹200.00 lakhs.
3. **Total Assets:**
   - Total Assets = ₹768.00 + ₹200.00 = ₹968.00 lakhs.
4. **Total Liabilities:**
   - Accrued Expenses = ₹12.00 lakhs.
5. **Net Assets:**
   - Net Assets = Total Assets - Liabilities = ₹968.00 - ₹12.00 = ₹956.00 lakhs.
6. **NAV per unit:**
   - Outstanding Units = 50 lakhs.
   - NAV = ₹956.00 lakhs / 50 lakhs units = ₹19.12 per unit.
Thus, the NAV per unit of the scheme is ₹19.12, which matches Option C.

Question 5
An investor is comparing Gold ETFs and Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) for a tax-efficient, 8-year investment. Which of the following statements is true under current Indian tax laws?
A. Gold ETFs are more tax-efficient because capital gains on maturity are completely exempt from tax.
B. Sovereign Gold Bonds are more tax-efficient because capital gains upon redemption at maturity are exempt from tax, and they pay an additional 2.50% interest p.a.
C. Both Gold ETFs and SGBs are exempt from capital gains tax if held for more than 5 years.
D. Gold ETFs are exempt from tax on maturity, but SGB interest is subject to 15% withholding tax.

Correct Answer: B
Explanation:
Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) have a major tax advantage for long-term investors:
1. **Tax Exemption on Capital Gains:** Under Section 47(viib) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, capital gains tax arising to an individual on redemption of SGBs at maturity (8 years) is completely exempt.
2. **Interest Income:** SGBs pay an additional interest of 2.50% p.a. on the nominal value (taxable at slab rate).
3. **Gold ETFs:** Gold ETFs are treated as non-equity mutual funds for tax purposes. Capital gains on Gold ETFs are taxed at the investor's marginal slab rates, without indexation benefits, regardless of the holding period. Therefore, SGBs are significantly more tax-efficient and yield higher returns.
